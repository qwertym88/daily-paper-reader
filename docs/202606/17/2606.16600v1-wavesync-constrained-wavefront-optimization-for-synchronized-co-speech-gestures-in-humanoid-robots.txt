Abstract: Expressive co-speech gestures are crucial for natural human–robot interaction, but generating them on physi-cal humanoid robots is difficult because gesture strokes must align with speech emphasis while satisfying strict kinematic and dynamic constraints. Unlike virtual avatars, humanoid robots cannot freely execute rapid or overlapping motions, making word-level synchronization and hardware-safe motion planning a coupled problem. We present WaveSync , a hybrid framework in which a Large Language Model decomposes dialogue responses into structured semantic schemas and assigns per-word importance weights, constructing a continuous Semantic Importance Wave. Gesture trajectories are shaped through Dynamic Movement Primitives, enforcing kinematic feasibility while enhancing expressiveness. A Wave-front Optimization stage aligns peak-to-peak gesture–speech synchronization and resolves residual kinematic violations through gesture-duration compression and forward propagation. Experimental evaluation based on five dialogue scenar-ios shows that our method achieves high synchronization accuracy and outperforms three baselines in both objective and subjective evaluations. Each component in WaveSync plays a necessary role in producing gestures that are expressive, semantically grounded, and kinematically compliant. The code, resources, and videos are available at WaveSync 

Keywords: Human-Robot Interaction, Body Language, Large Language Model, Dynamic Motion Primitives. 

1. INTRODUCTION 

In Human–Robot Interaction (HRI), expressive body language is essential to make robotic communication feel natural and engaging [1, 2]. Many studies show that human-like gestures and kinematic cues clearly increase social presence and user trust [3]. Successful applica-tions include humanoid robots [4, 5], robotic arms [6], zoomorphic platforms [7], and unmanned aerial vehicles [8]. On these platforms, nonverbal signals such as pos-ture, gesture, and movement timing help users understand a robot’s intent and build trust-based interactions [9]. Generating expressive motion usually involves three stages: defining the gesture, selecting actions from the conversational context, and synchronizing those actions with speech. Researchers have proposed three main ap-proaches to handle this pipeline. Rule-based systems [10, 11] are stable but lack flexibility. They compute ges-tures offline from fixed templates and cannot adapt to live prosody. Data-driven models [3,4] increase gesture diver-sity. However, they often lack enough emphatic gestures in their training data and can produce trajectories that vi-olate physical hardware limits. Finally, Large Language Model (LLM)-based planners [12, 13] provide strong se-mantic grounding. However, they can hallucinate joint configurations and create latency that undermines real-time responsiveness [14]. The third stage, synchronization, is critical for nat-uralness [15] but remains the least addressed. Exist-ing state-of-the-art co-speech gesture generation methods mainly focus on virtual avatars [16, 17]. However, physi-cal robots are critically different from avatars. Although avatars have unconstrained kinematics, physical robots operate under strict structural and motor velocity limits. They cannot execute the rapid, high-frequency motions that avatars easily perform. Meanwhile, the few studies deployed on physical platforms rely on simple duration matching: stretching the audio or changing the keyframe speed so both streams just end at the same time [4]. This method ignores sub-utterance structure. As a result, the stress peaks in speech fail to align with gesture stroke maxima, which weakens the synchronization. To address this gap, this paper introduces WaveSync .This framework leverages an LLM to assign importance weights to each word, and these are then combined with Whisper-aligned [18] timestamps to construct a Seman-tic Importance Wave (SIW). Gesture trajectories are then shaped by Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) [19], which automatically adapt timing and adjust motion style while preserving the original movement. Finally, a Wave-front Optimization module places these gestures onto the SIW. It uses a crossover rule to prevent consecutive ges-tures from overlapping at their peaks. Additionally, a Forward Propagation mechanism fixes remaining con-flicts by adding natural pauses instead of distorting the speech signal. This approach achieves precise peak-to-peak alignment between vocal stress and gesture strokes within the robot’s physical constraints. The core contri-butions of this work are as follows:  

> •

A hybrid architecture that converts LLM-driven se-mantic intent into robot motions via DMPs to circumvent the risks associated with generating raw joint angles.

2. METHODOLOGY 

We present the WaveSync pipeline in three stages, il-lustrated in Fig. 1. First, an LLM assigns per-word impor-tance weights that are fused with Whisper-aligned times-tamps into an SIW. Second, retrieved gestures are adapted via DMPs for time and amplitude scaling. Finally, a wavefront optimizer aligns each stroke peak to its cor-responding SIW peak within the robot’s kinematic limits.

3. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

3.1 Experimental Setup 

All experiments are conducted in PyBullet with a hu-manoid upper-body model. Speech is synthesized of-fline via a neural TTS engine and streamed to the LLM pipeline. The gesture library contains clips covering beat, deictic, and iconic gesture categories, captured from human demonstrations and retargeted to the robot via SMPL-based kinematic mapping. Clips are labeled with semantic tags that the LLM uses to retrieve the appropri-ate motion for each prosodic segment. 

Dialog Scenarios. Five scenarios (S1–S5) spanning the principal communicative functions in HRI are evalu-ated (Table 1). Table 1.: Dialogue scenarios used in the evaluations.

> S1 Greeting Hello everyone, welcome to our research space.
> S2 Casual This issue may seem small, but if overlooked, it leaves significant consequences.
> S3 Descriptive When mentioning elephants, the first impression is they are enormous:they reach very high and their bod-ies are extremely large.
> S4 Warning Attention! You absolutely must not step over this safety line.
> S5 Thinking Wait a moment. . . Let’s see what we can do.

Ablation Baselines. Three ablated variants isolate the contribution of each WaveSync component:  

> •

w/o SIW : Gestures trigger uniformly at word bound-aries. This variant disables peak-to-peak alignment with the important words.  

> •

w/o DMP : Pre-recorded keyframes replace the DMP execution. This removes kinematic shape modulation and time-scaling ( τ ).  

> •

w/o Wavefront : Removes the hierarchical scheduler; conflicting gestures are hard-clipped or reset to rest pose, with no actuator-limit awareness or state-space blending.

4. CONCLUSION 

We presented WaveSync, a system that synchronizes expressive robot gestures with speech under kinematic and semantic constraints. By combining Semantic Impor-tance Weights, DMP-based trajectory editing, and wave-front optimization, the system achieved accurate peak-to-peak alignment between speech emphasis and ges-ture strokes. This alignment was done without chang-ing the speech rhythm or violating robot physical lim-its. Experiments across five dialogue scenarios showed that WaveSync outperformed all ablated variants across all perceptual dimensions. The ablation study confirmed that each component is necessary. However, the sys-tem depends on a fixed action library, limiting cover-age of unconstrained dialogue. Future work will ad-dress this through automatic gesture synthesis, and test-ing WaveSync on real robot hardware remains our next critical step.
