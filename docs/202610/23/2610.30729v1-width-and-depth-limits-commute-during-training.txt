WIDTH AND DEPTH LIMITS COMMUTE DURING
TRAINING IN LINEAR RESIDUAL NETWORKS
Ruijia Zhang
Soufiane Hayou
Department of Applied Mathematics and Statistics
Johns Hopkins University
ABSTRACT
Recent work showed that width and depth limits in residual networks with branches scaled with
1/√depth commute at initialization: no matter how those limits are taken (e.g. width first, then
depth, or depth first, then width, or with a fixed width/depth ratio, etc), the network output converges
to the same limit at initialization. In this paper, we extend this result to commutativity during training
for linear residual networks: we show that under the same 1/√depth branch scaling (Depth-µP)
and with µP parameterization for the width, for any fixed number of gradient steps with gradient
clipping, the network output converges in L2 as min{width, depth} →∞, implying commutativity
of the width and depth limits during training. We further show that such result does not hold without
gradient clipping, and a weaker result can be obtained in that case under the weaker convergence in
probability.
Keywords Width–Depth Commutativity · µP · Neural Network Scaling Limits

Introduction
Modern neural networks are increasingly scaled along multiple architectural dimensions. The rapid growth and
success of large language and vision models has further motivated the study of neural network scaling limits as a
way to understand how model behavior changes with scale [1, 2]. Among these limits, width and depth play distinct
roles: increasing width gives rise to asymptotic regimes such as neural tangent kernel and mean-field limits [3, 4, 5],
while increasing depth connects residual networks to many continuous-time dynamics [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14].
Understanding how these two limits interact is therefore fundamental when both dimensions grow simultaneously.
A central question is whether the width n and depth L limits commute, that is, whether the same asymptotic behavior is
obtained regardless of the order or relative rate at which the two dimensions scale. For residual networks with branches
scaled by L−1/2, Hayou and Yang [15] showed that the width and depth limits commute at initialization, so the same
limit is obtained along arbitrary joint width–depth scaling paths. This leaves open whether commutativity persists
during training. At initialization, the residual weights are independent across layers, a property used in existing joint
commutativity analysis [15, 16]. During training, gradient descent couples the weights across layers. As a result, the
arguments developed for initialization output convergence cannot directly extend. It is therefore unclear whether the
width and depth limits continue to commute under the same L−1/2 branch scaling.
We first show that standard gradient descent exhibits a sharp distinction between convergence modes: after a single step,
the width and depth limits of the trained output commute in probability, but not in L2. Specifically, we show that for
every fixed width n, the trained output converges in probability as L →∞, while its second moment diverges in the
large-depth limit. This implies that commutativity cannot hold in L2, but we show that it holds in probability. We then
show that, under clipped gradient descent, the width and depth limits commute in L2 for any fixed number of training
steps, with convergence rates n−1/2 in width and L−1/2 in depth.
Our proof proceeds by taking the two limits in sequence. We first establish the depth limit at finite width by comparing
the trained residual network with a continuous-depth model, with estimates uniform in width, which allow us to then
take the infinite-width limit of this continuous-depth system and identify the deterministic trained output.

Related Work.
Infinite-width limits have been studied through Gaussian-process, neural tangent kernel, and mean-
field descriptions [3, 4, 17, 18]. Similarly, infinite-depth limits of residual networks were studied as continuous-time
descriptions of network dynamics [6, 8, 12, 11, 13, 14], and more heuristic analyses of training dynamics [10, 13]. The
interaction between these two limits was studied in [16, 15], who proved width–depth commutativity for the neural
covariance of ResNets with residual branches scaled by L−1/2. Hayou [16] extended this analysis to more general
residual scalings, while non-commutative limits have been identified for some MLP and ResNet parameterizations
[19, 20]. More recently, Chizat [14] studied the large-depth training limit of ResNets scaled with L−1 branch multiplier
at fixed network width. Chaintron et al. [21] is arguably the closest work to our paper. The authors showed that
commutativity of the width and depth limits holds during training with L−1 branch scaling. Such scaling leads to
ODE-style infinite-depth dynamics, which is substantially different from the SDE-style dynamics obtained with L−1/2.
Our work establishes commutativity of the width and depth limits during training under the L−1/2 branch scaling
consistent with Depth-µP [10]. These two scaling choices lead to significantly different dynamics and therefore require
different proof machinery to establish commutative limits.

6
Conclusion
We studied whether width and depth limits continue to commute during training in linear residual networks. Under
ordinary gradient descent, the limits commute in probability after any fixed number of training iterations, while the
depth-first L2 limit already fails after one step at every fixed finite width. Under clipped gradient descent, we prove
commutativity in L2 for any fixed number of training iterations, with errors of order n−1/2 and L−1/2.

Lazy Gaussian implementation.
The largest synthetic model has (n, L) = (61,440, 3,072), for which explicitly
storing the residual matrices in float32 would require approximately 46.4 TB of memory. We instead use an exact-in-law

matrix–vector products without materializing the residual matrices. This reduces the peak GPU memory usage to 46.9
GB and makes the largest experiments computationally feasible.
