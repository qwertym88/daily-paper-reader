# DiffDreamer: Coupling World Models with Differentiable Simulation for Agile Quadrotor Control 

Fanxing Li, Yuxiang Huang, Shengyang Wang, Fangyu Sun, Shuyu Wu, Tianbao Zhang, Dexin Zuo, Yan Yufei, Danping Zou, Wenxian Yu 

Abstract —Tasks such as quadrotor dynamic-gate racing re-quire agents to anticipate external motion while maintaining pre-cise high-frequency control. World-model-based reinforcement learning (RL) provides a natural solution by learning latent dynamics for future imagination, yet it often entangles environ-mental evolution and the robot’s own dynamics in a single learned rollout, where model bias can affect policy optimization efficiency. We propose DiffDreamer, a differentiable reinforcement learning framework that tightly couples a learned world model with differentiable simulation for actor optimization. In one opti-mization step, the learned world model propagates latent states that capture external dynamics and history-dependent features, while the differentiable simulator propagates the robot’s explicit physical state. We further decompose the actor objective into a critic-driven pathway for non-differentiable task rewards and a simulator-driven pathway for differentiable motion rewards. Experiments on aggressive tracking and dynamic-gate racing show that DiffDreamer improves training convergence over the model-free, model-based, and differentiable-RL baselines, and final performance over these baselines and classical planning and control methods. Additional experiments assess reward decompo-sition and the odometry-conditioned relative-state representation through ablations. We also provide proof-of-concept real-world flight experiments for validation. 

Index Terms —Differentiable Simulation, Reinforcement Learn-ing, World Model. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Many robotic tasks require agents to anticipate external en-vironmental evolution to achieve precise control. This structure is particularly prominent in agile aerial robotics. A represen-tative example is dynamic-gate racing with quadrotors [1]– [3], where the robot must predict the future motion of moving gates and generate accurate control commands to pass through narrow openings at high speed. Existing solutions [2] often address this challenge through modular pipelines that decouple prediction, planning, and control. Although such designs are interpretable and effective, they usually rely on task-specific engineering and optimize each module separately. World-model-based reinforcement learning [4], [5] provides an integrated alternative. By learning compact latent dynamics from interaction, methods such as Dreamer [4] can imagine future trajectories and optimize policies in latent space. How-ever, in agile robotic control, a learned world model often has to capture both the evolution of the external environment and the robot’s high-frequency rigid-body dynamics within the same learned rollout. This entangles two sources of dynamics with different structures: the external dynamics are partially observable and history-dependent, whereas the robot’s internal dynamics are often smooth and available through physical modeling. As a result, policy optimization through purely learned rollouts may suffer from model bias and less accurate gradients for precise physical control. This motivates using differentiable simulation for the robot’s physical motion, so that the learned world model can focus on latent external dynamics. Differentiable reinforcement learning (DiffRL) [6], [7] of-fers a complementary direction. By backpropagating through a differentiable physical model, it can provide low-variance first-order gradients for policy optimization and be particu-larly effective for smooth robotic systems. However, differ-entiable RL alone typically assumes that the transition and reward structures are differentiable and explicitly available. This makes it difficult to handle partially observable external dynamics, moving targets with unknown future motion, and non-differentiable task events such as gate-crossing rewards. To address this problem, we propose DiffDreamer, which structurally separates robot dynamics from external environ-ment dynamics and couples their rollouts for policy opti-mization. DiffDreamer synchronizes latent world-model imag-ination with differentiable physical rollouts: under the same action, the world model updates an implicit state representing external time-varying features, while the simulator propagates the explicit robot state. The actor receives both states as input, and its objective is decomposed into a critic-driven pathway for non-differentiable task rewards and a simulator-driven pathway for differentiable motion rewards. We compare DiffDreamer with RL, trajectory planning, and model predictive control (MPC) baselines on aggressive tracking and dynamic-gate racing, and demonstrate its effectiveness through transfer to real quadrotor flight. A. Agile Quadrotor Flight and Modular Pipelines 

Agile quadrotor flight, including autonomous racing, typ-ically relies on modular pipelines for perception, state es-timation, trajectory planning, and low-level control under tight real-time constraints [8]. In dynamic environments with moving targets or gates, these pipelines often use motion-prediction modules to support downstream planning and con-trol. Representative approaches combine learned gate per-ception with trajectory generation and tracking [1], learned data abstraction with nonlinear filtering and time-optimal planning [9], or model-predictive and time-optimal control for aggressive waypoint or gate traversal [10]–[12]. Policy search can also tune model-predictive controllers for agile flight [2]. Elastic Tracker combines target-motion prediction with visibility-aware spatio-temporal trajectory optimization for aerial tracking [13]. These modular pipelines are effective and interpretable, especially when the environment structure and task objectives are well specified. However, their modules are usually designed and optimized separately, so prediction or planning errors may propagate to downstream control. 

B. Model-based Reinforcement Learning 

Model-based reinforcement learning uses learned or known dynamics models for planning and policy optimization, sup-porting sample-efficient end-to-end policy learning. Model-free methods learn directly from interaction with the envi-ronment and typically require more data [14], [15]. Classical and modern approaches have explored probabilistic dynamics and uncertainty-aware planning, such as PILCO and PETS [16], [17], short model-generated rollouts for value/policy learning, such as MBPO [18], and latent dynamics for planning or imagination-based actor-critic learning, such as MuZero, Dreamer, and TD-MPC [4], [5], [19]–[22]. Despite their sample efficiency, model-based methods re-main sensitive to model fidelity, especially in high-accuracy and high-frequency robotic control [23]. In agile quadrotor tasks, a single learned rollout must often capture both partially observable external motion and fast rigid-body dynamics, which introduces distinct learning challenges. III. D. DiffDreamer: Learning Behaviors from Imagination and Prediction 

Differentiable RL has shown strong sample efficiency in smooth robotic control but requires differentiable dynamics and rewards within the optimization graph. In many dynamic robotic tasks, a calibrated differentiable simulator can model or approximate ego dynamics, while separate prediction or IEEE ROBOTICS AND AUTOMATION LETTERS 4

These external models are feasible and often effective, but are usually optimized independently of the control policy, which can contribute to prediction-control mismatch and error propagation at high speeds. This separation is consistent with the intuitive process of predicting how the external world may evolve while planning body motion using an internal physical model. For example, humans catching an incoming ball anticipate its trajectory while coordinating their body motion, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Based on this separation, we introduce DiffDreamer , a unified framework coupling predictive latent imagination with differentiable physical prediction. The implicit pathway uses a learned world model to encode history-dependent external dynamics and predict future latent states. The explicit pathway propagates the robot’s physical state under the same action through a differentiable simulator, providing gradients for differentiable motion rewards. Actor training through these synchronized pathways combines the latent imagination of world-model-based RL with DiffRL’s physical precision.

We implement both benchmarks in VisFly [27], a high-fidelity quadrotor simulator with explicit differentiable physics written in PyTorch. Aggressive Tracking requires the quadro-tor to continuously track a high-speed target, keeping it centered in the field of view (FOV) at a stable relative dis-tance. Dynamic-gate Racing requires the quadrotor to traverse moving gates sequentially in the shortest possible time. The quadrotor’s explicit state sexp = {p, v, q, Ω} com-prises position, velocity, orientation, and angular velocity. We use the full nonlinear rigid-body dynamics in Eq. The policy outputs end-to-end collective thrust and body rate (CTBR) commands at = [ f dt , Ωdt ], where Ωdt = [ ωdx, ω dy , ω dz ],providing the control authority needed for aggressive ma-neuvers. C. Training Results 

On both benchmarks, we train DiffDreamer, DreamerV3, proximal policy optimization (PPO), and backpropagation through time (BPTT), evaluating each across five random seeds to account for statistical variance. Fig. 5 shows the training reward curves. All methods share the same obser-vations, rewards, and uniform randomization of target trajec-tories, speeds, and initial states, with fixed identified physics parameters. Baselines are tuned individually, while methods in the same methodological family share core hyperparameters, including prediction horizons for differentiable methods. The baselines and DiffDreamer share a similar training architecture: DreamerV3 learns the full latent rollout through its world model from these observations; BPTT uses the dif-ferentiable quadrotor simulator and differentiable reward terms without a learned latent-imagination pathway; DiffDreamer combines both pathways, using the world model to predict external dynamics and the differentiable simulator to propagate the quadrotor’s physical state. DiffDreamer converges faster and achieves higher final rewards than the baselines in both tasks, with larger gains in dynamic-gate racing. This suggests that coupled latent imagination and differentiable rollouts particularly benefit tasks requiring both future prediction and precise optimization of robot motion.

F. Real-World Experiments 

We evaluate the sim-to-real performance of DiffDreamer policies for both tasks on a custom-built quadrotor with 3.5-inch propellers, an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX onboard com-puter, and a Betaflight flight controller. The trained policies run onboard at over 100 Hz, sending collective thrust and IEEE ROBOTICS AND AUTOMATION LETTERS 8

body rate (CTBR) commands to the flight controller. An external motion-capture system provides odometry for the quadrotor and moving targets/gates, allowing us to evaluate DiffDreamer’s control and planning with external state feed-back. We carefully identify the quadrotor’s physical parame-ters and incorporate them into the differentiable simulator to reduce the sim-to-real gap. All experiments use bounded thrust commands for safety in a controlled indoor environment. The successful trajectories in Fig. 3 provide proof-of-concept evi-dence of transfer to physical flight under calibrated dynamics and motion-capture state feedback. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented DiffDreamer, a differentiable reinforcement learning framework for agile quadrotor tasks that require both external-motion prediction and precise physical control. During actor optimization, DiffDreamer couples two rollouts: the learned world model propagates latent states capturing external dynamics and history-dependent features, while the differentiable simulator propagates the robot’s explicit physical state. This separation reduces the burden of learning high-frequency robot dynamics within the world model. The actor objective is decomposed into a critic-driven pathway for non-differentiable task rewards and a simulator-driven pathway for differentiable motion rewards. Experiments on aggressive tracking and dynamic-gate racing show improved training con-vergence over model-free, model-based, and differentiable-RL baselines, and higher final performance than these baselines and classical planning and control methods. Ablation studies assess the contributions of reward decomposition and the odometry-conditioned relative-state representation. Real-world flight experiments further provide proof-of-concept validation. VI. L IMITATIONS AND DISCUSSION 

DiffDreamer has been evaluated only on quadrotors. Ex-tending its coupled latent/physical rollouts and reward decom-position to other embodiments requires suitable differentiable models and additional task-specific engineering. Contacts, discontinuities, actuator limits, and perception modules can complicate differentiation [29]. In addition, real-world valida-tion relies on motion-capture feedback for both the vehicle and external objects. Fully onboard perception and robustness to perception noise remain untested.
