A TRANSFORMER-BASED APPROACH FOR REAL-TIME MULTI-AGENT PATH PLANNING WITH VARYING INITIAL/FINAL CONDITIONS 

QIAN ZHANG, SHANQING LIU, JEROME DARBON, AND GEORGE KARNIADAKIS ∗

Abstract. We address the problem of real-time multi-agent path planning under pre-scribed initial and final configurations, formulated as a high-dimensional optimal control problem subject to inter-agent collision avoidance and obstacle constraints. Classical solvers based on the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation or Pontryagin’s maximum principle (PMP) become computationally intractable as the number of agents grows, due to the curse of dimensionality and the non-convexity introduced by interaction constraints. We propose OCFormer, a transformer-based neural network that parameterizes the inverse of a coordi-nate transformation mapping a simple, analytically tractable latent space, in which agents evolve independently under a decoupled Hamiltonian, to the physical space of the coupled, interacting system. The model is trained by minimizing a physics-informed loss compris-ing the Hamiltonian ODE residual from the PMP and a boundary matching term, without requiring pre-computed trajectory data. The self-attention mechanism endows OCFormer with permutation equivariance over agents, a structural symmetry that prior symplectic network approaches do not natively enforce. When trained on a distribution of boundary conditions, the amortized OCFormer produces near-optimal trajectories for unseen initial and final configurations in a single forward pass ( ∼220 ms), enabling real-time inference without retraining. We further introduce a lightweight transfer learning framework that adapts a pretrained OCFormer to out-of-distribution perturbations in seconds via L-BFGS fine-tuning. Numerical experiments on two- and three-dimensional multi-agent navigation with up to 128 agents demonstrate the scalability and efficiency of the proposed approach. 

1. Introduction 

1.1. Motivation and Context. Optimal control problems arise in a wide range of appli-cations, from robotics and autonomous navigation to multi-agent coordination and financial optimization. The goal is to steer a dynamical system from prescribed initial conditions to a desired final state while minimizing a cost functional. Two complementary theoreti-cal frameworks underpin numerical approaches to these problems: Bellman’s dynamic pro-gramming principle [Bel57], which characterizes the value function as the unique viscosity solution of the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation, and Pontryagin’s maximum prin-ciple (PMP) [AS13, Tr´ e05], which gives first-order necessary optimality conditions through the introduction of a co-state variable. Despite their theoretical power, both frameworks face severe computational challenges as the state dimension grows. Grid-based methods for the HJB equation suffer from the curse of dimensionality [Bel57]: their complexity scales exponentially with the number of state variables, making them infeasible beyond a few di-mensions. Indirect shooting methods based on the PMP avoid explicit gridding of the state space but require repeated numerical integration of the Hamiltonian system and are highly

In multi-agent path planning, where M agents with individual state dimension dx yield a joint state of dimension M d x, these difficulties are compounded by the need to enforce inter-agent collision avoidance constraints that introduce non-convexity and long-range coupling among the agents, rendering classical solvers impractical for real-time use at scale. To overcome these scalability limits, deep learning has motivated mesh-free methods that parametrize solutions directly with neural networks, avoiding explicit state-space discretiza-tion. Building on early residual-based parametrizations of differential equations [LLF98], high-dimensional extensions such as the Deep Ritz method [EY18] and backward-stochastic-differential-equation reformulations [HJE18] scaled these ideas to hundreds of dimensions, and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) [RPK19] unified them by encoding governing equations and boundary conditions as soft loss constraints, a paradigm that has since grown into the broader field of scientific machine learning [ZSX +22, ZKKS24, TZK +24, ZWK +25, ZKK25, KZGK26, WZK26, ZK26]. In the optimal control setting, the PMP’s Hamiltonian ODE system provides a natural PINN residual; SympOCNet [MZDK22] pioneered this for multi-agent path planning by pairing a symplectic architecture [JZZ +20] with PINN train-ing, later extended to time-dependent and non-convex settings [ZWL +24]. However, these architectures process agents in a fixed sequential order and impose a possibly-unnecessary symplectic constraint. Neither property reflects the permutation symmetry inherent to multi-agent problems. Beyond the symplectic-PINN line of work, several alternative paradigms merit mention: optimal-transport-based continuous normalizing flows for mean-field control [OWFLR21], adaptive deep learning for the HJB equation [NZGK21], and Hopf–Lax representation for-mulas that solve Hamilton–Jacobi equations without spatial gridding [DO16, LCO18]. In the multi-robot community, graph neural networks [LGRP20] target decentralized colli-sion avoidance with a similar permutation-equivariance motivation but distributed rather than centralized optimality. Classical planners such as ORCA [VDBGLM11], conflict-based search [SSFS15], and sequential convex programming [ASD12] remain practical workhorses that trade global optimality for tractability. The transformer architecture [VSP +17] directly addresses this gap. Its self-attention mech-anism computes pairwise interactions among all sequence elements simultaneously, captur-ing long-range dependencies and permutation symmetries that convolutional or recurrent architectures handle only approximately, and it is theoretically a universal approximator of continuous, permutation-equivariant sequence-to-sequence functions [YBR +19], consis-tent with its empirical dominance across NLP [DCLT19, BMR +20] and vision [DBK +21]. This permutation-equivariant structure is especially relevant for multi-agent problems, where agents are interchangeable and a solution method should be invariant to their indexing, al-lowing a transformer to learn collective, collision-avoidance behaviors that generalize across agent configurations without a fixed ordering. 1.2. Contribution. In this work we propose OCFormer, a transformer-based framework that addresses multi-agent optimal control by combining the structural inductive biases of attention with physics-informed training. The central idea is to learn a parameterized inverse coordinate transformation ˜ϕθ that maps analytically tractable latent trajectories, corresponding to non-interacting agents with a decoupled Hamiltonian, to the physical tra-jectories of the interacting system. OCFormer is built from alternating transformer blocks A TRANSFORMER FOR MULTI-AGENT PATH PLANNING 3

that mirror the leapfrog structure of St¨ ormer–Verlet integration [JZZ +20], and is trained by minimizing a physics-informed loss comprising the Hamiltonian ODE residual from the PMP together with a boundary matching term enforcing prescribed initial and final config-urations. Unlike SympOCNet [MZDK22], OCFormer is not constrained to be symplectic; instead, it achieves permutation equivariance through the self-attention mechanism, which is essential for generalization over varying agent counts and configurations. Once trained on a distribution of boundary conditions, OCFormer infers near-optimal trajectories for un-seen initial and final configurations in a single forward pass ( ∼220 ms), enabling real-time multi-agent path planning. We further develop a transfer learning strategy that efficiently adapts a pretrained OCFormer to perturbed boundary conditions, avoiding costly retraining from scratch. The framework is validated on two- and three-dimensional multi-agent navi-gation benchmarks, demonstrating competitive trajectory quality and significantly reduced inference time compared to existing methods. 2. Remark 2 (Symplecticity, permutation equivariance, and limitations) . Unlike SympOCNet [MZDK22] ,OCFormer is not constrained to be symplectic and is only invertible in the latent space. As shown in Proposition 1, the PINN loss directly enforces the Hamiltonian dynamics, making a structural symplectic guarantee unnecessary. OCFormer is permutation equivariant over agents: any permutation σ of the agent in-dices applied to the input produces the same permutation of the output. This follows directly from the architecture: the tokenwise embeddings E and deembedding MLPs D are applied in-dependently to each agent via vmap , the self-attention mechanism in Tup /Tlow is permutation-equivariant by construction, and the residual connection I preserves equivariance. Formally, if Pσ denotes the permutation matrix corresponding to σ, then ˜ϕθ(Pσx, P σp) = Pσ ˜ϕθ(x, p ).A notable failure mode arises from this design: with no positional encoding, two agents that share identical latent inputs (yi, q i) = ( yj , q j ) produce identical outputs and can never be sep-arated. This symmetry-breaking limitation is particularly relevant for the symmetric bench-mark in §5, where symmetry breaking must be driven by random weight initialization alone. For problems where agents are not interchangeable (e.g. the 3D room with role-differentiated targets in §5), we use PositionalOCFormer , a variant with per-agent weight matrices that deliberately breaks permutation equivariance. Amortized OCFormer: Training on a Distribution of Boundary Conditions. 

The per-instance training in §5.1.1 solves one pair of boundary conditions at a time. We now demonstrate that OCFormer can be amortized : trained on a distribution of boundary conditions and evaluated zero-shot on unseen pairs, without any architectural change. Conclusion and Future Work 

We have presented OCFormer, a transformer-based neural network for multi-agent optimal control that parameterizes the inverse of a coordinate transformation from a decoupled latent space to the physical interacting system. Trained with a physics-informed loss combining Hamiltonian ODE residuals and boundary matching, OCFormer solves the two-point bound-ary value problem for fixed initial and final configurations without requiring pre-computed trajectory data. A lightweight transfer-learning framework adapts a pretrained model to rigid-body transformations and random perturbations of the boundary conditions, requiring only seconds of L-BFGS fine-tuning rather than full retraining. Our numerical experiments demonstrate that OCFormer produces collision-free trajecto-ries on 2D and 3D multi-agent benchmarks with up to 128 agents, achieving costs within a few percent of rigorous lower bounds. The permutation-equivariant architecture provides a useful inductive bias for symmetric problems, while its positional variant handles asymmetric settings. The amortized OCFormer demonstrated in §5.2 confirms that zero-shot generalization is achievable with no architectural change, but the current training is limited to rotated versions of a fixed configuration. Scaling amortized training to broader distributions (e.g., arbitrary start/goal pairs, varying agent counts) and improving feasibility at higher densities are natural next steps. Other directions include development of a symplecticity-preserving OCFormer variant, extension to general nonlinear dynamics fi(xi) + Biui beyond the current ˙x = v setting and integration with feedback control for online replanning.

Up to rare cases, the system (25) can only be solved approximately using numerical meth-ods.
