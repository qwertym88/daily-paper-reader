# Constrained Koopman Control Parametrization: Convex Controller Synthesis for Tracking under Input Constraints 

Taha Ondogan, Ran Jing, Andrew P. Sabelhaus, and Roberto Tron 

Abstract — Koopman operator theory represents nonlinear systems with linear and bilinear terms in the state and input observables, greatly simplifying control design. However, incorporating input constraints into Koopman-based controller synthesis remains challenging, limiting their applicability to constrained control tasks such as tracking. This paper develops a constrained Koopman Control Parametrization (cKoCoP) framework for synthesizing state-feedback controllers that satisfy prescribed input constraints with Lyapunov-certified stability of the identified lifted closed loop. The cKoCoP offers nonlinear state-feedback via linear-in-observables feedback, enabling linear controller design. The proposed controller is obtained through convex optimization: we incorporate ellipsoidal or polytopic input constraints with the S-procedure or Farkas-lemma-based conditions. We also introduce a Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) for trajectory tracking which incorporates input con-straints. The approach is validated on two canonical nonlinear systems, a double pendulum and a unicycle, demonstrating data-driven tracking while respecting input constraints. Experiments validate the method’s robustness to measurement noise, and runtime improvements over Koopman Model Predictive Control (KMPC) baselines. The results demonstrate that cKoCoP achieves competitive tracking performance with substantially reduced online computational cost, supporting its potential for real-time constrained tracking applications. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Nonlinear dynamics arises in a wide range of robotic systems. However, nonlinear control and stability analyses are limited compared to linear systems, as linear systems offer simple-to-formulate closed form controllers (e.g., LQR [1]). To overcome limitations of locality and linearization while retaining the benefits of linear methods, Koopman operators transform a nonlinear system into a higher (potentially infinite) dimensional linear system. Koopman operator control methods have been adopted in robotics systems [2], [3], fluid systems [4], [5], and power systems [6]. However finding such high dimensional systems is often challenging, as the model is often not available, and we can only get approximations from data that often contain noise. Furthermore, it results in finite-dimensional approximation of the Koopman operator. Therefore, many algorithms utilize learning these systems from the data either with a preselected set of observables [7], [8], or deep learned observables [9]–[11]. Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) algorithm is widely used to fit

Koopman operators have been shown to capture long-term system evolution effectively [15], making them well suited for tracking applications. In this context, receding-horizon methods such as model predictive control (MPC) are widely adopted in the Koopman literature [8], [16], [17]. These approaches typically follow a two-stage framework: first, a data-driven linear Koopman model is identified using a set of predefined observables (such as EDMD); second, an MPC controller is designed and applied to the resulting linear controlled Koopman system approximation. In the standard EDMD-based setting, the resulting controller is often a linear MPC problem in the lifted coordinates. To improve modeling capability, [18] introduced bilinear Koopman system represen-tations within the MPC framework, enabling a broader class of input-affine nonlinear systems to be captured. This extension has demonstrated improved tracking performance in several settings, including nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and bilinear MPC (BMPC). Despite these advantages, such approaches remain computationally demanding, as they require solving an optimization problem over a finite prediction horizon at every control step. A crucial property desired in tracking control systems is the satisfaction of actuation constraints, especially since data-driven Koopman approximations may exhibit model mismatch relative to the actual nonlinear dynamics. While MPC naturally allows the incorporation of input constraints, it further increases the online computational complexity [19]. Moreover, even if the admissible set is convex in the original state space, its image under the nonlinear lifting map may become nonconvex or difficult to characterize explicitly, further complicating the use of MPC in the lifted Koopman coordinates. A preliminary version of this work proposed the Koopman Control Parametrization (KoCoP), a lifted-state feedback controller structure [20]. The method identifies a linear closed-loop structure in the lifted coordinates and synthesizes the controller through a convex optimization problem that is solved once offline. As a static state-feedback controller, KoCoP is computationally inexpensive compared with MPC formulations. However, the original KoCoP formulation does not account for actuation limits and is restricted to stabilization about the origin. Building on the preliminaries reviewed in section II and the original KoCoP construction summarized in section III, this paper makes the following contributions:

Then, an online CLF-based quadratic program is used to compute the tracking input under either polytopic or ellipsoidal actuation constraints.
