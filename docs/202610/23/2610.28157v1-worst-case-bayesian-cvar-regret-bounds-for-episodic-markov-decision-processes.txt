# Worst-Case Bayesian CVaR Regret Bounds for Episodic Markov Decision Processes 

Raghav Bongole, Amirreza Zamani, Tobias J. Oechtering, and Mikael Skoglund 

KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden, {bongole, amizam, oech, skoglund }@kth.se 

Abstract —We study the worst-case, over priors, Bayesian conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) of cumulative pseudo-regret in time-homogeneous finite-horizon episodic Markov decision processes. A high-probability guarantee for UCBVI-BF yields a prior-predictive upper bound. A lower bound follows by a three-state MDP and applying a reverse-KL inequality. For a constant number of states, the bounds have the same polynomial dependence on horizon, actions, and episodes, with a logarithmic gap in the risk level. 

Index Terms —Bayesian regret, conditional value-at-risk, episodic reinforcement learning, UCBVI, reverse KL 

I. I NTRODUCTION

T For the upper bound, we use the algorithm called UCBVI-BF, introduced by Azar et al. [1]. Although UCBVI-BF is a frequentist, prior-free algorithm, it remains a good candidate for the Bayesian optimization problem: for every prior, the best achievable Bayesian CVaR is no greater than that attained by any particular admissible algorithm. Moreover, the high-probability regret guarantee for UCBVI-BF holds uniformly over all MDPs in the class. We can therefore integrate its tail bound under an arbitrary prior and subsequently take the worst case over priors without changing the guarantee.

For the lower bound, we use the instantiation pattern for Bayesian CVaR converses in [2]. The three-state family is a bandit type construction simplified from [3]: only the first action is informative, and its Bernoulli outcome determines all later rewards. 

A. Contributions 

We give explicit upper and lower bounds for (1). The upper bound converts the UCBVI-BF tail guarantee into a bound uniform over priors. The lower bound transfers a bandit-like construction to episodic MDPs and proves the resulting Bayesian CVaR bound by reverse KL. To our knowledge, these are the first explicit bounds stated for the worst-case prior-predictive CVaR of episodic-MDP pseudo-regret. The arguments establish a baseline and isolate what a sharper, MDP-specific result must improve. In particular, for a constant number of states, the upper and lower bounds on worst-case Bayesian CVaR regret match in their polynomial de-pendence on H, A, and K, and differ only by a factor of plog(1 /(1 − α)) in their dependence on the risk level. Closing this logarithmic gap, and thereby obtaining a sharp characterization, remains an open problem. 

B. Related work a) Minimax regret for episodic MDPs: Azar et al. [1] introduced optimistic value-iteration algorithms for tabular finite-horizon MDPs. UCBVI-BF has a model-uniform high-probability bound with leading term eO(√HSAT ), T = KH ,matching their minimax lower-bound scale for this model class. Our argument uses its uniform tail statement. For the more general stage-dependent setting, Jin et al. [4] gave an optimistic Q-learning bound and discussed lower bounds built from the communicating-MDP construction of Jaksch et al. [5]. 

b) Information-theoretic bounds for interactive learning: 

Chen et al. [6] developed interactive Fano, a reference-law method unifying Le Cam, Assouad, classical Fano, and DEC-based converses for adaptive data. Their fractional covering number measures how well one exploration distribution covers near-optimal decisions; combined with the DEC, it yields high-probability ExO+ bounds. Those results concern frequentist minimax regret and quantiles, whereas our reverse-KL proof is a direct prior-predictive event transfer for a finite hard family giving rise to worst case Bayesian CVaR bounds. arXiv:2610.28157v1 [cs.IT] 20 Oct 2026 c) CVaR criteria: Risk-sensitive bandit and RL papers often place CVaR on a different random object. Tan and Weng [7] analyze the CVaR of bandit regret, while Wang et al. [8] and Du et al. [9] study environment-wise or nested CVaR objectives for returns. By contrast, [10] lower bounds CVaR under the joint prior-predictive law, and [2] instantiates this framework for Gaussian estimation and bandits. We apply that viewpoint to episodic-MDP pseudo-regret, take the worst case over priors, and strengthen the risk-level dependence on α

We established upper and lower bounds on worst-case Bayesian CVaR regret for episodic MDPs. The upper bound converts the high-probability guarantee of UCBVI-BF, while the converse applies reverse KL to a three-state MDP family. For fixed S, the bounds match polynomially in H, A, K ,but differ by a factor of plog(1 /(1 − α)) in their risk-level dependence. The lower bound also has no nontrivial S-dependence. Closing these gaps likely requires a multistage hard family in which uncertainty and regret accumulate across states and stages. AI S TATEMENT
