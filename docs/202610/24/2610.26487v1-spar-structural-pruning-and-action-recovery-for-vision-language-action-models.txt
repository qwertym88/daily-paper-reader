Abstract — The limited onboard computing resources of robots make the inference demands of vision-language-action (VLA) models costly to accommodate. Reducing these demands through aggressive compression, however, can degrade manip-ulation performance beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone recovers. We present SPAR (Structural Pruning and Action Recovery), which simultaneously prunes the hidden and feed-forward intermediate dimensions of the large language model (LLM) backbone through pruning-aware tuning, then applies reinforcement learning (RL) to recover policy performance. Applied to π0.5, SPAR reduces the language backbone from 2.34B to 0.09B parameters and the full model from 3.37B to 1.12B, removing approximately 96% and 67% of their parameters, respectively. At this compression level, RL raises average success across the four LIBERO suites from 84.55% to 93.45%, an 8.90-percentage-point gain. The compressed policy achieves a 1.98 × full-policy inference speedup over the un-pruned model. These results identify substantial intermediate-dimension redundancy and show that post-pruning RL can recover manipulation performance in compact policies. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models such as RT-2 [1], OpenVLA [2], and π0.5 [3] transfer pretrained representa-tions to robotic manipulation, but their inference demands strain limited onboard compute. In π0.5, the LLM accounts for 2.34B of 3.37B parameters. This motivates a central ques-tion: how much language-model capacity do manipulation tasks require, and how can a smaller policy retain useful capabilities? 

Efficiency approaches include compact policies such as SmolVLA [4] and NORA [5], preference-based adaptation in NORA-1.5 [6], and synthetic pretraining in GraspVLA [7]. For existing policies, SP-VLA [8] and DART [9] reduce tokens, while EfficientVLA [10] and MoLe-VLA [11] reduce layer computation. Token reduction alone leaves backbone weights intact. EcoVLA [12] dynamically selects channels and attention heads; we instead permanently reduce back-bone dimensions to produce a smaller dense model. Permanent compression introduces a second challenge: recovering manipulation capabilities lost when parameters are removed. RLRC [13] combines structured pruning with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), while SimpleVLA-RL [14] demonstrates that interac-tion can improve policies beyond SFT. The hidden dimension couples representations across Trans-former blocks, whereas the intermediate dimension controls expansion within feed-forward layers. Their different roles motivate separate retention targets for the two dimensions. We introduce SPAR (Structural Pruning and Action Re-covery), which couples dimension-aware pruning with post-pruning RL to recover task performance (Fig. 2). Building on pruning-aware tuning (PAT) [15], SPAR jointly learns masks for the hidden and intermediate dimensions with separate retention targets and a regularized mask-learning objective. Channel selection and supervised adaptation are performed together, followed by physical pruning and fine-tuning of the reduced model. The resulting compact policy provides the initialization for RL, which further improves manipulation performance through interaction. Our evaluation on LIBERO [16] and LIBERO-Plus [17] examines both task success and robustness under compres-sion. Pruning only the intermediate dimension preserves high task success, while additional hidden-dimension pruning at a fixed intermediate-dimension retention target causes larger losses that RL can substantially mitigate. As a representative result, SPAR achieves 93.45% average LIBERO success with a 0.09B language backbone (1.12B total), approximately 4% of the original LLM size (Fig. 1). These findings support dimension-aware compression and recovery as complemen-tary tools for compact robotic policies. Our main contributions are: 

• A dual-dimension pruning framework that jointly learns masks for the hidden and intermediate dimen-sions with separate retention targets and a regularized mask-learning objective. 

• An empirical analysis of dimension sensitivity : prun-ing only the intermediate dimension preserves high task success, while additional hidden-dimension prun-ing causes larger performance losses in our evaluated tasks. 

• An evaluation of recovery and efficiency across com-pression levels, showing improvements in task success and robustness after RL, together with measured full-policy inference acceleration. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models 

VLA models connect pretrained vision-language represen-tations to robotic control. OpenVLA [2] predicts discretized actions autoregressively, whereas OpenVLA-OFT [18] uses parallel decoding and action chunking. CogACT [19] sep-arates action generation into a diffusion expert. The π0

family [20] couples a PaliGemma backbone [21] with a flow-matching action expert; π0.5 [3] adds semantic subtask prediction. TinyVLA [22] instead combines a compact mul-timodal backbone with a diffusion decoder. 

B. Pruning for VLA models 

LLM compression includes weight sparsification in SparseGPT [23], coupled-structure removal in LLM-Pruner [24], and row/column deletion to form smaller dense 2matrices in SliceGPT [25]. PAT [15] jointly performs hidden-dimension pruning and fine-tuning through trainable chan-nel masks and Hybrid Sparsification Modules. For VLA models, DART [9] and SP-VLA [8] reduce visual tokens; EfficientVLA [10] removes low-impact layers, and MoLe-VLA [11] dynamically skips layers. EcoVLA [12] adaptively selects channels and attention heads with parallel sparsity updates and custom kernels. Building on PAT, SPAR learns masks for both the hidden and intermediate dimensions with separate retention targets, physically reduces the dense backbone, and applies RL recovery. Our evaluation examines the effects of compression on both manipulation success and robustness. 

C. Reinforcement learning for VLA models 

RL for VLA models includes both offline and online approaches. Offline RL learns from a fixed dataset with-out additional environment interaction, whereas online RL collects new experience as training proceeds. The RECAP recipe for π∗ 

> 0.6

[26] starts with offline RL pretraining, then alternates robot data collection with offline updates using advantage-conditioned policies. For online VLA training, SimpleVLA-RL [14] optimizes policies through environment interaction with outcome-based rewards, while RLinf [27] provides scalable training infrastructure. In the compression setting, RLRC [13] combines structured pruning, supervised fine-tuning, and RL to recover policy performance. 

D. Manipulation Benchmarks 

LIBERO [16] evaluates knowledge transfer across language-conditioned manipulation tasks. ManiSkill2 [28] offers 20 task families spanning stationary and mobile ma-nipulation, single- and dual-arm robots, and rigid and soft objects. Robustness-oriented extensions probe behavior be-yond standard task completion: LIBERO-PRO [29] perturbs manipulated objects, initial states, instructions, and environ-ments to examine sensitivity to memorized task conditions. LIBERO-Plus [17] evaluates seven perturbation dimensions, including camera viewpoints, robot initial states, and scene appearance. We use LIBERO to evaluate task success and LIBERO-Plus to assess robustness under perturbations after compression. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

SPAR combines structural pruning with post-pruning RL (Fig. 2). We adapt pruning-aware tuning (PAT) [15] to jointly learn hidden and intermediate channel masks with separate retention targets. After mask learning, we physically remove inactive channels and finetune the compact policy before RL recovery. a) PAT background: PAT’s Hybrid Sparsification Mod-ule [15] combines channel selection with adaptation of pretrained weights. For a linear map W ∈ Rdo×di , its forward operation can be written as

The original formulation regularizes channel sparsity and the low-rank transformation’s orthogo-nality while optimizing the supervised objective. This joint optimization allows pretrained representations to adapt as channel selection changes. SPAR adopts the pruning-aware training principle and introduces separate retention targets for the hidden and intermediate dimensions, a sigmoid mask schedule, and a differentiable count penalty. The following subsections formulate these channel-selection components and their role in preparing the compact policy for recovery. 

A. Dual-Dimension Structural Pruning 

The hidden dimension determines the width of the repre-sentation passed between Transformer blocks, whereas the intermediate dimension controls the expansion within each feed-forward network (FFN). Pruning the hidden dimension therefore requires coordinated changes to the attention and FFN projections to preserve residual connections. In contrast, pruning the intermediate dimension reduces the FFN ex-pansion while leaving the residual-stream width unchanged. SPAR uses separate retention targets for the two dimensions to control these distinct aspects of model capacity. [0 , 1] df f n , with retention targets ρhid and ρint . Both masks are shared across layers. Writing their diagonal forms as

A. Main Results 

Pruning only the intermediate dimension preserves high task success (Table I). A 10% intermediate-dimension re-tention target reduces the LLM from 2.34B to 0.82B with 96.45% average success, only 0.35 percentage points be-low the baseline’s 96.80%. This suggests substantial FFN intermediate-dimension redundancy on these tasks, motivat-ing ρint = 0 .1 for subsequent experiments. Additional hidden-dimension pruning causes larger losses (Table II): reducing ρhid from 0.9 to 0.1 lowers average success without RL from 95.90% to 84.55%, most sharply on Long. RL substantially mitigates these losses, improving all five pruned averages by 1.90–8.90 percentage points versus 60.90 for the baseline. Notably, at ρhid = 0 .7 and ρint = 0 .1,RL achieves 97.55%, just 0.15 points below the unpruned RL baseline’s 97.70%, with approximately 52% fewer total and 75% fewer LLM parameters. Even the smallest policy (1.12B total / 0.09B LLM) improves from 84.55% to 93.45%, recovering much of the loss while remaining 4.25 points below that baseline. 

B. Robustness on LIBERO-Plus 

LIBERO-Plus [17] tests changes in camera viewpoints, robot initial states, language instructions, lighting, back-ground textures, sensor noise, and object layouts. Table III reports cross-suite scores by perturbation category and the aggregate score under Overall. RL substantially improves all five pruned overall scores by 5.52–9.35 percentage points, versus 0.47 for the baseline. The smallest policy improves from 47.46% to 56.81% but remains 27.64 points below the unpruned RL baseline, par-ticularly under robot-initial-state and language perturbations. Even at ρhid = 0 .9 and ρint = 0 .1, the recovered policy scores 78.20% versus the baseline’s 84.45%. This 6.25-point gap, despite nearly matched standard LIBERO performance, indicates greater sensitivity to perturbations after compres-sion. Robustness-critical applications may therefore require less aggressive pruning or further RL adaptation with broader perturbation exposure. 

C. Real-Robot Evaluation 

On an AgileX dual-arm platform, we evaluate six tasks with 20 trials each (Table IV); Fig. 4 illustrates Task 5. Pruning only the intermediate dimension with ρint = 0 .2

yields 2.02B total / 0.99B LLM parameters, approximately 40% / 58% fewer than the baseline. The compact policy retains much of the baseline’s manipulation performance: 76/120 successes (63.33%) versus 85/120 (70.83%), a 7.50-percentage-point decrease. It matches the baseline on Task 1 and records successes on all six tasks. These results show that intermediate-dimension pruning preserves practical ma-nipulation capability on physical hardware despite substantial parameter reduction.

V. L IMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

Although SPAR approaches or slightly exceeds the un-pruned RL baseline on LIBERO under some aggressive compression settings, a robustness gap remains on LIBERO-Plus. Evaluation is also limited to one backbone and one intermediate-dimension pruning configuration across six real-robot tasks, motivating broader backbone studies, joint hidden-dimension and intermediate-dimension pruning on robots, and energy measurements across deployment plat-forms. To further improve recovery, we envision using Sim-Foundry [32] to reconstruct real-world AgileX task scenes as simulation environments. TiPToP [33] and ASPIRE [34] could then generate trajectories through planning and pro-gram execution, with MimicGen [35] expanding their cover-age for supervised adaptation of compact policies. Building on this initialization, the policies would undergo online reinforcement learning with PPO through direct interaction in the real-to-sim environments reconstructed by SimFoundry. VI. C ONCLUSION 

SPAR jointly prunes the hidden dimension and inter-mediate dimension with separate retention targets, com-bining pruning-aware adaptation with PPO-based recovery. 7Intermediate-only pruning preserves high success, while RL partially restores hidden-dimension losses. With a 0.09B LLM and 1.12B total parameters, the compact policy achieves a 1.98 × inference speedup, while RL raises its LIBERO success from 84.55% to 93.45%. On AgileX, 20% intermediate retention leaves a 7.50-point gap to the baseline.
