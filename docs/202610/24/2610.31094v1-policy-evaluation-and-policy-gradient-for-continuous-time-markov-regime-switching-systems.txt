# Policy Evaluation and Policy Gradient for Continuous-Time Markov Regime-Switching Systems 

## Minghui Zhang ∗ Xun Li † Xin Zhang ‡

## October 23, 2026 

Abstract 

We study policy evaluation (PE) and policy gradient (PG) for continuous-time stochastic con-trol with Markov regime-switching. The main challenges are the coexistence of diffusion and regime jumps, the coupling of regime-dependent value functions, and the construction of model-free learn-ing rules from the coupled generators. We establish a martingale characterization of PE that incor-porates both Brownian and switching martingales, and extend martingale loss and orthogonality methods to this setting. We further derive a coupled PG equation and a trajectory-based gradient representation that, under an action-independent switching generator, avoids explicit knowledge of the system coefficients and transition rates. These results lead to offline and online actor-critic algorithms. We also establish convergence and study the finite-grid approximation required for implementation. Numerical experiments for a regime-switching linear-quadratic problem illustrate the proposed methods. 

Keywords: Reinforcement learning, Markov regime-switching, Continuous-time stochastic con-trol, Policy evaluation, Policy gradient, Actor-Critic learning, Martingale methods. 

# 1 Introduction 

Reinforcement learning provides a data-driven approach to stochastic control when the underlying dynamics are unknown or only partially known. A typical RL procedure consists of exploration, policy evaluation, and policy improvement. Policy evaluation estimates the value function under a given policy, while policy gradient methods improve a parameterized policy using the gradient of its performance criterion. Their combination leads naturally to actor-critic methods, in which the critic evaluates the current policy and the actor updates it. Most classical RL theory is developed for discrete-time Markov decision processes. Many stochastic control problems, however, evolve continuously in time and are more naturally modeled by stochastic differential equations. A systematic continuous-time RL theory has developed rapidly in recent years. Wang et al. (2020) introduced an entropy-regularized exploratory control framework in which randomized policies are incorporated through stochastic relaxed controls. Jia and Zhou (2022b) subsequently established a martingale framework for PE. They showed that the value function can be characterized through the martingale property of the value process plus accumulated rewards. This perspective leads to mar-tingale loss and martingale orthogonality methods, and also explains why the mean-square temporal-difference error is generally not an appropriate PE objective for stochastic diffusions. This representation forms the basis of offline and online actor-critic algorithms. The standard diffusion framework does not capture abrupt changes in the operating environment. Such changes arise naturally in financial markets, engineering systems, and economic models. Acommon approach is to introduce a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain that modulates the coefficients of the diffusion process. The resulting Markov regime-switching model contains both continuous fluctuations and discrete regime transitions. Its value function becomes regime dependent, and the corresponding dynamic programming equations form a weakly coupled system through the generator of the Markov chain. This hybrid structure creates additional difficulties for RL because the learning problem must account for both diffusion uncertainty and regime-transition risk. Recent studies on continuous-time RL with regime-switching can be broadly divided according to their main objectives. One line focuses on financial control under partially observed regimes. Wu and Li (2024) study continuous-time meanvariance portfolio selection when the market regime is unobservable. They combine Wonham filtering with an entropy-regularized exploratory formulation and develop an actor-critic algorithm based on semi-analytical representations of the value function and policy. Thus, the main difficulty there is the partial-information structure: the hidden regime is first converted into a belief state, after which PE and policy updates are performed in the filtered state space. More recently, Gao et al. (2026b) adopt a related idea for dividend optimization under a partially observed regime-switching diffusion. Their method again relies on belief-state filtering and on the special semi-analytical structure of the underlying control problem to parameterize the critic and actor. A second line concentrates on exploratory control and policy improvement for specific regime-switching portfolio models. Chen et al. (2025) study exploratory meanvariance portfolio optimization with regime-switching. They derive a policy improvement theorem and propose an orthogonality-condition learning method based on the martingale property of the optimal value process. Their analysis also points out difficulties associated with applying standard TD learning directly in this setting. Xing et al. (2026) extend exploratory meanvariance optimization to regime-switching markets with constant-elasticity-of-variance dynamics. They first exploit the analytical structure of the HJB system and then construct a reinforcement learning scheme with linear approximations for the value function and policy. These studies demonstrate that regime information can be incorporated effectively into continuous-time exploratory control. Their analyses, however, are closely tied to the structure of particular portfolio models and their explicit or semi-explicit HJB solutions. A different research direction treats the regime itself as part of the control. Huang et al. (2025)Huang, Li, Yu, and Zhou (2025, revised 2026) study continuous-time RL for optimal switch-ing among multiple regimes. In their formulation, the agent randomizes both the switching time and the destination regime by controlling the generator matrix of a finite-state Markov chain. They es-tablish well-posedness of the exploratory HJB system, prove policy improvement and policy-iteration convergence, and construct a model-free algorithm using a martingale characterization of PE. This setting is conceptually different from the present one. In optimal switching, regime transitions are decision variables. Here, the Markov chain represents an exogenous random environment, while the control acts on the continuous state dynamics. Regime-switching RL has also begun to appear in stochastic differential games. Hu et al. (2026) consider entropy-regularized linear-quadratic Stackelberg differential games under Markovian regime-switching. Their formulation leads to exploratory weakly coupled HamiltonJacobiBellmanIsaacs equations, and neural networks are used to approximate regime-dependent value functions in high-dimensional settings. This line emphasizes hierarchical equilibrium computation rather than a general PEPG theory for a fixed exogenous regime process. Closely related from a technical perspective is the recent extension of continuous-time RL from dif-fusions to jump-diffusions. Gao et al. (2026a) formulate exploratory control for general jump-diffusion dynamics and develop martingale-based RL methods in the presence of jumps. A key observation is 2that, although jumps alter the Hamiltonian and the exploratory HJB equation, certain sample-based PE and q-learning algorithms retain the same form because jump effects are already contained in ob-served value increments. They also establish a connection between the theoretical exploratory process and discretely sampled randomized controls. Regime-switching diffusions are structurally different from general jump-diffusions because the jump component is a finite-state Markov chain and directly couples a vector of regime-dependent value functions. Nevertheless, this work highlights an important principle for the present study: jump terms may be essential in the theoretical generator while being absorbed implicitly by observed increments in model-free learning algorithms. These recent developments show that continuous-time RL with regime-switching is becoming an active research direction. At the same time, the existing literature is largely centered on particular con-trol problems, hidden-regime filtering, optimal switching, or structured HJB solutions. What remains less developed is a general martingale-based theory that treats PE and PG jointly for a controlled diffusion evolving in an exogenous Markovian regime environment. In particular, several issues do not follow directly from the pure-diffusion theory. The main difficulty arises from the hybrid nature of the regime-switching system. Under a fixed pol-icy, the value process is no longer continuous because regime transitions introduce jumps in addition to the Brownian fluctuations of the state process. Its semimartingale decomposition therefore con-tains both continuous and discontinuous martingale components. This alters the predictable quadratic variation and introduces an additional contribution generated by regime changes. As a result, the connections among temporal-difference errors, quadratic variation, martingale loss, and martingale orthogonality cannot be obtained by a direct extension of the pure-diffusion theory. They must be re-established in a framework that explicitly accounts for the jump structure induced by the Markov chain. This additional regime dependence also propagates into the policy-gradient analysis. The value functions associated with different regimes are coupled through the switching generator, and differentiating the policy-evaluation equations with respect to the policy parameters preserves this cross-regime coupling. Hence, the regime-switching terms are indispensable in the theoretical deriva-tion. At the same time, a model-free implementation should avoid explicit estimation of the switching generator. This creates a nontrivial gap between the analytical representation and the learning rule. When the switching mechanism is independent of both the action and the policy parameters, the action-independent generator terms can be removed from the score-weighted representation, while the effects of realized regime jumps are already embedded in the observed increments of the value pro-cess. Establishing this separation is essential for deriving a trajectory-based PG representation that remains model-free despite the presence of regime-switching. A further difficulty appears when the continuous-time theory is translated into implementable learning algorithms. The relaxed process used for the analysis is an averaged object, whereas the data available to the learner consist of state, regime, and randomized action trajectories. The randomized implementation must preserve the environmental martingale structure and the prescribed switching compensators, and regime-dependent predictable quantities must be evaluated with respect to the pre-jump regime. Moreover, finite-grid sampling of actions does not automatically reproduce the occupation law of the continuous-time relaxed model. Therefore, a separate consistency argument is required to justify the passage from the continuous-time PE and PG representations to discretized actor-critic algorithms. Motivated by these issues, this paper develops a unified theory of PE and PG for continuous-time Markov regime-switching stochastic control. We consider a controlled diffusion whose drift, diffusion, and reward may depend on a finite-state exogenous Markov chain. The control is generated by a parameterized stochastic policy, while the switching generator is assumed to be independent of the control and policy parameters. Our analysis is carried out in continuous-time, and time discretization is introduced only at the implementation stage. The contributions of this paper follow the progression from policy evaluation to policy improve-ment and, ultimately, to implementable actor-critic learning. We begin by establishing a martingale characterization of PE for stochastic policies under Markov regime-switching. The resulting formula-3tion incorporates both Brownian and regime-transition martingales, which allows the martingale loss and martingale orthogonality approaches to be extended to the hybrid diffusion-jump setting. This analysis also identifies the additional contribution of regime transitions to the predictable quadratic variation and clarifies how the PE theory differs from its pure-diffusion counterpart. Building on this foundation, we derive the policy-gradient equation for the coupled regime-dependent value functions and obtain a probabilistic representation of the gradient. By combining the score identity with the semimartingale decomposition of the value process, the generator-based expression is transformed into a trajectory-based representation that avoids explicit evaluation of the drift, diffusion coefficients, and switching generator. These PE and PG results are then integrated into a continuous-time actor-critic framework. The critic is constructed from the PE martingale conditions, whereas the actor is driven by the trajectory-based PG representation in the offline setting and by first-order martingale orthogonality conditions in the online setting. To connect the continuous-time theory with practical implementation, we further analyze the finite-grid approximation and specify the predictability and the Assumption 2 under which discretely sampled randomized controls are consistent with the underlying continuous-time formulation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the problem formulation and preliminaries. Section 3 develops policy evaluation under Markov regime-switching. Section 4 establishes the corresponding policy gradient theory. Section 5 constructs offline and online actor– critic algorithms based on the PE and PG results. Section 6 applies the proposed method to a regime-switching linear–quadratic control problem and reports numerical results. Section 7 concludes the paper.

# 3 Policy Evaluation under regime-switching 

This section develops the policy-evaluation framework for stochastic policies under Markov regime-switching. We first derive the coupled system of policy-evaluation equations associated with the regime-dependent value functions. We then establish an equivalent martingale characterization that explicitly incorporates both the Brownian and regime-switching martingale components. Based on this representation, we examine the mean-square temporal-difference error and identify the additional jump contribution arising from the predictable quadratic variation. Finally, we formulate the martingale loss and martingale-orthogonality conditions, which provide the theoretical basis for the critic construction used in the subsequent actor-critic analysis.

Remark (Interpretation and implementation) . The preceding results establish the connection between the continuous-time Actor-Critic framework and its finite-grid batch implementation. Their implica-tions are threefold. First, the discrete TD increment in (60) retains the observed value-function changes, including those arising from regime transitions. As a result, the critic and actor directions can be computed from sampled trajectories without explicit evaluation of the environmental drift, diffusion coefficients, or switching generator. The operator La appears in the consistency analysis through the PE residual (64), but it does not need to be evaluated in the algorithm. Second, the occupation and time-discretization consistency conditions are essential for connecting finite-grid sampling to continuous-time theory. A sample-and-hold policy generally does not satisfy exact Assumption 2 at a fixed nonzero mesh, since actions remain unchanged between decision times 45 even when the state or regime changes. Therefore, the continuous-time PE and PG identities are recovered through limiting arguments rather than being assumed to hold exactly for the finite-grid implementation. Third, the batch and two-time-scale constructions address different aspects of the learning problem. Batch averaging reduces the variability of the estimating directions, while time-grid refinement reduces discretization bias. Two-time-scale stochastic approximation is intended to allow the critic to track the evolving policy, thereby controlling the approximation error entering the actor direction. A complete convergence analysis must account for all three effects, together with the stability and identification properties of the underlying mean-field equations. Consequently, the proposed finite-grid batch Actor-Critic scheme provides an implementable ap-proximation of the continuous-time martingale-based PE and PG framework. Under the stated consis-tency conditions, the exact-critic batch directions recover the continuous-time PE zero-moment con-dition and policy-gradient representation in the vanishing-mesh limit, while the additional stochastic approximation conditions govern the behavior of the coupled parameter updates.

# 7 Conclusions 

This paper develops a unified martingale framework for policy evaluation, policy gradient, and Actor-Critic learning in continuous-time stochastic control with Markov regime-switching. The key feature of the framework is that the diffusion dynamics and regime transitions are treated within the same semimartingale structure. For policy evaluation, the value process is characterized through martingale conditions that account for both Brownian and regime-transition martingales. This leads to regime-switching extensions of martingale loss and martingale orthogonality methods. For policy improvement, the regime-dependent value functions remain coupled through the switching mechanism, but the result-ing policy-gradient representation can be reformulated in terms of observable trajectory increments. Under an action-independent switching generator, this representation avoids explicit evaluation of the drift, diffusion coefficients, and regime transition rates. These results provide a common theoretical basis for the offline and online Actor-Critic algorithms developed in the paper. The analysis also clar-ifies the role of regime-switching in data-driven continuous-time learning. Regime transitions affect the theoretical generator, the semimartingale decomposition of the value process, and its predictable quadratic variation, but their effects can be incorporated through observed value increments in the trajectory-based learning rules. This distinction is important for preserving the model-free character of the resulting algorithms. Together with the finite-grid consistency analysis, the proposed frame-work connects continuous-time PE and PG theory with implementable regime-switching Actor-Critic methods. 59 References
