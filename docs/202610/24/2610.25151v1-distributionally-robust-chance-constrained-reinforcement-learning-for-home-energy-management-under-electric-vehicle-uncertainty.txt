# Distributionally Robust Chance-Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Home Energy Management under Electric Vehicle Uncertainty 

Meng Yuan, Senior Member, IEEE, Ye Wang, Senior Member, IEEE, Yi Mei, Senior Member, IEEE, 

Ramesh Rayudu, Senior Member, IEEE, Alan Brent, Member, IEEE, Xinghuo Yu, Life Fellow, IEEE, and Mengjie Zhang, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —The electrification of residential energy systems and adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) create demand-side flexibility. However, random driving behaviour makes it difficult to ensure EV readiness at departure and maintain thermal comfort. To address this, we formulate residential scheduling as a distributionally robust (DR) chance-constrained Markov decision process, whose 2-Wasserstein ambiguity set guards against tail risk and distribution shift in EV behaviour. The intractable DR chance constraint is then replaced by a sufficient tightened expectation constraint under the nominal distribution. The resulting problem is solved by a Lagrangian soft actor–critic with distributional cost critics, and an online boundary guard enforces EV reachability and thermal comfort within the actuator limits at every step. Five controllers are compared over one year in Wellington, New Zealand, under a degraded forecast. The proposed controller is the cheapest controller that meets the EV target on every day and both chance constraints under forecast error and random driving behaviour. Compared with the rule-based baseline, the only other controller to do so, it lowers the annual cost by 8.6% and the comfort violation hours by 69%. The ablation study confirms that the boundary guard ensures pointwise feasibility within the actuator limits, and the DR chance constraint reduces tail risk. 

Index Terms —Chance constraints, distributionally robust opti-misation, electric vehicle uncertainty, home energy management, reinforcement learning, Wasserstein distance. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Around 40% of the energy consumed in the European Union is used in buildings, and the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive requires new buildings to be ready for rooftop photovoltaic (PV) systems and smart electric vehicle (EV) charging [1]. Electrification and the adoption of rooftop PV, stationary batteries and EVs are turning homes into active energy hubs. Coordinating PV, storage, EVs, heat pumps and controllable appliances under time-varying prices and comfort requirements is a sequential decision problem addressed by home energy management systems (HEMS). Reinforcement learning (RL) learns energy management policies for homes and buildings directly from data without an explicit model [6], [7]. However, almost all such methods enforce operational limits through penalty terms in the reward. A separate line treats constraints explicitly: constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs) [8] solved by trust-region and Lagrangian methods [9], [10], chance-constrained program-ming [11] and Wasserstein ambiguity sets for distributional robustness [12]. Distributionally robust constrained RL has begun to combine these ideas [13]. However, it limits only the expected cost, so rare but severe violations are left unbounded. A distributionally robust chance constraint bounds such vio-lations, but it is intractable for Lagrangian RL. In HEMS, it has been applied to optimisation-based scheduling under PV uncertainty [14]. However, it has not been converted into a trainable constraint for RL, nor combined with a safety layer acting on the measured state. Although stochastic EV driving schedules and user behaviour have been incorporated into building energy management and EV charging scheduling [15], [16], existing works typically optimise expected costs and leave tail-risk violations unconstrained. Three gaps motivate this work. First, most RL-based HEMS add fixed penalty terms to the reward. Their weights need manual tuning, and they bound only the average. However, occupants judge a controller by its worst days, so the tail of the violations, not only their mean, must be bounded. Second, although the EV behaviour driving these violations is heavy-tailed, it is usually treated deterministically. A survey-fitted travel distribution also changes with season, tariff and household, so a policy safe in training can violate its constraints in deployment. Finally, while MPC relies heavily on day-ahead forecasts, its comfort performance under realistic forecast errors is rarely evaluated, as most studies assume perfect forecasts. To address these gaps, this paper proposes a distributionally robust (DR) chance-constrained RL framework for HEMS. The scheduling problem is formulated as a CMDP with a DR chance constraint, which guards against both tail-risk violations and distribution shift in EV behaviour. We show that this constraint is satisfied whenever the expected constraint cost under the nominal distribution stays below a tightened budget. Unlike the Lagrangian SAC framework of [18], which bounds the constraint costs only in expectation under the fitted EV distribution, the proposed framework constrains tail risk and EV distribution shift by a DR chance constraint with distributional cost critics. An online boundary guard further enforces EV reachability and thermal comfort at every step within the actuator limits. The overall DR chance-constrained SAC (DRCC-SAC) framework is shown in Fig. 1. To validate the proposed strategy, year-long simulations are conducted using real Aotearoa New Zealand household travel survey data and Wellington climate profiles, with chemistry-specific battery degradation costs. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first integration of Wasserstein DR and chance constraints within a CMDP frame-work for home energy management. The main contributions of this work are: 1) A CMDP formulation for HEMS with a Wasserstein DR chance constraint against tail risk and EV distribution shift, paired with a provably sufficient tightened expec-tation constraint for tractable Lagrangian SAC training. 2) An online physical boundary guard, applied at every time step in both training and deployment, that enforces EV reachability and thermal comfort pointwise within the actuator limits and complements the chance constraints. 3) A year-long case study on real New Zealand data under forecast error, which reports violation incidence, mag-nitude and chance-constraint rate separately and shows that the proposed controller is the cheapest controller meeting the daily EV target and both chance constraints. Solving the DR chance-constrained MDP (17) poses two challenges: the DR chance constraint is intractable to enforce directly, requiring a tail probability under a worst-case distri-bution, and standard Lagrangian RL operates on expectation constraints rather than probabilistic ones. The proposed method has four components. We first show that a tightened expectation bound under the nominal distribution is sufficient for the DR chance constraint. Distributional cost critics supply the constraint gradient, and the spread of the episode cost is estimated online from realised costs. Lagrangian SAC with adversarial scenario augmentation then optimises the policy, and an online physical boundary guard enforces feasibility at every step. PREPRINT 5

VI. C ASE STUDIES

The main controller comparison uses a degraded day-ahead forecast. Three metrics are reported for every controller. The violation count is the number of hours outside the comfort band and of days on which the EV departs below SoC EV req . The violation magnitude is the mean daily constraint cost, comparable with 

di. The chance-constraint rate cPr( ZCi > nd i) is the fraction of consecutive n-day blocks whose cost exceeds the block budget, comparable with ηi. The third metric estimates the chance constraint of (17) on the test year.

2) Baseline Controllers and Forecast Degradation: The proposed DRCC-SAC is compared against four controllers. Two proximal policy optimisation (PPO) agents [39], a plain and a projected variant, apply the same fixed penalty weights to comfort and EV violations, and the projected variant adds a one-step predictive comfort projection. The rule-based heuristic and the projected PPO apply heuristic EV reachability corrections and one-step thermal preconditioning. All controllers are subject to the same actuator and mode limits. Only the day-ahead forecasts seen by the controllers are perturbed, while the plant uses the measured signals. The per-turbation combines a daily bias with a first-order autoregressive error, additive for temperature and multiplicative for PV and price. The degraded forecast has a root mean square error of about 1.3 ◦C on temperature, 18% on PV and 9% on price, and a moderate forecast has half these errors. Every controller sees the same forecast without retraining, and the ablation, feasibility checks and distribution-shift study use the perfect forecast, so that only the varied quantity changes.

E. Ablation Study 

The ablation uses the nominal EV distribution and perfect forecasts, with at least 5 seeds per variant. In Table V, A denotes the Lagrangian multipliers, B the DR layer and C the guard. The DR layer consists of (i) the budget tightening and (ii) scenario augmentation. In the deployable configuration of Table V(a), training with the guard in the loop nearly halves comfort violation relative to applying it only at deployment. Replacing the Lagrangian multiplier with a fixed penalty raises comfort violation by 15%. Removing the guard lowers the cost, but comfort violation rises more than 8 times and the EV target is missed on about a third of the days. A paired t-test across seeds gives p = 0 .42 for the annual costs of the full and no-DR controllers. EV tail-risk control is examined in the stress-test configuration without the EV reachability bound. In the stress-test configuration of Table V(b), the EV constraint binds, and the first three rows are averaged over 15 seeds. Adding the budget tightening alone to A+C lowers EV-violation days by about 40, with p = 0 .032 , and lowers the chance rate from 0.177 to 0.090. Scenario augmentation trains on perturbed rather than nominal EV scenarios, and it leaves the nominal EV violation magnitude and chance rate statistically unchanged, with p = 0 .64 and p = 0 .56 . The chance constraint is essential: an expectation constraint with a scalar critic raises 

cPr( ZC2 > nd 2) far above η2, with p = 0 .0010 .

F. Feasibility Conditions and Parameter Sensitivity 

The design conditions (21) and (25) are examined by sweeping their parameters. In the stress-test configuration, raising ϵ from 0 to 0.10, across the boundary ϵ < d 2

√n/L 2,more than halves EV-violation days, while cost rises by NZD 202. Beyond this boundary, the tightened budget saturates at its floor. For comfort, η1 = 0 .05 violates (25), since the Cantelli term then leaves ˜d1 below E[ZC1 ]. The condition holds at η1 = 0 .10 , where ˜d1 is 34% of nd 1. Tightening d2 from 0.05 to 0.005 violates (21) and nearly doubles cPr( ZC2 > nd 2).

G. Robustness to EV Distribution Shift 

A severe out-of-distribution EV shift is applied without retraining. It moves the median departure 2.6 h earlier and the median return 5.0 h later, and makes the median trip 3.1 times longer. The resulting daily distribution lies about 26 ambiguity radii from ˆν, testing robustness beyond the Wasserstein ball. With the perfect forecast, every controller that is feasible under nominal conditions stays within 1 EV-violation day, while the MILP-MPC degrades from 5 to 14 missed departures. The proposed controller’s annual cost rises by 6.99% under this shift, less than the 8.68% rise without DR. VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we formulated home energy management as a distributionally robust chance-constrained MDP, replaced the DR chance constraint by a proven sufficient expectation bound, and solved it via a Lagrangian soft actor–critic with distributional critics and an online physical boundary guard. Over a full test year under degraded forecasts, the proposed controller is the least expensive one that meets the EV target on every day and both chance constraints. Compared with the rule-based heuristic, the only other feasible controller, it costs 8.6% less and cuts comfort violation hours by 69%. The guard enforces pointwise feasibility within the actuator limits, and the proposed controller’s constraint metrics vary by at most 1 comfort hour across seeds. With the DR layer, the cost rises less under distribution shift, and in the stress test the tightening and the chance constraint reduce EV violations and tail risk. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
