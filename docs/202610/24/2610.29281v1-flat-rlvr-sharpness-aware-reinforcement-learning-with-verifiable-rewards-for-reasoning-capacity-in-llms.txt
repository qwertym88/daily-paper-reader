## Abstract 

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly through group-relative pol-icy optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a cen-tral paradigm for the post-training of large lan-guage models (LLMs). While RLVR is highly effective at enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, it commonly induces distribution sharp-ening , whereby the policy distribution becomes excessively peaked and rare-yet-correct trajecto-ries are progressively suppressed, thus diminish-ing sample diversity. To this end, we promote flat-reward optima in RLVR to smooth the pol-icy distribution. We theoretically link flat reward RLVR to reduced distribution sharpening, and em-pirically show improved sample diversity while maintaining single-attempt accuracy. Our method, called Flat -RLVR, searches for flat reward re-gions via sharpness-aware minimization during LLM post-training, combining this with GRPO variants to achieve better trade-offs between sam-pling efficiency and reasoning capacity.

However, a critical doubt has been raised, stemming from empirical observations that GRPO impairs its efficacy when evaluated across multiple samples by checking whether any one succeeds; so-called Pass@ K performance metric (Shao et al., 2024). Specifically, base LLM models without post-training even surpass their RLVR-tuned counterparts un-der Pass@ K with sufficiently large K (Yue et al., 2025). Further evidence reinforces this view: RLVR has been shown to amplify only responses that al-ready carry high probability mass while neglecting the long tail of rare-yet-correct samples, thereby reducing sample diversity (He et al., 2025); and to concentrate exclusively on regions already solvable by the base model, failing to 1arXiv:2610.29281v1 [cs.LG] 21 Oct 2026 Flat -RLVR: Sharpness-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards for Reasoning Capacity in LLMs 

acquire novel capabilities (Yue et al., 2025). Taken together, these findings further support the interpretation that the cur-rent RLVR mechanism induces distribution sharpening —a phenomenon in which the model reinforces only the easier, already-solved instances and loses its exploratory capacity. This is particularly problematic in light of the emerging paradigm of inference-time compute scaling, which lever-ages sample diversity for effective LLM deployment (Wang et al., 2023; Brown et al., 2024; Chow et al., 2025; Handa et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b). These strategies operate on a simple principle—generate many candidate solutions and rely on a verifier to identify the correct ones—and are par-ticularly well aligned with RLVR’s domains of mathematics and code, where deterministic verifiers naturally enable reliable selection (Cobbe et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021; Brown et al., 2024). In this regard, distribution sharpening from RLVR, which is observed to trade output diversity for efficiency, directly undermines LLM’s benefits of inference-time computation. narrows the path to a few answers discarding diversity. In response to this growing need for sample diversity, we apply reinforcement learning (RL) to flatten the reward sur-face of the RLVR framework, thereby smoothing policy distributions and diversifying the generated candidate sam-ples. Recently, it has been shown that a flatter maximum on the reward surface implies a robust policy against uncertain perturbations on actions, states, and rewards (Lee & Yoon, 2025). Motivated by the flat RL concept, our key insight is that, rather than updating the LLM toward steepest loss descent, steering it to flatten the surface may smooth the distribution sharpening. In this paper, we find that sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) variants (Foret et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022; Hassan et al., 2025) applied to RL optimization within the RLVR paradigm indeed mitigate distribution sharpening by yield-ing a flatter reward (as illustrated in Figure 1). In addition, we formalize how the flat-search optimization mathemati-cally lifts suboptimal candidates by smoothing the reward surface. Our contributions are highlighted as follows: i) We propose a novel strategy to alleviate the distribution sharp-ening of GRPO by borrowing the concept of flat-minima search. ii) We provide a theoretical analysis of how a flat-inspired GRPO elevates suboptimal candidates, leading to the smoothed output distribution. iii) We suggest a Flat -RLVR framework by integrating SAM into GRPO methods. 

Flat -RLVR, instantiated by combining with GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), and CISPO (Chen et al., 2025), consistently improves Pass@ 256 mathematical reasoning capacity while preserving accuracy with our LLM post-training adaptations. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Group-Relative RLVR 

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains a large language model on binary rewards provided by deterministic verifiers, eliciting strong reasoning behav-ior without human-preference data (Guo et al., 2025; Lam-bert et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2026). Within this paradigm, group-relative methods have become dominant, yielding computation benefits by normalizing rewards across a group of rollouts sampled from the same prompt and thereby re-moving the need for a separate value network (Shao et al., 2024). The original instance, GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), has since been extended in several directions. DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) introduces asymmetric clipping bounds that allow low-probability tokens to bring larger upward updates, together with dynamic sampling that discards groups whose advantages collapse to zero. CISPO (Chen et al., 2025) clips the importance-sampling weight to prevent dropping tokens that carry essential information. Our work integrates a sharpness-aware optimization perspec-tive into the reinforcement learning loop, showing a general applicability across the group-relative RLVR family. 

2.2. Distribution Sharpening in RLVR 

While the distribution sharpening phenomenon of RLVR is now well documented (Yue et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), existing mitigation strategies all intervene at the level of the reward or token distribution. F-GRPO (Plyusov et al., 2026) applies a Focal Weight to down-weight easy prompts, the unlikeliness reward (He et al., 2025) amplifies signals from rare correct trajectories, and clipping-based variants such as DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) relax token-level updates to preserve low-probability tokens. We address distribution sharpening via optimization geom-etry itself , biasing updates toward flat regions of the loss landscape via SAM—a direction is novel and orthogonal to the above approaches. 

2.3. Advances in SAM 

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) has been improved as a primary driver of flat-minima search by addressing its drawbacks. The first concern is its sensitivity to parameter re-scaling: ASAM (Kwon et al., 2021) addresses this by introducing a scaling operator that adaptively adjusts the perturbation radius according to parameter magnitudes. The second issue is SAM’s vulnerability to noisy gradients, par-ticularly during the early stages of training; GCSAM (Has-san et al., 2025) mitigates this through Gradient Central-ization, which removes the mean of the gradient to yield a more reliable perturbation direction. The third concern is 2Flat -RLVR: Sharpness-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards for Reasoning Capacity in LLMs 

the computational overhead of SAM; LookSAM (Liu et al., 2022) amortizes this cost by computing the SAM update only periodically, while Momentum-SAM (Becker et al., 2025) integrates Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) into SAM to directly improve computational efficiency. Lastly, the observation that SAM does not invariably converge to flat minima leads to GSAM (Zhuang et al., 2022) adopting a surrogate gap formulation for the sharpness metric. Our work adopts the idea of several SAM-based variants to build the Flat -RLVR framework; applying the SAM update periodically, inspired by LookSAM (Liu et al., 2022) and Gradient Centralization, inspired by GCSAM (Hassan et al., 2025). Notably, Flat -RLVR is not limited to a certain type of SAM approaches. 

2.4. Flat-Minima and Generalization 

Flat minima have been studied as being linked to improved generalization (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997; Keskar et al., 2017) emphasized in domain generalization (Cha et al., 2021) and adversarial robustness (Wei et al., 2023) with un-derlying principles (Zhang et al., 2024; Yoo & Yoon, 2026). Researchers have extended flat-minima toward various learn-ing frameworks, including generative modeling (Lee et al., 2025), federated learning (Qu et al., 2022; Lee & Yoon, 2024), and reinforcement learning (RL) (Lee & Yoon, 2025). Notably, RL with flatter reward yields more robust poli-cies to action, state, and reward uncertainties (Lee & Yoon, 2025). Such flat reward insight makes us anticipate poten-tial benefits in RLVR, but it is nontrivial to identify how flatness-aware RL steers LLM behavior. Our work is the first attempt to employ SAM in LLM post-training, revealing its benefits in relieving distribution sharp-ening in RLVR.

Concretely, Pass@ K metrics measure the boundaries of reasoning ability by sampling k outputs from the model. ## 7. Conclusion 

In this work, we present a way to mitigate the distribution sharpening in group-relative RLVR, by utilizing flatness-seeking optimization. Concretely, we provide a mathemat-ical analysis of the one-step parameter update in RLVR, showing that flat-minima search dampens the abrupt in-crease in probability, thereby mitigating distribution sharp-ness of LLM post-training. We propose the Flat -RLVR framework by integrating sharpness-aware minimization into RLVR. Throughout experiments, Flat -RLVR imple-mented as Flat -GRPO/DAPO/CISPO mitigates the distri-bution sharpening induced by RLVR, consistently improv-ing the diversity of the output distribution while preserving accuracy with our LLM post-training adaptations. These imply flatness serves as the central catalyst to mitigate the distribution sharpening. 9Flat -RLVR: Sharpness-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards for Reasoning Capacity in LLMs

## C. Ablation Study 

This appendix quantifies the individual contribution of each strategy introduced in Section 5. We conduct a systematic ablation on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with same environments of main results. Only difference stems from the application of each strategy. Full training details are in Appendix F. Table 9 reveals a clear gain per each strategy: • Just naively applicating SAM in RLVR can improve reasoning capacity (+1.5), but sacrificing much of ac-curacy (-5.4) compared with GRPO. • Cosine Annealing strategy gradually decays the SAM’s perturbation radius, which make training process more stable in terms of accuracy (+4.0) compared with GRPO+SAM. • Gradient Centralization strategy subtracts the gradi-ent norm before ascent/descent step, thereby promot-ing stable (+1.8) and diversity (+0.1) compared with GRPO+SAM. • Focal Weight strategy decays the radius of perturbation according to the success rate in group. It promotes stable training process (+5.7) reducing the amount of radius if success rate is low and diversity (+1.2) almost not decaying if success rate is high compared with GRPO+SAM. In short, flatness-aware optimization can work itself for im-proving pass@k reasoning coverage, requiring some strate-gies for stablizing training process and amplifying its effect to some extent.

E.2. Solvable-problem coverage 

For each problem, we utilize the per-problem correctness rate computed during the Pass@K evaluation in Section 6.2, where each problem is sampled n = 256 times. We define a problem as solvable by a given model if at least one of its 

n sampled completions is correct. For each base–RL pair, every problem is then assigned to one of four categories according to whether it is solvable by the base model and by the RL-tuned model: solvable by both, by the base model only ( forgotten ), by the RL-tuned model only ( newly solved ), or by neither. The reported percentages are the fraction of problems in each category, averaged across all benchmarks. 

## F. Experimental details 

F.1. Full Training Details 

All methods are trained with a token-level loss function, and the GRPO variants are implemented following the official guidelines of the TRL framework (von Werra et al., 2020). Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B is trained on 5× NVIDIA Quadro RTX 8000 GPUs; Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct on a single Quadro RTX 8000; Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti; Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct on a single NVIDIA A100. The full set of hyperparameters is summarized in Table 12. 

F.2. Full Training Details: Flat -RLVR 

For training the models in Flat -RLVR Framework, we decay the SAM hyperparameter ρ and use the lightweight approximation for memory efficiency. Additional details are in Appendix D and Appendix B.3. The full set of hyper-parameters for Flat -RLVR is summarized in Table 11. Note that ρfocal min acts as a lower bound on the perturbation radius after focal-weight modulation, ensuring that the per-turbation retains a non-trivial effect even when the focal weight becomes small. 

F.3. Full Training Details: F-GRPO 

We implement F-GRPO following the official formulation of Plyusov et al. (2026). For each prompt x, we compute the empirical success rate ˆμpos (x) over its group of rollouts and scale the group-relative advantage by the Focal weight 

g(x) = (1 − ˆμpos (x)) γ , which down-weights updates on high-success prompts to preserve solution diversity. Fol-lowing the original work, we set γ = 1 and keep all other hyperparameters identical to the GRPO baseline.
