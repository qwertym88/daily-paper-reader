## ABSTRACT 

In goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL), quasimetric learning mod-els goal-reaching costs as quasimetric distances, connecting local constraints to global value geometry. Its local constraints, however, should reflect the direction-dependent effects of control composition over a finite horizon together with en-vironmental feasibility. We propose ReQRL , which constrains the critic’s value gradients through finite-horizon reachability. Drawing on state-constrained opti-mal control, we decouple dynamical reachability from boundary geometry, esti-mating both from data. On OGBench , our method outperforms or rivals existing quasimetric approaches and other offline GCRL methods. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) provides a framework for learning a shared policy across many goals, where experience collected while pursuing one goal can help the agent generalize to reach others. Specifying tasks through goals also reduces reliance on task-specific reward shaping (Schaul et al., 2015; Andrychowicz et al., 2017). A central challenge is learning optimal goal-conditioned values that describe the minimum cost of reaching each goal and guide policy learning and planning (Pong et al., 2018; Park et al., 2023). For instance, reaching a goal may require a long detour because an obstacle blocks the direct path or the available controls do not allow the agent to move directly toward it. Thus, the value function, or critic , must reflect this global cost of reaching a goal, accounting for all the composed actions needed to follow the optimal feasible path. The goal-reaching costs above have a useful structure. They are nonnegative and zero when already at the goal, but may differ with the direction of travel. Moreover, the optimal cost of reaching the goal g from a state s cannot exceed the cost of going through an intermediate state s′. These properties precisely make the optimal goal-reaching costs −V ∗(s, g ) a quasimetric . This distance structure has motivated various approaches (Wang et al., 2023; Myers et al., 2026; Giammarino & Qureshi, 2026), which restrict the critic’s hypothesis class through quasimetric parameterization. In particular, to learn an environment’s reaching costs, Quasimetric RL (QRL) (Wang et al., 2023) imposes local constraints that keep predicted distances consistent with the costs of observed feasible transitions (s, s ′). On the other hand, EikHiQRL (Giammarino & Qureshi, 2026) directly constrains gradient magnitudes to regulate how rapidly its predicted distances change with state. The appeal of the quasimetric structure is that it links, or stitches , reaching costs through intermediate states, so local supervision can inform global goal-reaching distance estimates. Quasimetric parameterization links reaching costs across states, but does not specify which local changes in the state are physically possible or which paths are blocked. Local constraints help convey this information to the critic, i.e., the learned value geometry depends on what these lo-cal constraints tell the critic about the environment’s (i) control dynamics (how actions change the state) and (ii) domain feasibility (how the environment restricts feasible paths). Although QRL and EikHiQRL provide effective local supervision, their constraints remain limited. In practice, QRL enforces its local bounds on sparse sampled transitions in data. This leaves the critic to infer how its value should change along other feasible motions. EikHiQRL constrains local variation through a simplified isotropic dynamics model without an explicit boundary condition, which may impose 1arXiv:2610.00778v1 [cs.LG] 30 Sep 2026 Preprint a local distance geometry that does not match the agent’s directional control capabilities or the do-main’s feasibility. This paper. We ask: can we provide local critic supervision that faithfully reflects both the system’s control dynamics and domain feasibility? To answer this, we show this supervision need not rely only on instantaneous control directions but can instead retain the effects of controls composed over time. Geometric control theory (Jean, 2014) explains why: composing controls over a finite du-ration can produce state changes unavailable instantaneously. We capture these state changes through a finite-horizon reachable set ,and derive a finite-horizon Hamiltonian encoding these effects in a gradient constraint by linearizing only the critic. To make reach-ability estimation tractable from transition data, we then draw on state-constrained Hamilton-Jacobi theory (Soner, 1986) to decou-ple dynamical reachability from boundary geometry. We thus sup-ply two complementary local training signals: (i) what composed controls can achieve dynamically and (ii) how domain boundaries restrict feasible progress toward a goal. Together they let the critic capture global goal-reaching geometry (Figure 1). Our contributions: (a) we formulate quasimetric local constraints through a finite-horizon Hamilto-nian retaining the effects of composed controls and decouple dynamical reachability from boundary feasibility using state-constrained Hamilton-Jacobi theory, (b) introduce ReQRL , a GCRL instanti-ation estimated from offline transition data, with closed-form zonotopic reachability constraints and boundary regularization, and (c) on OGBench , ReQRL largely outperforms QRL and EikHiQRL in success rate, matching or exceeding recent state-of-the-art GCRL methods on many tasks.

## 4 DECOUPLING : D YNAMICS IN INTERIOR , F EASIBILITY AT BOUNDARY 

Our formulation above combines two questions: (i) what state changes can the controls generate over duration τ and (ii) which trajectories remain feasible in S? Estimating this jointly from data requires accounting for how composed controls interact with environmental constraints over the full horizon (Tebjou et al., 2023). State-constrained Hamilton-Jacobi theory (Soner, 1986; Aubin et al., 2011) suggests a natural way to approach this difficulty: the ambient control dynamics give the interior Hamiltonian, and the geometry of the feasible domain shows up via a boundary viability condition. Following this structure, we approximate the finite-horizon reachability constraints using separate estimates of dynamical reachability for interior constraints and domain geometry for the boundary constraint. We will assume the underlying dynamics are defined independently of S.

This follows the level-set characterization of boundary normals (Delfour & Zol´ esio, 2011). We enforce the boundary penalty near the estimated boundary, assuming adequate coverage of the feasible region. 

Finite time scale k. The horizon k ≥ 1 sets the duration τ = k∆t over which controls are com-posed. We choose k by comparing the extent of the control-generated part of Gk with the nominal center displacement cs in the goal coordinates. ## 7 RELATED WORK 

Goal-conditioned RL. GCRL provides a framework for learning reusable policies across goals (Schaul et al., 2015; Pong et al., 2018) and turning achieved outcomes into self-supervision through hindsight relabeling (Andrychowicz et al., 2017; Ghosh et al., 2019). Offline GCRL extends this to reward-free datasets without further interaction (Chebotar et al., 2021; Park et al., 2025). Ap-proaches include weighted imitation (Yang et al., 2022), policy regression via occupancy matching (Ma et al., 2022), and contrastive value learning (Eysenbach et al., 2022). To tackle longer horizons, others use subgoal reaching (Park et al., 2023), advantage estimates via temporal abstraction (Ahn et al., 2026), flat-policy bootstrapping (Zhou & Kao, 2026), and graph-based stitching (Baek et al., 2025). A complementary line exploits the metric and geometric structure of goal-reaching values. 

(Quasi)metrics in GCRL. Viewing costs as distances motivates reward shaping via dynamical dis-tances (Hartikainen et al., 2019) and subgoal search (Eysenbach et al., 2019), planning embeddings for navigation (Yang et al., 2020), and metric representations for skill discovery (Park et al., 2024). A key insight is that quasimetrics encode both directional costs and path composition. QRL turns this into a learning principle linking local transition constraints to global distances (Wang et al., 2023). CMD and TMD combine quasimetric structure with contrastive temporal-distance learning (Myers et al., 2024; 2026). EikHiQRL regularizes critic gradients with Eikonal constraints under simplified dynamics (Giammarino & Qureshi, 2026). Grounding local supervision in composed reachability and state feasibility remains challenging, motivating geometric and optimal control. 

Geometric and optimal control. Geometric control shows composing controls can generate motion beyond the instantaneous span (Jean, 2014). In optimal control, Hamilton-Jacobi theory connects dynamics to value gradients (Bardi et al., 1997), while state-constraint boundary conditions encode feasibility (Soner, 1986). In RL, these ideas motivate HJB-based derivative regularization (Lien et al., 2024) and Eikonal-based TD learning (Giammarino et al., 2026). ReQRL unifies these in local supervision: a finite-horizon Hamiltonian preserves control composition while linearizing only the critic and boundary constraints encode feasibility. 

## 8 CONCLUSION 

We formulated finite-horizon Hamiltonian constraints for learning global goal-reaching geometry through local quasimetric critic supervision. Our approach retains the effects of controls composed 9Preprint over time through the reachable set. We further separate dynamical reachability from boundary geometry, providing complementary signals about control dynamics and feasibility. With this, we developed ReQRL for GCRL, with data-driven zonotopic reachability estimates and boundary reg-ularization. Experiments on OGBench show improvements over quasimetric baselines and compet-itive performance against other GCRL methods. We discuss limitations in Appendix A.

## A LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

We see several natural directions for extending our method. Our instantiation in Section 5 uses an affine dynamics model and occupancy-based boundary estimates to realize tractable evaluation of the constraints in (13). While these approximations are effective in our OGBench experiments, the affine model may not capture all state-dependent and nonlinear effects of the underlying control dy-namics. The estimates also depend on coverage of relevant states in the training data D, a concern shared by offline baselines such as QRL and EikHiQRL (Wang et al., 2023; Giammarino & Qureshi, 2026). Richer state representations and nonlinear reachability estimators could broaden applicabil-ity; an important challenge is retaining efficient constraint evaluation. Our formulation in Section 3 also assumes deterministic dynamics, and the experiments in Section 6 evaluate offline tasks. Ex-tending the formulation to stochastic control systems and evaluating the method with online data collection are promising directions for future work.

We note that the above theorem provides global value error bounds in terms of the constraint residual, the first-order approximation error, and the assumed accuracy on Bg . While this bound gives some insight for the value recovery of our method in the idealized setting, this analysis should thus be interpreted only as conditional guarantee for the true reachable set. The practical feasibility and effectiveness of the method are more directly established via our experiments in Section 6.

We compare against quasimetric methods (QRL, EikHiQRL) and non-quasimetric methods (GCIQL, CRL, HIQL, SAW, OTA, GAS). GCIQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021) learns a goal-conditioned value by expectile regression. HIQL (Park et al., 2023) learns an action-free value and extracts subgoal and low-level policies from it. SAW (Zhou & Kao, 2026) trains a single flat policy whose actions for distant goals are regressed toward those of a short-horizon policy. CRL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) trains the critic as a contrastive classifier over future states. OTA (Ahn et al., 2026) learns the high-level value over temporally abstracted transitions. GAS (Baek et al., 2025) plans over a graph of key states in a learned temporal-distance space. Among the qausimetric methods, QRL (Wang et al., 2023) learns an IQE critic with transition-cost constraints and EikHiQRL (Giammarino & Qureshi, 2026) adds an Eikonal gradient penalty and a hierarchical actor of HIQL. All methods are reimplemented in PyTorch that follow each method’s released JAX code, sharing the same training loop, evaluation protocol, and seeds. All baselines including our method are written in PyTorch and are compiled before running. Hyperparameters follow the authors’ per-environment settings when available and are otherwise tuned, all following the same sweep protocol. C.5 TRAINING AND HYPERPARAMETERS 

All networks use three hidden layers of width 512 with GELU activations. Critics additionally use LayerNorm. The quasimetric critic uses IQE (Wang & Isola, 2022) with a 512 -dimensional latent representation, organized into 64 components of size 8. Our QRL and EikHiQRL baselines use the same IQE critic architecture. Actors parameterize diagonal Gaussian distributions with fixed unit standard deviation, and the low-level value function has two heads. We optimize the networks with Adam and batch size 1024 . The inherited policy-training settings follow EikHiQRL: the low-level discount is γ = 0 .995 , the expectile is 0.7, and the Polyak update coefficient is 0.005 .
