Amortized Score–Hamiltonian Policy Iteration: A Grid-Free
Scheme for Relaxed Stochastic Control Problems
Qi Feng∗and
Gu Wang†
Abstract
We develop an amortized, grid-free implementation of continuous Langevin dynamics based
policy-value iteration for entropy-regularized, infinite-horizon relaxed stochastic control prob-
lems. The improvement rate of the exact iteration is a discounted aggregate of relative Fisher
information between the policy and the Gibbs law of its Hamiltonian. The associated score resid-
ual is the velocity with which the control’s Langevin dynamics transport its law. We project this
velocity onto a conditional sampler shared across states, instead of one Langevin dynamics per
state, and the value dynamics onto a parametric critic, estimating both projections at sampled
states to obtain coupled actor–critic flows. The score loss measures the actor’s agreement with
the current critic, while the policy-evaluation residual measures the critic’s agreement with the
actor. We also derive gradient and Hessian residuals, including a Feynman–Kac representation
for the gradient equation, to control errors not detected by the projected value iteration. An ex-
act decomposition of the HJB residual combines these errors into a policy-suboptimality bound
under verification and logarithmic Sobolev assumptions. In the linear-quadratic class, both pro-
jections are exact and recover the pointwise iteration, and we provide numerical experiments on
general models to demonstrate the coupled actor–critic learning in high-dimensions.
Keywords. Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation; Reinforcement learning; Policy iteration; Score
matching; Diffusion policy; Relaxed control; Langevin dynamics.

Introduction
Policy iteration algorithms, in which the policy of a stochastic control problem and its value are
improved alternately, have recently received renewed attention in continuous-time models, in par-
ticular within the relaxed control framework (Fleming and Nisio, 1984; Zhou, 1992) with entropy
regularization, where the randomization of the control is interpreted as exploration in the sense of
reinforcement learning (Wang et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2022; Reisinger and Zhang, 2021). Con-
vergence of policy iteration for entropy-regularized problems is established in Huang et al. (2025);
Ma et al. (2026); Tran et al. (2025), and for classical controlled diffusions in Kerimkulov et al.
(2020, 2021). In parallel, Jia and Zhou (2022a,b, 2023) developed the martingale approach to pol-
icy evaluation, policy gradient and q-learning in continuous time, where the optimal policy is the
Gibbs measure generated from the q-function, and Zhao et al. (2023) formulated policy optimization
through occupation times. Convergence rates of policy gradient methods were obtained in Reisinger

contributions include the random-measure formulation of continuous-time reinforcement learning

In conventional policy iteration, the state evolves in continuous time while policies are updated
in discrete steps, each using an exact or approximate policy evaluation, obtained from a partial
differential equation (PDE) or a learning algorithm.
A second line of work makes the iteration itself continuous. ˇSiˇska and Szpruch (2024) construct
gradient flows on the space of probability measures for regularized control problems, Kerimkulov
et al. (2023, 2024); Sethi et al. (2025) study Fisher-Rao gradient flows, mirror descent and en-
tropy annealing for measure-valued controls, Zhang et al. (2018) treat policy optimization as a
Wasserstein gradient flow, and Zhou and Lu (2023, 2024) propose actor-critic flows with linear
convergence. In Feng and Wang (2026), we introduced a continuous policy-value iteration in which
the approximations of the value function and of the control are updated simultaneously through
coupled Langevin-type dynamics in a fictitious time τ, for relaxed control problems with controlled
drift and diffusion. Policy improvement holds along the flow, and convergence is established under
the additional monotonicity and dissipativity hypotheses. The exact iteration is pointwise: at every
state x the policy π(x, ·) follows its own Langevin dynamics in the control variable, and the value is
evolved as a function of the state. Both are out of reach in high dimension. The goal of this paper
is to develop a numerical method which implement the this iteration scheme in high dimensions for
both control and state.
The key observation is contained in the proof of the policy improvement result of Feng and
Wang (2026): the rate of improvement is a discounted integral, along the state trajectory and over
the policy history, of ∥∇uH −λ∇u ln π∥2 averaged under the corresponding policy π, where H is
the Hamiltonian and λ the entropy parameter. Since the Gibbs policy of the Hamiltonian has
score ∇uH/λ, this quantity is λ2 times the relative Fisher information between the current policy
and the current Gibbs target. Fisher-type discrepancies also underlie score matching (Hyv¨arinen,
2005; Vincent, 2011), which is the training principle behind score-based diffusion models (Song and
Ermon, 2019; Song et al., 2021). In discrete-time reinforcement learning, diffusion models have
recently been used as policy classes (Wang et al., 2023; Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023; Chi et al.,
2023; Ren et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2023), going back to the energy-based policies
of Haarnoja et al. (2017), and Psenka et al. (2024) train a diffusion policy by matching its score to
the action-gradient of a Q-function. Our identity relates this principle to continuous-time control,
with the Hamiltonian in place of the Q-function: a discounted integral of the score discrepancy over
the policy history is the improvement rate of the iteration of Feng and Wang (2026), and hence the
dissipation rate of its value gap V −vτ.
It also shows that the residual of the match ∇uH −λ∇u ln π is the velocity field with which the
Langevin dynamics of Feng and Wang (2026) transport the law of the control. What we propose in
this paper is to move the samples of the policy along this velocity, as far as the policy class allows,
instead of to descend the loss. The main difference between our method and the continuous-time
form of Q-score matching, as proposed by Hua et al. (2025), is that: in the latter the action itself
is a diffusion in physical time whose drift is the control. The drift is penalized by a quadratic cost,
and the “score” matched to λ−1∇aQ is the first-order condition of this cost. The Boltzmann law of
Q appears as the stationary law of the action at a frozen state, and Q is learned from data through
a martingale condition. In our method, we assume that the model is known, and the policy is a
conditional density on the control space. The Gibbs law of the Hamiltonian is the exact maximizer
of the relaxed HJB equation, and the discounted score discrepancy gives the improvement rate of
an iteration in fictitious time.
2


Based on the above observation, we develop a score-Hamiltonian policy iteration for relaxed
control problems in which both the policy and value parts of the pointwise iteration are projected
onto parametric families and run in its fictitious time. The value function (the critic) is a parametric
family updated by the projection of the value dynamics onto the parameters, a Dirac–Frenkel pro-
jection whose relation to the pointwise flows is made explicit in Section 3.1. Then we amortize the
state-wise dynamics for the control, in the sense of amortized inference (Gershman and Goodman,
2014; Kingma and Welling, 2014). A single conditional sampler (the actor) u = Tϕ(x, ξ), shared
by all states, is moved by projecting the Langevin velocities ∇uH −λ∇u ln πϕ of (10) at sampled
states onto its parameters, so a policy sample at any state costs one evaluation of Tϕ. Thus no grid
in the state space is needed.
We show that the HJB residual of the critic decomposes into the policy-evaluation residual plus
λ times the Kullback-Leibler divergence of the actor from the Gibbs law of the critic, the latter
bounded by the score discrepancy when the Gibbs law satisfies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality.
Together with verification and occupation-measure assumptions, these residuals yield a bound on
the suboptimality of the learned policy.
The joint zero-residual solutions for policy and value
iterations satisfy the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation and its Gibbs policy relation and
thus uncover the optimal solution to the stochastic control problems. In the linear-quadratic case,
our policy and value iterates are obtained in closed form, where the projected Langevin flow is
the moment equation of the Langevin iterate, and the projected value dynamics coincide with a
Lyapunov flow and the stationary points satisfy an algebraic Riccati equation.
This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we recall the relaxed control problem and
the continuous policy-value iteration of Feng and Wang (2026). We represent the improvement
rate using the relative Fisher information. Section 3 introduces the parametric actor and critic
flows, presents their numerical algorithm, and describes the residual diagnostics and derivative
calculations used in the experiments. Section 4 discusses the linear-quadratic case, and Section 5
report numerical experiments including bimodal actor in high-dimensions.

3
Amortized Score-Hamiltonian policy-value iteration
The main results of this paper is the numerical method which realize the continuous policy-value
iteration in Feng and Wang (2026) in high dimensions. It represents the policy by a conditional
sampler and the value by a smooth function. Their parameters evolve together, using batches of
states in place of a state grid. We first give the two parameter flows and their implementation,
then the residuals used to monitor the computation.
