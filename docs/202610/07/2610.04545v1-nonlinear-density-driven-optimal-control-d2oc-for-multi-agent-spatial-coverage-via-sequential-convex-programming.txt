# Nonlinear Density-Driven Optimal Control (D 2OC) for Multi-Agent Spatial Coverage via Sequential Convex Programming 

Julian Martinez 1 and Kooktae Lee 1

Abstract — This paper presents a nonlinear extension of Density-Driven Optimal Control (D 2OC) for multi-agent spatial coverage with prescribed density distributions. Rather than assigning individual target locations, D 2OC drives the collective spatial distribution of agents toward a desired density through a Wasserstein-based objective. We extend this framework to multi-step finite-horizon control for discrete-time control-affine nonlinear systems using sequential convex programming. At each control update, the nonlinear dynamics are locally lin-earized over the prediction horizon, yielding a strictly convex quadratic program that preserves the Wasserstein barycentric structure while directly incorporating input constraints. We further characterize the effect of constrained control deviations and nonlinear Taylor remainders on the accuracy of the local linear prediction, establishing an explicit finite-horizon error bound and a two-step specialization for receding-horizon implementation. The resulting method retains the decentralized, distribution-driven nature of D 2OC while providing a compu-tationally efficient optimization procedure for nonlinear multi-agent systems. Simulations with unicycle and quadrotor teams show coverage performance comparable to nonlinear model predictive control, while substantially reducing computation time. These results demonstrate a tractable and theoretically characterized framework for density-driven spatial coverage under nonlinear dynamics. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-agent spatial coverage aims to distribute au-tonomous agents over a spatial domain according to sensing, monitoring, or mission-dependent requirements. Classical coverage control methods, such as the Voronoi-based frame-work, partition the workspace into agent-specific Voronoi cells and steer agents toward their cell centroids [1]. While effective for geometric coverage, such methods do not directly address non-uniform agent distributions. This motivates formulating non-uniform coverage as a spatial distribution-matching problem. Optimal transport (OT) provides a principled framework for comparing and matching spatial distributions through Wasserstein distances [2], [3]. This perspective has also been incorporated into multi-agent coverage control, where the objective is formulated directly in terms of the discrep-ancy between the empirical distribution of agents and a prescribed spatial density, rather than through individually assigned target locations [4]. Building on this distribution-based viewpoint, Density-Driven Optimal Control (D 2OC)

formulates non-uniform multi-agent coverage as a decen-tralized optimal-control problem that drives the empirical agent distribution toward a reference density through local sample selection, weight updates, and neighbor information exchange [5], [6], [7], [8]. The D 2OC framework was initially developed for linear time-invariant (LTI) dynamics and later extended to linear time-varying (LTV) dynamics [6], [7]. Its extension to non-linear systems, however, has so far been limited to a one-step prediction horizon. A multi-step nonlinear formulation must account for nonlinear agent dynamics while preserv-ing the Wasserstein-based coverage objective, decentralized structure, and input constraints. NMPC provides a framework for nonlinear dynamics and constraints through repeated finite-horizon optimization [9], while distributed receding-horizon methods address computational and communication requirements in multi-agent systems [10]. Sequential convex programming (SCP), in contrast, locally approximates non-linear dynamics and solves a sequence of convex subprob-lems, and has been applied to real-time nonlinear optimal control and successive convexification [11], [12], [13]. This makes SCP a natural candidate for extending D 2OC to multi-step nonlinear coverage although the resulting local approximation introduces a discrepancy between predicted and actual trajectories that must be characterized. In this paper, we extend D 2OC to discrete-time control-affine nonlinear systems using sequentially linearized local models. Each optimization step yields a strictly convex local Quadratic Program (QP) with input constraints while re-taining the Wasserstein-based coverage objective. We further characterize constraint-induced control deviations and derive a finite-horizon bound on the resulting linearization error. Thus, the main contributions of this paper are summa-rized as follows: 1) We extend the Wasserstein-based D 2OC formulation to discrete-time control-affine nonlinear systems while preserving its decentralized coverage mechanism and distribution-matching objective; 2) We formulate each non-linear D 2OC update as a strictly convex local QP via sequen-tial convex programming, retaining the Wasserstein barycen-ter structure and actuator constraints while enabling efficient computation; 3) We quantify input-saturation effects through the deviation between constrained and unconstrained QP solutions and incorporate this deviation into the linearization-error analysis; 4) We establish a finite-horizon error bound accounting for control deviations and second-order Taylor remainders, with a receding-horizon specialization for first-input application. The resulting framework combines the distribution-level arXiv:2610.04545v1 [cs.MA] 3 Oct 2026 objective of D 2OC with efficient local optimization and explicit prediction-error characterization for nonlinear multi-agent dynamics. Rather than solving the global Wasserstein problem cen-trally, the Density-Driven Optimal Control (D 2OC) frame-work operates through the following decentralized cycle [5], [7], [8]: 1) Local sample selection and control: each agent selects nearby sample points with nonzero remaining weights and computes a feasible control input to reduce its local Wasserstein cost. 2) Weight update: after executing the control action, the agent decreases the remaining weights of the sample points it covers, recording its coverage progress. 3) Neighbor information exchange: when agents commu-nicate, they synchronize their sample weights using the information received from neighboring agents, avoiding redundant coverage. VI. S IMULATION 

We evaluate the proposed controller on two control-affine nonlinear multi-agent systems: a unicycle team and a quadrotor team. The proposed SCP procedure relinearizes the dynamics at each decision step and solves the resulting strictly convex local QP, as characterized in Theorem 1. We compare its coverage performance and computational cost with a nonlinear NMPC baseline under the same objective, horizon, and input constraints. The simulation also evaluates the linearization error bound established in Theorem 2. 

A. Unicycle Model 

Five agents with state x = [ px, p y , θ ]⊤ and input u =[v, ω ]⊤ cover a 100 × 100 m domain. The sampling time and prediction horizon are set to T = 0.1 s and H =20 , respectively. Only the first input of each horizon is applied before relinearization. The reference distribution ν

contains 500 samples drawn from a five-component Gaussian mixture, as shown in Fig. 1. The initial positions of the five unicycle agents are indicated by cross marks. To evaluate the proposed method, ten simulation runs are performed, with the reference sample points independently redrawn for each of the ten seeds. The sample weights are initially assigned uniformly as 1500 and then decreased according to the rule in [5] as the agents cover the domain. The simulation is terminated when the sum of the remaining sample weights falls below one percent of its initial value.

This paper extended D2OC to discrete-time control-affine nonlinear multi-agent systems using sequential con-vex programming. The proposed approach preserves the Wasserstein-based coverage objective and barycentric struc-ture while reducing each local update to a strictly convex QP with input constraints. We further quantified the devi-ation between the constrained and unconstrained local QP solutions and incorporated this control deviation into a finite-horizon bound on the error between the nonlinear trajectory and its linearized prediction. Simulations with unicycle and quadrotor teams showed that the proposed method achieves coverage performance comparable to NMPC while substan-tially reducing computation time. These results demonstrate the potential of the proposed formulation for computationally efficient nonlinear coverage control.
