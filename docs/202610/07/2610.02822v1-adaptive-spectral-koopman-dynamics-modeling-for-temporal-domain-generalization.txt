## ABSTRACT 

Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG) has emerged to address real-world streaming data with distribution shifts over time. However, existing methods are either prone to overfitting to domain-specific noise in the data space or be-come overly complex and less interpretable in the parameter space. To bridge these gaps, we propose AdaSpecK , a spectral-Koopman framework with adap-tive context extraction for TDG. To mitigate noise fitting to irregularly sam-pled domains, we introduce spectral-regularized Koopman dynamics modeling, which applies spectral-aware filtering in the latent space to extract denoised low-frequency trajectories and learn a Koopman operator to model the system dy-namics in a linearized space. To model complex historical environments under non-stationarity, we design a context-informed heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism. Specifically, we employ a target-conditioned attention module to attend to distinct past windows, producing a dynamic, target-specific historical summary. By constructing an environmental signature from the current evolu-tionary pattern, our model adaptively perceives which aspects of the past context are most informative for future prediction via a learned router. Extensive experi-ments on eight diverse classification and regression benchmarks demonstrate that AdaSpecK achieves state-of-the-art performance. The code and datasets are avail-able at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Ada-Spec-K. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Conventional machine learning typically assumes that training and test data are independently and identically distributed (IID), which expects a model to generalize across training and test sets. How-ever, this assumption is rarely satisfied in real-world applications where data often arrive as con-tinuous streams and their underlying distributions shift dynamically over time (Han et al., 2024). For instance, in financial markets, trading behaviors evolve as economic conditions change; in so-cial media, linguistic trends and topics shift rapidly over time. This phenomenon, known as concept drift (Lu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025), leads models trained on past data to degrade when deployed in distributionally shifted environments (Gama et al., 2014). This has motivated the emergence of Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG), which aims to leverage evolving historical data to build models capable of generalizing to near-future domains (Cai et al., 2024). Existing approaches to TDG can be broadly categorized into data-centric and model-centric paradigms. Data-centric methods operate on the premise that temporal robustness can be achieved at the data or feature level. They employ strategies such as data augmentation (Liu et al., 2018) or generative modeling to simulate potential future distributions (Zeng et al., 2024), or leverage repre-sentation learning (Gong et al., 2019) to extract domain-invariant features and disentangle domain-shared features from domain-specific ones. In contrast, model-centric approaches focus on adapting to evolving domains by dynamically predicting or extrapolating model parameters. These methods utilize techniques such as gradient interpolation (Nasery et al., 2021) or probabilistic frameworks to characterize latent evolutionary dynamics within the parameter space (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Predicting future OOD domains requires modeling dynamic temporal patterns across evolving historical environments. TDG often involves observations at irregular timestamps. Directly learning a predictive model in the data space risks overfitting to domain-specific patterns or noise, hindering reliable general-ization to future out-of-distribution (OOD) domains (Lu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025). Although some methods instead model temporal evolution in the parameter space, this introduces its own diffi-culties: the parameter space is inherently more abstract and higher-dimensional, and its performance is tightly coupled to the architecture and design choices of the predictive model itself. Furthermore, the dynamics governing how parameters evolve across irregular time steps are typically highly non-linear and lack clear structural priors, making it unstable and prone to error. 

Challenge 2: Data non-stationarity complicates the extraction of dynamic temporal patterns across historical environments. Temporal non-stationarity, manifested as shifting data distribu-tions and evolving feature semantics, undermines the assumption of stable statistical regularities over time (Wang et al., 2023b). As a result, periodicity, seasonality, or recurring temporal structure that are readily captured under stationarity often become obfuscated or highly volatile. This ren-ders traditional temporal modeling techniques often fail to generalize in real-world TDG scenarios (Gama et al., 2014). Moreover, future target domains may prioritize distinct contextual cues drawn from historical environments. For instance, some domains may place greater emphasis on long-term trends and macro-level patterns, while others may be highly sensitive to recent, short-term dynam-ics. Therefore, a robust TDG model should not only adapt to the continuous evolution of temporal patterns but also possess the flexibility to generalize across heterogeneous domain structures. To address these challenges, we introduce AdaSpecK , a spectral-Koopman framework with adaptive context extraction for temporal domain generalization. To mitigate noise fitting to irregularly sam-pled training domains, we introduce spectral-regularized Koopman dynamics modeling. It applies spectral-aware filtering in the latent space to extract denoised low-frequency trajectories. By focus-ing on robust, slowly evolving signals, the model enhances resilience to irregular domain shifts and improves generalization to OOD scenarios. It then leverages a Koopman-style transition to model future time evolution and optimizes a Koopman operator that captures system dynamics in a lin-earized space. To address the difficulty of extracting historical information under non-stationarity, we design a context-informed heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism. Specifically, we employ a target-conditioned attention module, which enables each future step to attend to distinct subsets of the historical windows. This effectively produces a dynamic, target-specific historical summary of past observations that adapts to the anticipated characteristics of the future domain. Furthermore, by constructing an environmental signature from the current evolutionary pattern, our model adap-tively perceives which aspects of the past context are most informative for future prediction via a lightweight, learnable router. Overall, our main contributions are summarized as follows: 2• We introduce spectral-regularized Koopman dynamics modeling, which applies spectral-aware filtering in a latent space to extract denoised low-frequency trajectories and learn a Koopman operator to model the system dynamics. • We propose a context-informed heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism that leverages target-conditioned attention to exploit the most relevant past for each prediction step, en-abling the model to selectively integrate historical contexts through adaptive expert routing. • Extensive experiments on eight diverse benchmarks that span five classification and three regression tasks show that AdaSpecK achieves state-of-the-art performance. 

## 2 RELATED WORKS 

Temporal Domain Generalization. Domain Generalization (DG) aims to learn from multiple source domains and generalize to unseen target domains (Muandet et al., 2013; Motiian et al., 2017; Li et al., 2017; Balaji et al., 2018; Dou et al., 2019; Yu et al., 2022; Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz, 2021). DG methods can be classified into three strategies (Wang et al., 2023a): data manipulation (Liu et al., 2018; Qiao et al., 2020), representation learning (Gong et al., 2019; Li et al., 2018b), and learning strategies such as meta-learning (Cai et al., 2023; Li et al., 2018a) and ensembles (Mancini et al., 2018). Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG) extends DG to time-evolving environments, where domain distributions shift over time. Early efforts primarily adopt data-driven strategies. S-MLDG (Li et al., 2020) frames TDG as a sequential meta-learning problem, while TKNets (Zeng et al., 2024) capture temporal dynamics by minimizing the divergence between predicted and actual future domain distributions for classification. Other methods focus on adapting model parameters to evolving domains. Gradient Interpolation (Nasery et al., 2021) extrapolates model updates by super-vising the first-order Taylor expansion of the learned function. LSSAE (Qin et al., 2022) introduces a probabilistic latent space to uncover structural regularities in predictive models, and DRAIN (Bai et al., 2023a) uses a recurrent architecture to generate time-adaptive parameters. More recently, TDG has been formalized as a continuous-time setting (Cai et al., 2024), with emerging work ex-ploring frequency-domain analysis of parameter trajectories (Yu et al., 2025) to better characterize temporal concept drift (Gama et al., 2014). Despite this progress, existing methods remain limited: those operating in the data space are often prone to overfitting to domain-specific noise, while those in the parameter space tend to become overly complex and less interpretable. Moreover, none ex-plicitly model dynamic historical environments under non-stationary conditions, overlooking how past contextual information should be adaptively leveraged for future prediction. 

Continuous Dynamics and Frequency Learning. Continuous Dynamical Systems (CDS) model temporal evolution via differential equations, offering a natural framework for systems that evolve without discrete time steps. Neural extensions such as Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018b) and Latent ODEs (Rubanova et al., 2019) enable flexible, continuous-time representations in deep learning. In parallel, frequency-based learning methods analyze non-stationary temporal patterns by transform-ing signals into the spectral domain (Wu et al., 2021; 2023), uncovering periodic structures and multi-scale trends that remain obscured in raw time-domain data. For instance, prior works (Zhou et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2024) leverage spectral decomposition to explicitly disen-tangle temporal signals into low-frequency structures and high-frequency residuals or noise, and model these components separately to enhance both robustness and predictive performance. Despite their strengths, CDS-based approaches often overlook distribution shifts and structured contextual information, while spectral methods typically lack explicit dynamic modeling. These limitations motivate our core insight: integrating evolving dynamics with frequency-aware representations of-fers a promising pathway toward improved temporal generalization.

## 4 METHODOLOGY 

We present AdaSpecK , a spectral-Koopman framework with adaptive context extraction for TDG (Figure 2). First, it employs spectral-regularized Koopman dynamics modeling to mitigate domain-specific noise fitting. By applying spectral-aware filtering in a latent space, it extracts denoised, low-frequency trajectories and learns a compact Koopman operator to model future evolution in a ro-bust, linearized space. Second, it incorporates a context-informed heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism to model complex historical environments under non-stationary. By employing a target-conditioned attention, the model generates dynamic, task-specific historical summaries that attend to distinct subsets of the historical windows. Furthermore, a learned router utilizes environmental signatures to adaptively select the most informative historical information for future prediction. 4.1 SPECTRAL -R EGULARIZED KOOPMAN DYNAMICS MODELING

## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

5.1 EXPERIMENT SETTINGS 

Datasets. Following Koodos (Cai et al., 2024) and FreKoo (Yu et al., 2025), we evaluate AdaSpecK on eight datasets spanning both classification and regression tasks. The five classification datasets include Rotated MNIST (Rot-MNIST) (Deng, 2012), Twitter Influenza Risk (Twitter) (Zhao et al., 2017; Bai et al., 2023b), and YearBook (Yao et al., 2022), Online News Popularity (ONP) (Fernandes et al., 2015), and Shuttle (UCI Machine Learning Repository, 2025); the three regression datasets consist of Tropical Cyclone Intensity (Cyclone) (Chen et al., 2018a), House Prices (House) (Cock, 2011), and ApplianceEnergy (Appliance) (Candanedo et al., 2017). These datasets capture diverse forms of temporal domain shift and real-world non-stationarity, providing a comprehensive bench-mark for evaluation. More details of the datasets are provided in Appendix A.1. 

Baselines. We compare our method against baselines from three representative categories: (1) Time-agnostic methods, including Offline (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025), LastDomain (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025), IncFinetune (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025), IRM (Arjovsky et al., 2019), and V-REx (Krueger et al., 2021); (2) Discrete temporal domain adaptation/generalization methods, including CIDA (Wang et al., 2020), TKNets (Zeng et al., 2024), DRAIN, and DRAIN-∆t (Bai et al., 2023a); and (3) Continuous temporal domain generalization methods, including DeepODE (Chen et al., 2018b; Rubanova et al., 2019), Koodos (Cai et al., 2024) and Frekoo (Yu et al., 2025). Main Results. Table 1 shows that AdaSpecK achieves the best performance across all eight bench-marks, highlighting its superior capability in handling non-stationary temporal shifts. Time-agnostic baselines (e.g., Offline, LastDomain, and IncFinetune) suffer significant performance degradation on long-horizon datasets, confirming that naive reuse of historical data is inadequate under evolving temporal drift. Compared to state-of-the-art continuous TDG baselines (e.g., Koodos and Frekoo), AdaSpecK demonstrates consistent superiority. Notably, our method achieves the most substan-tial gains on Twitter, YearBook, Cyclone, and Appliance, where the datasets are characterized by multi-step future evolution. This resilience suggests that our learned latent dynamics provide a more stable and robust representation of temporal evolution than approaches relying solely on short-range interpolation or adjacent-domain transitions, avoiding overfitting to domain-specific ar-tifacts or local noise. Moreover, AdaSpecK explicitly models evolving historical contexts through a context-informed, heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism, adaptively determining how past information should be leveraged for future prediction. In summary, the proposed spectral-Koopman framework with adaptive context extraction not only improves average predictive accuracy but also enhances robustness and stability under persistent temporal distribution shifts. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

This paper proposes AdaSpecK , a spectral-Koopman framework with adaptive context extraction for temporal domain generalization. To mitigate noise fitting to irregular sampling, we introduce spectral-regularized Koopman dynamics modeling. It applies spectral-aware filtering in a latent space to extract denoised low-frequency trajectories and learns a compact Koopman operator to model system dynamics in a robust, linearized space. To handle non-stationarity in historical contexts, we design a context-informed heterogeneous pattern extraction mechanism. A target-conditioned attention module enables each future timestep to attend to distinct subsets of the histori-cal windows, while an environmental signature derived from the current evolutionary pattern guides a learned router to adaptively identify the most predictive past context. A.1 DATASETS 

Following Koodos (Cai et al., 2024) and FreKoo (Yu et al., 2025), and focusing on the datasets that are both discussed in the two reference papers and implemented in the new codebase, we evaluate AdaSpecK on eight benchmarks covering classification and regression under different temporal drift patterns. Their main characteristics are summarized as follows: • Rotated MNIST (Rot-MNIST). This is a synthetic image classification benchmark built from MNIST digits (Deng, 2012), where concept drift is created by gradually rotating the images across domains as in the temporal benchmark protocols of Koodos (Cai et al., 2024) and FreKoo (Yu et al., 2025). The domains are temporally ordered, and the prediction target always lies in later unseen domains. Its main difficulty comes from a steadily increasing geometric transformation rather than label noise. • Twitter. This dataset uses tweet streams to estimate influenza activity. The domains corre-spond to temporally separated collection windows during multiple flu seasons, so the drift is driven by changes in tweet volume, seasonal disease activity, and evolving user behav-ior (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Compared with synthetic rotation datasets, it reflects a real-world temporally evolving classification problem with stronger non-stationarity. • YearBook. This is a gender classification benchmark built from frontal yearbook portraits collected over many decades, following the YearBook temporal-shift benchmark in Wild-Time (Yao et al., 2022). Each domain corresponds to one sampled year in chronological order. The drift arises from long-term changes in clothing, hairstyle, imaging quality, and broader social norms, making it a representative visual benchmark with slow but persistent temporal shift. • Cyclone. This regression dataset predicts tropical cyclone intensity from satellite imagery. Each domain is associated with temporally ordered cyclone observations, and the shift is induced by seasonal and regional variations in storm appearance and wind intensity (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). It is therefore a visually complex forecasting benchmark with substantial temporal variation. • House. This regression benchmark predicts house prices from tabular features collected over multiple years. The domains are organized chronologically, and the underlying drift mainly reflects changing market conditions and temporal economic variation, making it a real-world nonstationary tabular dataset (Cock, 2011; Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). • Online News Popularity (ONP). This classification dataset contains news articles from Mashable with 58 heterogeneous features, and the task is to predict article popularity (Fer-nandes et al., 2015). The data are split into time-ordered domains over roughly two years, with the first five domains used for training and the last one for testing in the TDG protocol. Its drift is relatively mild compared with the other benchmarks, but it still captures gradual changes in how content attracts social attention over time. • Shuttle. This dataset is a temporally ordered multi-class classification benchmark derived from flight-status data and is known for its strong class imbalance (UCI Machine Learning Repository, 2025; Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). The domains are formed chronolog-ically, with earlier periods used for training and the latest period used for testing. The challenge comes from real-world distribution changes in operational patterns over time rather than from manually constructed perturbations. • ApplianceEnergy (Appliance). This regression benchmark predicts appliance energy con-sumption from indoor sensor readings in a low-energy building (Candanedo et al., 2017). The data are recorded at 10-minute intervals over several months and then partitioned into chronological domains. The drift is associated with changing usage behavior and envi-ronmental conditions across time, which makes it a practical benchmark for periodic and real-world temporal variation. 14 A.2 BASELINE 

We compare AdaSpecK with several families of baselines that reflect different ways of handling tem-poral drift. Offline, LastDomain, and IncFinetune follow the standard non-temporal or sequential training baselines used in recent TDG studies (Cai et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025), where Offline trains a single model on the union of all source domains and ignores temporal order entirely, LastDomain uses only the most recent observed source domain to test whether short-range recency alone is suffi-cient, and IncFinetune updates the predictor sequentially from earlier to later domains as a practical continual-training baseline. IRM and V-REx are invariant-learning methods that aim to suppress domain-specific shortcuts by favoring predictors whose risks are stable across source domains (Ar-jovsky et al., 2019; Krueger et al., 2021), although they do not explicitly model domain evolution over time. CIDA incorporates temporal or domain information as a continuous conditioning variable and aligns representations across domains (Wang et al., 2020), while TKNets models transitions be-tween temporally ordered domains through a dedicated temporal transfer mechanism (Zeng et al., 2024). DRAIN and DRAIN-∆t forecast future predictors by learning parameter dynamics from the observed domain sequence, with the latter additionally incorporating time-gap information for non-uniform intervals (Bai et al., 2023a). DeepODE models temporal evolution in a latent dynamical system defined by neural ordinary differential equations (Chen et al., 2018b; Rubanova et al., 2019), and Koodos introduces Koopman-space parameter evolution with structured dynamic constraints (Cai et al., 2024). We also report FreKoo as a strong recent temporal generalization reference that combines spectral decomposition and Koopman modeling to separate low-frequency temporal struc-ture from high-frequency variations (Yu et al., 2025). Together, these methods cover time-agnostic training, invariant learning, temporal alignment, parameter-forecasting approaches, and frequency-aware temporal dynamics. A.3 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
