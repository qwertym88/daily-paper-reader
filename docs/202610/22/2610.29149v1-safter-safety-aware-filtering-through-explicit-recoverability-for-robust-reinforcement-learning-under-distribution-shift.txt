## ABSTRACT 

Learning safe policies that generalize to unseen environments is difficult under bounded actuation, where an unsafe decision may become unrecoverable. Con-ventional control barrier function (CBF) filters enforce only instantaneous safety and ignore future recoverability. We propose SAFTER (Safety-Aware Filtering Through Explicit Recoverability), which embeds a differentiable, closed-form backup-CBF filter into Soft Actor-Critic training. SAFTER unifies current safety, finite-horizon backup safety, terminal recoverability, and input admissibility into a single differentiable certificate. Its CBF condition yields an exact minimum-intervention projection without iterative online optimization. In quadruped navi-gation, SAFTER reaches 90% training success with 30 .6% fewer transitions than Vanilla SAC and 34 .1–35 .1% fewer than current-state CBF-filtered SAC base-lines. Across unseen simulated environments, SAFTER improves success by up to 42 .4 and 61 .4 percentage points over the state-of-the-art methods CBF–RL and SEA–Nav, respectively, and achieves 73 .3% and 76 .7% success on two real-world navigation maps. Ablations identify accurate differentiation through the safety filter as a key contributor to robustness, in which replacing the projection Jaco-bian with the identity approximation reduces success rate in a High-Shift environ-ment from 61 .4% to 6.2% . These results show that recoverability-aware filtering serves as both a runtime safety mechanism and a learning mechanism for sample-efficient, robust policies. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) can produce adaptive navigation policies directly from interaction and local sensory observations, but trial-and-error exploration can generate unsafe actions during both training and deployment (Brunke et al., 2022; Guerrier et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2023). Reward penal-ties can discourage collisions or constraint violations, yet they provide only an indirect learning signal and do not ensure that each executed action satisfies a prescribed safety condition (Krasowski et al., 2023). For safety-critical robotic navigation, a useful learning architecture should therefore protect the system during interaction while still allowing the policy to improve from the intervention it receives. Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide a principled way to enforce state constraints by minimally modifying a nominal control input so that a safe set remains forward invariant (Ames et al., 2019). This reactive view, however, is incomplete under bounded actuation. A system can satisfy all in-stantaneous geometric constraints and still enter a state from which the available control authority is no longer sufficient to prevent a future violation. Backup CBFs (bCBFs) address this distinction by asking a stronger question: if the nominal behavior becomes unsafe, does a prescribed recovery maneuver remain feasible over a finite horizon and reach a terminal safe set? (Gurriet et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2021). This notion of future recoverability is particularly relevant to learning-based nav-igation because the policy should ideally avoid actions that are safe only momentarily but eliminate subsequent braking or avoidance authority. 1arXiv:2610.29149v1 [eess.SY] 21 Oct 2026 The challenge is to make this predictive notion of safety practical inside RL training. A sam-pled bCBF introduces many simultaneous conditions: current-state safety, safety at multiple future backup states, terminal recoverability, and bounded-input constraints. Direct enforcement therefore leads to a multi-constraint online optimization that must be solved at every environment interaction. Existing work has separately developed smooth CBF composition, closed-form CBF filters, backup-based safe sets, and differentiable safety layers (Rabiee & Hoagg, 2025; Wang et al., 2025; van Wijk et al., 2026; Yun et al., 2026). What remains important for learning is a formulation that preserves finite-horizon backup recoverability, handles bounded commands, collapses the resulting constraint collection to an analytical minimum-intervention map, and exposes the exact filter Jacobian to the task policy during training. We introduce SAFTER , a recoverability-aware safety-filtered RL framework built around this ob-jective. Figure 1 summarizes the central idea. The actor proposes a task-oriented nominal action. SAFTER predicts a prescribed backup maneuver, composes current, future, terminal, and input-admissibility conditions into a single differentiable certificate, and projects the nominal command through an exact closed-form safety map. During training, the filtered action is the action actually executed and evaluated by the critic, while the actor differentiates through the analytical safety cor-rection. During deployment, the actor is frozen and the same filter remains active. Thus, the safety layer is used not only to shield the robot but also to shape what the policy learns. Our contributions are threefold. First , we construct a recoverability-aware composite certificate that combines current-state HOCBF conditions, sampled backup-horizon safety, terminal recov-erability, and bounded-input admissibility after control-input augmentation. The construction re-duces the sampled predictive-safety conditions to one affine CBF constraint whose exact minimum-intervention solution is available in closed form. Second , we embed this analytical filter directly into SAC so that the critic learns from executed filtered actions and the actor receives gradients through the exact recoverability-aware filter mapping. Third , controlled quadruped experiments separate learning efficiency, policy internalization, distribution-shift robustness, and filter-gradient effects. The results show faster transition-based convergence in a matched moderate-speed study and substantially improved navigation success in unseen, geometrically shifted environments; ablations identify the analytical filter Jacobian as an important learning mechanism. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Safety-filtered reinforcement learning. Safety layers and shielding mechanisms separate task learning from explicit safety enforcement, while constrained and safety-critic formulations provide alternative mechanisms for safe policy learning (Chow et al., 2021; Hsu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023). Reward shaping can bias policies away from unsafe behavior (Nilaksh et al., 2024), while explicit locomotion constraints can reduce reward engineering (Kim et al., 2024). Training-time safety filtering has also been shown to help the policy account for filter interventions and improve sample efficiency (Bejarano et al., 2025; Van Wijk et al., 2024). Reachability-based approaches can switch between task and recovery policies (He et al., 2024), while observation-conditioned reach-ability filtering has been developed for quadruped navigation in previously unknown environments (Lin et al., 2026). In legged navigation, SEA–Nav learns safe and agile LiDAR-based locomotion (Chen et al., 2026), and CBF–RL combines closed-form CBF filtering with barrier-inspired reward shaping so that the task policy can internalize safety behavior (Yang et al., 2025). These approaches motivate training-time safety integration but do not directly provide sampled finite-horizon backup recoverability through the task-policy action map. 

Backup CBFs and predictive safety. Backup CBFs characterize recoverability through the flow of a prescribed backup controller, beginning with active-set invariance formulations (Gurriet et al., 2018) and later extensions using time-varying backups (Singletary et al., 2022). RLBUS learns a neural backup policy to enlarge the finite-time safe backward image (Rabiee & Safari, 2025); PS2-RL couples task-policy learning with a differentiable backup-CBF projection and considers both learned and analytical backup constructions (Yun et al., 2026). Backup-CBF filtering has also been developed for bounded-input and higher-order systems (van Wijk et al., 2026; Lin & Wu, 2026). SAFTER instead combines an augmented composite backup certificate with an explicit projection in surrogate-control space and differentiates through this map during off-policy task-policy learning. 2Composite and explicit safety filters. LSE composition has been used to combine multiple current-state CBF constraints and obtain a closed-form safety filter for end-to-end RL (Wang et al., 2025). CBF-based quadruped navigation has also used smooth LiDAR-derived safety functions with on-line QP filtering (Unlu et al., 2024). For backup barriers, smooth minimum/maximum constructions aggregate finite-horizon conditions while retaining optimization-based control synthesis (Rabiee & Hoagg, 2025). van Wijk et al. (2026) derive a closed-form bounded-input bCBF controller by inter-polating between nominal and backup controls, restricting the correction to that interpolation direc-tion. SAFTER instead constructs a single composite affine constraint in an augmented surrogate-input space and applies the exact minimum-Euclidean correction in the full surrogate control space. The differentiable mapping is then used directly in the SAC actor update.

Problem 3.1 (Recoverability-aware differentiable safe RL) . Given πϕ, the safety model (f, g ), safe set CS , bounded input set U, backup controller ub, and terminal set CxB , construct Fη such that the executed command: (i) satisfies the encoded current, sampled backup, terminal, and input conditions whenever feasible; (ii) minimally modifies the nominal task command; and (iii) can be evaluated efficiently and differentiated during SAC training. Direct sampled bCBF enforcement satisfies the predictive requirement but retains a multi-constraint optimization inside every RL interaction. Section 4 develops SAFTER to remove this bottleneck by combining bounded-command augmentation, finite-horizon backup prediction, a single composite certificate, and an analytical minimum-intervention projection whose local Jacobian can be used directly in policy optimization. 

## 4 RECOVERABILITY -A WARE DIFFERENTIABLE SAFETY FILTERING 

SAFTER realizes Problem 3.1 through the analytical pipeline in Figure 1. It augments the bounded physical command with first-order dynamics, evaluates finite-horizon backup recoverability in the augmented model, and combines the resulting current, future, terminal, and input-admissibility mar-gins into one differentiable certificate. The CBF condition of this certificate is a single affine con-straint in a surrogate input, yielding an exact minimum-intervention projection and an exact local Jacobian wherever the filter is differentiable. The central technical contribution is this reduction from sampled multi-constraint backup-CBF en-forcement to a single analytical projection after the constituent safety margins have been evaluated. ## 5 EXPERIMENTS AND ANALYSIS 

The experiments are organized to test the three mechanisms isolated in the problem formulation. 

RQ1 asks whether finite-horizon backup recoverability, rather than current-state filtering alone, im-proves transition-based learning efficiency, and whether its analytical realization reduces safety-filter latency relative to QP-based synthesis. RQ2 asks whether the resulting policy–filter system remains effective as obstacle geometry shifts away from the training maps. RQ3 asks whether learning through the exact analytical projection Jacobian matters when the forward safety-filter mapping is unchanged. The baselines and SAFTER are evaluated in MuJoCo 3.3.6 using a Unitree Go2 with a frozen 50 Hz 

low-level locomotion policy. The high-level policy operates at 10 Hz using local LiDAR, goal, measured-velocity, and command-history observations. SAC-based methods use 256 parallel envi-ronments, five independent training seeds, and a maximum budget of 20 .48 M transitions per seed. In both training and evaluation, failure denotes episode termination caused by obstacle collision, falling, or contact with the environment boundary wall. The bCBF–LSE–QP baseline uses the same composite half-space, backup model, terminal set, command dynamics, and finite-rate realization as SAFTER.

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduced SAFTER, a recoverability-aware safe-RL framework that combines command-state augmentation, hierarchical LSE composition, and an exact minimum-Euclidean projection into a dif-ferentiable training map. Experiments show improved transition-based sample efficiency and strong performance under geometric distribution shift in both simulated and real-world environments, while the Jacobian ablation highlights the importance of differentiating through the analytical safety-filter map. Theorem 4.2 is a continuous-time result for the reduced safety model. Two sampling gaps remain outside that theorem: (i) the backup trajectory is checked only at the selected τk, and (ii) the safety filter is recomputed only at the 10 Hz high-level updates. In the reported finite-rate implementation, the same physical command limits included in the composite certificate are also enforced by a final component-wise clipping operation. This ensures that the executed command remains within its prescribed bounds, but the continuous-time theorem does not guarantee that the clipped command satisfies the composite CBF condition between high-level updates. The LiDAR certificate is also local to the current scan. During one filter computation, observed endpoints are fixed while the reduced pose and command states evolve relative to them. The result therefore assumes that this local model and the zero-surrogate backup adequately represent the high-level motion over the prediction horizon. It does not certify full-body tracking error, unobserved or occluded geometry, moving obstacles absent from the model, or arbitrary LiDAR noise. Conse-quently, the formal guarantee applies to the stated reduced safety model and measured scan-defined obstacles, not to every full quadruped state.
