# ABSTRACT 

Reinforcement learning has become the foundational paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Yet the dominant approach, Proximal Policy Op-timization (PPO), relies on a geometrically degenerate clipping proxy that cannot adapt its resistance to policy shifts, leading to premature clipping and degraded exploration. Subsequent variants alleviate these issues through fragmented and heuristic modifications. We propose Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization (GPPO) , a principled and unified framework for understanding and designing LLM RL algorithms through the lens of divergence. We establish a general-ized f -divergence trust-region theory with a monotonic performance improvement guarantee, and show that different f -divergences induce distinct geometries and policy update dynamics. We further introduce novel estimator topologies that provide more faithful divergence estimates than the conventional single-sample Monte Carlo estimation. Under this framework, PPO and recent variants such as DAPO, DPPO, and RIPO emerge as special cases, establishing a principled two-dimensional design space of Divergence × Estimator . Extensive experiments on eight competition-level math and coding benchmarks show that both dimensions are critical. The best-performing GPPO instantiation surpasses standard GRPO by up to 50% and 14% on math and coding, delivering SOTA performance. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) has established itself as the primary paradigm for advancing com-plex reasoning in LLMs (Guo et al., 2025; Comanici et al., 2025; OpenAI, 2024), powering break-throughs across long-horizon domains such as mathematical reasoning (Shao et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025), code generation (Dou et al., 2024; Anthropic, 2024), and deep search (OpenAI, 2025; Jin et al., 2025). Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) has become a dominant approach for stable and effective RL training, with its “proximal” behavior enforced through clipping. Yet be-neath this heuristic mechanism lies a geometrically degenerate proxy: PPO implicitly constrains a single-sample Monte Carlo (MC) estimate of the Total Variation (TV) divergence, whose generating function has zero curvature and strictly symmetric bounds. This linear geometry cannot adapt its resistance to the magnitude of policy shifts, treating small and large deviations alike. This rigidity can lead to premature clipping, distorted trust-region scaling, and exploration collapse (Yu et al., 2025; Cai et al., 2026; Qi et al., 2026). To alleviate these pathologies, recent variants (e.g., DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), DPPO (Qi et al., 2026), RIPO (Cai et al., 2026)) refine trust-region constraints through relaxing clipping bounds or adjust-ing Riemannian metrics. Despite their empirical gains, these methods are largely motivated by specific empirical observations, leading to fragmented modifications of the trust-region constraint. Consequently, existing approaches offer no unified, first-principles account of how these seemingly

We propose Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization (GPPO), a principled framework that uni-fies the fragmented landscape of LLM RL through the lens of divergence. Rather than introducing another isolated algorithm, GPPO establishes a principled two-dimensional design space of Diver-gence × Estimator , enabling systematic design of proximal policy optimization. At its core, GPPO establishes a generalized f -divergence trust-region theory, showing that policy optimization under f -divergence level-set constraints admits a monotonic performance improvement guarantee. Crucially, we show that different f -divergences induce distinct geometries, providing a principled basis for understanding their different policy update dynamics. Beyond the choice of divergence, GPPO further generalizes how divergences are estimated, in-troducing novel estimator topologies that reduce single-sample Monte Carlo variance and provide more faithful divergence estimates. Under this unified lens, PPO and recent variants such as DAPO, DPPO, and RIPO emerge naturally as special cases of GPPO. Empirically, our experiments reveal that proximal policy optimization is highly sensitive to both di-vergence geometry and estimator topology. Compared with the linear and symmetric TV geometry, divergences with non-zero curvature generally yield stronger performance. Estimator topology is equally important for that Atomic estimation consistently outperforms single-sample MC across all evaluated divergences. Notably, KL with Atomic estimation achieves the strongest performance, improving the average score by 50% on math and 14% on coding relative to the standard TV-MC formulation. These results validate the proposed two-dimensional design space that divergence ge-ometry and estimator topology are both critical to effective RL optimization. In summary, our contributions are threefold: • We propose Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization (GPPO), establishing a two-dimensional design space of Divergence × Estimator , which unifies PPO and recent proximal variants, and inspires principled exploration of new proximal policy optimization algorithms. • We establish a generalized f -divergence trust-region theory with a monotonic performance im-provement guarantee, and illustrate how divergence geometry shapes policy update dynamics. • We introduce novel estimator topologies that provide more faithful divergence estimates than single-sample Monte Carlo estimation, achieving SOTA performance on math and coding.

Table 1 summarizes the main experimental results across eight competition-level benchmarks. Fig 2 visualized the training dynamics of different GPPO instantiations on math and coding. 

GPPO establishes a new state-of-the-art across reasoning tasks. As highlighted in Table 1, our top-performing GPPO instantiation (KL divergence under Atomic estimation) achieves an average of 41 .5 on mathematical reasoning and 59 .7 on code generation, drastically improving upon the standard TV–MC baseline (GRPO) by +49 .8% and +13 .9% , respectively, outperforming all exist-ing RL algorithms. Beyond this optimal configuration, GPPO instantiations consistently dominate the design space, every divergence equipped with an Atomic or Binary estimator outperforms the strongest baseline (GMPO). These gains are uniform rather than dataset-specific, confirming that systematic exploration of the Divergence × Estimator interplay provides a fundamentally superior foundation for policy optimization than ad-hoc clipping heuristics. 

Divergence geometry shapes policy optimization dynamics. Under single-sample MC, KL im-proves over TV by 24 .9% on math and 3.6% on code, while Hellinger yields gains of 14 .8% and 

3.8% , both enhancing the entropy level of TV. TV–MC and χ2–MC share the same results for that our matched calibration of single-sample MC coincidentally assigns them the same symmetric inter-val r ∈ [0 .8, 1.2] , their geometric differences become contributing when estimated more faithfully with Atomic or Binary. Under Atomic estimation, KL surpasses TV by +14 .3% and +8 .7% on math and code. Binary reverses this on math but yields only modest gains for non-TV divergences on code, showing that divergence geometry is filtered through estimator topology rather than uni-versally ranked. One possible explanation is that TV–Binary directly constrains the absolute mass change |π(a | s) − μ(a | s)| ≤ δ, whereas curved divergences convert the same radius into a 

μ(a | s)-dependent ratio penalty that can be tighter for rare actions. This could favor TV when use-ful mathematical updates require reallocating mass among a few low-probability reasoning branches, while coding’s larger set of valid implementations may attenuate the difference. 

Estimator topology determines how this geometry is enforced under sampling. Across four divergences, both proposed probability-aware estimators improve substantially over MC, while Atomic ranks first in every column of the code table and achieves the best math average. Aver-9aged over the four divergences, Binary improves over MC by 21 .3% on math and 3.2% on code, whereas Atomic yields larger gains of 28 .3% and 7.7% , respectively. As Figure 2 shows, the en-tropy of MC collapse rapidly toward zero with the score curves plateau early at comparatively low levels. In contrast, Atomic and Binary maintain moderate range of entropy and continue improv-ing over a longer horizon. This phenomenon follows directly from the estimator: Atomic avoids clipping large but low-probability exploratory updates by weighting with reference probability, and Binary instead accounts for the complementary probability mass. 

# 5 CONCLUSION 

This paper introduces GPPO, a principled framework that unifies proximal policy optimization through a two-dimensional design space of divergence geometry and estimator topology, supported by a generalized f -divergence trust-region theorem. Across eight mathematical reasoning and code generation benchmarks, GPPO substantially outperforms standard PPO, demonstrating that both dimensions are critical for effective LLM reinforcement learning.
