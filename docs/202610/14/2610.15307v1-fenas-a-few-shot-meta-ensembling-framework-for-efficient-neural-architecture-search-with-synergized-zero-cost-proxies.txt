# FENAS: A Few-Shot Meta-Ensembling Framework for Efficient Neural Architecture Search with Synergized Zero-Cost Proxies 

Yifeng Mao 1, Yuze Feng 1, Yutong Shen 1, Liyang Gao 1, and Zhixiang He 1*

Beijing University of Technology, Beijing, China 

maoyifeng@emails.bjut.edu.cn, zxhe@bjut.edu.cn *Corresponding author 

Abstract. Efficient neural architecture search (NAS) requires accurate performance estimation under limited evaluation budgets. Existing zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) provide fast training-free signals, but each proxy usu-ally captures only a partial property of an architecture, leading to unsta-ble rankings across datasets and search spaces. In contrast, GNN-based predictors can improve accuracy, but often depend on labeled architec-tures, graph encoders, and costly representation learning, which makes them less suitable for extreme few-shot settings. We propose FENAS, a topology-agnostic few-shot prediction framework that turns complemen-tary initialization priors into robust ranking signals. Its core component, CAST, is a feature pipeline that combines copula alignment and syn-ergy transformation. CAST maps heterogeneous ZCPs into a compact feature manifold, enabling lightweight meta-learners to produce reliable rankings from scarce supervision. On NAS-Bench-201, with only 0.5% la-beled samples, FENAS achieves a Kendall’s Tau ( τ ) of 0.70, outperform-ing representative topology-based few-shot predictors in this low-label regime. In closed-domain search, FENAS evaluates the NAS-Bench-201 space in approximately 1138 seconds and achieves competitive test ac-curacy. By providing a fast, reliable, and structurally simple evaluation mechanism, FENAS supports the design of automated neural architec-tures under limited evaluation budgets. 

Keywords: Neural Architecture Search · Zero-Cost Proxies · Few-Shot Prediction · AutoML · Efficient Deep Learning

Zero-Cost Proxies (ZCPs) offer a promising solution by estimating architec-ture quality at initialization from a single forward or backward pass. They can quickly screen weak candidates before expensive training and thus greatly re-duce search cost. However, individual proxies are often unstable because each one captures only a limited physical property of an architecture, such as ex-pressivity, trainability, or connectivity. As a result, their rank correlations vary significantly across datasets, tasks, and search spaces [23,31], making raw ZCPs difficult to use as universal and reliable performance indicators. Few-shot predictors based on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or other topol-ogy encoders [24,32,4] provide another way to reduce evaluation cost. By ex-plicitly modeling architecture graphs and learning topology-aware representa-tions, these methods can improve prediction quality under limited supervision. Nevertheless, they introduce additional overhead from graph construction, mes-sage passing, and structured inference. More importantly, in extreme few-shot regimes, e.g., less than 1% labeled samples, learning high-dimensional topology representations from scratch can be sample-inefficient and prone to overfitting. Our experiments show that on topology-stable benchmarks such as NAS-Bench-201, a lightweight topology-agnostic predictor can already outperform heavy topology-based methods; with only 78 labeled architectures, FENAS achieves a τ of 0.695 on CIFAR-10, surpassing DCL-ENAS [36] (0.610) and CAP [11] (0.600). To address the tension between the instability of individual ZCPs and the complexity of topology-based predictors, we propose FENAS (Few-shot meta-Ensembling Neural Architecture Search), an efficient and topology-agnostic per-formance prediction framework. Our key insight is that, instead of spending model capacity on explicit topology learning, it is more effective to directly model the intrinsic priors of architectures and learn how to combine them with minimal supervision. Specifically, we extract features from four complementary physical dimensions: expressivity, trainability, connectivity, and parameter ca-pacity. To transform these heterogeneous signals into a compact and learnable FENAS 3

feature manifold, we design the CAST (Copula Alignment and Synergy Transfor-mation) operator. Based on this representation, a lightweight meta-learner only needs a very small number of labeled architectures to capture the task-dependent synergy among these priors. Extensive experiments show that FENAS achieves strong ranking quality with very limited labeled architectures while avoiding ex-plicit topology representation learning. In end-to-end search, FENAS remains competitive under constrained budgets, completing the closed-domain search on NAS-Bench-201 in only ∼1138 seconds and remaining in the most efficient tier on DARTS with 0.3 GPU days. The main contributions of this paper are as follows: 

– We propose a lightweight topology-agnostic NAS prediction frame-work with strong efficiency advantages. FENAS bypasses expensive graph construction and message passing, and instead performs ranking with multi-dimensional priors extracted at initialization. Under extremely lim-ited supervision, it demonstrates strong sample efficiency and competitive search performance, while keeping the overall pipeline structurally simple and computationally lightweight. 

– We design CAST to synergize heterogeneous physical priors. FE-NAS systematically integrates four complementary dimensions of architec-ture dynamics: expressivity, trainability, connectivity, and capacity. Through distribution alignment and synergy transformation, CAST mitigates scale inconsistency and improves the usability of raw ZCP signals for few-shot prediction. 

– We show that minimal supervision can turn unstable zero-cost signals into robust predictors. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FENAS consistently improves over individual ZCPs and achieves particu-larly strong results in extreme few-shot settings. The interpretability analy-sis further shows that FENAS adaptively adjusts the importance of different physical dimensions across tasks, providing a direct explanation for its im-proved robustness and cross-dataset generalization. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Neural Architecture Search (NAS) 

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) aims to automate the design of high-performing neural networks, but its practical use is often limited by the high cost of evalu-ating candidate architectures in large search spaces. Early NAS methods based on reinforcement learning and evolutionary algorithms [37,26] typically require training or partially training many architectures, leading to substantial com-putational overhead. To improve efficiency, differentiable and low-cost search strategies [15] have been proposed to reduce the reliance on exhaustive evalua-tion. In parallel, benchmark suites such as NAS-Bench-201 [7] provide standard-ized search spaces with ground-truth performance records, enabling controlled analysis of efficient NAS algorithms. 4 Y. Mao et al. 

2.2 Zero-Cost Proxies (ZCPs) 

Zero-Cost Proxies (ZCPs) estimate architecture quality at initialization using statistics from a single forward or backward pass [1]. Existing proxies characterize architectures from different physical perspectives. Gradient and pruning-based methods, such as SynFlow [27], measure parameter sensitivity or information flow, while activation-based methods such as NASWOT [21] capture expressivity through feature-map statistics or activation patterns. Although these proxies provide useful training-free priors, each individual proxy usually reflects only one local property of an architecture, making its ranking reliability highly dependent on the dataset and search space. 

2.3 Few-Shot Prediction and Proxy Fusion 

Few-shot NAS predictors aim to reduce the number of labeled architectures re-quired for accurate performance estimation. Recent methods usually rely on graph topology encoding, self-supervised learning, or supernet-based mecha-nisms [33,29,17,16]. While topology-aware predictors can improve prediction quality, they introduce additional overhead from graph construction, message passing, and structured inference. Under extremely limited labels, learning high-dimensional architecture embeddings from scratch may also suffer from cold-start difficulty and overfitting. Another related direction studies the fusion of multiple ZCPs, for example through adaptive weighting [14]. However, purely zero-cost fusion often relies on manually designed aggregation rules or search heuristics, and thus cannot di-rectly learn task-dependent relationships between proxy signals and final accu-racy. FENAS bridges few-shot prediction and ZCP fusion by representing each architecture with complementary topology-agnostic physical priors and using minimal ground-truth supervision for data-driven integration. With CAST, het-erogeneous proxy scores are aligned and transformed into a compact feature space, allowing lightweight meta-learners to adaptively combine expressivity, trainability, connectivity, and capacity cues for robust architecture ranking. 

## 3 Methodology 

We now detail the main components of FENAS. The overall idea is to con-vert each architecture into a compact feature manifold using multi-dimensional zero-cost proxy signals, and then train a lightweight meta-predictor for few-shot performance estimation.

## 4 Experiments 

We evaluate FENAS from three perspectives: few-shot ranking quality, end-to-end architecture search, and the contribution of individual design components. The experiments cover NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, and the DARTS search space, and are designed to verify whether the proposed CAST-based proxy fusion is more robust than individual ZCPs and more efficient than topology-heavy predictors. 

4.1 Experimental Settings 

Benchmarks and protocols. NAS-Bench-101 [34] contains 423,624 CIFAR-10 [13] architectures with diverse graph topologies, while NAS-Bench-201 [7] contains 15,625 operation-on-edge architectures with complete records of CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 [13], and ImageNet16-120 [5]. We also evaluate practical search in the large-scale DARTS space [15], an open-domain search space that contains approxi-mately 10 18 candidate architectures. FENAS uses a lightweight Random Forest meta-learner over four CAST-processed ZCPs: Fisher, NASWOT, SynFlow, and #Params. We compare it with SOTA few-shot predictors such as CAP [11] and PINAT [16], as well as pure ZCP baselines. Ranking is measured by Kendall’s τ

and Spearman’s ρ; search quality is measured by top-1 test accuracy and search cost. To ensure statistical robustness, all results are averaged over 10 indepen-dent runs with fixed random seeds, and we report both mean and standard deviation.

2) Search and Retraining on the DARTS Open Domain. 

We further evaluate FENAS in the large-scale DARTS search space [15], which contains approximately 10 18 candidate architectures. Since unconstrained prior-based search may favor degenerate architectures with excessive “none” opera-tors [35,6], we apply a lightweight structural validity constraint during evolution-ary search [26]. In practice, FENAS is fine-tuned using 100 randomly sampled architectures trained for 50 epochs, then used to rank 100,000 valid candidates. The top-3 predicted architectures are retrained from scratch for 600 epochs. As shown in Table 4, FENAS remains highly efficient, requiring only 0.3 GPU days while achieving 97.25% ± 0.09% test accuracy after retraining. Although FENAS does not outperform PINAT comparing the accuracies, its topology-agnostic design avoids costly graph processing and additional pretraining, en-abling direct ranking through lightweight meta-ensembling of initialization-based priors. These results show that FENAS is a practical open-domain NAS method under strict computational budgets.

2) CAST Module. 

The CAST module aligns heterogeneous ZCP distributions through ECDF and enhances cross-feature interactions through synergy transformation. We evaluate CAST on NAS-Bench-201/CIFAR-10 using 1% labeled architectures for meta-training and the full benchmark for testing. Four strategies are compared: raw ZCP features, ECDF-only alignment, Synergy-only, and the complete CAST pipeline. To verify both effectiveness and model-agnosticity, we test CAST with linear regression (LR) and random forest (RF). As shown in Table 6, LR benefits substantially from ECDF alignment, improving τ from 0.491 to 0.545, while the full CAST pipeline further increases it to 0.560. For RF, the gain is more modest. The synergy-only variant obtains a slightly higher τ of 0.733, whereas full CAST achieves the best ρ of 0.904. These results suggest that CAST is most beneficial for simpler learners and provides a smaller but consistent refinement for the default nonlinear predictor. 

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we presented FENAS, a topology-agnostic few-shot NAS predictor that integrates multi-dimensional zero-cost proxies with the CAST feature trans-formation pipeline. By aligning heterogeneous proxy distributions and modeling their complementary interactions, FENAS achieves accurate and stable architec-ture ranking under extremely limited supervision. Experiments on NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, and the DARTS search space show that FENAS provides competitive ranking and search performance with low computational cost. These results suggest that physically grounded proxy fusion is an efficient alternative to topology-heavy predictors, especially when only a small number of evaluated architectures are available. 14 Y.
