# Dynamic Programming for A Linear-Gaussian Dual Control Problem ∗

## Ying Wang, Yuchao Li, and H˚ akan Hjalmarsson October 14, 2026 

Abstract 

We consider an infinite-horizon discounted dynamic programming (DP) problem for a countable-innovation belief-state model. The model is a sur-rogate for a linear-Gaussian dual control problem involving an unknown parameter vector and Gaussian observation noise. The surrogate preserves the first two moments of the Gaussian innovation and the resulting pos-terior second-moment structure. We allow unbounded belief and control spaces and nonnegative stage costs with quadratic growth in the posterior mean and covariance. Using a posterior-moment weight, we show that the Bellman operator is a contraction in a weighted sup-norm space. We char-acterize the optimal cost as the unique solution of the Bellman equation in this space, establish the existence of optimal stationary policies. We further prove weighted sup-norm convergence of value iteration, policy iteration, and optimistic policy iteration under vanishing approximation errors. The convergence results under vanishing approximation errors rely on general weighted sup-norm contraction properties and are applicable beyond the particular dual control problem considered here.

Our objective is to develop a tractable discounted infinite-horizon dynamic programming (DP) theory motivated by this linear-Gaussian dual control problem, while accommodating unbounded belief and input spaces and stage costs with quadratic growth. This problem has a long history in the control and economics literature. In particular, Prescott [Pre72] studied this regression problem and emphasized the tradeoff between current performance and experimentation. Kiefer and Nyarko subsequently developed an exact discounted infinite-horizon DP theory [EK88; KN89]. Under the assumptions of a compact input space and bounded contin-uous costs, they established continuity of the optimal cost function, the Bell-man optimality equation, and the existence of stationary optimal policies by a contraction argument on the space of bounded continuous functions. How-ever, their theory does not cover the unbounded input spaces and quadrati-cally growing costs considered here. Subsequent work has focused primarily on computational or approximate approaches, including numerical value itera-tion [Kie89; Wie00a; Wie00b], rollout [HLS06], convex-optimization-based ap-proximations [LB99; Wan+24], and receding-horizon approximations [Wan+25]. Related linear-Gaussian observation models have also been studied in the Bayesian linear-bandit literature, with an emphasis on regret analysis; see, e.g., [ZDV26]. The first difficulty in extending the infinite-horizon theory in [EK88; KN89] to the present problem is that the relevant cost functions are generally un-bounded. As a result, the usual contraction analysis on bounded functions is not directly applicable. A natural alternative is weighted sup-norm meth-ods [Lip75; Wes77; NW78; Ber12b; Ber22; MST22; Tod24]. In particular, the analyses in [MST22; Tod24] rely on an inequality of the form 

E{v(xk+1 ) | xk = x, u k = u} ≤ cv (x),

2for some constant c > 0, where v(x) is a weight function used to define the weighted sup-norm space. For the quadratic-growth costs considered here, we introduce the posterior moment weight 

v(x) = 1 + ∥m∥2 + tr( P ) (2) for the state x = ( m, P ), where ∥·∥ denotes the Euclidean norm and tr( ·) denotes the trace function. In contrast to the inequality used in [MST22; Tod24], a key observation of this paper is that this particular weight satisfies an exact preservation identity under the belief-state transition. Specifically, we prove that 

E {v(xk+1 ) | xk = x, u k = u} = v(x) (3) for every state-input pair ( x, u ), as will be shown in Section 3. This exact iden-tity is the main structural property underlying the contraction and algorithmic analysis developed in this paper. A second and logically separate difficulty concerns measurability. For the original linear-Gaussian problem, the posterior-mean update is driven by a con-tinuous Gaussian innovation. With continuous innovations and an uncountable input space, one must ensure that the conditional expectations and minimiza-tions appearing in the DP analysis and algorithms preserve the appropriate measurability properties. In particular, even when a function is Borel measur-able in the state and input, its infimum over the control need not be Borel measurable as a function of the state; see [BS78; SB78]. These issues can be treated within the general theory of stochastic control on Borel spaces, including the universal-measurability framework of Bertsekas and Shreve [BS78]. Under additional continuity and compactness-type condi-tions, Feinberg et al. [FKZ12; FKZ16] develop results for Markov decision pro-cesses and partially observed Markov decision processes (POMDPs) with Borel state and control spaces, allowing noncompact control sets and unbounded stage costs. Their theory establishes the Bellman equation, lower semicontinuity of the optimal cost, existence of stationary optimal policies, and pointwise con-vergence of value iteration (VI for short). More recent work further develops verifiable continuity conditions for broad classes of partially observed models and nonlinear filters, including Kalman-type filters [Fei+24; Fei+25]. Thus, the continuous-innovation model may in principle be treated within this more general Borel-space theory. Our objective here is different. We seek fixed-point and algorithmic conclu-3sions that exploit the particular posterior-moment structure of the regression model. The general Borel-space results described above do not directly provide a contraction of the Bellman operator in a function space accommodating the quadratic growth considered here, or uniqueness of the Bellman fixed point in such a weighted space. Nor do they directly establish weighted sup-norm con-vergence guarantees for the DP algorithms considered below under vanishing approximation errors. To isolate this contractive structure without introducing the additional mea-surable function and measurable selection machinery required in the general Borel-space theory, we replace the continuous Gaussian innovation by a countable-support innovation with the same first two moments, namely zero mean and unit variance. The resulting model is a surrogate for the original linear-Gaussian problem rather than an equivalent representation. This choice preserves the posterior second-moment identity underlying the weighted-space analysis, while reducing the conditional expectations in the Bellman operators to countable sums. It also has a natural computational interpretation, since numerical treat-ment of the original Gaussian expectation generally requires some form of dis-crete approximation. Quantifying the relation between the optimal costs and policies of the countable- and continuous-innovation models is left for future research. A further issue arises for policy-based algorithms. Although the Bellman operator is contractive on the weighted space, a policy-specific Bellman operator need not map this space into itself for an arbitrary policy. We therefore identify a class of policies, which we call proper policies, for which the policy-specific operators are well posed and contractive, and show that policy iteration (PI) and optimistic PI generate policies that remain within this class. This makes it possible to establish convergence even when the policy-improvement steps are performed with vanishing approximation errors. Within this countable-innovation model, we develop a discounted infinite-horizon DP theory for unbounded belief and input spaces and nonnegative stage costs with quadratic growth. Specifically, our contributions are as follows. 

• We introduce the posterior-moment weight in Eq. (2) and prove the weight-preservation identity (3). Using this identity, we establish self-map and contraction properties of the Bellman operator and of the policy-specific Bellman operators associated with proper policies on the corresponding weighted sup-norm space. 4• We characterize the optimal cost as the unique solution of the Bellman equation in the weighted space, establish its lower semicontinuity, and prove the existence of optimal stationary policies. We also show that mem-bership of the cost function of a policy in the weighted space is equivalent to properness of the policy, and that every optimal stationary policy is proper. 

• We establish weighted sup-norm convergence of VI, PI, and optimistic PI under vanishing approximation errors. For PI and optimistic PI, we fur-ther show that the generated policies remain proper; for optimistic PI, both the iterated cost functions and the cost functions of the generated policies converge to the optimal cost. These vanishing-error convergence results are not specific to the present belief-state model and extend to general weighted sup-norm contractive DP models under suitable assump-tions. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formulates the countable-innovation belief-state DP problem. Section 3 introduces the weighted sup-norm space, proves the posterior weight-preservation property, and establishes the self-map and contraction properties of the Bellman operators. Section 4 connects the Bellman fixed point with the optimal cost function and proves the existence and properness of optimal stationary policies. Section 5 studies VI, PI, and optimistic PI. Section 6 presents a numerical example, and Section 7 concludes the paper.

83 Weighted Sup-Norm Space, Bellman Opera-tors, and Proper Policy 

This section develops the weighted sup-norm framework used throughout the remainder of the paper. Because the stage cost and the resulting cost functions may be unbounded, we first introduce a weighted sup-norm space based on the posterior second moment. We then establish a weight-preservation property of the belief-state dynamics, which is used to prove self-map and contraction properties of the Bellman operators. Finally, we introduce the class of proper policies and establish the equivalence between properness and membership of the corresponding policy cost function in the space. These results provide the foundation for the optimality and algorithmic results developed in the subse-quent sections. For several of the standard results in this section, we provide the main argument and its connection to the weight-preservation property, while detailed proofs are given in Appendix D.

# 5 Value and Policy Iteration Algorithms 

In this section, we develop the convergence analysis of three standard DP algo-rithms within the weighted sup-norm space framework established above: VI, classical PI, and optimistic PI. We show that the functions computed via VI converge geometrically to the optimal cost function from any initial function 17 in B+(X). For PI and optimistic PI, we further show that the policies gener-ated by the algorithms remain proper and that the corresponding cost-function sequences converge to the optimal cost in the weighted sup-norm. Note that the specific structure of the belief-state dynamics is used in Sec-tion 3 to establish the weighted sup-norm contraction properties of the asso-ciated Bellman operators. The convergence analysis developed in this section relies primarily on these contraction properties and monotonicity, rather than the particular belief-state dynamics of the present problem. As a result, the vanishing-error convergence results for VI, PI, and optimistic PI extend to the general contractive DP models studied in [Ber22, Chapter 2], where the Bell-man operator and all policy-specific Bellman operators are contractions on a common weighted sup-norm space.

# 7 Concluding Remarks 

In this work, we developed a weighted sup-norm space DP analysis for a countable-innovation surrogate of a linear-Gaussian dual control problem with unbounded belief and control spaces and quadratically growing stage costs. The key struc-tural property is the preservation of the posterior-moment weight under the belief-state transition. This property makes it possible to work directly in a weighted sup-norm space despite the unbounded costs. In particular, it yields self-map and contraction properties of the Bellman operator and of the policy-specific Bellman operators associated with proper policies. Building on these properties, we characterized the optimal cost as the unique solution of the Bell-man equation in the weighted space and established the existence and properness of optimal stationary policies. 25 0240510 15 20 -3 -2 -1 25 0 1 230 3Figure 1: Computed optimal cost function J∗(m, P ) for the scalar example with

ν(m, P ) = m.26 We further established weighted sup-norm convergence of VI, PI, and opti-mistic PI under vanishing approximation errors. For PI and optimistic PI, we showed that the generated policies remain proper; for optimistic PI, both the iterated cost functions and the cost functions of the generated policies converge to the optimal cost. The convergence guarantees for VI, PI, and optimistic PI under vanishing approximation errors are based on general weighted sup-norm contraction arguments and thus extend beyond the specific dual control model studied here. The notion of a proper policy used here is closely connected with the regular-policy framework of the abstract DP introduced in [Ber17]. In that framework, a stationary policy μ is regular with respect to a function space S if its cost function belongs to S, satisfies the corresponding Bellman’s equation, and the iterates T kμ J converge to Jμ for every J ∈ S. In the present problem, taking 

S = B+(X), this notion coincides with our definition of properness. Indeed, if μ

is proper, then Tμ is a contraction on B+(X), so that Jμ ∈ B +(X) is its unique fixed point and T kμ J → Jμ for all J ∈ B +(X). Conversely, regularity with respect to B+(X) requires Jμ ∈ B +(X), which is equivalent to properness by Prop. 3.5. Thus, the proper-policy class Mv provides a concrete characterization of the regular policies for the weighted function space used in this paper. This connection also highlights a distinction between our theory and the broader regular-policy theory. The regular-policy framework is designed to han-dle semicontractive DP models in which some policies may exhibit pathological fixed-point or iteration behavior and the Bellman operator need not be con-tractive on the function space of interest. In our problem, the posterior weight-preservation identity yields a genuine contraction structure on B+(X). As a result, the regularity of proper policies follows directly from contraction, while the Bellman operator itself has a unique fixed point in the weighted space. More-over, under the assumptions used in this paper, every optimal stationary policy is proper. Hence, optimization over the proper-policy class does not exclude optimal stationary policies. Several directions remain open. The countable-innovation model preserves the first two moments of the Gaussian innovation and hence the posterior-moment identity that underlies the present analysis, but the relation between its optimal cost and policy and those of the original continuous-innovation prob-lem remains to be quantified. Besides, it would be useful to determine how far the weighted sup-norm space and proper-policy arguments developed here can be extended to broader classes of partially observed control problems with 27 unbounded state and control spaces. Some additional DP algorithms, such as 

λ-PI discussed in [LJM20], may also worth exploring in the present context.
