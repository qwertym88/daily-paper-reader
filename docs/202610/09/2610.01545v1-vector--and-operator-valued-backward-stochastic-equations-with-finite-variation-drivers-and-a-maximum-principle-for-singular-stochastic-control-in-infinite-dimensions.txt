# Vector- and operator-valued backward stochastic equations with finite-variation drivers and a maximum principle for singular stochastic control in infinite dimensions 

## Ying Hu ∗ Guomin Liu † Shanjian Tang ‡

Abstract 

We study a mixed regular–singular control problem for stochastic evolution equations in a Hilbert space with possibly unbounded random linear operators, a nonconvex regular-control domain, and a state-dependent singular coefficient. The singular control is an adapted nondecreasing c` adl` ag process whose terminal value need not be bounded. We prove well-posedness and weighted moment estimates for the forward and backward equations, and characterize the second-order adjoint by a conditionally expected operator-valued backward stochastic integral equation. An Itˆ o-type formula for the quadratic form of this adjoint, together with spike and convex variations, yields a second-order Hamiltonian condition for the regular control, as well as nonnegativity and a contact condition for the optional singular Hamiltonian. 

Keywords. Stochastic evolution equations, singular stochastic control, nonconvex control domain, maximum principle, finite-variation drivers, operator-valued equations. 

AMS 2020 Subject Classifications. 93E20, 60H15, 49K27.

1arXiv:2610.01545v1 [math.OC] 1 Oct 2026 Singular controls act through random measures and arise, for example, in monotone-follower problems [6, 23], transaction-cost and limit-order-book models [14, 25], and singular or reflected SPDE models [32, 1]. Their finite-dimensional theory uses dynamic programming and maximum principles; see [6, 8, 23, 14, 18, 19, 20, 7, 4]. For regular stochastic control problems, Peng [34] established a general maximum principle when the con-trol domain need not be convex and the diffusion coefficient may depend on the control. A spike variation produces a first state variation of order O(ρ1/2), so the cost expansion must retain the second state varia-tion. The resulting maximum principle involves a second-order adjoint process and a quadratic Hamiltonian correction. For mixed regular–singular control with an additive term G(t) dξ (t), Bahlali and Mezerdi [4] combined regular spike variations with singular convex perturbations, allowing a nonconvex regular-control domain. The relaxed formulations of Bahlali, Djehiche, and Mezerdi [3] and Andersson [2] allow a measure-valued regular component, with uncontrolled and control-dependent diffusion, respectively. Further developments include [26, 35, 38]. State-dependent singular coefficients have been treated by different methods. Dufour and Miller [12] used a time transformation to derive a maximum principle for a nonlinear singular system with a state-independent singular direction. Their subsequent work [13] allowed the coefficient multiplying the singular control to depend on the state. Neither formulation contains an independent regular control in the drift or diffusion coefficient. Kushner [24] developed a numerical approximation method for stochastic singular control problems with state-dependent singular controls, again without a regular control. Wang, Zhao, Wang, Song, and Ma [37] studied a one-dimensional partial-information regular–singular jump-diffusion with a state-dependent singular coefficient. Their necessary maximum principle is based on first-order directional variations under the assumption that the regular-control domain is open and convex. Early maximum principles for distributed parameter systems and semilinear stochastic evolution equa-tions were established by Bensoussan [5] and Hu and Peng [21]. Subsequent work developed general maximum principles for nonconvex regular controls in infinite dimensions; see, for example, [11, 15, 31, 36, 30]. A cen-tral issue in this setting is the characterization of the operator-valued second-order adjoint process. The available formulations include stochastic bilinear forms, transposition solutions, and operator-valued back-ward equations. Liu and Tang [30] introduced a conditionally expected operator-valued backward stochastic integral equation (BSIE) that characterizes the second-order adjoint process dynamically without treating the nonseparable operator space L(H) as a Hilbert space. These works concern regular controls and do not include a singular finite-variation component. Infinite-dimensional singular control has also been studied under different structural assumptions. Øksendal, Sulem, and Zhang [32] considered SPDEs in which the singular measure is multiplied by the state-dependent coefficient λ(t, x, Y (t, x )). They derived a necessary maximum principle and a sufficient counterpart under a concavity condition, and related the adjoint equation to a reflected backward SPDE. Agram, Hilbert, and Øksendal [1] derived necessary and sufficient maximum principles for SPDEs with space-mean dynamics. Their adjoint equation is a reflected backward SPDE with space-mean dependence. Federico, Ferrari, Riedel, and R¨ ockner [16] formulated a class of linear singular control problems on partially ordered infinite-dimensional spaces and derived necessary and sufficient first-order optimality conditions by semigroup and convex-analytic methods. None of these formulations includes an independent regular control. The state equation (1.1) combines a nonconvex regular-control domain and control-dependent diffusion with a nonlinear state-dependent singular coefficient in an infinite-dimensional setting. It also allows the random linear operators A(·) and B(·) to be unbounded when regarded as operators on H. The state dependence of γ enters both the forward variations and the adjoint equations. The first- and second-order expansions of the singular term produce finite-variation terms in the corresponding variational equations; see (5.5) and (5.6). Accordingly, the first-order adjoint equation is a backward stochastic evolution equation with a finite-variation driver, whereas the second-order adjoint equation is an operator-valued BSIE with a 2finite-variation term. The first analytic issue is the well-posedness of forward and backward stochastic evolution equations driven by dt , dW , and a nondecreasing c` adl` ag process. Hu and Peng [22] studied adapted solutions of semilinear backward stochastic evolution equations. In finite dimensions, Pardoux and Zhang [33] treated generalized BSDEs driven by a continuous nondecreasing process, and Li and Tang [29] studied a recursive optimal control problem governed by such BSDEs. Here the driver ξ may have jumps. We use exponential weights with separate parameters for time and the Stieltjes driver to estimate the dt - and dξ -integrals and the jump terms. The second issue is that L(H) is generally nonseparable and not a Hilbert space. We extend the condi-tionally expected BSIE formulation of [30] to include a c` adl` ag finite-variation term. A contraction argument in a weighted space, together with aggregation of scalar conditional expectations, gives a weakly progres-sively measurable operator-valued solution. We also establish the Itˆ o-type formula for its quadratic form along a spike-generated state variation. A general nondecreasing c` adl` ag driver need not satisfy ξ(t0 + ρ) − ξ(t0) = O(ρ). The moving-spike estimates use right continuity and uniform bounds for the homogeneous flow. We combine regular spikes with convex singular perturbations. Vector-valued duality eliminates the linear state-variation terms from the cost expansion, and the operator-valued Itˆ o formula yields the quadratic diffusion correction in the regular Hamiltonian. Optional projection and stopping-time tests then yield nonnegativity and the contact condition from the singular variational inequality. Section 2 introduces the notation and operator-valued conditional expectation. Section 3 establishes estimates and solvability results for SEEs and BSEEs, and Section 4 treats operator-valued BSIEs and their Itˆ o-type formula. Section 5 formulates the mixed regular–singular stochastic control problem and derives the maximum principle. The Appendix contains the bounded-driver constructions and the deferred technical proofs.
