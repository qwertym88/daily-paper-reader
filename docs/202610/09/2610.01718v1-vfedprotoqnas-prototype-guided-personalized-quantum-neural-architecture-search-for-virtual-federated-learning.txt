## Abstract 

Quantum federated learning (QFL) has emerged as a promising approach for col-laboratively training compact quantum neural networks (QNNs) over distributed private data on resource-constrained devices. However, differences in device ca-pabilities make a single shared QNN architecture unsuitable for all clients. While personalized quantum neural architecture search (QNAS) allows each client to select a device-specific QNN, averaging parameters across structurally different QNN architectures mixes semantically inconsistent circuit operations. To address this, prototype-guided personalized QNAS for virtual FL (vFedProtoQNAS) is proposed, where model parameters are never aggregated across clients and feder-ated collaboration is achieved through class-wise prototype sharing. Each client independently searches and trains a client-specific QNN, computes class-wise local prototypes from latent representations, and refines them using global proto-types from the server as federated semantic anchors. Experiments demonstrate that vFedProtoQNAS improves accuracy by 3.70% over FedAvg and enhances class-consistent representation alignment. 

## 1 Introduction 

Federated learning (FL) is a distributed learning paradigm that enables multiple clients to collab-oratively train a model while preserving local data privacy [Kwon et al., 2020, Kang and Park, 2025]. This makes FL suitable for privacy-sensitive and communication-efficient applications, such as mobile devices, edge devices, and internet of things (IoT) networks. Most FL methods assume that clients share the same model architecture, allowing the server to construct a global model by averaging local parameters [Son and Park, 2025]. However, this assumption is often unrealistic in practical FL environments, where clients differ in computational resources, memory capacity, and energy budget Kang et al. [2025], Zhang et al. [2024b], Khare et al. [2024], Horváth et al. [2021], Mei et al. [2022]. Such device heterogeneity makes it difficult to deploy a single unified architecture across all clients Park and Ko [2024], Alam et al. [2022], Yi et al. [2024a]. A high-capacity architecture may exceed the resource budget of low-resource clients, whereas an overly lightweight architecture may

Therefore, heterogeneous FL requires compact, expressive, and adaptable model architectures that reflect client-specific capabilities, motivating resource-aware architecture design for distributed learning across diverse devices. Quantum neural networks (QNNs) are a potential compact model structure for distributed learn-ing [Son and Park, 2025]. QNNs efficiently transform classical inputs into quantum feature spaces. Integrating QNNs with FL yields quantum FL (QFL), a promising approach for resource-constrained distributed setups [Chehimi et al., 2024]. However, deploying identical QNN architectures across clients is often unsuitable. Device-specific factors, such as circuit depth and gate type, affect per-formance and efficiency [Son et al., 2025, He et al., 2024]. High-performance devices benefit from complex QNNs, while limited devices require simpler ones, necessitating client-specific architecture design. Manual design is challenged by interacting factors. Neural architecture search (NAS), and specifically quantum neural architecture search (QNAS), automates this process, enabling client-specific QNNs [Son et al., 2025]. Applying personalized QNAS to QFL addresses the architecture mismatch and allows each client to select an optimal QNN based on device constraints and local data [Yao and Li, 2024]. However, personalized QNAS complicates aggregation: FedAvg assumes uniform models and aligns client parameters, enabling meaningful averaging [Yi et al., 2024b]. Personalized QNAS breaks this alignment, so parameter indices across clients may represent different operations. Averaging without structural alignment mixes inconsistent information, undermining the specialization gained from personalized QNAS. To address this issue, this paper proposes vFedProtoQNAS: a prototype-guided, personalized QNAS framework for virtual FL. Unlike conventional FL, virtual here means model parameters are not aggregated, as shown in Fig. 1. vFedProtoQNAS shares class-wise prototypes for representation-level federated collaboration. Clients retain client-specific QNN architectures and model parameters locally, and collaborate with the server via prototype-based sharing. As shown in Fig. 2, each client performs personalized QNAS tailored to its device constraints and local data, selects a QNN architecture, and trains its parameters. Each trained QNN maps samples to latent features, from which class-wise local prototypes are computed. These prototypes summarize class-level representations and are shared with the server Tan et al. [2022], Zhang et al. [2024a]. The server aggregates them using sample weights to generate a global prototype for each class, serving as a federated semantic anchor that integrates class-level representations across clients. The server sends global prototypes back. Clients refine their representations, encouraging alignment with the global prototype for the target class and separation from other classes. Client architectures and parameters are not averaged, preserving local specialization from personalized QNAS. Therefore, vFedProtoQNAS enables client-specific architecture search and federated collaboration in heterogeneous QFL, sharing knowledge at the representation level across different architectures. The main contributions of this paper are as follows. (i) This paper proposes vFedProtoQNAS, a personalized QNAS framework that accounts for client-specific device constraints and local data characteristics in heterogeneous QFL. (ii) To mitigate structural mismatches between different QNN architectures, vFedProtoQNAS introduces a virtual federated collaboration method based on class-wise prototype sharing instead of parameter averaging. (iii) Through global prototype-based refinement, vFedProtoQNAS induces class-consistent representation alignment and achieves improved classification performance over major baselines. 2Figure 2: The overall framework of vFedProtoQNAS. 

## 2 Preliminary 

QNN. A QNN consists of an encoder, a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC), and a measurement stage. The encoder maps a classical input x into a quantum representation using data-dependent rotation gates U (x) [Roh et al., 2025]. The PQC applies trainable operations parameterized by θ,where 1-qubit gates perform local rotations and 2-qubit gates generate entanglement. The measure-ment stage produces observables, such as Pauli-Z expectations ⟨O⟩x,θ ∈ [−1, 1] |y|, which are used for classical readout. The gradient ∇θ L(θ) is evaluated using the parameter-shift rule Mitarai et al. [2018], and the circuit parameters are updated as θ ← θ − η∇θ L(θ).

QNAS. QNN performance strongly depends on PQC design, including gate type, gate placement, circuit depth, and entanglement topology. More expressive circuits can improve representation capacity but also increase cost, noise sensitivity, and trainability issues. QNAS automates this design by searching over candidate circuit architectures Wu et al. [2023], Amer et al. [2024], Lu et al. [2023]. To handle the rapidly growing search space over qubits, layers, and operations, one-shot NAS trains a shared supernet and evaluates candidates through weight sharing. 

QFL. QFL integrates FL with QNNs, enabling clients to collaboratively train QNNs without sharing local data Yamany et al. [2023], Chehimi et al. [2024], Chen and Yoo [2021], Chehimi and Saad [2022]. ## 3 vFedProtoQNAS Framework 

3.1 Problem Formulation 

This paper considers a heterogeneous QFL environment with K clients. Each client i ∈ { 1, . . . , K }

owns a private local dataset Di, which is not shared with other clients or the server. The overall data distribution is non-IID across clients. Clients may have different computational resources, memory capacities, supported circuit depths, and executable quantum operations. Therefore, the feasible QNN architecture space can vary across clients. The architecture search space of client i is denoted by Ai. Although the internal QNN architectures may differ, vFedProtoQNAS constrains all clients to use a common latent interface defined by the same number and ordering of measured observables for prototype computation. QNAS changes the internal PQC structure while preserving this common output interface, allowing class-wise prototypes to be represented and aggregated in the same measurement-defined latent space. The objective of vFedProtoQNAS is to identify a personalized QNN architecture that reflects both the local data distribution and the device constraints of each client. FedAvg assumes that all clients share the same model architecture and that their parameters belong to an aligned parameter space. This assumption does not hold when clients select different QNN architectures. The same parameter index may correspond to different quantum operations, circuit depths, or representation paths. Direct parameter averaging can therefore mix structurally misaligned information. To address this problem, vFedProtoQNAS replaces parameter-level aggregation with representation-level collaboration based on class-wise prototypes. 

3.2 Architecture 

As shown in Fig. 2, vFedProtoQNAS proceeds sequentially through personalized architecture search, local training, prototype aggregation, and prototype-guided refinement. Each client first performs personalized QNAS within Ai, fixes the selected architecture αi, and trains its parameters θi using local data. The trained QNN then computes class-wise local prototypes, which are sent with the corresponding class sample counts to the server while raw data and model parameters remain local. The server aggregates them into global prototypes and returns them to the clients. Each client finally refines θi using the global prototypes while keeping αi fixed, thereby preserving personalized architectures and local specialization.

## 4 Performance Evaluation 

4.1 Simulation Setup 

The experiment was conducted on the MNIST (0 –3) four-class classification task. Non-IID data were distributed across five clients using a Dirichlet distribution. Each QNN used four latent tokens and up to four layers. The operation set and circuit depth were configured based on client-specific device constraints. All experiments were repeated over five random seeds, and the results are reported as mean ± standard deviation. Each client was locally trained for 100 epochs, followed by one prototype aggregation step and a 100-epoch prototype-guided refinement phase. Prototype aggregation and refinement were performed once rather than over repeated federated communication rounds. Contrastive prototype loss was applied with βproto = 0 .10 . Adam was used as the optimizer with a batch size of 64.

Overall, vFedProtoQNAS operates stably under typical non-IID settings. However, local prototype reliability becomes a key limiting factor under extreme heterogeneity.

## 5 Conclusion 

This paper presented vFedProtoQNAS, a prototype-guided personalized QNAS framework for het-erogeneous QFL under client-specific data distributions and device constraints. Unlike conventional QFL approaches that require clients to employ an identical QNN architecture, vFedProtoQNAS allows each client to independently search for and train a personalized architecture. To support collaboration among structurally different QNNs, the proposed framework replaces parameter aggre-gation with class-wise prototype sharing. The aggregated global prototypes serve as semantic anchors for refining client-specific representations while preserving personalized architectures and local specialization. Experimental results demonstrated that vFedProtoQNAS outperforms conventional parameter-averaging, local-only training, and personalized QNAS baselines. The ablation results further confirmed that personalized architecture search and prototype-guided collaboration provide complementary benefits. Personalized QNAS enables architectural adaptation to local data and device capabilities, while prototype sharing transfers class-level semantic knowledge across heterogeneous client models. In addition, the prototype margin analysis showed that global prototype guidance improves class-consistent representation alignment by encouraging local representations to become closer to the correct class prototype and more distinguishable from incorrect ones. Overall, vFed-7ProtoQNAS provides an effective representation-level collaboration strategy for heterogeneous QFL without requiring structurally aligned client models. This demonstrates prototype-based knowledge sharing can reconcile client-specific architectural personalization with federated collaboration.
