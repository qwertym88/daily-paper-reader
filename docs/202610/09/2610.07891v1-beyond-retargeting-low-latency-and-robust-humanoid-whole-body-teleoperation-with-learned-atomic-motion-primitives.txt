# Beyond Retargeting: Low-Latency and Robust Humanoid Whole-Body Teleoperation with Learned Atomic Motion Primitives 

Xiayan Xu 1,†, Jiyu Yu 2,†, Xingzhou Chen 1, Siyi Qian 3, Zongyu Ma 3, Lilu Liu 4, Ling Shi 1, and Haodong Zhang 3,∗

Abstract — Humanoid whole-body teleoperation translates hu-man motion into stable robot behavior in real time. Existing systems typically rely on online motion retargeting to bridge human–robot morphological differences, but this process adds latency and can produce physically infeasible targets. Meanwhile, diverse, noisy, and partial human-motion observations often fall outside the training distribution, potentially causing unstable robot behavior. We propose a retargeting-free policy that maps raw human motion directly to robot joint commands in asingle forward pass, eliminating online kinematic adaptation. To improve robustness, we learn a codebook of full-body motion primitives that projects out-of-distribution observations onto plausible motion prototypes and recovers full-body motion from partial inputs. Experiments on a Unitree G1 in simulation and on hardware, using virtual reality, optical mocap, text-to-motion generation, and monocular video inputs, show that our method outperforms baselines in latency and robustness. 

I. INTRODUCTION Humanoid robots provide a general-purpose embodiment for environments designed around humans. Whole-body tele-operation maps an operator’s full-body motion to a humanoid in real time through virtual reality (VR), motion capture, or other interfaces, enabling intuitive whole-body control and demonstration collection for downstream learning. However, direct human-to-humanoid control is challenging because the two bodies differ in link proportions, joint topology, and actu-ation limits, while teleoperation requires low latency, accurate tracking, and stable behavior. Most existing systems use online retargeting: human mo-tion observations are first converted into robot-joint reference trajectories, which are then executed by a tracking policy [1, 2, 3, 4]. Although this decomposition decouples morphology adaptation from control, retargeting lies on the command path and typically requires per-step kinematic optimization or iterative inverse kinematics (IK). Its delay compounds sensing, communication, and actuation latency, reducing responsive-ness in dynamic teleoperation. Retargeting-free alternatives [5, 6] often use only head and hand signals, but provide limited lower-body control. Robustness is an equally important challenge. Such inputs are therefore frequently out of distribution (OOD) in teleoperation, and may drive a tracking policy toward unstable or dynamically infeasible behavior [7]. This challenge is amplified in retargeting-free whole-body control. Without online retargeting, a single policy must bridge morphology and sensing gaps, maintain balance-aware dy-namics, and infer plausible full-body motion from unreliable observations. We address this problem with a learned full-body motion prior. Instead of predicting actions directly from each raw observation, our policy maps the input to a compact set of learned motion prototypes and predicts actions from the recovered descriptor. This discrete prior pulls noisy or partial observations toward the training support and selects a plausi-ble full-body completion rather than averaging incompatible motions. Our framework uses two training stages. First, a privileged Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) [8] teacher is trained with prox-imal policy optimization (PPO) [9] to track a large library arXiv:2610.07891v1 [cs.RO] 6 Oct 2026 of retargeted mocap clips. Second, the teacher is distilled into a codebook student whose input is only a raw human-motion descriptor and proprioceptive history. At deployment, the student outputs robot joint commands in one forward pass, without online retargeting or kinematic adaptation— to our knowledge the first whole-body teleoperation policy that directly maps raw human motion to robot commands without online retargeting or future-reference conditioning. The factorized categorical codebook is learned from clean motion references and allows the student to recover feasible motion prototypes from noisy, partial, or OOD observations. A. Humanoid teleoperation and online retargeting 

Early systems mainly used inverse kinematics, task-space retargeting, model predictive control, and whole-body con-trol to convert human motion into feasible robot commands online [10, 11, 12, 13]. Recent learning-based methods have expanded teleoperation input interfaces—from RGB and VR to human shadowing, scalable data collection, and isomorphic cockpits—and have made real-time whole-body teleoperation practical [14, 5, 1, 3, 15]. Despite this progress, most still rely on an online retargeting or target-generation stage before control, which improves feasibility but adds latency and may fail when targets are delayed, discontinuous, or dynamically infeasible. 

B. Direct human-to-robot control 

A more direct alternative maps human observations to robot actions without an explicit retargeting module [5, 6]. For instance, ExtremControl [16] constructs robot-space link targets through an online, morphology-dependent Cartesian mapping based on human–robot body scales, shoulder loca-tions, limb lengths, and fixed foot offsets. However, such hand-designed or directly regressed mappings remain vulnerable to the distribution shift between clean training references and real deployment observations [7]: because robot actions must satisfy balance, contact, and morphology constraints, they can behave unreliably once inputs depart from their training or calibration support. In contrast, our policy learns the human– robot morphology mapping jointly with control directly from human-frame full-body observations, retaining the low-latency benefit of direct control while using a learned primitive prior to constrain action prediction toward plausible whole-body behavior. 

C. Whole-body humanoid motion tracking 

Adversarial motion priors, latent skill spaces, and large-scale tracking policies have enabled diverse whole-body mo-tion tracking for simulated characters and humanoids [17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25]. General systems such as GMT further improve reference tracking through adaptive sampling and MoE policies [26]. These methods typically require dense robot-frame references at runtime, often supplied by an external retargeter in teleoperation. In contrast, our method uses robot references only during teacher policy training; the deployed student directly maps human observations to robot commands. 

D. Motion priors and discrete primitives 

Motion priors are widely used to regularize high-dimensional motion generation and control. Continuous la-tent models, including conditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), learn compact spaces for physically valid charac-ter motions [27, 28], while phase-based and local-motion representations organize motion to improve prediction and control [29, 30]. For teleoperation, continuous regression can transmit corrupted observations or average multiple plausi-ble full-body completions into infeasible motion. Discrete representation learning, including vector-quantized autoen-coders [31], instead maps inputs to a finite learned code set and favors motion patterns supported by training data. We use this property within the deployed policy: the codebook is supervised by clean full-body motion and acts as a denoising and completion prior before robot-action prediction. III. METHOD

Teacher supervision. The student is distilled from a frozen privileged teacher πT trained with PPO on retargeted mocap references. The teacher observes proprioception, a robot-frame reference with short look-ahead, and privileged root linear velocity and foot-contact signals that are unavailable at de-ployment. To model the diverse action distribution induced by the motion library, the teacher uses a sparse MoE actor [24, 5]. A learned router selects the top-k fine-grained experts and combines their outputs with a shared expert to predict the action mean. IV. EXPERIMENTS We evaluate the codebook student in terms of: (Q1) tracking accuracy and end-to-end latency against baseline trackers; (Q2) robustness to OOD human-motion inputs; and (Q3) transfer to a real Unitree G1 driven by diverse motion sources. We additionally ablate the teacher, codebook capacity, and input keypoint count. 

A. Experimental Setup 

Simulators and data. We train policies on a Uni-tree G1 in NVIDIA IsaacSim [38] using a retargeted AMASS/LAFAN1/OMOMO library with 14 ,147 training and 

4,073 testing clips. Cross-simulator evaluation is conducted in MjLab on 6,495 SONIC bones-seed trajectories, which are more OOD than the training library. 

Metrics. A rollout succeeds if it finishes the reference clip without a pelvis-height error exceeding 0.25 m. We report success rate (SR) and seven tracking metrics. Local fidelity is measured by mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) [rad], mean per-joint velocity error (MPJVE) [rad/s], and maximum key-body position error (MPKPE) [m]. Global fidelity is measured by root-position error ( Eroot pos ) [m], root linear-velocity error ( Eroot lin vel ) [m/s], root yaw error ( Eroot yaw )[rad], and root yaw-rate error ( Eroot yaw vel ) [rad/s]. End-to-end (E2E) command latency is reported in ms. 

Baselines. We compare against TWIST [2], TWIST2 [3], GMT [26], CLONE [6], and ANY2TRACK [37], as well as a matched MLP student trained without codebook matching.

Five OOD conditions. We evaluate five structured deployment shifts, each parameterized by a scale s: (i) observation dropout, which independently zeros channels with probability s; (ii) keypoint noise, N (0 , s 2I) applied to each 3D keypoint; (iii) root-height drift, an AR(1) perturbation combining constant bias, low-frequency drift, and high-frequency jitter in the key-point z-coordinate; (iv) initial bias, fixed offsets of magnitude 

s in root xy, root yaw, and joint angles; and (v) unseen Motion-X ballet and martial-arts sequences outside the training library. All perturbations are injected online at test time, without fine-tuning. 

Overall results. Table III reports SR at representative pertur-bation scales, with Clean ( s=0 ) as the reference upper bound. The codebook student performs best under all four noise-driven conditions and unseen motions, while remaining within one point of the best initialization-bias result. Under 0.30 m keypoint noise, it retains 0.9487 SR, compared with 0.8675 for the matched MLP and at most 0.6344 for external baselines. Under root-height drift, it drops only 0.10 from Clean, whereas CLONE, TWIST2, and GMT drop by at least 0.51 .

C. Real-World Deployment 

We deploy one checkpoint on a real Unitree G1 across four human-motion input sources: a VR headset, an optical motion-capture system, monocular video, and text-to-motion generation. Only the upstream module producing ht changes across settings; no fine-tuning is used. 

Teleoperation with sparse and dense observations. We evaluate real-time teleoperation with sparse VR headset ob-servations and dense full-body optical mocap. Our causal policy uses only the current descriptor and proprioceptive history, enabling low-latency control without retargeting or look-ahead. With VR observations, the operator drives the robot to approach an object, grasp it bimanually, turn, and squat to place it (Figure 5); with optical mocap, it tracks arm raises, punches, bending, squatting, walking, and lateral stepping (Figure 6).

CONCLUSIONS We presented Beyond Retargeting , a retargeting-free frame-work for low-latency and robust humanoid whole-body tele-operation. A privileged MoE teacher is used only during training; at deployment, the student directly maps human motion and robot proprioception to actions in one causal forward pass. Its discrete codebook projects partial, noisy, and OOD observations onto plausible full-body motion prototypes. Across cross-simulator tests, our method achieves the best task completion and six of seven tracking metrics among com-pared systems, while retaining a low-latency deployment path. It also outperforms continuous regression across diverse OOD conditions and transfers to real-robot control from various human-motion input sources without fine-tuning. The current codebook is frame-wise and does not explicitly model long-term motion structure. Future work will explore temporal primitives, uncertainty-aware recovery, and contact-aware teleoperation.
