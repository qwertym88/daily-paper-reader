# Learning to Solve Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming with Neural Networks and Sequential Quadratic Programming Refinement 

Viet-Anh Le, Member, IEEE , and Rahul Mangharam, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract — We propose a learning-to-optimize framework for solving parametric mixed-integer nonlinear programs arising in model predictive control and trajectory optimization. The proposed method combines neural network prediction with a sequential quadratic programming (SQP) refinement layer. First, neural networks are trained using a combination of supervised and self-supervised losses to predict integer solutions together with compatible continuous warm starts. To account for prediction inaccuracies, the trained predictor generates multiple candidate integer solutions, each paired with a con-tinuous initialization. The integer solutions are then fixed, and a differentiable SQP layer refines the associated continuous variables over a prescribed number of iterations. The SQP-layer parameters, including the curvature regularization and step size, are trained to improve both feasibility and objective value after every iteration. By separating integer prediction from continuous optimization-based refinement, the proposed framework improves robustness to imperfect neural predictions while maintaining a predictable computational budget. We demonstrate the proposed framework on contact and trajectory optimization for a planar box-pushing example. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Mixed-integer optimization arises naturally in many con-trol applications involving coupled continuous control and discrete decision-making, including autonomous driving [1], [2], robotics [3], [4], and hybrid systems [5], [6]. Despite the advantages offered by mixed-integer optimization for trajectory optimization and model predictive control (MPC), its real-time implementation remains challenging because solving the mixed-integer programs combines the combi-natorial complexity of discrete optimization with the com-putational burden of continuous optimization. Learning-to-optimize (L2O) provides a promising approach for reducing the computational cost required for online decision-making by replacing or accelerating iterative solvers with learned mappings from problem parameters to solutions. In recent years, there has been growing research interest in L2O methods for mixed-integer programming. Classical supervised-learning approaches for predicting integer so-lutions in mixed-integer optimization have been proposed in [4], [7], [8]. These methods primarily focus on mixed-integer convex programs, where fixing the learned integer variables reduces the original problem to convex continuous optimization problems. In [9], a hybrid-action reinforcement learning approach was proposed to jointly learn discrete and continuous control decisions for mixed-integer optimal

Self-supervised learning approaches [10], [11] have been developed for solving mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLPs) by training neural networks (NNs) with loss func-tions that combine the original objective with penalty terms for constraint violations. Though self-supervised learning can improve feasibility by penalizing constraint violations during training, such approaches may yield highly suboptimal so-lutions, since gradient-based training can converge to poor local optima. In [12], we developed a hybrid L2O frame-work for mixed-integer quadratic programming (MIQP) that combines neural prediction of the integer variables with QP-based continuous refinement and is trained using a combi-nation of supervised and self-supervised loss functions. Though NNs trained with either supervised or self-supervised losses can approximate MINLP solutions, pre-diction errors may still be present and lead to suboptimal or infeasible solutions. To improve reliability, an online correction mechanism is therefore necessary. For example, [8] considers evaluating multiple high-probability discrete strategies predicted by the NNs and solving the corre-sponding optimization problems sequentially until a feasible solution is found. However, this approach is primarily suited to mixed-integer convex programs, where fixing the discrete strategy yields a convex continuous optimization problem. In this paper, we propose an L2O framework for MINLPs using NNs to generate multiple solution candidates and a sequential quadratic programming (SQP) refinement layer. The NNs are trained to learn both optimal integer decisions and continuous initializations. Each candidate is then refined by a differentiable elastic trust-region SQP layer while keeping the integer decisions fixed, with trainable SQP parameters optimized to improve the continuous solution within a few iterations. During online inference, given a fixed number of candidate integer solutions and their corresponding con-tinuous solutions refined through SQP iterations, the final solution is selected based on feasibility and objective value. Thus, this design incorporates the constrained optimization structure of the original problem while maintaining a pre-dictable online computational budget. We demonstrate the performance of the proposed framework on a contact and trajectory optimization problem for planar box pushing. Compared to the most relevant work in the literature [10], which also develops an L2O framework for MINLP, this paper differs in several aspects. First, rather than predicting relaxed MINLP solutions with an NN, we train the NN to arXiv:2610.05746v1 [math.OC] 5 Oct 2026 directly predict mixed-integer solutions that are subsequently refined by the optimization layer. Second, while the cor-rection layers in [10] are designed primarily to enforce the integrality of relaxed MINLP solutions, our refinement layer is trained to further improve the predicted mixed-integer candidates online with respect to feasibility and optimality. Finally, by incorporating an SQP as a refinement layer, our framework enables online optimization to be more explicitly embedded within the L2O architecture. B. Results and Discussions 

We generate a labeled dataset consisting of 300 problem instances using the GUROBI optimizer [20] to train the NN using a combination of supervised and self-supervised losses in Phase 1. Note that obtaining ground-truth labeled solutions is computationally expensive, which limits the number of available training data points. As a result, this example is particularly useful for evaluating the proposed method under limited data. We additionally generate a separate set of 100 

scenarios to evaluate the trained model. Video visualizations of the results and implementation details are available on the supplemental website: https://sites.google.com/ view/l2o-minlp . In Figure 2, we show two representa-tive planar-pushing trajectories, where the pusher switches between box faces to steer the object toward the target pose. In both cases, the box successfully reaches the desired configuration using the trained L2O model with B = 8 and 

K = 3 .Next, we focus on evaluating the effect of the SQP re-finement using different numbers of SQP layers and solution candidates. We compute and report the following metrics: (1) the optimality gap with respect to the reference solution obtained using the GUROBI optimizer and (2) the worst-case constraint violation, as shown in Figure 3. The figure shows that the SQP refinement layer improves both optimality and feasibility relative to the NN predictor, with constraint viola-tions generally decreasing as the number of SQP layers and solution candidates increases. At B = 8 and K = 3 , the SQP refinement reduces the constraint violation by approximately 

10 times compared with the NN-only prediction. Overall, the results consistently indicate that these improvements can be achieved with only a few SQP layers and solution candidates, which supports the use of fixed-depth refinement for real-time implementation. Next, we show in Table I the online computation time of the proposed L2O framework with different K and B,together with the baseline GUROBI solver. For this example, GUROBI faces a challenging optimization problem because the discrete contact-mode decisions are tightly coupled with nonlinear force and torque variables through nonconvex contact dynamics, resulting in weaker relaxations and a more difficult branch-and-bound search. Overall, these runtime comparison results demonstrate a trade-off between computational cost and solution quality as the numbers of candidates and SQP layers increase. 

Limitations: Although the proposed framework demon-strates promising performance, we identify several limita-tions. First, the training and inference costs increase as the number of SQP layers grows. Second, scalability to high-dimensional problems remains challenging, as generating sufficient high-quality training data for such problems may be difficult, and both the forward and backward passes through the QP layers can become computationally ex-pensive. Addressing these challenges will be an important direction for future work in L2O for MINLP. VI. C ONCLUSIONS 

This paper presented an L2O framework for solving parametric MINLPs that combines NN prediction of inte-ger solutions and continuous initializations with trainable SQP refinement. By generating multiple candidate integer solutions and refining their associated continuous warm starts with a trainable SQP layer, the framework leverages the underlying problem structure to improve feasibility and optimality, while reducing the computational effort required to solve the MINLPs. The performance was demonstrated on contact scheduling and trajectory optimization for planar box pushing, where the proposed framework improves solution quality relative to using the NN predictor solely, and achieves significant reductions in computation time compared with solving the MINLP using a state-of-the-art numerical solver.
