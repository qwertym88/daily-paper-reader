Abstract
This paper studies the complexity of convex optimization using lazy second-order oracles (Doikov,
Chayti, and Jaggi, ICML 2023), where an algorithm queries gradients every iteration and Hessians once
per m iterations. Under this setting, we show a lower bound of Ω(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7) on the number of total
iterations to find an ϵ-solution using a novel block zero-chain construction. Then we propose a novel
method that achieves a new upper bound of ˜O(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7), which significantly improves the prior one
(Chen, Liu, Luo, and Zhang, COLT 2026) of ˜O(m + m13/21ϵ−2/7) and is tight up to logarithmic factors.

Introduction
In this work, we consider the complexity of solving the convex optimization problem
min
x∈Rd f(x).
(1)
Following the seminal work by Nemirovskij and Yudin [21], we consider the oracle complexity for convex
optimization. In this framework, we assume that every algorithm is interacting with an oracle O(x) that
returns the local information of query x. Two fundamental oracles are: (1) first-order oracle O1(x) = ∇f(x)
returns the gradient information; (2) the second-order oracle O2(x) = (∇f(x), ∇2f(x)) additionally returns a
Hessian matrix that reflects the curvature information. The core question in oracle complexity is the minimal
number of oracle queries T required to find an ϵ-solution to Problem (1).
Both the optimal complexities are known in the standard oracle model that counts the complexity by
#O1(x)
or
#O2(x)
(2)
for first- or second-order methods, respectively. In 1983, Nesterov [22] discovered the optimal first-order
methods, which can find an ϵ-solution to the problem in the first-order complexity of O(ϵ−1/2) that matches
the lower bound in Nemirovskij and Yudin [21] up to constants. Over the past two decades, a series of
works studying how to use second-order information for further acceleration. In 2006, Nesterov and Polyak
[25] introduced the cubic regularized Newton (CRN) method as the first globally convergent second-order
method, based on which Nesterov [23] in 2008 proposed the accelerated CRN with the second-order oracle
complexity of O(ϵ−1/3). In 2013, Monteiro and Svaiter [20] proposed a faster accelerated Newton proximal
extragradient (A-NPE) method with the complexity of O(ϵ−2/7 log ϵ−1). In 2022, Carmon et al. [5], Kovalev
and Gasnikov [15] improved A-NPE to achieved the optimal complexity of O(ϵ−2/7) that matches the lower
bound in Arjevani et al. [4].

Although the optimal complexities have been established for the standard oracle model, we argue that
it may not reflect the computational costs of an algorithm. Firstly, the standard oracle model restricts the
algorithm to access either the first-order oracle or the second-order oracle. While in practice, the algorithm
may access O2(x) at some iterations and only O1(x) for other iterations. Secondly, the standard oracle
model does not distinguish the costs between the Hessian and the gradient. The computational cost of a
Hessian matrix is usually much higher the one of a gradient. Therefore, a more reasonable setup is to assume
that the cost of O2(x) is m times the cost of O1(x) and to use the following cost model, which measures the
complexity of an algorithm by:
#O1(x) + m · #O2(x).
(3)
Under this new cost model, the prior optimal first- and second-order methods have the complexity of O(ϵ−1/2)
and O(mϵ−2/7), respectively. We observe that the complexity of this cost model can be reduced to count the
complexity of m-lazy second-order oracle which returns O2(x) once every m iterations and O1(x) for the
other iterations [6, 11]. Very recently, Chen et al. [10] gives an ˜O(m + m13/21ϵ−2/7) upper bound of m-lazy
second-order oracle for convex optimization, which strictly improves all existing second-order methods and
achieves an optimal dependency in ϵ. However, the optimal dependency on m remains unknown.
Our contributions.
In this paper, we fully resolve the optimal complexity for convex optimization using
lazy second-order oracles, with the contributions summarized as follows.
1. We introduce a block zero-chain that results in a lower bound of Ω(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7) for any m-lazy
second-order algorithms. In contrast, prior applicable lower bounds based on standard zero-chains
[4, 24] can not distinguish the information of first- and second-order oracles, and can only give the
trivial lower bound of Ω(ϵ−2/7) even for m ≥1.
2. We also propose a novel second-order method that achieves the upper bound of ˜O(m+m1/7ϵ−2/7), which
matches our lower bound up to logarithmic factors. Our result builds upon the previous framework by
Chen et al. [10] that reduces the problem to solving a series of cubic proximal subproblems, while we
design a faster sub-solver based on a preconditioned accelerated gradient descent with segment search
on an extrapolated objective.
AI Usage.
The authors derived the lower bound of Ω(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7) and utilized this benchmark to
evaluate AI reasoning capabilities. While ChatGPT 5.5 Pro and 5.6 Pro Sol both failed to yield a non-trivial
dependence on m for the lower bound, ChatGPT 6 Astra ultimately recovered the exact same construction as
the authors. Meanwhile, the upper bound was established through multiple iterations with these AI models.
ChatGPT 5.6 Pro Sol first established an upper bound of ˜O(m + m3/7ϵ−2/7) by improving the cubic proximal
sub-solver via accelerated inexact second-order methods [9]. Later, through extensive interactions with the
newly released ChatGPT 6 Astra, the upper bound was first sharpened to ˜O(m + m1/3ϵ−2/7), and finally to
the near-optimal complexity of ˜O(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7). The initial responses by AI are publicly available 1.

Lower Complexity Bound
As a starting point, it is not hard to observe that, under the cost model (3), the number of gradient queries
of any optimal method must be m times that of the Hessians. Equivalently,
#O1(x) = m · #O2(x)
(4)
Otherwise, if the algorithm uses fewer gradient evaluations such that #O1(x) < m · #O2(x), then it is
strictly dominated by completing the missing gradient queries, which have access to more information without
incurring additional costs up to constants. Symmetrically, if it uses fewer Hessian evaluations such that
#O2(x) < #O1(x)/m, then it is also inferior to one that fills the remaining Hessian queries.
Therefore, we only need to consider second-order algorithms under constraint (4), for which we provide
lower bounds in the following: We first give lower bounds for the second-order algorithms [6, 11] in Section 3.1,
which will also imply the same lower bounds for general second-order algorithms under constraint (4) via a
simulation-based reduction in Section 3.2.

Conclusion and Future Works
In this paper, we study the oracle complexity of convex optimization using m-lazy second-order oracles. We
prove a lower bound of Ω(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7) and a nearly matching upper bound of ˜O(m + m1/7ϵ−2/7).
Looking ahead, our work opens up several promising avenues for future research. On the theoretical side,
while this paper resolves the convex setting up to logarithmic factors, the complexity for nonconvex settings
remains an open, where the best-known upper and lower bounds [10] for finding an ϵ-stationary point are
O(m + m1/3ϵ−3/2) and Ω(ϵ−3/2), respectively. It is also interesting to study optimality under the cost model
for solving minimax problems [7, 8] and nonlinear equations [16]. On the practical side, we hope our work
can serve as a principled starting point for designing efficient (inexact) second-order optimizers in practice,
especially given the ubiquity of lazy Hessian evaluation techniques in modern deep learning optimizers such
as KFAC [13, 18], Sophia [17], Shampoo [3, 14, 26], SOAP [27], ASGO [2], and Newton-Muon [12].
