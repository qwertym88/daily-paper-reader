Abstract
This paper introduces a graph convolutional reinforcement learning framework for the finite-time
stabilization of chaotic dynamics in a physical Chua circuit. The circuit is modeled directly in terms
of capacitor voltages, inductor current, and physical component parameters. This preserves the con-
nection between the mathematical model and the underlying electrical system. A unified, flexible
terminal sliding-mode controller is developed to achieve stabilization within a finite time in the pres-
ence of uncertainties in the lumped model, external disturbances, and bounded control inputs. The
controller incorporates state-dependent nonlinear exponents to provide flexible convergence character-
istics while avoiding singular terms near equilibrium. To minimize dependence on manual controller
tuning, the admissible controller parameters are optimized using an actor-critic reinforcement learning
scheme with a graph convolutional network that leverages the circuit dynamics’ coupling structure.
The learned parameters are constrained to the ranges required by the theoretical stability conditions.
Numerical experiments demonstrate the rapid suppression of chaotic oscillations and the finite-time
convergence of the state and sliding variables while respecting the prescribed input-saturation limits.
Compared to a reference control method, the proposed approach reduces the IAE, ISE, ITAE, and
ITSE by 56.27 %, 56.40 %, 61.69 %, and 64.70 %, respectively. These results suggest that graph-based
reinforcement learning can effectively tune parameters while retaining the robustness and analytical
structure of the underlying sliding-mode controller.
Keywords: Chua circuit; chaotic systems; finite-time control; sliding-mode control; reinforcement lear-
ning; graph convolutional networks.

Introduction
Chaos is a distinctive phenomenon in nonlinear dynamical systems, characterized by deterministic yet
highly irregular behavior and strong sensitivity to initial conditions. Since the pioneering observations
of Lorenz, chaotic dynamics have attracted considerable attention across mathematics, physics, and en-
gineering.
Their complex evolution and limited long-term predictability have made the analysis and
characterization of chaotic systems an important area of nonlinear dynamics [1]. The distinctive prop-
erties of chaotic dynamics have also been exploited in a wide range of practical applications, including
secure communication and encryption, signal processing, electronic circuits, robotics, power systems, and
chemical processes. While chaotic behavior can be deliberately exploited in applications such as informa-
tion security and signal generation, it may also lead to undesirable oscillations and degraded performance
in engineering systems. This dual role has motivated extensive research on both the utilization and effec-
tive control of chaotic dynamics [2]. Chua’s circuit remains one of the most extensively studied physical
realizations of chaos, particularly because of its simple electronic structure and rich nonlinear behavior,

including the well-known double-scroll attractor [3, 4]. Its dynamics are commonly represented through
normalized state-space equations, which provide a compact mathematical description and facilitate non-
linear analysis. However, normalization partly removes the direct correspondence between the model
variables and the electrical quantities of the original circuit, making the influence of individual circuit
components less transparent.
A circuit-level representation offers a different perspective by preserving the actual electrical states and
component parameters. In this formulation, the capacitor voltages VC1 and VC2 and the inductor cur-
rent iL remain the system states, while C1, C2, L, and RP explicitly appear in the governing equations.
The nonlinear resistor is also described through its physical slopes and breakpoint voltage. This repre-
sentation therefore maintains a direct link between the observed chaotic dynamics and the underlying
circuit configuration, allowing the influence of component values and parameter variations on the system
behavior to be examined more explicitly. Stabilizing chaotic dynamics is a challenging control problem
because strong nonlinearities and sensitivity to perturbations can significantly affect both transient and
steady-state behavior. Various strategies, including Ott-Grebogi-Yorke (OGY) control, active control,
adaptive and backstepping schemes, model predictive control, intelligent control methods, and sliding
mode control, have been developed to regulate chaotic systems toward prescribed equilibria or trajec-
tories [5–8]. Their practical effectiveness, however, remains closely tied to robustness against uncertain
dynamics and disturbances, convergence characteristics, model dependence, and constraints imposed on
the control input.
Sliding mode control (SMC) provides a robust framework for regulating nonlinear and chaotic dynamics
through the construction of an invariant sliding manifold. Its low sensitivity to matched uncertainties
and disturbances has supported its extensive use in chaos stabilization and synchronization [9]. In recent
years, SMC has received increasing attention, with various control schemes developed to address different
nonlinear systems and engineering applications. For example, in [10], an adaptive SMC reaching law was
developed for PMSM speed regulation, with the main focus on reaching speed and chattering reduction.
Gedefaw, Abdissa, and Lemma [11] developed a super-twisting SMC with a fuzzy PID surface for quadro-
tor trajectory tracking, where fuzzy logic was employed for online tuning of the controller gains. In [12],
a high-order SMC tuned by reinforcement learning was designed for a three-level NPC inverter, enabling
real-time gain adjustment under varying grid conditions and LVRT operation. An adaptive SMC com-
bined with radial basis function (RBF) neural networks was designed in [13] for robotic excavators, using
the neural network to estimate and compensate for unknown system dynamics. For mobile manipulator
systems, Ref. [14] proposed a backstepping fuzzy SMC whose control parameters were optimized using
PSO for simultaneous end-effector and platform motion tracking. Ref. [15] investigated two SMC schemes
for the chaotic Belousov–Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction, employing smoothed and adaptive fuzzy structures
to address tracking performance and chattering. In [16], a data-driven SMC framework was developed
for MIMO nonlinear systems with partially unknown dynamics, combining a robust reaching controller
with an SDP-based nominal controller.
Despite these developments, some limitations remain in the existing SMC literature: (1) the performance
of several SMC schemes is highly dependent on controller parameter selection, which may require consid-
erable tuning effort; (2) actuator saturation is not explicitly incorporated into the design of some existing
controllers, despite its practical importance in physical systems; (3) certain SMC approaches still rely
on sufficiently accurate knowledge of the system dynamics, which may restrict their applicability when
model information is incomplete; and (4) although intelligent optimization techniques have increasingly
been combined with SMC, the use of deep and graph-based learning for controller parameter optimization
remains relatively limited.
To address these issues, this study develops a graph-learning-based unified flexible terminal sliding mode
control (UFT-SMC) framework for the finite-time stabilization of chaotic dynamics. Unlike the com-
monly adopted normalized Chua models, the controller is implemented on a circuit-level representation
in which the capacitor voltages, inductor current, and physical circuit parameters are retained explicitly.
The control formulation accounts for lumped uncertainties, bounded disturbances, and input saturation,
while a GCN-based learning scheme is employed to optimize the controller parameters based on their
interdependencies and closed-loop performance.
Accordingly, the main contributions are summarized as follows: (1) a physical Chua circuit model is
considered to preserve the direct connection between chaotic dynamics and actual electrical quantities;
(2) a UFT-SMC scheme is developed for finite-time stabilization under uncertain dynamics and con-
strained control inputs; (3) a GCN-based framework is introduced for systematic optimization of the
2


controller parameters; and (4) the proposed approach is assessed through convergence, control effort,
integral performance indices, and comparative control studies.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the physical Chua circuit model
and its dynamical characteristics. Section 3 develops the proposed UFT-SMC and establishes the cor-
responding stability and finite-time convergence results. Section 4 presents the stochastic robustness
analysis of the proposed control method. Section 5 introduces the graph-learning-based parameter op-
timization framework. Section 6 presents the numerical results and comparative evaluations. Finally,
Section 7 concludes the paper.

Remark 1. Following the above stability analysis, the flexibility of the proposed UFT-SMC can be further
interpreted through the state-dependent exponents p + sign(xi) and q + sign(si) in the sliding surface and
control law, respectively. By choosing 0 < ω < 1, 1 < p < 2, 0 < β < 1, and 1 < q < 2, the exponents
switch between p+1 > 2 and p−1 ∈(0, 1) for the state dynamics, and between q+1 > 2 and q−1 ∈(0, 1)
for the sliding dynamics. This structure provides flexible shaping of the nonlinear convergence behavior
without requiring an explicit piecewise formulation. The outer sign functions preserve the restoring di-
rection toward the equilibrium and the sliding manifold, while the conditions 1 < p < 2 and 1 < q < 2
avoid negative-power singularities near the origin. Employing the same exponent-shaping mechanism in
both the state and sliding dynamics further yields a unified and structurally symmetric control design.

5.1
Learning-Based Parameter-Tuning Framework
The design parameters of the proposed sliding-mode controller directly influence the transient response,
reaching behavior, and control effort of the closed-loop system. To avoid extensive trial-and-error tuning,
their selection is formulated as a constrained sequential decision problem and addressed through a deep
reinforcement learning (DRL) framework [19].

parameter-selection policy is denoted by ΠϑA, where ϑA represents the trainable actor parameters.

Conclusion
The Chua circuit remains a representative nonlinear system for studying chaotic dynamics and assessing
control strategies under physically meaningful operating conditions.
In this work, a graph-learning-
assisted UFT-SMC was developed for its finite-time stabilization in the presence of input saturation,
uncertainties, and external disturbances. The controller suppresses the chaotic oscillations and drives
the circuit states to the desired equilibrium within a short transient period, while the sliding variables
converge to zero and the control inputs remain within their prescribed bounds. The graph-learning scheme
was used to tune the controller parameters according to their influence on the closed-loop response,
reducing the need for manual parameter adjustment.
The resulting design showed faster regulation,
smaller residual errors, and better integral performance indices than the existing method considered for
comparison. These results show that graph-based learning can be effectively incorporated into nonlinear
control design as a practical parameter-tuning tool without altering the underlying control structure.
Future work will consider experimental validation of the proposed approach and its application to other
nonlinear and fractional-order dynamical systems.
