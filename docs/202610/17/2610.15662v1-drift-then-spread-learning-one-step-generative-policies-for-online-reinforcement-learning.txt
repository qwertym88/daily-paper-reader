## ABSTRACT 

Generative policies offer expressive action distributions for online reinforcement learning (RL), but many diffusion- and flow-based policies require costly iterative sampling. We propose D RIFT SPREAD , a one-step generative policy based on Drifting for online maximum-entropy (MaxEnt) RL. To incorporate entropy without a tractable policy density, we build on an energy-based view of Drifting and derive critic-guided Drift and entropy-induced Spread from the negative MaxEnt objective. Our method learns these updates sequentially in the same actor, using action regression for value improvement and distribution matching to capture the effect of entropy. The actor updates require neither direct samples from the critic-defined Boltzmann target nor policy-density or score estimation. Under exact fitting with a fixed critic, we prove convergence to the Boltzmann target in the small-step limit followed by the long-time limit. On MuJoCo, D RIFT SPREAD achieves the highest aggregate normalized return among all evaluated methods, while outperforming the diffusion- and flow-based baselines in both training and inference efficiency.

Generative policies offer expressive action distributions for online reinforcement learning (RL). Diffusion- and flow-based policies can capture multimodal distributions that are difficult to represent 1arXiv:2610.15662v1 [cs.LG] 11 Oct 2026 Preprint with a single Gaussian (Dong et al., 2025; Celik et al., 2025). However, many of these approaches generate actions through iterative sampling, requiring repeated network evaluations during training and inference. Recent MeanFlow-based methods reduce this cost through few-step or one-step action generation (Dong et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2026). Drifting offers a different way to train one-step generators (Deng et al., 2026). At each training iteration, it uses target data to move generated samples toward the desired distribution, then trains the generator to reproduce the moved samples from the same inputs. Through these repeated updates, the generator learns to produce samples from the target distribution in a single forward pass at inference. This approach has shown better performance than MeanFlow variants in one-step image generation on ImageNet (Deng et al., 2026). These results motivate exploring Drifting for online RL, where its use remains less studied. We consider the maximum-entropy (MaxEnt) RL objective, which balances expected return with policy entropy to encourage exploration (Haarnoja et al., 2018). The critic defines a Boltzmann target distribution for policy improvement, but does not directly provide samples from it (Li et al., 2026). We therefore adopt an energy-based view of Drifting (Gretton et al., 2026; Han et al., 2026), using the negative MaxEnt policy-improvement objective as the energy. This energy guides updates to actions generated by the current policy. For the value term, critic gradients tell us how to move actions toward higher values. The entropy term is harder to use: an implicit actor generates actions but does not provide a tractable density for computing their distribution’s entropy. We therefore need training targets that capture the effect of entropy without explicitly computing it. We propose D RIFT SPREAD , a one-step generative policy in the Drifting model family for online MaxEnt RL. Our key idea is to derive two updates from this energy and fit them sequentially to the same actor through repeated Drift–Spread cycles. In each cycle, Drift uses critic gradients to move generated actions toward higher values. The actor then learns to reproduce these updated actions from the same inputs through regression. Spread captures the entropy contribution through diffusion of the action distribution. We approximate this diffusion by adding appropriately scaled independent noise to actions from the updated actor, then train the actor to match the resulting distribution. This allows the actor to learn the effect of entropy without explicitly computing it. For a fixed critic, we prove that the learned policy converges to the Boltzmann target distribution in the small-step, long-time limit. Our main contributions are: (1) We introduce D RIFT SPREAD , a Drifting model for online MaxEnt RL with expressive policies and one-step action generation. (2) We develop sequential Drift–Spread training through critic-guided regression and entropy regularization to address the challenge of entropy computation for implicit policies. (3) On MuJoCo, D RIFT SPREAD outperforms 13 baselines in aggregate normalized return, with lower training costs than the diffusion, flow, and MeanFlow baselines (Figure 1). 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Generative policies for online RL. Diffusion and flow models provide expressive action distribu-tions for online RL. One line of work optimizes policies by backpropagating critic gradients through diffusion or flow sampling (Wang et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2025). DIME also differentiates through diffusion sampling while optimizing a variational lower bound on the MaxEnt objective (Celik et al., 2025). Another line of work constructs training targets for learning critic-defined Boltzmann distributions. DQS uses weighted averages of critic gradients to construct score targets (Jain et al., 2025). SDAC and MaxEntDP instead use critic values to weight denoising supervision and noise estimates, respectively (Ma et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025). Reverse flow matching unifies these gradient-expectation and noise-expectation formulations through posterior-mean estimation (Li et al., 2026). These approaches use iterative action sampling, motivating few-step and one-step policies for online RL. MeanFlow was introduced for one-step generative modeling and learns average velocities (Geng et al., 2025). Recent online RL methods use this formulation to learn policies with fewer generation steps. FPMD trains rectified-flow and MeanFlow policies through policy mirror descent, with both variants supporting one-step inference (Chen et al., 2025). MFPO trains few-step MeanFlow policies using velocity estimates for critic-defined Boltzmann targets (Dong et al., 2026), while SOM 2Preprint constructs velocity targets from critic-based score estimates to train one-step MeanFlow policies (Kim et al., 2026). 

Drifting models and policy learning. Drifting learns one-step generators by repeatedly moving generated samples and training the generator to reproduce their updated positions. This approach has achieved state-of-the-art performance in one-step image generation on ImageNet (Deng et al., 2026). Subsequent work relates specific Drifting fields to Wasserstein gradient flows (Gretton et al., 2026) and uses optimal-transport couplings to construct sample updates (He et al., 2026). W-Flow derives sample movements from an objective over distributions and trains a one-step generator on the resulting samples (Han et al., 2026). Drifting has also been applied to policy learning. For offline RL, DriftQL combines critic-guided policy improvement with a Drifting regularizer based on the behavior distribution (Houssaini et al., 2026). For offline-to-online RL, Drifting Field Policy derives a KL-regularized policy update through Wasserstein gradient flow and constructs training targets from actions ranked by the critic (Koo et al., 2026). Our work focuses on online MaxEnt RL from scratch.

Why sequential fitting? Sequential fitting first minimizes the Drift regression loss LDrift , then constructs and fits the Spread target using the up-dated actor. After repeated fitting, Sequential closely matches the Boltzmann target, whereas Joint remains too narrow in one coordinate and too wide in the other, despite tun-ing its weight. We implement D RIFT SPREAD as an off-policy actor–critic algorithm that reuses past transitions stored in a replay buffer. Training combines data collection, critic learning, and sequential Drift–Spread actor updates. 

Data collection. At each environment step, we sample an action a ∼ πθ (· | s) from the current actor and execute it in the environment. We store the resulting transition (s, a, r, s ′, δ ) in the replay buffer for subsequent critic and actor updates. 

Critic training. Following prior generative-policy methods (Jain et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026), we use standard critic targets without an entropy term, while retaining entropy regularization in the actor updates. We maintain two critics and use the smaller of their target values to reduce overestimation (Fujimoto et al., 2018). For a replay transition (s, a, r, s ′, δ ), both critics minimize the squared error to the detached target y = r + γ(1 − δ) min i=1 ,2 ¯Qi(s′, a ′), where a′ ∼ π¯θ (· | s′). Here, ¯Qi and π¯θ

denote the target critics and target policy, respectively, and δ indicates termination. 

Actor training. After each critic update, we hold the critics fixed and perform one Drift–Spread cycle using states sampled from the replay buffer. For these states, the current actor generates the action samples used to construct the Drift targets. We take one optimizer step on the Drift regression loss LDrift . We then construct the Spread target from the updated actor and take one optimizer step on the Spread MMD loss LSpread .The full training procedure is provided in Appendix A.3.

## 6 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

We introduced D RIFT SPREAD , a Drifting-based approach to learning expressive one-step policies for online RL. Building on an energy-based view of Drifting, we derive critic-guided Drift and entropy-induced Spread from the MaxEnt objective. The two updates are learned sequentially by the same actor: Drift moves actions toward higher values, while Spread matches a broader distribution constructed from the updated actor. This design incorporates the effect of entropy without estimating policy densities or scores, while allowing each action sample to be generated in one forward pass. Under exact fitting, our analysis establishes convergence to the fixed-critic Boltzmann target in the small-step, long-time limit. MuJoCo experiments show that D RIFT SPREAD combines strong control performance with efficient training and inference, supporting Drifting as a practical approach to generative policy learning. Our work leaves two directions for future research. First, our evaluation is limited to simulation environments. Testing on real-world control tasks would help assess robustness to observation noise and changes in system dynamics. Second, we use a fixed spreading-noise distribution and scale during training. ## C SEQUENTIAL VERSUS JOINT FITTING 

Sequential fitting first learns the Drift target and then constructs the Spread target from the updated actor. A joint alternative constructs both targets from the actor before Drift and minimizes LDrift +

βLold Spread . Here, β > 0 weights the Spread MMD loss, and “old” indicates that its target uses the pre-Drift actor. An action can have multiple dimensions. A value of β that balances Drift and Spread in one dimension may place too much or too little weight on Spread in other dimensions. Because β scales the entire Spread loss, adjusting it cannot always balance all dimensions simultaneously. Using a separate weight for each dimension would also make hyperparameter tuning much harder, as action spaces can have dozens or even hundreds of dimensions. The limitation of a single β can arise even for simple action distributions. We construct a two-dimensional Gaussian example in which no choice of the scalar weight β can balance Drift and Spread in both dimensions at the Boltzmann target (see proposition C.1).
