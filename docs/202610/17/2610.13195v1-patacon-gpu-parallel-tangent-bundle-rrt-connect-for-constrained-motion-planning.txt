# PATACON: GPU-Parallel Tangent-Bundle RRT-Connect for Constrained Motion Planning 

Gahyun Oh 1, Junho An 1, Suhyun Jeon 2, Suhan Park 1,∗

Abstract — Fast whole-body motion planning enables contin-uous replanning for humanoid robots in dynamic environments while satisfying collision avoidance and kinematic constraints. However, constrained motion planning incurs substantial com-putational cost due to repeated constraint projection in addition to collision checking and nearest-neighbor search. Recently, GPU-based massively parallel planners have been proposed to reduce computational cost. Nevertheless, these planners still steer in the ambient configuration space, so projecting candidates that deviate from the manifold remains a significant source of overhead, particularly in highly constrained, high-dimensional problems. In this work, we introduce PATACON, a GPU-parallel bidirectional planner that explores tangent space to exploit the local manifold geometry induced by known constraint functions. Experiments on dual-arm closed-chain and humanoid whole-body constrained planning tasks show that PATACON outperforms the prior work as configuration-space dimension and constraint codimension grow, achieving up to an 11.25 times speedup. These results suggest that GPU-parallel tangent-space exploration brings constrained whole-body motion planning closer to real-time performance. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Dual-arm closed-chain and humanoid whole-body mo-tion require maintaining task-dependent kinematic relation-ships while satisfying joint limits and avoiding collisions. These kinematic requirements can be formulated as nonlin-ear equality constraints, which define a lower-dimensional constraint manifold embedded in a high-dimensional con-figuration space [1]. In sampling-based motion planning, collision checking and nearest-neighbor queries are generally major sources of computational cost [2], [3]. Planning on a constraint manifold introduces additional overhead because constraint projection is performed repeatedly throughout tree expansion. Reducing this computational burden is particu-larly important in scenarios that require repeated replanning as the target, obstacles, or task conditions change, where low planning latency is essential for responsive execution. A widely used approach generates candidate motions in the ambient configuration space and projects them onto the manifold as the tree grows [1]. cpRRTC [4] brought this approach to the GPU by assigning each thread block to steer from the nearest node toward a random sample, project the waypoints of the resulting straight-line segment in parallel, and perform collision checks with early termination. Our main contributions are as follows: 1) Tangent-space-guided GPU-parallel extension. PATA-CON replaces the ambient-space steering of existing GPU-parallel planners with extension along local tangent spaces, so that candidates follow the manifold geometry before projection. 2) Shared tangent-space banks. Tangent spaces are main-tained in tree-specific banks shared across thread blocks, together with the correspondence between projected man-ifold states and their tangent-space coordinates, so that multiple blocks can explore different regions of the man-ifold concurrently. 3) Speculative multi-edge expansion. From one sample and one nearest-neighbor query, PATACON generates consecutive candidate edges along a tangent direction, validates them concurrently, and inserts the longest valid prefix, unlike the step-by-step CONNECT heuristic of RRT-Connect [7]. We evaluate PATACON on eight benchmarks ranging from 7-DoF manipulation to 35-DoF humanoid whole-body planning. Although cpRRTC is faster on low-dimensional problems, PATACON shows increasing advantages with configuration-space dimension and constraint restrictiveness, reaching a 11 .25 × speedup with a 100% success rate. II. R ELATED WORK 

Sampling-based constrained motion planners enforce non-linear equality constraints through numerical projection or lo-cal approximations of the constraint manifold [1]. Projection-based methods range from randomized gradient descent for closed chains [8] to Jacobian-based tangent-space sampling and first-order retraction [6] and CBiRRT [9] with Task Space Regions [10], whereas AtlasRRT [11] and TB-RRT [5] grow trees on local charts or tangent spaces, and Kingston et al. [12] unify these mechanisms. These methods, however, are primarily sequential. Learning-based planners reduce online search through learned latent spaces [13]–[15] or constraint-aware sam-plers [16], but require offline data generation and training; we instead accelerate planning directly from the given constraint functions. Parallelization reduces the wall-clock cost of planning through GPU collision checking [17], [18], multiple or shared trees on CPUs [19]–[21], SIMD vectorization [22], [23], and GPU trajectory optimization [24]. pRRTC [25] and BatchRRT [26] run RRT-style tree growth on the GPU, and cpRRTC [4] extends pRRTC to constrained planning by pro-jecting motion-segment waypoints in parallel. However, the steering methods remain in the ambient space, so parallelism lowers the cost of each projection but not the off-manifold motion to be projected. Simply adding tangent-space steering to such planners is insufficient, because tangent-space management and local nearest-neighbor search add per-extension overhead. PATA-CON instead exploits the fact that tangent-space candidates stay close to the manifold: one sample and nearest-neighbor query yield several consecutive candidate edges, which are validated in parallel on the GPU to amortize this overhead. III. P ROBLEM DEFINITION 

Let C ⊆ Rn denote the joint-feasible robot configuration space, where q ∈ C represents a robot configuration. Task-dependent kinematic constraints are represented by a differ-entiable nonlinear equality-constraint function h : C → Rm,which defines the constraint manifold as 

M = {q ∈ C | h(q) = 0 }. (1) At a regular configuration q in the interior of C, we assume that the constraint Jacobian Jh(q) = ∂h (q)/∂q has full row rank m. The constraint manifold is then locally of dimension 

d = n − m, and its tangent space is given by 

Tq M = {v ∈ Rn | Jh(q)v = 0 }. (2) Equivalently, let N (q) ∈ Rn×d denote an orthonormal basis of the null space of Jh(q), such that Jh(q)N (q) = 0 

and N (q)⊤N (q) = Id. This basis provides the local tangent-space representation used by the planner. Let Cfree ⊆ C denote the set of configurations that are free of both self-collision and environmental collision. The feasible portion of the constraint manifold is then 

Mfree = M ∩ C free . (3) Given feasible start and goal configurations qstart , q goal ∈Mfree , the constrained motion-planning problem considered in this work is to find a continuous path 

σ : [0 , 1] → M free , (4) such that 

σ(0) = qstart , σ(1) = qgoal . (5) Thus, every configuration along the resulting path must satisfy the nonlinear kinematic constraints, joint limits, and collision-avoidance requirements. IV. GPU-P ARALLEL TANGENT -B UNDLE RRT-C ONNECT 

PATACON restructures tangent-space-guided RRT-Connect for GPU-parallel execution through two algorithmic components: Tangent-Guided Sampling (TGS) and 

Tangent-Guided Multi-Edge Projection (TGMP) . TGS guides exploration using local tangent-space geometry, while TGMP converts each TGS exploration decision into a multi-step expansion that generates multiple consecutive candidate edges. PATACON combines concurrent exploration across multiple tangent-space regions with GPU-parallel processing of the candidate edges generated by TGMP. In each exploration iteration, PATACON expands the se-lected tree through the TGS–TGMP pipeline, projects and validates the resulting candidate edges in parallel, and inserts only the longest consecutively valid prefix. A successful EXTEND is followed by a target-directed CONNECT to-ward the opposite tree. The following subsections describe these stages in order. C. GPU-Parallel Edge Projection and Validation 

PATACON parallelizes the projection and validation of the multiple candidate edges generated by TGMP. Different candidate edges are assigned to separate cooperative thread groups and processed concurrently, whereas the waypoint projection within each individual edge follows the original sequential projection order. For each candidate edge, intermediate waypoints are gen-erated according to the edge-discretization resolution G.Starting from the edge source, the waypoints are projected sequentially toward the constraint manifold so that each projection step follows the preceding projected waypoint. Collision checking and feasibility validation are performed for the projected edge, while multiple candidate edges un-dergo this procedure concurrently on the GPU. An edge is considered valid only when its sequential way-point projection converges within the prescribed projection tolerances and all projected configurations satisfy the joint limits and collision constraints. During target-directed CON-NECT, the target-progress condition described in Sec. IV-D is additionally applied. Although candidate edges are processed concurrently, their validation results are consumed in the original TGMP ex-pansion order. Only the longest consecutively valid prefix beginning with the first candidate edge is inserted into the tree. If the first candidate edge is invalid, EXTEND returns T RAPPED ; otherwise, the valid prefix is inserted and EXTEND returns A DVANCED with the final inserted configuration qend .Tangent-space renewal is determined separately from valid-prefix insertion. We conducted three sets of experiments to evaluate PATACON. First , we compare planner performance across regimes spanning increasingly restrictive constraints and higher-dimensional configuration spaces. Second , we per-form a cumulative ablation study by progressively introduc-ing Tangent-Guided Sampling (TGS) and Tangent-Guided Multi-Edge Projection (TGMP) to cpRRTC. Finally , we evaluate solution-quality improvement under a common any-time optimization framework. 

A. Experimental Environment 

We evaluate PATACON primarily against two represen-tative constrained motion planners: McVAMP [23] and cpRRTC [4], representing CPU SIMD-accelerated manifold-constrained planning and GPU-parallel projection-based RRT-Connect, respectively. We additionally include TB-RRT [5] as a tangent-space-based constrained planner on the single-arm Franka benchmark. For McVAMP, we use its publicly available implementa-tion. For cpRRTC, we implement the planner by extending the publicly available pRRTC codebase according to the algorithm described in the cpRRTC paper. For TB-RRT, we use the implementation provided by OMPL. Within each benchmark regime, all planners use the same robot model, environment, start–goal pairs, joint limits, con-straint definitions, feasibility tolerances, and planning time-out. For each regime, we randomly sample 100 feasible start– goal joint-state pairs, which are shared across all evaluated planners. All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D CPU, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU, and 64 GB of system memory. Unless otherwise specified, PATACON used Nblk = 512 ,

Kmax = 4 , ϵM = 0 .1, and an edge-discretization resolu-tion of G = 16 . One-time robot, environment, and device initialization was excluded from the timing measurements, while all computations required for each planning query were included. The benchmark robots span configuration spaces of differ-ent dimensions: (A) 7-DoF for the Franka Panda, (B) 14-DoF for the dual Franka, (C) 15-DoF for the fixed-base FFW-SG2, (D) 18-DoF for the mobile-base FFW-SG2, and (E) 35-DoF for the Unitree G1. We denote the constraints as follows: BK is a 6D bimanual kinematic-chain equality constraint on the relative end-effector pose; OR is a 2D orientation equality constraint that aligns the z-axes while leaving rotation about the aligned axis unconstrained; and FT is a 12D equality constraint that fixes the poses of both feet (6D per foot). CoM constrains the planar (x, y ) center of mass to lie within the support region using inequality constraints.

C. Ablation Study 

Starting from cpRRTC, +TGS introduces the tangent-guided exploration described in Sec. IV-A. +TGMP further generates multiple consecutive candidate edges within asingle EXTEND call, but processes them edge by edge: each candidate edge is projected and collision-validated before the next edge is processed. PATACON retains this tangent-guided multi-edge expansion, but projects and validates multiple candidate edges concurrently on the GPU while preventing backtracking toward previously explored tangent-space regions. TGS alone increases planning time because construct-ing and maintaining tangent-space information introduces additional overhead despite providing geometry-aware ex-ploration and a restricted nearest-neighbor search. TGMP reduces this overhead by reusing the same tangent-space context across multiple consecutive expansion steps, thereby amortizing the cost of tangent-space construction. In this work, we presented PATACON, a GPU-parallel tangent-bundle RRT-Connect framework for constrained mo-tion planning. PATACON integrates tangent-space-guided exploration with GPU-parallel tree expansion and validation, enabling efficient exploration of constrained configuration spaces. Experimental results across dual-arm and whole-body planning problems show that PATACON becomes increas-ingly advantageous as the constraints become more restric-tive and the planning problem becomes more challenging. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining local tangent-space guidance with massively parallel GPU execution for high-dimensional constrained motion planning. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS

After the initial submission, we found that a computational task had not terminated and was still running on the same GPU during benchmarking.
