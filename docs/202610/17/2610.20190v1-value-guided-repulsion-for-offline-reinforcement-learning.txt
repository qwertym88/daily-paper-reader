## ABSTRACT 

Drifting models have emerged as a highly efficient and stable paradigm for pol-icy design in offline reinforcement learning (RL). However, standard drift policies determine repulsion solely from geometric relationships among generated actions and therefore cannot distinguish them by expected value. To address this lim-itation, we introduce Value-Guided Repulsion Q-Learning (VRQL) to integrate action value estimates into the particle interaction field. For each state, VRQL combines geometric kernel distance with a normalized Q value scaling. This mechanism assigns greater repulsive influence to particles with lower values while strictly retaining attraction toward behavior action. Consequently, this value-guided interaction field aligns behavior regularization with policy improvement without adding an auxiliary behavior model stage. On 50 OGBench task config-urations and 6 D4RL AntMaze configurations, VRQL demonstrates highly com-petitive performance against established baselines, suggesting that value-guided particle interaction is an effective method for offline RL. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) aims to learn effective policies from static datasets (Levine et al., 2020). As modern datasets scale and aggregate heterogeneous sources of varying quality, they exhibit com-plex and multimodal behavioral distributions. This complexity requires expressive policies to accurately model the data support and enforce behavioral constraints (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021; Kostrikov et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). Generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching approaches, provide the expressive-ness required to capture complex, multimodal action distributions (Ho et al., 2020; Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Albergo &Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Tong et al., 2024) in of-fline RL. However, the iterative sampling pro-cess incurs high inference costs, and directly training flow or diffusion policies proves dif-ficult due to unstable backpropagation through time (Wang et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025b). Acommon strategy adopts a two stage pipeline: first fitting a generative behavior model, and then using its samples to extract a separate actor (Chen et al., 2023; Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025b). Furthermore, because offline datasets contain suboptimal trajectories, these decou-pled methods weight the training data using estimated Q values to bias the actor toward high return actions (Peng et al., 2020; Ding et al., 2024; NGUIMATSIA TIOFACK et al., 2026). While effec-1arXiv:2610.20190v1 [cs.AI] 14 Oct 2026 Preprint. Under review. tive at injecting value guidance, these two stage pipelines are inherently indirect and risk introducing compounding approximation errors. By directly constructing the policy without relying on iterative sampling or auxiliary models, drift-ing models provide a promising alternative that sidesteps both inference inefficiencies and two stage approximation errors (Deng et al., 2026; Houssaini et al., 2026). These models train a conditional generator using an interaction field defined by attraction toward dataset actions and repulsion among generated particles to maintain diversity. However, while drifting models address the structural bot-tlenecks of two stage pipelines, they currently lack a mechanism for value-aware target shaping. Standard drift policies compute repulsive influence relying solely on geometric relationships. Con-sequently, particles at similar distances exert identical repulsive forces, even if their corresponding critic estimates differ significantly (Figure 1, left). Thus, a fundamental limitation of existing drift policies is their inability to incorporate estimated action values into the policy constraint, thereby hindering policy improvement. 

To address this limitation, we propose VRQL, which integrates action values into the generated ac-tion interaction field. For each state, VRQL modulates the repulsion logits by combining kernel distance with a normalized value bias. Under this design, particles with lower values exert stronger repulsive influence, whereas those with higher values exert less forces (Figure 1, right), while the original attraction toward behavior action remains strictly preserved. To ensure stable optimiza-tion, we adaptively calibrate this value signal using a shared scale derived from the typical within state value variation. Thus, VRQL maintains a direct value maximization objective while injecting value awareness into the particle interaction. This integration dynamically reshapes the behavioral constraint within the generative process, avoiding the need for an auxiliary behavior model. In summary, our primary contributions are: • Value-Guided Repulsion: We propose a value-guided repulsion mechanism. By lever-aging estimated action values and geometric proximity, VRQL modulates the influence of generated neighbors particles within the drifting policy. • Adaptive Value Calibration: We introduce adaptive value calibration to control the strength of value guidance using the typical within state variation of particle values. • Extensive Empirical Validation: We evaluate VRQL across 50 OGBench tasks and 6 D4RL AntMaze environments (Fu et al., 2021; Park et al., 2025a), demonstrating that our method yields highly competitive performance in the offline RL setting.

## 3 METHODS 

Our method VRQL builds on drifting policies: it retains the actor network and behavior action anchor while redefining how generated particles repel each other. Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete training procedure. Specifically, for a given state, the actor generates a particle set, and the critic evaluates these particles. Then, a value signal combines with pairwise geometric distances to determine the repulsion weights. The resulting interaction field forms the drift target for actor optimization. Thus, VRQL reshapes the behavioral target through value-guided interactions while preserving the value maximization objective (Houssaini et al., 2026). Offline reinforcement learning. Offline reinforce-ment learning improves a policy from a fixed dataset without environment interaction, which makes extrap-olation error on out of distribution actions the central challenge: the critic assigns unreliable values to ac-tions the data never covers, and unconstrained maxi-mization exploits precisely those errors (Levine et al., 2020). One family of methods constrains the learned policy toward the behavior policy, either by restricting the action support through a generative model of the data or by adding an explicit divergence penalty to the actor objective (Fujimoto et al., 2019; Fujimoto & Gu, 2021). A second family regularizes the value function, penalizing estimates at unobserved actions so that the induced policy stays conser-vative (Kumar et al., 2020). A third family avoids querying out of distribution actions altogether, fitting values with in sample targets and extracting a policy by weighted regression (Kostrikov et al., 2022; Tarasov et al., 2023), and recent analyses indicate that policy extraction is often the binding 8Preprint. Under review. constraint on offline performance (Park et al., 2024). VRQL adopts the policy constrained formula-tion of Eq. 1 and contributes to the regularizer, leaving the value estimation components unchanged. 

Generative policies for offline reinforcement learning. Diffusion and flow policies provide ex-pressive conditional action distributions for behavioral modeling and policy extraction (Florence et al., 2022; Janner et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025b). A first line of work applies value improvement through an iterative sampler, which can require dif-ferentiating through multiple denoising or flow integration steps (Wang et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2023; Doo et al., 2026). A second line first fits an expressive behavior model and then uses its samples or predictions to regularize a separately trained policy (Chen et al., 2023; Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025b). This two stage design separates the policy objective from the behavior model, but introduces an additional model and an approximation error between the two stages. VRQL in-stead modifies the interactions among actions generated by the policy regularizer itself, within a direct policy training objective. Sampling cost has also been reduced by distilled or few step policy classes (Kang et al., 2023; Ding & Jin, 2024), and entropy or ensemble regularization has been used to preserve action diversity (Zhang et al., 2024). 

Value-aware behavioral regularization. Offline methods with value-aware behavioral regular-ization use estimated action quality to make behavioral fitting selective. Advantage weighted re-gression and actor-critic policy extraction methods emphasize behavior action with higher estimated advantage, while implicit value based extraction combines in sample value learning with a simi-larly selective fitting objective (Peters & Schaal, 2007; Peng et al., 2020; Nair et al., 2021; Kostrikov et al., 2022). Value-aware fitting has also been applied to expressive generative policies, where value or advantage estimates reweight flow matching or diffusion targets (Ajay et al., 2023; Ding et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). Auxiliary behavior model methods can use the same signal when fitting a behavioral model and then transfer that model’s samples or targets to a separate actor (NGUIMAT-SIA TIOFACK et al., 2026). These approaches establish that behavioral regularization need not treat all examples equally, but they place the value weight on behavior action, training examples, or model targets. Value-aware fitting methods use critic estimates to prioritize behavioral examples or auxiliary model targets; VRQL uses critic estimates to assign interaction weights to the generated neighbors that define repulsion. 

Drifting models and policies. Drifting models train a direct generator by regressing toward stopped targets constructed from attractive and repulsive sample interactions (Deng et al., 2026). A conditional offline policy construction specializes this principle to action generation by combin-ing attraction toward a behavior action, repulsion among generated actions, and a direct actor value objective (Houssaini et al., 2026). VRQL retains this geometric foundation and introduces a negative value dependent bias into the normalized repulsive weights. Kernel similarity determines geometric influence, while relative critic values determine which generated neighbors exert more or less of that influence. The resulting contribution is a value-aware interaction rule inside the drifting regularizer. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduced VRQL, an offline algorithm that incorporates estimated action values directly into the repulsive interactions of a drifting policy. By combining geometric similarity with value dependent weights, VRQL modulates the influence of generated neighbors while preserving attraction toward behavioral data and maintaining a separate value maximization objective. Furthermore, batch level scaling effectively calibrates this guidance against typical within state value variations. Empirical evaluations across the OGBench and D4RL benchmarks demonstrate that VRQL establishes strong performance and outperforms representative baselines. Ablation studies suggest that these empirical gains are associated with pairing low action values with high geometric repulsion. Disrupting this learned assignment severely degrades policy optimization, whereas proper within state calibration and moderate guidance temperatures ensure stable learning. However, a primary limitation of this approach stems from its inherent reliance on learned critic esti-mates. In environments where the critic produces incorrect value orderings, the guidance mechanism can inadvertently repel optimal actions and misguide the policy constraint. Future work will inves-tigate integrating uncertainty estimation into the particle interaction field, allowing the algorithm to 9Preprint. ## A IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

This section describes the implementation of VRQL, including the network architectures, hyperpa-rameter settings, and benchmark environments used in the experiments. A.1 NETWORK ARCHITECTURES 

We parameterize the policy generator fθ and the critic ensemble {Qϕh }Hh=1 using multilayer per-ceptrons (MLPs). The actor and critics have separate parameters and are trained with Adam. 

Actor network ( fθ ). The actor consists of four hidden layers with 512 units each and GELU activations, without layer normalization. Its inputs are the state s and a Gaussian noise vector z ∼N (0 , I ) whose dimension equals the action dimension. The output is clipped coordinatewise to the action box A = [ −1, 1] d, giving xθ (s, z ) = Π A(fθ (s, z )) . Sampling z induces the policy πθ (· | s).

Critic networks ( Qϕh ). We use an ensemble of H = 2 critics. Each critic takes a state and an action as input and consists of four hidden layers with 512 units each, GELU activations, and layer normalization. The actor objective uses the mean of the online critic estimates, whereas Bellman targets use the minimum of the target critic estimates, as specified in Eqs. 14 and 2. By default, particle value guidance also uses the minimum of the target critic estimates. Target parameters are updated by Polyak averaging with coefficient ρ.A.2 HYPERPARAMETERS
