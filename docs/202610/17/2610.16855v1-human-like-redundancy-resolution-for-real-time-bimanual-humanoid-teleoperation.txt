# Human-Like Redundancy Resolution for Real-Time Bimanual Humanoid Teleoperation 

Chao Tan, Ming Shin Cindy Sung, Shuxian Liang †, Chaoqun Wan, Zhen Huang, Xian-Sheng Hua †

Institute of AI for Engineering, Tongji University 

Abstract — Real-time bimanual humanoid teleoperation re-quires accurate wrist tracking while keeping arm configurations predictable. Because a 7-DoF arm admits multiple elbow configurations for the same wrist pose, wrist-only tracking can cause elbow drift or abrupt configuration changes. Fixed anthropomorphic constraints can stabilize elbow posture, but they can also block the redundant adjustments needed near singular configurations. We propose a human-like redundancy-resolution framework that combines adaptive elbow guidance with singularity-robust wrist tracking. Shoulder–wrist geomet-ric retargeting generates robot-scale wrist targets. An adaptive elbow-offset criterion uses the offset direction to guide posture and scales this guidance by the offset magnitude, so the redun-dant degree of freedom is progressively released as the human arm straightens. A hierarchical velocity solver prioritizes wrist tracking and projects the adaptive elbow guidance into the wrist-task null space. In a fixed-wrist-target ablation, adaptive guidance reduces mean elbow-direction error by 54.4% com-pared with removing this guidance. In a workspace-boundary stress test, singularity-robust tracking reduces peak raw joint-velocity demand by 97.9% compared with the undamped pseudoinverse. Real-world bimanual tasks and cross-platform retargeting show applicability across robot morphologies. 

Project website: https://human-like-redundancy.github.io/ 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Real-time bimanual humanoid teleoperation transfers hu-man motion to robots for dexterous manipulation and demon-stration collection [1]–[4]. Accurate wrist tracking preserves the operator’s end-effector intent, while predictable arm con-figurations and smooth motion support effective execution. For a 7-degree-of-freedom arm, a six-dimensional wrist task generally leaves one redundant degree of freedom; resolving this redundancy selects the elbow configuration without changing the commanded wrist pose. Wrist-only inverse kinematics leaves this redundant degree of freedom unconstrained, which can cause elbow drift or abrupt configuration changes between nearby wrist targets (the top left part of Fig. 1). Anthropomorphic retarget-ing instead uses human keypoints, geometric redundancy parameters, or posture objectives to improve human–robot configuration consistency [5], [6], [10], [12], [14]. However, enforcing an anthropomorphic target rigidly can block the re-dundant adjustment needed near a robot singularity, degrad-ing wrist tracking or making the objectives infeasible (the bottom left part of Fig. 1). The strength of anthropomorphic guidance should also reflect the geometric reliability of the observed human posture.

Two distinct failure modes must therefore be addressed. First, when the human shoulder, elbow, and wrist approach collinearity, the elbow’s lateral offset from the shoulder–wrist axis approaches zero. Its normalized direction then becomes sensitive to measurement noise, so strongly enforcing that direction can produce unnecessary posture corrections from weak geometric evidence. Second, when the robot wrist Jaco-bian is poorly conditioned, modest task-space commands can produce large joint-velocity requests. Weakening unreliable elbow guidance addresses the first failure mode by releasing the redundant degree of freedom, but it does not regularize the primary wrist task. Conversely, singularity-robust wrist execution limits velocity amplification but does not select a human-like elbow posture. We therefore propose a human-like redundancy-resolution framework that combines geometry-adaptive elbow guid-ance with singularity-robust wrist tracking (the right part of Fig. 1). Shoulder–wrist geometric retargeting first generates robot-scale wrist targets. An adaptive elbow-offset anthro-pomorphic criterion uses the offset direction to specify the desired posture and the offset magnitude to measure the reliability of that direction. As the human arm approaches full extension, the criterion progressively weakens and then deactivates elbow guidance. A hierarchical velocity solver suppresses near-singular joint-velocity amplification for the primary wrist task and applies the adaptive posture guidance in its null space. Experiments evaluate elbow-posture con-sistency, wrist tracking, and near-singular velocity demand, while cross-platform retargeting and real-world bimanual tasks assess practical applicability. A. Bimanual Humanoid Teleoperation 

Recent bimanual teleoperation systems transfer operator hand motion to humanoid platforms via immersive visual feedback or wearable interfaces [3], [4], with whole-body extensions coordinating torso and legs in real time [1], [23], [24], [25] and cross-platform frameworks integrating sensing, retargeting, and execution [17], [22], [26]. End-effector retargeting preserves task-space intent but leaves the elbow in the robot null space [13], [18], while keypoint-based methods add shoulder and elbow landmarks for richer posture information [7], [14] yet degrade near full extension or self-occlusion. Our method uses the same human key-points for redundancy guidance, but adapts their influence according to the geometric observability of the elbow and preserves wrist tracking through task-priority control. 

B. Anthropomorphic Retargeting 

Geometric redundancy parameterizations [5], [8], closed-form solvers [6], and posture-constrained mappings [9], [10] address the elbow redundancy that remains after the wrist pose is fixed. Other approaches encode anthropomorphic behavior through monocular arm imitation [11], [12], hy-brid redundancy resolution [19], or object-aware posture selection [20], [21]. Arm-plane-normal matching [10] rep-resents anthropomorphic posture through a unit direction. Our AEAC couples directional alignment with the elbow’s perpendicular offset magnitude, making guidance strength dependent on human arm geometry. This extends direction-only matching into a geometry-adaptive redundancy objec-tive that regulates both posture alignment and its activation. 

C. Analytical Geometric Redundancy Solvers 

Manipulability measures [27], [29], [35] and redundancy-resolution frameworks [28], [31] quantify singularity prox-imity and exploit the null space for secondary objectives; for 7-DoF arms, the wrist task leaves one degree of freedom that can be controlled through null-space motion [5], [6]. Singularity-robust damping and task-priority mappings [8], [13], [30] improve numerical behavior near poorly condi-tioned configurations, while hierarchical formulations [33], [34] explicitly preserve primary task priorities. Our method follows this task-priority principle with a J-PARSE velocity mapping for the stacked wrist task and uses adaptive elbow guidance only in the resulting null space. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

We develop a real-time motion-retargeting and hierarchical velocity-solving framework for bimanual teleoperation with two 7-DoF humanoid arms. Given XR shoulder, elbow, and wrist keypoints together with controller poses, the system generates robot wrist targets through geometric retarget-ing, resolves arm redundancy with an adaptive elbow-offset anthropomorphic criterion, and computes joint commands using a hierarchical velocity solver with singularity-robust wrist tracking. As shown in Fig. 2, the method comprises four components: (i) Shoulder–Wrist Geometric Retargeting (SWGR, Section III-B), (ii) the adaptive elbow-offset anthro-pomorphic criterion (AEAC, Section III-C), (iii) singularity-robust wrist tracking (Section III-D), and (iv) the hierarchical velocity solver (Section III-E).

C. Adaptive Elbow-Offset Anthropomorphic Criterion 

The 7-DoF wrist task leaves one redundant degree of freedom per arm. The Adaptive Elbow-Offset Anthropomor-phic Criterion (AEAC, Fig. 2(II)) uses the human elbow observation to guide this redundancy while reducing its influence when the elbow direction is poorly observable. Fixed-weight constraints [8], [10] can amplify tracking noise near full extension, where the shoulder, elbow, and wrist become nearly collinear. AEAC separates the elbow evidence into a direction and a magnitude: the direction defines the anthropomorphic objective, and the magnitude determines how strongly that objective is used.
