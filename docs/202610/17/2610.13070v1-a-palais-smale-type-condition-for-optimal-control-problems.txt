Abstract 

We consider an optimal control problem with a general control constraint and a fixed initial state, for which convexity assumptions are absent. To establish the existence of optimal pairs, we introduce a control version of the Palais–Smale-type condition adapted from crit-ical point theory and prove that optimal pairs exist under this condition (see Theorem 2.1). Subsequently, we verify that a specific class of optimal control problems satisfies our Palais– Smale-type condition (see Proposition 2.1). Furthermore, as an application, we present an example (Example 3.1) and show that, to the best of our knowledge, no existing result can be applied to establish the existence of an optimal pair in this case (see Section 3.1.1). Example 3.2 shows that finitely many Pontryagin maximum principle candidates do not guarantee the existence of an optimal control. Finally, Example 3.3 demonstrates that the proposed Palais–Smale-type condition is sufficient, but not necessary, for the existence of optimal controls. 

Keywords: Optimal control; Palais–Smale-type condition; Existence of optimal pairs

1. Introduction 

In optimal control problems, the proof of existence of optimal pairs usually depends on certain convexity conditions. If the problem is not convex, a standard approach is to introduce an associated relaxed problem, prove the existence of optimal pairs for this relaxed problem, and subsequently use them to construct optimal pairs for the original problem. For example, in optimal control problems governed by ordinary differential equations, [5, 7, 8] considered cases with free terminal states, whereas [1, 9] treated problems with terminal state constraints. This list of such references is only partial. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, there exist seemingly simple optimal control problems—such arXiv:2610.13070v1 [math.OC] 9 Oct 2026 as Example 3.1—for which the existence of an optimal pair cannot be established using existing results, as discussed in Section 3.1.1. At a fundamental level, optimal control problems can be viewed as a special class of optimization problems. In variational methods and critical point theory, a central compact-ness assumption is the Palais–Smale (PS) condition. Roughly speaking, for a C1 functional 

Φ on an infinite-dimensional space Z, the (PS) condition at a level C ∈ R requires that every sequence {zk} ⊂ Z satisfying 

Φ( zk) → C, Φ′(zk) → 0,

admits a convergent subsequence in Z. This compactness condition prevents minimizing or critical sequences from escaping without converging. It is therefore widely used as a sufficient condition for proving the existence of critical points or minimizers; see, for example, [6, Theorem 4.4, p. 82]. Inspired by this viewpoint, in this paper we propose to adapt the (PS) condition frame-work to optimal control problems by introducing an appropriate Palais–Smale-type ((PS)-type) condition that ensures the existence of optimal pairs. This allows us to prove the existence of optimal pairs without imposing the usual convexity assumptions on the control system. For example, Vakhrameev [11] investigated optimal control prob-lems in which the terminal state is constrained to lie on a submanifold with corners. Under suitable conditions, such problems can be transformed into optimization problems defined on infinite-dimensional manifolds with corners, thereby allowing established critical point theory to be applied. However, this approach has limitations: transforming the original optimal control problem into an infinite-dimensional optimization problem requires several additional assumptions. Among them, three funda-mental conditions must be met: 1) there is no constraint on the controls; 2) the terminal state constraint must define a submanifold with corners; 3) this submanifold must be contained in the reachable set of the control system. Clearly, our problem (P ) generally does not satisfy these requirements. In this work, we adapt methodologies from critical point theory, specifically by directly applying Ekeland’s Variational Principle to the control problem (P). This necessitates introducing an appropriate control-theoretic version of the (PS)-type condition (see Defi-nition 2.1), which ensures the existence of optimal pairs. This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the main results in this work. Section 3 is devoted to examples. The proofs of our main results are given in Section 4.
