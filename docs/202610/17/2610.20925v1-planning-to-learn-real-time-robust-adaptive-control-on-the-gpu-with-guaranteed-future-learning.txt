# Planning to Learn: Real-Time Robust Adaptive Control on the GPU with Guaranteed Future Learning 

Anutam Srinivasan 1 Siddhant Panse 1 Glen Chou 2

Abstract — Robust adaptive control is vital for keeping robots safe while actively reducing uncertainty in unfamiliar settings. However, traditional methods are often conservative since they fail to account for guaranteed future parameter learning during planning and struggle to scale to high-dimensional systems. In this work, we present A-SLS, a learning-aware robust adap-tive model predictive control (RAMPC) framework based on system-level synthesis. A-SLS forecasts future parameter uncer-tainty and preserves correlations between process disturbances, parameter error, and estimate updates to construct tight, guaranteed reachable sets that account for future information gain. We also develop a GPU-accelerated A-SLS solver to enable real-time performance. Across nonlinear robotic systems, A-SLS reduces reachable-tube conservativeness, maintains real-time performance, and scales to whole-body control for aquadruped with 61 states, 8 uncertain parameters, and 12 con-trol inputs. We further demonstrate the approach on hardware, where a physical Crazyflie drone learns an uncertain dynamics parameter online to stabilize on the fly.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Robots operating in unstructured environments must act safely despite uncertainty in their dynamics and surround-ings. Robust control is one approach to achieve this, pro-viding worst-case guarantees by treating uncertainty as per-sistently adversarial, propagating reachable tubes over ad-missible uncertainty, and tightening constraints accordingly. While this is appropriate for irreducible aleatoric uncertainty, it can be overly conservative for epistemic uncertainty aris-ing from unknown but learnable physical parameters such as mass, friction, wind, or motor damage. Because this structured uncertainty can be reduced through interaction, robust adaptive control can become less conservative as parameter estimates improve online. As data is collected and parameter uncertainty shrinks, controllers can safely exploit the reduced uncertainty to improve performance. Moreover, because the rate of learning depends on how informative the chosen trajectory is, control actions can directly influence future parameter uncertainty, creating a tradeoff between performance, robustness, and information gathering. However, designing robust adaptive controllers for robotics remains an open problem, with two central challenges. First, synthesizing adaptive controllers while jointly reasoning about estimation error, aleatoric uncertainty, and parametric uncertainty is computationally expensive for long-horizon,

Second, many existing methods update pa-rameter estimates online but plan using the current parameter uncertainty set, incorporating newly learned information only 

reactively after measurements are collected. As a result, they cannot exploit guaranteed future uncertainty reduction during planning and may produce unnecessarily large reachable tubes even along informative trajectories. For example, a drone traversing an uncertain but learnable wind field should be able to plan knowing that it can measure, estimate, and compensate for the wind during execution. Ignoring this future learning can degrade performance or prevent task completion, while also leading to myopic planning that fails to seek informative trajectories that accelerate uncer-tainty reduction. Addressing these challenges requires jointly modeling the state and parameter-estimate dynamics while efficiently forecasting the worst-case learning guaranteed under all admissible uncertainties. In this work, we close these gaps with A-SLS, a scalable robust adaptive policy optimization framework that explicitly accounts for future learning during planning. Building on system-level synthesis (SLS) [1], we augment the physical state with the online parameter estimate so that estimator evolution becomes part of the closed-loop dynamics. We propagate bounded parameter uncertainty and parameter-estimate shifts over the planning horizon, while adaptive SLS responses preserve correlations among process disturbances, parameter error, and estimate updates to construct tighter reachable tubes. Crucially, this enables assured future learn-ing : for each candidate trajectory, we forecast the parameter uncertainty guaranteed to remain after future observations, allowing the optimizer to exploit worst-case future tube con-traction without assuming favorable measurements. Because informativeness depends on the trajectory, the same for-mulation can also favor motions that accelerate uncertainty reduction. Finally, the structured SLS policy class admits efficient parallel reachability and robust constraint tightening, enabling GPU-parallel optimization for long-horizon, high-dimensional nonlinear systems. Our contributions are:  

> •

We formulate learning-aware robust adaptive control as an SLS problem that augments the state with the online parameter estimate, returning policies that adapt to on-line data while remaining robust to residual uncertainty.  

> •

We derive reachable sets that preserve correlations be-tween disturbances, parameter error, and estimate shifts, enabling tightened robust tubes that account for and guarantee future learning. These information-sensitive tubes also enable planning that favors informative trajec-tories that reduce uncertainty and improve performance.  

> •

We develop a GPU-parallel solver that enables real-time long-horizon control on high-DoF nonlinear systems.  

> •

We evaluate safety, tube tightness, parameter learning, active information gathering, and scalability on high-dimensional nonlinear robotic systems and hardware. II. R ELATED WORK 

Classic robust adaptive control [2]–[6] establishes stability guarantees under disturbances and parametric uncertainty. Recent work extends these ideas to nonlinear and unmatched uncertainty using contraction theory [7]–[10] and L1-LPV control [11]. Other approaches develop SLS-based robust control under parametric uncertainty [12] and robust adap-tive control for linear systems [13]. While these methods provide strong stability guarantees, they generally do not optimize long-horizon constrained trajectories or anticipate how planned actions reduce uncertainty; contraction-based approaches also require challenging metric synthesis and online geodesic computation. In contrast, our method uses SLS for robust adaptive nonlinear trajectory optimization, coupling closed-loop reachability with guaranteed future learning and real-time GPU-parallel synthesis. Other methods incorporate adaptation into safety-critical control. Adaptive and robust-adaptive control barrier func-tions (CBFs) provide forward-invariance guarantees under parametric uncertainty [14]–[16], with later applications to robotics [17], [18]. Many reduce online control to small QPs or impose Lyapunov conditions within MPC. However, synthesizing adaptive CBFs/CLFs can be challenging, and these methods typically provide pointwise invariance or stability guarantees without explicitly planning trajectories to improve parameter learning. Our approach instead cou-ples closed-loop reachability and planning through shared uncertainty sources affecting physical states and future pa-rameter estimates, yielding tighter robust tubes while making information gathering part of the trajectory optimization. Robust adaptive model predictive control (RAMPC) com-bines online model learning with constrained planning. Early work used conservative constraint tightenings or separate robust safety and learned performance models [19], [20]. Set-membership identification was later integrated with robust MPC for linear [21] and nonlinear systems [22], [23], with extensions addressing convergence [24], safety cer-tification [25], L1 adaptation [26], contraction [27], and distributed control [28]. However, most methods exploit learning only after uncertainty updates are observed rather than anticipating guaranteed future estimator evolution, and can be computationally demanding online. Our adaptive SLS formulation instead jointly propagates parameter-estimation and disturbance dynamics, preserves their correlations for tighter tubes, and enables real-time GPU-parallel synthesis. Finally, dual and active-learning MPC recognize that control affects both task execution and future model qual-ity [29]. Persistent-excitation constraints can promote pa-rameter convergence in RAMPC [30], while [31] embeds predicted set-membership updates in linear MPC for infor-mative exploration. These methods closely align with our planning-to-learn objective. However, persistent excitation is an indirect proxy for task-relevant uncertainty reduction, while exact future set-membership reasoning can be expen-sive and difficult to scale. Our method instead optimizes worst-case parameter-estimation dynamics within a scalable SLS framework, exploiting assured future tube contraction while retaining robust nonlinear constraints and GPU-parallel computation for long-horizon, high-dimensional systems. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented A-SLS, a robust adaptive control frame-work that accounts for guaranteed future parameter learning while preserving correlations between process disturbances, parameter error, and estimate updates to construct tighter reachable tubes. Across trajectory optimization and RAMPC experiments, A-SLS reduces conservatism and tracking error, supports active information gathering, and achieves real-time performance on nonlinear robotic systems, including whole-body quadruped control. Hardware experiments fur-ther demonstrate online parameter learning and stabilization on a Crazyflie with an incorrect initial parameter estimate.
