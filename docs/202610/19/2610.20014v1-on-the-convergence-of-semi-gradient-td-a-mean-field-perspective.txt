## Abstract 

Semi-gradient temporal-difference learning is a core algorithmic component of rein-forcement learning, but bootstrapping with function approximation generally induces a non-conservative vector field and can lead to instability. We analyze this effect for two-layer neural networks in the mean-field regime and identify a simple stability mech-anism in which entropy-induced relaxation dominates the sensitivity of the moving bootstrap target. Under this condition, we prove exponential convergence to a unique fixed point and quantify its bias relative to the unregularized Bellman solution. We also show that the same mechanism extends to entropy-regularized off-policy control through soft Q-learning. 

## 1 Introduction 

Temporal-difference (TD) learning is one of the basic mechanisms for value estimation in reinforce-ment learning. Its semi-gradient form is particularly simple, updating the current prediction toward a bootstrapped target without differentiating through that target. This simplicity, however, also makes its dynamics fundamentally different from ordinary gradient descent. With function approximation, the expected semi-gradient update generally defines a non-conservative vector field, and the resulting dynamics can diverge or oscillate even in the absence of sampling noise (Baird, 1995; Gordon, 2000; Asadi et al., 2023). Understanding this instability becomes particularly challenging with neural-network function approx-imation, where the representation itself evolves during learning. Despite the practical importance of neural TD methods, convergence theory for semi-gradient TD beyond linear or near-initialization regimes remains limited. The mean-field regime provides a tractable framework for studying fea-ture learning in wide two-layer neural networks by describing training through the evolution of a distribution over parameters. Yet mean-field analyses of semi-gradient TD remain scarce. Zhang et al. (2020) establish a best-iterate guarantee in function value, but do not prove convergence of the learning dynamics themselves. Agazzi & Lu (2022) characterize the equilibrium structure of mean-field semi-gradient TD and show that a convergent trajectory induces the true value function under suitable assumptions, while their convergence guarantee is obtained in a lazy-training regime. Thus, convergence of nonlinear mean-field semi-gradient TD dynamics, and the mechanism that can stabilize them, remain poorly understood. In this work, we study noisy semi-gradient TD with two-layer neural networks in the mean-field regime. ## 5 Numerical Illustration 

We illustrate the effect of regularization on finite-width semi-gradient TD in Asterix and Breakout (MinAtar) using fixed pretrained policies. We compare unregularized TD, quadratic regularization, and quadratic regularization with Langevin noise. The quadratic-only baseline separates the effect of the confining regularization from the additional effect of Langevin noise. All methods use the same transition data and network initialization, so that the comparison isolates the effect of regularization. Experimental details are given in Appendix D. Figure 1 shows two regimes. On Asterix, the unregularized objective deteriorates after an initial decrease, while regularization suppresses this long-run increase. On Breakout, unregularized TD is already stable and the improvement is smaller. The additional effect of Langevin noise is modest at the scale considered here. The contrast between the two environments suggests that the benefit of regularization is most visible when the unregularized semi-gradient dynamics exhibit unfavorable long-run behavior. These experiments provide a qualitative illustration of the stability mechanism. We do not estimate ρ or Lp in these environments, so they are not a direct verification of the sufficient condition ρτ > L p.

## 6 Conclusion 

We studied noisy semi-gradient TD with two-layer neural networks in the mean-field regime and identified a stability mechanism in which relaxation toward the frozen-target minimizer dominates the sensitivity of the moving bootstrap target. Under this condition, we proved exponential convergence to a unique self-consistent equilibrium and quantified its error relative to the unregularized value function. We also showed that the same mechanism extends to entropy-regularized off-policy soft Q-learning. 10 Scope and limitations. Our analysis focuses on the population continuous-time dynamics in order to isolate the stability mechanism caused by the moving bootstrap target. We do not provide finite-width, time-discretization, or stochastic-gradient guarantees in this work. Related propagation-of-chaos results for mean-field TD (Agazzi & Lu, 2022) and finite-particle and discretization analyses for mean-field Langevin dynamics (Suzuki et al., 2023) provide natural starting points for such extensions. Establishing their quantitative counterparts for the present semi-gradient dynamics is left for future work.

Environments and data collection. We consider policy evaluation on Asterix and Break-out in MinAtar. For each environment, we use the pretrained PPO checkpoint dis-tributed with the gymnax-blines repository ( agents/Asterix-MinAtar/ppo.pkl and 

agents/Breakout-MinAtar/ppo.pkl , respectively), and keep the policy fixed throughout the experiment. The repository provides the PPO training pipeline together with the corresponding pretrained checkpoints in agents/<env_name>/ppo.pkl . Using these fixed stochastic policies, we collect a transition pool by running 2048 parallel environments for 128 steps, yielding 262 ,144 

transitions per environment. Terminal transitions use the final observation before the environment reset. From each pool, we use a fixed set of 16 ,384 transitions for training and a disjoint set of 

65 ,536 transitions for evaluation. The same data split is used for all methods. These finite-rollout distributions are used only for the numerical illustration and are not intended to exactly represent a stationary state distribution.
