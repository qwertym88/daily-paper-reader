## ABSTRACT 

In reinforcement learning, advantage-based value-learning methods, unlike stan-dard Q-learning, share information across actions by decomposing the action-value function into a state-dependent value component and action-specific ad-vantages. While this mechanism has proven effective in both tabular and deep reinforcement learning, its theoretical understanding remains incomplete. We in-troduce V-SHARE , an advantage-based value-learning algorithm that combines cross-action value sharing with an importance-weighted Bellman-residual correc-tion. To our knowledge, we provide the first complete theoretical characterization of an advantage-based algorithm, including both asymptotic convergence and non-asymptotic finite-sample guarantees. We identify a regime in which V-SHARE 

mitigates the rare-action bottleneck of asynchronous Q-learning and uncover a contraction–variance tradeoff between stronger deterministic contraction and in-creased importance-weighting noise. Motivated by this tradeoff, we develop de-creasing residual-preconditioning schedules that combine faster early contraction with lower asymptotic noise. We further develop a two-timescale analysis that removes the single-timescale residual-weight threshold while preserving conver-gence guarantees. Experiments on discrete Markov decision processes (MDPs) and deep reinforcement-learning benchmarks corroborate our results. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) (Sutton & Barto, 1998) algorithms learn through trial and error: they try actions, receive a reward in return indicating the quality of that action and, over time, try to learn an optimal mapping from the current state to the best action to take, which we call the policy. Q-learning (Watkins & Dayan, 1992) is one of the canonical RL algorithms that learns a Q-function as an intermediate object. The Q-function estimates, for every state-action pair, the long-term return, i.e., the sum of received rewards over a trajectory, obtained by taking that action in that state and be-having optimally thereafter. If the state and action spaces are finite, we can explicitly store estimated action-values for each state-action pair. In the standard asynchronous setting, the learner observes one transition at a time and updates only the estimate for the sampled state-action pair. This simple coordinate-wise structure, together with the contraction of the Bellman optimality operator, under-lies the classical convergence theory of Q-learning (Tsitsiklis, 1994; Jaakkola et al., 1993). The same basic principle also proved remarkably successful with function approximation through Deep Q-Networks (DQN) (Mnih et al., 2015), showing that Q-learning can scale to high-dimensional control problems. With function approximation, however, Q-learning becomes more susceptible to instability, and estimating the full action-value of every action separately can be inefficient when much of the in-formation is shared. These observations motivated the dueling architecture of Wang et al. (2016), which represents the Q-function as the sum of a state-value function that assigns a value to every state defined by the long-term return obtained by behaving optimally after visiting said state, and an 1arXiv:2610.21448v1 [cs.LG] 15 Oct 2026 action-dependent advantage function learned separately. Intuitively, rather than repeatedly estimat-ing the full value of every action, the learner can first estimate a potentially large value component common to all actions and then learn the comparatively smaller action-dependent advantage com-ponents. This value-advantage perspective has subsequently proved useful both empirically and theoretically. Advantage-based architectures improve learning performance in deep reinforcement learning (Wang et al., 2016; Nagarajan et al., 2026), while recent tabular advantage-based algo-rithms exhibit accelerated learning relative to standard Q-learning (Tang et al., 2023; Daley et al., 2025; Lee, 2026). The algorithmic idea itself dates back to Advantage Updating (Baird, 1993), and the central mech-anism of all these approaches is value sharing : updating the state-value component simultaneously changes the represented values of all actions at the state being updated, allowing information ob-tained from one action to propagate immediately to the others. Value sharing, however, comes with a theoretical cost. In Q-learning, each state-action value is up-dated through its own temporal-difference error, which leads to a simple contraction-based analysis. Advantage-based methods couple the action-values within a state through a shared value component, and this coupling breaks that standard argument. Existing analyses therefore rely on stronger struc-tural or sampling assumptions, such as synchronous updates over all state-action pairs, to establish convergence in the tabular setting (Lee, 2026; Tang et al., 2023). This raises a natural question: 

Can we retain the acceleration benefits of cross-action value sharing while recovering a tractable convergence theory and finite-sample guarantees under stochastic sampling? 

Contributions. We answer this question through V-SHARE , an advantage-based value-learning algorithm that augments cross-action value sharing with a tunable, importance-weighted Bellman-residual correction parametrized by β. This leads to a residual preconditioning view of value shar-ing, showing when cross-action coupling accelerates learning, when it breaks the usual contraction argument, and how to recover convergence guarantees. 1. We show that the iterates generated by V-SHARE are stable and converge almost surely to the optimal action-value function Q∗ under Markovian sampling and suitable parameter choices. 2. We derive finite-time guarantees using constant- and diminishing-stepsize schedules. The result-ing rates quantify a contraction-variance tradeoff and yield the first finite-time guarantees of an advantage-based algorithm under Markovian sampling. We show how decreasing-β and decay-to-zero stepsize schedules retain strong early contraction while reducing terminal stochastic error. 3. We also analyze a two-timescale formulation that removes the single-timescale large-β require-ment and show convergence guarantees for all β > 0. The analysis requires extending single-timescale Markovian stochastic approximation to vanishing drift perturbations, yielding results that may be of independent interest. 4. We evaluate V-SHARE and its variants on tabular MDPs, where the experiments expose the pre-dicted effects of value sharing. We additionally extend V-SHARE to deep reinforcement learning and compare it with recent advantage-based methods on MinAtar (Young & Tian, 2019) and Classic Control tasks. 

Related work. Since the number of Q-learning variants and analyses is vast, we focus here only on the key theoretical results, major practical milestones, and methods that explicitly learn advantages. The term advantage-based is used in several different senses in reinforcement learning. Policy-gradient and actor–critic methods such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) use advantage estimates to construct policy updates, often through generalized advantage estimation (Schulman et al., 2015). However, the advantage is used primarily as an estimator of relative action quality to train the policy. Our focus is instead on advantage-based value learning : algorithms that explicitly represent action values through a shared state-value component and action-specific advantages, so that updating the value component propagates information across actions. This viewpoint dates back to Advantage Updating (Baird, 1993) and underlies the dueling architecture of Wang et al. (2016). Recent work has revisited this value–advantage decomposition in both tabular and deep RL (Tang et al., 2023; Daley et al., 2025; Nagarajan et al., 2026). These methods exploit the shared value com-ponent to accelerate learning, while Lee (2026) studies the dynamics of tabular dueling Q-learning through spectral arguments. Mustafin et al. (2025) introduces the RB-S algorithm, which uses advantage-preserving transformations to recover an optimal policy. Our approach is complemen-tary: we modify the coupled value-sharing dynamics through residual preconditioning and analyze 2the resulting algorithm under Markovian sampling, obtaining both asymptotic and finite-time guar-antees. Technically, our analysis builds on the ODE method for stochastic approximation (Borkar & Meyn, 2000; Liu et al., 2025) and the generalized Moreau-envelope framework for contractive stochastic approximation (Chen et al., 2020; 2024). For brevity, we henceforth refer to advantage-based value-learning algorithms as advantage-based algorithms .

This yields the same scalar Lyapunov recursion as in the single-timescale analysis, up to modified constants. Unrolling the recursion gives the desired rate.

## 5 DISCUSSION AND FUTURE WORK 

Key take-aways. We introduce V-SHARE , an advantage-based RL algorithm, and provide the first asymptotic and non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for an advantage-based method un-der Markovian sampling. Our results suggest several practical principles for advantage-based value learning. First , they support separating the shared state-value component from action-specific ad-vantages. Second , the residual weight β controls how aggressively the algorithm corrects infre-quently sampled actions, motivating stronger advantage updates early and weaker ones later. Third ,when low-advantage actions are also sampled infrequently, applying a higher effective learning rate to them can improve learning. Finally , the two-timescale analysis supports using a larger effective learning rate for advantages than for state values in tabular MDPs, while our deep RL experiments suggest that reducing the advantage learning rate can improve stability by limiting noise. 9Future work. Several directions remain open. First, while there is supporting intuition, our analysis does not yet explain the empirical acceleration of dueling Q-learning over standard Q-learning. A re-lated question is whether value sharing can yield sharper problem-dependent or sample-complexity guarantees. Second, extending the analysis of V-SHARE to function approximation is an important direction. Finally, our analysis assumes a fixed behavior policy; extending it to iterate-dependent sampling policies, such as ϵ-greedy exploration, is another natural direction. AI USE STATEMENT

F.1 DISCRETE MDP We provide further experiments of V-SHARE and it’s variants in various settings in Figure 2. Fig-ure 2(b) reveals an interesting separation between the roles of β and the timescale ratio r := αV /α A.Empirically, β largely determines the terminal noise floor, whereas r primarily controls how rapidly the algorithm approaches that floor. This suggests a potentially useful two-timescale design princi-ple: the advantage stepsize may determine the accuracy ultimately reached, while the relative value stepsize controls the transient speed. A natural direction for future work is to determine whether this separation can be formalized into sharper sample-complexity guarantees, with αA (or β) controlling the attainable ϵ-accuracy and αV controlling how quickly that accuracy is reached.
