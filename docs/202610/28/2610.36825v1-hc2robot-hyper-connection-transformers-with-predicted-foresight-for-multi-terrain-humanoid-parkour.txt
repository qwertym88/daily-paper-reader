# HC2Robot: Hyper-Connection Transformers with Predicted Foresight for Multi-Terrain Humanoid Parkour 

Wenhao Xu ∗, Yanheng Mai ∗, Yifei Fu ∗, Nan Zheng, Yanbo Zhou, Zirui Huang, Mingsheng Ling, Xinjue Wang, and Renjing Xu †

Abstract —Humanoid parkour requires robots to reason about future footholds and whole-body motions over highly discontin-uous terrain. We revisit the information asymmetry in asym-metric actor-critic learning for perceptive humanoid locomotion: the critic exploits privileged simulator signals during training, whereas the deployable actor operates solely from onboard perception. We argue that the key is not exposing privileged information to the actor, but learning a deployable approximation of it to narrow the information gap between training and deployment. We present HC2Robot, a deployable perceptive humanoid parkour framework consisting of a Predicted Foresight Module (PFM) and a Hyper-Connection (HC) Transformer. PFM predicts from deployable observations the policy-conditioned timing, location, and support quality of each foot’s next touch-down event, converting privileged supervision into a deployable latent representation that provides the actor with predicted touchdown information without privileged runtime sensing. The HC Transformer further enhances temporal reasoning through learnable multi-stream routing, while a future motion prediction head provides short-horizon motion priors for regularization. Across stairs, gaps, high platforms, and slopes, HC2Robot achieves a mean simulated traversal success rate of 97.60% and consistently higher safe foothold rates than strong perceptive locomotion baselines, supporting improved traversal performance and geometric foothold support under deployable sensing. 

Index Terms —Perceptive locomotion, predicted foresight, hyper-connection, humanoid parkour. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# HUMANOID parkour demands traversal of stairs, gaps, platforms, and slopes while maintaining dynamically feasible whole-body motion [1], [2]. Success requires the controller to coordinate perception, contact timing, and body motion well before each foothold is realized. While massively parallel reinforcement learning has enabled robust locomo-tion [3], [4], proprioception-only policies lack foresight of upcoming terrain discontinuities [5], [6]. Depth-conditioned perception extends this horizon with geometric priors [7], [8], enabling scalable perceptive parkour over challenging terrain [2], [9]. Two challenges remain for deployable perceptive parkour. First, temporal representations must effectively integrate pro-prioceptive and depth-conditioned information. Conventional

Second, simulator-derived contact and support signals enhance learning but are unavailable at deployment [5], [15], [16]. We train an auxiliary estimator with simulator-derived labels to make task-relevant predictions available to the actor. HC2Robot addresses these challenges through two com-plementary mechanisms. A Hyper-Connection (HC) Trans-former [17] replaces the conventional single residual pathway with learnable multi-stream aggregation and mixing, enabling richer cross-layer information exchange while evaluating each attention and feed-forward sublayer only once. A Predicted Foresight Module (PFM), trained with asymmetric actor–critic learning [15], [18], forecasts each foot’s next touchdown event—its time-to-contact, touchdown location, and support quality—from deployable actor features and fuses the forecast into the policy. Additionally, an auxiliary motion predictor adopted from ParkourFormer [19] provides two-step future AMP states for motion regularization [20]. All prediction pathways remain deployment-compatible, using simulator in-formation solely as training supervision. We evaluate HC2Robot on stairs, gaps, high platforms, and slopes; the results demonstrate that combining HC fea-ture reuse with PFM foothold foresight consistently improves traversal success and safe foothold rate over strong baselines. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Perceptive Locomotion and Humanoid Parkour 

Massively parallel reinforcement learning supports robust blind locomotion [3], [4], while depth- and vision-conditioned policies extend it to terrain that proprioception alone cannot resolve [5]–[7]. Perceptive parkour has since been demon-strated on quadrupeds [8], [21], [22] and humanoids [1], with deployable sequence control and multimodal terrain fusion becoming common design choices [2], [9], [12]–[14], [23]– [25]. HC2Robot adopts this deployable-perception setting but focuses on using a predicted, task-relevant foothold event to narrow the training–deployment information gap; its HC backbone and PFM complement these locomotion and parkour systems. 

B. Privileged-State Methods and Learning Paradigms 

Asymmetric actor–critic learning separates privileged critic inputs from the deployable policy [15], [18]. Lee et al. [5] and RMA [16] use deployable observations to support terrain adaptation or latent dynamics estimation, while concurrent estimator–controller training learns hidden robot quantities jointly [26]; teacher–student and latent-scaffold methods trans-fer privileged actions or representations [27]–[30]. These ap-proaches estimate current state/dynamics or imitate a privi-leged policy, whereas privileged-state rewards only shape op-timization and do not expose a deployable representation. PFM instead uses simulator descriptors solely as training labels: from proprioception and depth, it predicts the next policy-conditioned touchdown time, position, support attributes, and uncertainty, then fuses that event forecast without privileged actor inputs or teacher actions. 

C. Foothold Reasoning, Predictive Supervision, and Motion Priors 

Terrain-aware perception, terrain imagination, and internal predictive models improve representations of terrain or hidden dynamics [6], [31], [32]; their targets are not the next contact event. TAGA [33] predicts a task-relevant terrain region with active gaze, while PFM predicts the next touchdown time, location, support attributes, and uncertainty. ParkourFormer [19] combines predictive supervision with humanoid sequence modeling, whereas PFM uses foothold-specific labels and a censoring-aware event objective. All variants use the same base deployable observations, critic-side privileged inputs, training budget, and evaluation protocol. control periods, instead of at the next touchdown, and omits the event-time loss and event-time input. The offset h0 is fixed across terrains and random seeds. We evaluate physical realizations of the training terrain classes and mixed courses combining stairs, gaps, and slopes at three difficulty levels. Single-terrain trials test sim-to-real transfer; mixed courses test compositional generalization to terrain arrangements and transitions not encountered during training, rather than new terrain primitives. Each setting uses 20 trials under the simulation failure criteria; Table VII re-ports complete-trial success rates, and Fig. 7 shows traversal examples. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented HC2Robot, combining HC multi-stream rout-ing and PFM touchdown-event foresight under asymmetric actor–critic training. PFM learns from simulator supervision to predict each foot’s next touchdown timing, location, and sup-port quality, while deployment uses only proprioception and depth. Held-out evaluation quantifies touchdown timing, po-sition, and support-ratio errors. Across four simulated terrain classes, HC2Robot achieves mean SR/SFR of 97.60%/97.94%, outperforming the evaluated baselines. Ablations assess PFM, HC, and the motion prior; Auxiliary-only supports the benefit of supervised touchdown-event features beyond shared one-step kinematic predictions. The fixed-horizon comparison eval-uates the complete event-based design against a fixed-offset, no-event-time alternative.
