# Local Koopman modes of saddle-point diffusions: exact singular functions and a data-driven estimator 

Xuehao Ding 1

> 1

Flatiron Institute, Simons Foundation 

Coherent sets—regions of phase space that resist mixing—organize transport in stochastic dynam-ical systems and are encoded in the leading singular functions of the stochastic Koopman operator. These are typically computed numerically and globally; a local, analytic theory near a fixed point has been missing, although fixed points and the transitions between them organize the flow. We supply the local theory for a saddle, the transition state through which probability flows between basins. Naively the construction fails—probability escapes along unstable directions, the invariant measure is non-normalizable, the singular functions leave L2, and singular values exceed one. We show that conditioning on non-escape (a Doob’s h-transform) repairs this; that the required h is the unique stabilizing solution of an algebraic Riccati equation; and that the resulting slow modes decay at the escape rate along the unstable manifold. We obtain these modes in closed form—products of Hermite polynomials in canonical coordinates times the h-function—and use their unitary struc-ture to derive a data-driven estimator: canonical correlation analysis on conditioned trajectories. When a single unstable direction dominates, the leading mode is the reaction coordinate, locally recovering the stable manifold and committor of transition-path theory. The construction connects coherent sets to transition-path theory, to the stabilizing Riccati solution of linear control, and to the time-reversed dynamics of generative diffusion models. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

A nonlinear dynamical system is organized by its fixed points and by the transitions between them: trajecto-ries dwell near stable equilibria and cross between them through the neighborhoods of unstable equilibria [1, 2]. Local behavior around fixed points therefore controls the global dynamics, and characterizing it is a natural route to understanding the system. For deterministic dynamics, this local analysis is standard—the Jacobian’s eigenvectors give normal modes and its eigenvalues give growth rates. For stochastic dynamics one would like the analogous local theory of the slow, collective modes that govern how probability is transported through the neighborhood. Those modes are the singular functions of the stochas-tic Koopman operator whose singular values lie close to one, the spectral objects underlying coherent sets [3–5]: subsets of phase space that remain dynamically coher-ent rather than dispersing. At present, coherent sets are studied almost exclusively numerically and globally : one discretizes a transfer or Koopman operator over the en-tire phase space of a nonlinear system—using Ulam-type discretizations [6, 7], finite elements [8], extended dy-namic mode decomposition [9], or diffusion maps [10]— and reads off a partition [3, 5]. This yields neither a closed form nor a local law : no statement of what the coherent modes look like near a given fixed point. The present paper supplies that missing local, analytic de-scription. A stable fixed point (a sink) linearizes to a con-tracting Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process: an iso-lated metastable well with no transport through it. The dynamically interesting fixed points are saddles —hyperbolic points with at least one unstable direction. A saddle is a transition state: the bottleneck through which trajectories pass between basins, where reaction coordi-nates and committor functions are defined [11–13]. Its linearization is a continuous OU process whose drift ma-trix has eigenvalues of both signs, and this is the regime we solve. The breakdown is easy to see. When the drift matrix has unstable eigenvalues, there is no normalizable sta-tionary measure: probability escapes along the unstable manifold. As a consequence, the naive extension of sin-gular functions from stable fixed points to saddles leads to non-integrable functions and singular values greater than one [14] (Fig. 2b). This breaks the singular-value problem at its foundation. The singular value represents the correlation coefficient between the left singular func-tion evaluated at the initial time and the right singular function evaluated at time τ . In the coherent-set picture, where these functions approximate indicators of regions, this correlation is essentially the fraction of trajectories that begin in the coherent set and remain there after time 

τ —a survival probability, which cannot exceed one. The breakdown is not merely analytic: the data-driven route fails in tandem, because the empirical covariances that any CCA-type estimator [5, 15, 16] relies on diverge along the unstable directions as the trajectories escape, so the correlation matrix has no finite limit and the canonical problem is ill-posed on the raw data (Fig. 2b). Any cor-rect theory of the saddle case must first restore a proper Hilbert space. Our resolution, and the conceptual core of the paper, is to condition the dynamics: restricting to trajectories that have not yet escaped is a Doob’s h-transform [17] that stabilizes the drift and makes the slow modes well defined. We show (i) that the required conditioning is the unique stabilizing solution of an algebraic Riccati equation [18], selected automatically as the attractor of a Riccati flow (Sec. III); (ii) that the conditioned slow arXiv:2610.35627v1 [math.DS] 26 Oct 2026 2modes admit closed form whose decay rate is the escape rate along the unstable manifold, and that multiplica-tion by h is a unitary map relating them to ordinary Hermite modes (Sec. IV); (iii) that this unitary struc-ture yields a data-driven estimator—canonical correla-tion analysis on conditioned trajectories—which recovers the analytic modes exactly (Sec. V); (iv) that in the spe-cial case with a single slow unstable mode, the leading predictive direction converges to the unstable left eigen-vector, justifying the reaction coordinate in classical the-ories (Sec. VI); and (v) that the construction establishes bridges to transition-path theory, stochastic thermody-namics, linear control, and diffusion-model time reversal (Sec. VII).

> V. DATA-DRIVEN ESTIMATOR A. Koopman singular functions and canonical correlation analysis

The mainstream data-driven methods for approximat-ing transfer operators from trajectory data—extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) [9], the varia-tional approach for Markov processes (VAMP) [16], and other variants—are, at their core, correlation methods: they lift the state through a dictionary of observables and solve a generalized eigenproblem of empirical instan-taneous and time-lagged covariance matrices. When the object sought are the leading singular functions rather than eigenfunctions, this reduction is exactly a canoni-cal correlation analysis (CCA) between the observables at the initial and final times [5, 16]. T over which the estimate is unbiased before the variance penalty dominates; one selects T at the short end of the plateau.

> VII. DISCUSSION AND CONNECTIONS

Transition-path theory. A saddle is a transition state; its unstable manifold is the linearized reaction coordi-nate, and the conditioned process is the local linear the-ory of the reactive ensemble. When the saddle has a single unstable direction whose escape is the slowest pro-cess in the system—the classical rare-event regime of Kramers’ and Eyring’s rate theory [11, 12]—the leading singular function is aligned with the linear committor di-rection (Sec. VI). 

Linear control and stochastic realization. The stabi-lization solution is the attractor of an algebraic Riccati flow [18], and the eigenvalue reflection of Proposition 1 is familiar from acausal stochastic realization [23, 32]. The agreement is not only formal: eA is the optimal closed-loop generator of the linear-quadratic regulator, and the same solution is selected there because the regulated dy-namics must not diverge—the control-theoretic counter-part of requiring the conditioned process not to escape. Canonical correlations between past and future of a sta-ble stochastic system are likewise classical [23, 32]; what is new here is the escaping case, where no invariant prob-ability measure exists, and the correlation problem must 9first be repaired by conditioning. 

Stochastic thermodynamics. The selective time rever-sal of Proposition 1 reverses the time-irreversible part of the drift only on the unstable manifold, flipping the entropy-producing arrow on the escaping directions while leaving the recurrent ones untouched—a subspace-resolved statement about dissipation. 

Generative diffusion models. The all-unstable case, Eq. (15), is exactly the reverse-time score-based SDE of generative sampling [21, 22]. It is the extreme point of a family whose interior is the saddle, with conditioning acting only on the escaping subspace: reverse-time gener-ative dynamics and transport through a transition state are two ends of one construction. 

Escape rate and survival. The decay e− Tr(Λ +)τ sets the rate at which coherence is lost, and ( L†, h, − Tr(Λ +)) is the principal eigen-triple of a killed (conditioned) semi-group: h is the survival function, small where escape is imminent, and ρ eA = hν is the stationary law of the asso-ciated Q-process, with ν its dual eigenfunction living on the unstable side [33]. The same mechanism underlies the metastable spectra of open systems more broadly: a de-cay rate becomes visible only once an invariant probabil-ity measure is given up in favor of a survival-weighted or otherwise non-normalizable reference, as in the Pollicott– Ruelle resonances of chaotic flows and the Gamow states of quantum scattering [34, 35]. Here that role is played by the reference weight eρ0 itself. 

Limitations. Real eigenvalues are assumed for clar-ity only; the complex case extends by flipping conjugate pairs together (Appendix S4 D). Timescale separation is needed for the committor correspondence, not for the closed forms. Linearity is a genuine restriction: the esti-mator applies to nonlinear systems only within a neigh-borhood whose radius must be established empirically, and its variance grows with the escape rate, limiting the usable horizons. With several unstable directions, the flip preserves the unstable left subspace but distinguishes no direction within it, leaving open what plays the role of the reaction coordinate. The framework is local by design, complementing global numerical coherent-set computa-tions.
