# Abstract 

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms have been observed to lose the ability to adaptively learn from new data, a phenomenon termed Loss of Plasticity (LoP). While many metrics have been shown to correlate with it, the causal mechanisms and the geometric conditions from which it can arise have not been clearly identified. We analyze LoP in Proximal Policy Optimization with isotropic Gaussian policies, the state-of-the-art formula-tion for many continuous action spaces in RL. We re-port multiple metrics which characterize the effect that the policy standard deviation σ has on LoP, and we also suggest related explanations for the effectiveness of widely adopted LoP mitigation techniques. We ob-serve a significant effect of σ on the extent of LoP, and accompany this with both theoretical and experimen-tal supporting arguments. Our experimental validation consists of LoP benchmark evaluations against standard baselines as well as real-world policy deployment on a 7-DoF robotic arm. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

In the last decade, reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved impressive results in a broad set of domains, ranging from natural language processing [34, 13] to robotics [33, 14]. A popular class of RL algorithms are policy gradient (PG) methods, originating with REINFORCE [48] and achieving improved success rates by integrating natural gradients [2, 3, 23]. A relevant RL subclass are on-policy gradient methods, in which the samples used for training are obtained by collecting rollouts with the current policy. The development of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [43] represents a landmark in this field, due to its relative simplicity, stability, and sample efficiency. PPO is used to solve complex robotics tasks [18, 39, 28] and is considered the state-of-the-art technique for on-policy RL. To

model problems in which the agent acts in a continuous action space, a typical approach is to adopt Gaussian policies [6, 37], even though alternative exploration strategies, such as Policy Gradients with Parameter-based Exploration (PGPE) [44] or natural evolution strategies [47, 40] have also been proposed. Despite its success, PPO is not well studied theoret-ically, and its use is accompanied by a proliferation of heuristics [11, 4, 17] and known technical issues, such as the premature collapse of the Gaussian policy’s stan-dard deviation σ [19]. In fact, most of the theoretical research focuses on vanilla PG methods such as REIN-FORCE, either deriving the optimal learning rate and batch size for safe PG [38, 36, 35], or showing that PG’s gradients with Gaussian policies suffer from increased variance as σ → 0 [49]. However, results for PPO are very limited, with [16] only informally mentioning that gradients scale inversely with respect to σ, yielding large updates and instability. Another issue observed in deep RL is the diminished ability of neural networks to learn new objectives after being trained, commonly referred to as Loss of Plasticity (LoP). Indeed, [9, 8] observe that PPO asymptotically collapses and that this process is accelerated under non-stationarity. Some explanations include the lack of fea-ture representation richness [20, 26, 30], the sharpening of the loss landscape [31, 29], or dormant neurons [45]. Additionally, [33, 32] study gradient norms correlated with this phenomenon. Finally, [22] studies LoP in PPO specifically, but only focuses on discrete actions. To mitigate LoP, multiple strategies have been pro-posed. [31, 5, 10, 8] show that tweaking the Adam optimizer [24], either by setting the first and second moment coefficients β1 and β2 to the same value, or by partially resetting the optimizer parameters, is ef-fective in reducing LoP. Furthermore, [46] shows that increasing the strength of entropy regularization under non-stationarity is effective in improving task adapta-tion. Finally, [21] proposes formalized mathematical con-ditions that could explain LoP, showing that there exist 

LoP manifolds in which the weights of a neural network 1arXiv:2610.36309v1 [cs.LG] 26 Oct 2026 would be trapped after entering, with any gradient de-scent method regardless of the objective. 

Contributions While LoP has been thoroughly stud-ied in recent years, there is no consensus on its causes. Furthermore, for many of the proposed causes, there ex-ist counter-examples. For this reason, we focus on a specific setting, PPO with Gaussian policies, and show formally and experimentally a novel link between the policy’s standard deviation σ and LoP. In summary, our contributions are: 

• The derivation of bounds linking multiple LoP metrics to σ → 0, formally proving and numerically verifying that under mild integrability assumptions the gradient norm and the Hessian sharpness diverge, while the Hessian approximate rank collapses, as σ → 0; 

• Experiments on an established benchmark showing that σ is related to the observed LoP phenomenon and that the effectiveness of existing techniques coun-teracting LoP is explained by their indirect influence on σ;

• Task-adaptation experiments with real-world robotic deployment demonstrating that our results also apply to a complex scenario with a continuous action space.

Loss of Plasticity The phenomenon of Loss of Plas-ticity (LoP) is defined as the observed diminished abil-ity of neural networks to fit new objectives after being trained on a different goal. We propose this parameter as one of the main contributors to the observed LoP.

Given the relationship between step size and sharpness, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3 would suggest the follow-ing heuristic scaling of the learning rate: ηt = η0 σ2

Interestingly, this is the same scaling proposed in Safe Policy Gradi-ents [35]. However, our results formally motivate scal-ing by ∥¯g(B)∥, which was originally proposed simply as a heuristic. We also extend the results to the complex loss structure of PPO. An additional degree of novelty in our derivation is the use of a fundamentally different approach, based on geometric attributes of the PPO loss landscape. Moreover, since Theorem 3.1 implies that the gradi-ent norm diverges as σ → 0, this motivates why setting 

β1 = β2 in Adam, instead of the conventional values of 

β1 = 0 .99 and β2 = 0 .999, reduces the step-size [8]. In fact, the ratio between β1 and β2 also determines how much weight is given to the latest gradient compared to the previous ones. Since the gradient norm diverges, this implies that increasing β1/β 2 makes the update larger in magnitude. Corollary 3.3 complements these consid-erations, motivating why reducing the step size should mitigate, but not eliminate, the asymptotic collapse. In fact, smaller steps are preferable under increasing sharp-ness conditions. However, step-size reduction is not di-rectly related to the sharpness itself and does not pre-vent collapse. Moreover, our results explain the success of methods that normalize updates such as Adam or RMSProp. Indeed, these approaches avoid effective step size blow-up during optimization, provided that σ col-lapses smoothly enough. Finally, our work motivates the findings of [4] that, when using RMSProp and changing the momentum coefficient from 0 to 0.9, the learning rate needs to be reduced. If the gradient norms were to go to 0, which normally occurs during optimization, a learning rate increase should instead have been neces-sary. In conclusion, our results provide insights into the learning dynamics of PPO, formally explaining previous 4heuristics.

## 4.2 The unreported role of σ in a com-mon benchmark 

In this experiment, we explore whether σ plays a role in the effectiveness of state-of-the-art LoP mitigation tech-niques, and if it can explain asymptotic PPO collapse. We do so using the Slippery Ant environment, which was popularized by [9, 8] and is generally acknowledged as a benchmark for LoP in PPO [27, 1, 25]. It is based on the MuJoCo Ant environment 3, with the twist that every 2M timesteps the friction coefficient between the robot and the ground is randomized within the range [0.02, 2.00], so that the environment is non-stationary. The main result reported by [8] is that regular PPO col-lapses in this setting, whereas tuning Adam such that 

β1 = β2 = 0 .99 delays the collapse, and adding L2 regularization prevents it. However, it underperforms the Continual Backpropagation (CBP) technique [8], in which a low percentage of weights with low utilization scores are randomly reinitialized.

Results Figure 3 demonstrates how the analyzed tech-niques implicitly affect σ and how its collapse is associ-ated with the reward collapse. [8] shows that Standard PPO collapses after 4 million iterations, tuning Adam is only able to delay this collapse, and L2 and L2+CBP show no collapse. We include these results in Figure 3a. Interestingly, as shown in Figure 3b, the latter two tech-niques are also the ones showing stronger σ regulariza-tion, supporting our theoretical insights. Figure 4a con-tains the results of Experiment 1. Reducing the learning rate, according to the sharpness increase proven in The-orem 3.2, is sufficient for avoiding the asymptotic col-lapse. However, only using an adaptive learning rate in Standard PPO does not recover the ability to adapt to new environments. Indeed, while other methods achieve rewards of approximately 4000, this approach stagnates at rewards slightly above 2000. Figure 4b shows that simply matching the evolution of σ to the L2 regular-ization baseline yields comparable results. Moreover, Figure 5 shows that the evaluation of the two deter-ministic policies obtained is completely equivalent. It also highlights that σ is a major confounding factor, as CBP significantly underperforms the other two meth-ods, in contrast with the findings of [8], achieving an average reward that is 20% lower. Indeed, these results indicate that many state-of-the-art techniques that try to mitigate LoP by modifying the reward structure or performing operations on the neural networks’ weights are indistinguishable from approaches that simply focus on σ. Their effectiveness can be fully explained by the standard deviation alone.

# 5 CONCLUSIONS 

We have shown a novel connection between LoP and exploration with Gaussian policies, with both theoreti-cal and experimental results. We derived mathematical bounds characterizing the PPO loss landscape, show-ing how σ → 0 causes the gradient norm and Hessian sharpness to diverge and the Hessian approximate rank to collapse. We then numerically verified these results and provided experimental evidence, including on a real-world robotic task, that LoP can be caused by σ → 0. Future work will examine whether policy entropy af-fects LoP for other state-of-the-art RL methods and in-vestigate its relationship with catastrophic forgetting.

## E.1 Training setup for Slippery Ant

The difference with the base task lies in the fact that every 2 million steps, the friction is resampled log-uniformly in the range [0 .02 , 2.00].
