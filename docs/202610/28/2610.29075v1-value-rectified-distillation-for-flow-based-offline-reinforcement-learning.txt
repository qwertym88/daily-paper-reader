Abstract 

We aim to address the problem of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Mode Averaging , a critical problem that emerges when distilling Flow Matching (FM) based behavior policies in offline Reinforcement Learning (RL): When learning over multi-modal datasets, standard distillation inadvertently forces the policy to interpolate across different behavioral modes, causing the generated actions to collapse into invalid, OOD regions. To resolve this, we propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) ,a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided generative trajectory alignment problem. By employing a dynamic coupling mech-anism, VRD actively rectifies flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behav-ioral modes, cleanly isolating high-value actions. Specifically, we instantiate VRD through two complementary algorithms: VRD-Q , which utilizes value-quantile behavior distillation to statistically filter out low-value behaviors, and VRD-R ,which reorganizes the latent space to geometrically isolate high-value generative trajectories from the FM-based behavior policy. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that VRD improves existing generative baselines across diverse contin-uous control and planning tasks on the D4RL, OGBench, and Minari benchmarks, especially over those multi-modal, non-expert datasets. 

1 Introduction 

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) aims to derive a high-capacity policy from a fixed, pre-collected dataset without further environment interaction [16, 15], which is particularly vital for the applications where online exploration is costly or hazardous. Recently, generative models, particularly Flow Matching (FM) [18], have emerged as a powerful alternative to traditional Diffusion-based methods [9, 2, 5], where FM-based policies, such as Flow Q-Learning (FQL) [21], offer significant advantages in modeling complex, multi-modal behavior distributions while circumventing the training instabilities typically associated with backpropagating through ODE solvers. Despite their empirical promise, we identify a fundamental pathology in how current flow-based RL frameworks distill high-value actions from multi-modal behavior policies. Specifically, when learning from multi-modal data, our analysis reveals that Flow Matching networks inherently suffer from Latent Fusion : the topological entanglement between the generative trajectories of optimal and sub-optimal behavioral modes, caused by the vector field’s training dynamics, as is shown in Figure 1 (left). In previous works, like FQL [21], it first pre-trains a behavior flow policy to capture the full multi-modal behavior distribution [4] from the dataset, and subsequently applies Q-value guidance to distill the optimal policy. However, because such distillation operates on a continuous

Although recent approaches like Guided Flow Policy (GFP) [24] incorporate reward information to amplify optimal behavior modes in FM-based behavior policy, their FQL-style distillation mechanism still suffers from OOD Mode Averaging .To address this issue, our core insight is that during the distillation phase, policy refinement must go beyond simply cloning from FM-based behavior policy; rather, it inherently requires rectifying the flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behavioral modes along the data manifold. Therefore, we introduce Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) , a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided trajectory alignment problem, of which the core lies in a dynamic coupling mechanism that precisely modulates the flow dynamics to explicitly decouple and exclude sub-optimal behavioral modes, ensuring that the generated actions are confined to the optimal action manifold during inference. To instantiate this unified framework, we derive two distinct algorithms from complementary mathe-matical perspectives: VRD-Q (Statistical Rectification) : this variant performs in-place vector field alignment by employing asymmetric weight modulation based on value quantiles, which statistically filters sub-optimal regions without altering the underlying flow topology. VRD-R (Geometric Recti-fication) : this variant actively reshapes the flow’s topology, which constructs a value-gravity field that transports the generative trajectories toward a highly compact optimal support set, geometrically isolating them from sub-optimal modes. In summary, our primary contributions include: 1) Tracing OOD Mode Averaging: We theoretically trace the root cause of the OOD Mode Averaging problem in FQL-style distillation mechanism, revealing how its distillation objective interacts with latent fusion to generate invalid OOD actions on multi-modal data. 2) The VRD Framework: We propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) with two implementations VRD-Q and VRD-R, reformulating offline policy distillation as a value-guided trajectory alignment problem to safely extract high-value policies without mode averaging. 3) Em-pirical Validation: Extensive evaluations demonstrate that VRD-Q and VRD-R effectively mitigate this OOD issue, outperforming or performing competitively with existing generative baselines across various continuous control and planning tasks.

Motivation. The observation that standard Euclidean distillation is vulnerable to mode averaging on multi-modal datasets motivates our Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) framework. As analyzed in this section, directly penalizing the action difference can inadvertently pull the actor into OOD regions. To effectively mitigate this issue, policy extraction must move beyond simple action-space regularization. Instead of linearly interpolating actions across empty spaces, VRD rectifies the continuous latent mapping by dynamically aligning the flow dynamics with value guidance, hence encouraging the policy to safely extract high-value actions along the data manifold.

In summary, both VRD-Q and VRD-R prevent catastrophic out-of-distribution (OOD) mode-averaging by using dynamic Q-guidance to distill optimal actions along the pre-trained behavior manifold. However, they differ in mechanism and trade-offs: VRD-Q (In-place Dynamical Inter-vention): Truncates ODE gradient flows toward sub-optimal modes in-place via quantile modulation. It preserves the spatial geometry and multi-modal diversity of expert behaviors, which makes VRD-Q ideal for complex, multi-modal tasks requiring diverse path preservation and trajectory stitching (e.g., OGBench navigation). VRD-R (Geometric Reshaping): Rescales high-value latent vectors into a compact region around the origin. It acts as a symmetry-breaker to isolate a single, highly stable peak mode, which enable VRD-R for continuous control tasks where single-mode precision and stability are prioritized (e.g., standard D4RL locomotion). 4 Related works 

Gaussian Policies. Traditional offline RL methods often rely on Gaussian distributions to parame-terize the policy. This category includes standard Behavior Cloning ( BC [17]) as well as prominent value-based algorithms like Implicit Q-Learning ( IQL [14]) and ReBRAC [23]. While Gaussian poli-cies are computationally efficient, in complex offline datasets where behavior is often multi-modal or skewed, Gaussian-based actors suffer from limited expressivity [21], often leading to mode-averaging and suboptimal performance when the data distribution is non-Gaussian. 

Diffusion Policies. To overcome the expressivity bottleneck, recent works such as IDQL [9], 

SRPO [2], and CAC [5] leverage the power of score-based diffusion modeling to capture highly complex and multi-modal action distributions. However, directly optimizing an iterative diffusion process to maximize a Q-function remains challenging, often requiring unstable backpropagation through the sampling steps or complex reward-guided sampling techniques [12]. 

Flow Policies. Flow matching [18] has emerged as a compelling alternative to diffusion, which is simpler to train and faster to infer. To address the difficulty of directly optimizing iterative flow models, Flow Q-Learning ( FQL ) [21] proposed distilling an expressive one-step policy from a flow-matching model guided by RL objectives, along with two variants: FAWAC [1] and FBRAC [25], which bridges the gap between expressivity and efficiency. More recently, Guided Flow Policy (GFP ) [24] and Decision Flow ( DF ) [11] develops tailored optimization techniques, focusing on specifically extracting high-value actions within the flow framework.

6 Conclusion 

This paper proposes the Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) framework, reformulating policy distilla-tion as a value-guided trajectory alignment process to solve the latent fusion problem in offline RL over multi-modal dataset. VRD-Q and VRD-R mitigate OOD Mode Averaging problem via statistical filtering and geometric reshaping respectively. Experiments show VRD successfully decouples the high-value behavior mode from the multi-modal data, which enables VRD outperform baselines on various benchmarks and datasets, demonstrating exceptional robustness for sub-optimal data noise. 

Limitations (see Appendix D). The proposed VRD relies on the underlying value network’s accuracy, where severe Q-value overestimation may mislead the alignment process. Future work could explore more robust value estimation or extend this framework to discrete action spaces.

C.2 Minari: External comparison study 

The MuJoCo task suite within the Minari dataset [26] serves as a standardized benchmark widely adopted in offline reinforcement learning (RL) research. Developed by the Farama Foundation as a modern successor to the D4RL dataset, these tasks are built upon classic physics simulation environments, including HalfCheetah , Hopper , and Walker2d . Minari provides a tiered classification system to evaluate algorithms across varying data qualities: • Simple Datasets : These are primarily generated by random or early-stage training policies. They contain a high volume of low-value, exploratory trajectories characterized by a diver-gent distribution and a low signal-to-noise ratio, testing an algorithm’s ability to extract fundamental control logic from poor-quality data. • Medium Datasets : Collected from policies at intermediate performance levels, these datasets are goal-oriented but frequently contain sub-optimal actions and failed attempts. They serve as a core benchmark for evaluating whether an algorithm can effectively isolate optimal modes and outperform the data average. • Expert Datasets : Generated by fully converged, high-performance policies, these represent optimal behavior with a narrow, high-quality distribution. They are designed to verify if an algorithm can achieve high-precision policy alignment and maintain expert-level performance under limited data diversity. By adhering to the Gymnasium API and providing comprehensive metadata, Minari serves as an ideal testbed for evaluating capacity for mode separation and distributional correction within complex, multi-modal, and non-expert datasets. Flow Q-Learning (FQL) [21] and Guided Flow Policy (GFP) [24] are selected as the main baselines for comparison study.

C.3.3 Practical Hyperparameter Tuning Suggestions 

Based on our empirical analysis, we offer the following guidelines for applying VRD to new offline RL tasks: 1. Tuning the Quantile Threshold τ (VRD-Q): 

• Default: Start with τ = 0 .7.• High-Noise / Exploratory Datasets: While Theorem 2 suggests a larger τ under an oracle critic, on noisy datasets (e.g., “random” or “medium-replay”) the learned Q-network inherently suffers from higher estimation variance. Setting τ too close to 

1.0 makes the suboptimal anchor weight 1 − τ vanishingly small, causing the actor to exploit spurious OOD Q-peaks and destabilizing training. Thus, we recommend decreasing τ slightly (e.g., to 0.6 ∼ 0.7) to retain a necessary behavioral regularization 24 margin ( 1 − τ ∈ [0 .3, 0.4] ) for training stability while still down-weighting suboptimal modes. • Expert / Structured Datasets: For datasets with high-quality demonstrations where value estimation is accurate and stable, a larger τ (e.g., 0.8 ∼ 0.9) can be safely employed to aggressively concentrate the policy onto the peak optimal trajectories without risking OOD divergence. 2. Tuning the Truncation Radius σ (VRD-R): 

• Default: Set σ = 10 −4.• Geometric Adjustment: If the agent exhibits "mode collapse" (too little variety in actions), increase σ to 10 −3. If the agent still chooses sub-optimal actions in multi-modal settings, decrease σ to 10 −5 to force more aggressive centering. 3. Tuning the Balance Coefficient λ:

• We recommend λ ∈ [0 .05 , 0.1] . A smaller λ (e.g., 0.02 ) is preferred when the Q-function is well-trained and accurate. If the environment is complex and requires stronger regularization to prevent OOD mode-averaging, increase λ to 0.5.

This sharp decline in Normalized ∆Q is a key indicator of successful latent reshaping. • Spatial Isolation of Modes: The observed gradient confirms that VRD-R has successfully decoupled the intertwined modes found in the original multi-modal datasets. Specifically, it forces the optimal modes (high-value behaviors) to consolidate within the central region of the latent space (near the origin, ∥z∥ ≈ 0), while simultaneously pushing sub-optimal modes (low-value behaviors) toward the peripheral, high-variance regions (larger ∥z∥). • Strategic Alignment with Gaussian Prior: Given that the standard Gaussian prior utilized during inference naturally assigns the highest probability density to the region around the origin, this "center-optimal, edge-suboptimal" topology is highly rational. It ensures that standard sampling is highly likely to hit the high-value action manifold. In conclusion, the sensitivity results demonstrate that VRD-R effectively rectifies the flow dynamics by geometrically isolating high-value trajectories. This mechanism fundamentally prevents the Mode Averaging issue by ensuring that sub-optimal behaviors are spatially excluded from the primary inference region, thereby significantly enhancing the policy’s performance and robustness.

VRD-Specific Hyperparameters and Task Dependency The two algorithmic variants of our framework require specific hyperparameter configurations to effectively decouple sub-optimal and optimal behavioral modes. Empirically, we observe a strong dependency between the dataset profile (i.e., multi-modality and sub-optimality) and the optimal hyperparameter selection: • VRD-Q (Quantile-based Filtering): The primary hyperparameter is the quantile threshold 

τ ∈ (0 .5, 1) , which assigns a smaller distillation weight 1 − τ to suboptimal trajectories and a larger weight τ to optimal ones. For highly complex, exploratory, or noisy multi-modal datasets (e.g., OGBench mixed datasets and D4RL antmaze) where Q-value estimation is noisy, a moderate threshold ( τ ∈ { 0.6, 0.7}) is strictly required to balance suboptimal mode suppression with sufficient behavioral anchoring ( 1 − τ ∈ { 0.3, 0.4}), preventing policy collapse from Q-overestimation. Conversely, for datasets with cleaner distributions or expert-level demonstrations (e.g., D4RL expert or high-quality Minari tasks) with reliable critic gradients, a higher threshold ( τ ∈ { 0.8, 0.9}) is preferred to maximize peak mode extraction efficiency. • VRD-R (Latent Reorganization): The geometric isolation of high-value generative trajec-tories in the latent space is governed by the margin parameter σ, searched over a logarithmic grid σ ∈ { 10 −2, 10 −3, 10 −4, 10 −5}. In tasks exhibiting severe Out-of-Distribution (OOD) mode averaging risks, such as OGBench humanoid control, a relatively larger margin (σ = 10 −2) is enforced to guarantee a wider geometric decoupling. For simpler, structured tasks (e.g., D4RL locomotion expert datasets), a strictly smaller margin ( σ = 10 −5) pre-vents over-regularization and maintains the natural topological structure of the generative manifold. D Discussion 

While the proposed Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) framework, including its two instantiations (VRD-Q and VRD-R), successfully addresses the fundamental Latent Fusion and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Mode Averaging problems in flow-based offline RL, there are several limitations and open challenges that warrant further investigation. 28 1. Reliance on Q-value Accuracy. The core mechanism of VRD inherently relies on the accuracy of the underlying value network to guide the trajectory alignment. Specifically, the quantile weight 

ωτ (z) in VRD-Q and the geometric reshaping function ΦQ(s, z ) in VRD-R are directly derived from the estimated Q-values. If the Q-network suffers from severe OOD overestimation—a notoriously common issue in offline RL—the VRD framework might inadvertently align the generative flow toward falsely optimistic, low-density regions. Although our empirical results demonstrate robustness, future work could explore integrating more rigorous conservative value estimation methods (e.g., CQL-style penalties or uncertainty-aware ensembles) directly into the value-rectification functional to mitigate this risk. 

2. Restriction to Continuous Action Spaces. The theoretical foundation of VRD (as proven in Theorem 1 and 2) and the Flow Matching parameterization are built strictly upon continuous vector fields and continuous topological mappings. Consequently, the current framework is restricted to continuous control tasks. Extending this value-guided flow rectification paradigm to discrete action spaces (e.g., Atari games or discrete routing problems) is non-trivial. Future research could explore incorporating Categorical Flow Matching or Discrete Diffusion processes, adapting the latent distribution alignment functional to discrete categorical probabilities. 

3. Potential Diversity Collapse in VRD-R for Multi-Modal Experts. By design, the geometric rectification in VRD-R utilizes a centripetal gravitational field, as Eq.(12), that maps all high-value behaviors towards the origin of the latent space ( ∥z∥ ≈ 0). While this strictly isolates optimal modes from sub-optimal noise, it may lead to an unintended diversity collapse if the underlying optimal behavior itself is inherently multi-modal (i.e., there are multiple distinct but equally optimal strategies). By forcing all optimal trajectories into a single highly compact hypersphere, VRD-R might destroy the topological diversity of expert modes. VRD-Q avoids this by employing an in-place 

statistical filtering, which explains why VRD-Q generally exhibits broader stability across diverse environments. Future iterations of VRD-R could explore multi-centric gravitational fields (e.g., using Gaussian Mixture priors) to preserve expert multi-modality. 29 NeurIPS Paper Checklist
