DATA-DRIVEN STOCHASTIC OPTIMAL CONTROL OF AIR-CONDITIONING SYSTEMS WITH DELAYED ACTIVATION 

CHRISTIAN BAYER, RA ´UL TEMPONE, AND LEON WILKOSZ ∗

Abstract. We consider a stochastic optimal control problem arising in the operation of industrial air-conditioning systems with delayed activation effects. The delayed transition between inactive and operational regimes induces a non-Markovian structure in the controlled dynamics. We derive a regression-based discrete dynamic programming algorithm on nested state-control grids. We give a probabilistic proof of convergence of the idealized time-discrete dynamic pro-gramming problem to the continuous-time value function. Numerical experiments based on calibrated multidimensional forecast-tracking Ornstein–Uhlenbeck mod-els illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed methodology. 

1. Introduction 

The operation of large-scale air-conditioning systems in industrial and scientific environments requires the reliable maintenance of strict environmental conditions over long time horizons. Typical examples include airport infrastructures, semicon-ductor manufacturing facilities, and pharmaceutical laboratories in which deviations from prescribed temperature and humidity regimes may result in substantial opera-tional losses or production failure [16, 2]. In such systems, heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning infrastructure constitutes one of the dominant sources of operational energy consumption, often accounting for a substantial fraction of the total facility energy demand [20, 23]. Simultaneously, failure to maintain the required environ-mental conditions may compromise product quality, interrupt sensitive production processes, or damage critical infrastructure, thereby inducing severe economic con-sequences [2]. Consequently, the operation of industrial air-conditioning systems naturally gives rise to a sequential control problem balancing the energy costs as-sociated with active cooling against the economic penalties caused by violations of prescribed environmental conditions. A central difficulty in the mathematical modeling of such systems is the delayed response of the cooling infrastructure. Existing optimization and stochastic con-trol approaches for HVAC systems typically assume that control decisions affect the environmental dynamics instantaneously, while uncertainty enters only through

The idea is that under sufficiently small time steps, this algorithm produces ε-optimal feedback controls within the framework of open-loop control. The treatment is related to several distinct strands of the stochastic control literature. Impulse control problems with delayed reaction have been studied, for instance, by reducing the delayed intervention problem to a sequence of no-delay optimal stopping prob-lems [17]. Closely related finite-horizon impulse control problems with decision lag and execution delay have also been analyzed through time-discontinuous optimal stopping formulations and discrete-time approximations [19]. Separately, regression Monte Carlo methods have been developed for stochastic impulse control, where continuation and intervention functions are learned from simulated trajectories [15]. The weak-convergence argument used below to pass from the discrete dynamic pro-gramming problem to the continuous-time value function follows the general philoso-phy of Markov-chain approximation methods for continuous-time stochastic control [13]. The convergence properties of regression-based dynamic programming methods for discrete-time stochastic control problems with continuous state spaces have al-ready been studied rigorously in [3]. instead focuses on the complementary question of convergence of the underlying discretized delayed control problem to the original continuous-time value function. The stochastic dynamics used in the numerical examples are calibrated on forecast and observation data obtained from OpenWeather [18] through a multidimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model with correlated noise structure. We adopt the idea of [7] to regard the deviation of the environmental parameters from their forecast as a zero-mean stochastic process. The real-data simulations are implemented within a rolling horizon framework over sufficiently short decision intervals, so that the pro-gressive deterioration of forecast quality over longer horizons can be neglected within each optimization window. The use of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck dynamics is consistent with established stochastic models for environmental variables such as temperature processes [4]. More general bounded diffusion models, such as Wright–Fisher dynam-ics, are equally compatible with the theoretical framework developed in the present work and may be particularly natural when modeling bounded quantities such as relative humidity. In fact, [7] also develops a rigorous calibration methodology for such models. However, application of the latter to our case would unnecessarily enlarge the numerical study. The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows. First, we formulate the delayed air-conditioning problem as a weak stochastic optimal con-trol problem. Second, we develop a rigorous foundation for the resulting delayed stochastic control problem. We introduce a regression-based dynamic programming algorithm adapted to the delayed switching structure and establish optimality of the associated idealized discrete dynamic programming formulation within the class of open-loop controls. Most importantly, we prove convergence of the idealized discrete dynamic programming value functions to the value function of the orig-inal continuous-time stochastic control problem. Finally, we combine the method with a calibrated forecast-tracking model and demonstrate its practical performance in a rolling-horizon study based on real environmental data, providing a complete pipeline from forecast uncertainty quantification to delayed stochastic control and operational cost comparison. We developed a stochastic optimal control framework for air-conditioning systems with delayed activation. The resulting problem was formulated in weak open-loop form, with optimization taken over admissible stochastic bases as well as controls. We introduced a regression-based dynamic programming approximation on nested grids and proved convergence of the idealized discretized value functions to the continuous-time value function. On the computational side, the method combines a forecast-tracking Ornstein– Uhlenbeck model, monthly maximum-likelihood calibration, regression-based dy-namic programming, and rolling-horizon deployment. The numerical experiments show that the learned feedback controls exhibit anticipatory switching behaviour: the system is activated before the comfort region is violated in order to compensate for the warm-up delay. The real-data Berlin experiments demonstrate systematic cost reductions rela-tive to a permanently active climate-control system, while maintaining conservative behaviour in the presence of large violation penalties. The synthetic forecast exper-iments indicate that substantially larger savings are possible when forecast quality improves. This suggests that, in the present implementation, the practical perfor-mance is limited more by forecast accuracy than by the control methodology itself.
