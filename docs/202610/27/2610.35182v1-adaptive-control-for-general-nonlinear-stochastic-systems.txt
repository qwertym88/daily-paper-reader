# Adaptive Control for General Nonlinear Stochastic Systems 

Haizheng Li ∗† 

Abstract 

This paper extends the least-squares and certainty-equivalence minimum-variance frame-work of classical linear self-tuning regulators to a broad class of nonlinearly parameterized systems with compact parameter spaces, including commonly used deep neural network models. First, historical nonlinear least-squares fitting guarantees are converted into online one-step prediction bounds under a general modulus of continuity, with explicit regret rates under parameter Lipschitz continuity and finite-moment noise. Second, approximate NLS estimation combined with certainty-equivalence control achieves closed-loop stability and asymptotically optimal tracking under mild conditions without requiring excitation or pa-rameter identifiability. Explicit closed-loop regret and tracking error rates are established in the parameter-Lipschitz case, and closed-loop examples show that the regret rates cannot, in general, be improved for this regulator. Finally, robustness to small unmodeled dynamics is established by showing that closed-loop stability is preserved and the tracking error bounds comprise a vanishing term and a residual term determined by the model discrepancy. 

Keywords: Nonlinear stochastic systems; self-tuning regulation; nonlinear least squares; one-step prediction; convergence rates; unmodeled dynamics. 

# 1 Introduction 

1.1 Background and motivation 

Modern applications in embodied robotics, intelligent manufacturing, and energy systems in-creasingly combine feedback control with dynamical models that are estimated from data and may be highly nonlinear [1]. Accurate models may be difficult to obtain before operation, while measured input–output data can be used to update the model and improve control performance during operation. The self-tuning regulator (STR) provides a direct way to incorporate this information into feedback: estimate the model from past observations and compute the next control input using the current estimate. Its classical realization combines least-squares (LS) estimation with certainty-equivalence (CE) control [2, 3]. Extending this approach to nonlinear model classes would provide a theoretical basis for adaptive regulation using general nonlinear models estimated from data. The central difficulty is the interaction between open-loop prediction and closed-loop feedback control. The estimated model determines the control input, which in turn affects the subsequent system trajectory and the observations available for estimation. The predictor must therefore remain accurate along the trajectory generated by the adaptive regulator. For nonlinear parame-terizations, a small fitting error over past observations alone does not ensure a small one-step prediction error, and feedback may amplify prediction errors before sufficient information has been obtained. Stability and tracking analysis must therefore account for prediction errors before boundedness of the closed-loop signals has been established.

The objective is to establish closed-loop stability and asymptotically optimal tracking for this nonlinear STR. To this end, a one-step prediction analysis is developed from the historical least-squares comparison, allowing the regressors to be unbounded, dependent, and nonstationary. 

1.2 Related work 

For linear stochastic systems, the stability and optimality of the Åström–Wittenmark self-tuning regulator and an extended least-squares adaptive tracker were established in [4]. A unified treatment of stability, optimality, consistency, and logarithmic convergence rates for LS-based regulators was subsequently developed in [5]. These classical works provide a methodological foundation for the present study through their analysis of the interaction between prediction errors and closed-loop signal growth. However, extensions to nonlinear systems are subject to fundamental limitations that apply to any causal controller. A critical growth phenomenon for scalar-parameter nonlinear stochastic systems was identified in [6], while limitations arising from uncertainty growth and parameter dimension were established in [7, 8]. For nonlinear parameterizations, feedback capability was related to parameter sensitivity and model structure in [9]. These results motivate the search for conditions under which nonlinear adaptive regulation can be achieved. Early work on nonlinear adaptive prediction in [10] studied nonlinear autoregressive and con-trol models with unknown parameters in a compact set and established an asymptotically exact logarithmic law for cumulative nonlinear least squares (NLS) one-step prediction errors under smoothness, identification, and a positive-definite limiting information condition. Subsequently, [11] used NLS to achieve simultaneous identification and stabilization for scalar-parameter systems. In the analysis of control regret, [12] related prediction regret to control regret for linearly parameterized systems under suitable incremental stability conditions. More recently, [13] established asymptotically optimal output regulation with stable, nonlinearly parameterized exosystems and derived NLS identification rates, while [14] obtained polylogarithmic expected regret across episodes for nonlinearly parameterized systems under a uniformly bounded dy-namics map, an identifying initial policy, and excitation of the optimal policy. Recent recursive approaches include a nonlinear WLS-type estimator and adaptive feedback under quantitative monotonicity and Lipschitz conditions [15], and a modified stochastic-gradient predictor with prediction and CE control rates under a weak-convexity condition on the prediction loss [16]. The present work establishes stability and tracking properties of an adaptive regulator that combines approximate cumulative NLS estimation with CE control for general nonlinearly parameterized systems. The analysis requires neither excitation nor parameter convergence and does not impose additional model structure conditions such as uniformly bounded dynamics, identifying information conditions, or monotonicity or convexity assumptions used in the respective studies above. From a statistical learning perspective, [17] obtained rate-optimal non-asymptotic bounds for the quadratic prediction error method under dependence and identifiability conditions. Their performance criterion evaluates the final model estimate on an independent copy of the regressor trajectory. The criterion considered here is the cumulative one-step prediction error along the actual system trajectory, with the model estimate updated at each time. Under heavy-tailed errors, [18, 19] characterized how noise moments, function-class complexity, and local envelopes govern least-squares rates. Those results concern statistical prediction risk based on independent sample pairs. The present analysis allows predictable regressors generated from past observations, as occurs under feedback control. 21.3 Main contributions and organization 

The main contributions are as follows. (i) One-step prediction error bounds are established for predictable nonlinear model families with compact parameter sets. Approximate least-squares estimation based on past observations yields cumulative prediction error bounds under a general modulus of continuity. Under parameter Lipschitz continuity, explicit almost-sure convergence rates are obtained for finite-moment noise. The bounds account for the accumulated energy, peak values, and higher-order sample moments of the possibly unbounded prediction envelope. (ii) With the CE control input, the prediction bounds establish time-average closed-loop stability and almost-sure convergence of the average squared tracking error to the noise variance, the asymptotically minimum achievable level. Explicit convergence rates are obtained under parameter Lipschitz continuity. Closed-loop examples show that the derived regret convergence rates cannot, in general, be improved for the regulator considered. (iii) The robustness of the same adaptive regulator to small unmodeled dynamics is established. Closed-loop stability is preserved when the model discrepancy is sufficiently small. The tracking error bounds contain a vanishing term and a residual term due to the unmodeled dynamics. Section 2 presents the system assumptions and performance criteria. Section 3 specifies the estimator and controller. The main results and their proofs are given in Sections 4 and 5, respectively. Section 6 concludes the paper. Proofs of the lower bounds on the closed-loop regret and of the auxiliary martingale lemmas are given in Appendices A and B, respectively.

This paper has studied a least-squares-based adaptive regulator for general nonlinear stochastic systems. Compactness of the parameter space and the prescribed continuity bound in the parameters allow the historical least-squares comparison to be used to bound the cumulative one-step prediction error along the closed-loop trajectory. Together with model inversion and the minimum-phase condition, these bounds establish time-average closed-loop stability, sublinear growth of squared signal peaks, and asymptotically optimal tracking. The results allow a fixed optimization tolerance and do not require convergence of the parameter estimates. For Lipschitz parameterizations, explicit prediction and tracking convergence rates are obtained under finite-moment noise. Closed-loop examples show that the derived regret convergence rates cannot, in general, be improved for the regulator considered. The robustness analysis further shows that small unmodeled dynamics preserve time-average stability and contribute a nonvanishing term to the tracking error bound. Further work includes identifying classes of nonlinear systems for which the approximate global least-squares requirement can be satisfied with efficient online computation. Extensions to adaptive control with integrated offline and online learning, time-varying parameters, and constrained control inputs also merit investigation.
