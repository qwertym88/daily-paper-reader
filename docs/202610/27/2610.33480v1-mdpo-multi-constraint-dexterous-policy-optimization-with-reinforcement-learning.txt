# MDPO: Multi-constraint Dexterous Policy Optimization with Reinforcement Learning 

Ao Zhou 1,2, Zongyou Yu 1,2, Ke Wu 3,4, Tao Xu 1,2, Junqiao Zhao 1,Wenchao Ding 3,4, Alois Knoll 5, Guang Chen 1,2*

Abstract — Dexterous manipulation requires high-dimensional control policies to accomplish complex contact-rich tasks while simultaneously satisfying multiple safety requirements, such as joint limits, unsafe finger configurations, arm collisions, and unstable object interactions. Constrained reinforcement learning (RL) provides a principled framework for incorporating these requirements into dexterous manipulation policy learning. However, existing constrained RL methods for dexterous manipulation primarily focus on cumulative cost constraints and struggle to simultaneously handle multiple heterogeneous constraints during dexterous policy learning. In this work, we propose Multi-constraint Dexterous Policy Optimization (MDPO) for safe dexterous manipulation. We first formulate dexterous manipulation as a multi-constraint policy optimization problem involving both cumulative cost constraints and state-wise cost constraints. Based on this formulation, we integrate multi-constraint projection into the dexterous manipulation policy optimization process. Specifically, we employ constraint-specific policy projections to map the intermediate policy that maximizes task performance onto the feasible regions defined by the cumulative cost constraint and the state-wise cost constraint. Extensive experiments on ReDMan, including safe joint control, finger safety, and grasping tasks, demonstrate that MDPO achieves superior constraint satisfaction while maintaining competitive task performance compared with the baselines. Our code is available at this link. 

I. INTRODUCTION Dexterous manipulation is a fundamental capability for embodied robots, enabling them to perform complex tasks that require high-dimensional coordination, precise contact control, and flexible interactions with objects [1]. Recent advances in reinforcement learning (RL) have demonstrated considerable potential for learning dexterous manipulation policies directly through interactions with the environ-ment [2]. Unlike conventional model-based approaches that rely on accurate dynamics models and manually designed controllers [3], RL provides a data-driven paradigm that optimizes manipulation strategies directly from environmen-tal feedback [4]. This characteristic makes RL particularly attractive for high-dimensional dexterous manipulation tasks involving complex contact dynamics and nonlinear interac-tions. However, applying RL to real-world dexterous manip-ulation remains challenging due to the presence of multiple physical cost constraints. Therefore, simultaneously considering dexterous manipulation performance, cumulative safety constraints, and state-wise safety constraints is crucial for achieving reliable dexterous manipulation. Constrained RL provides a principled framework for incor-porating safety requirements into policy optimization through Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDP) [6]. Al-though constrained RL has improved the safety of dexterous manipulation policies [5], existing methods mainly focus on a single cumulative cost constraint and rarely consider the simultaneous regulation of cumulative and state-wise safety requirements [7]. Conventional penalty or Lagrangian approaches handle constraints by introducing manually de-signed or adaptively learned coefficients [8], [9]. How-ever, extending such methods to heterogeneous constraints requires multiple penalty factors, increasing optimization difficulty and potentially leading to unstable or overly conser-vative policy updates. Projection-based methods provide an alternative by mapping updated policies back into feasible constraint regions [10], [11], [12], but are still primarily developed for the single constraint settings. Moreover, cu-mulative and state-wise constraints exhibit different temporal characteristics and may interact during dexterous manipu-lation: improving task performance by relaxing cumulative safety can increase instantaneous safety risks. Therefore, an effective multi-constraint RL algorithm is needed to jointly regulate long-term cumulative risks and state-wise safety violations while preserving manipulation performance. In this work, we propose Multi-constraint Dexterous 

Policy Optimization (MDPO), a RL algorithm for multi-constraint safe dexterous manipulation. Based on this formulation, MDPO performs a regularized policy improvement step to obtain an intermediate policy that improves manipulation performance. The intermediate policy is then projected back into the joint safe feasible region defined by both cumulative and state-wise cost constraints. RELATED WORK In this section, we review the literature most relevant to our work, including RL for dexterous manipulation and constrained RL for safe policy optimization. 

A. RL for Dexterous Manipulation 

Dexterous manipulation requires coordinated control of high-dimensional joints and contacts for complex object interactions [13]. RL has become an effective paradigm for learning such skills through direct interaction with en-vironments [2]. Early studies demonstrated successful pol-icy learning in simulation [14] and transfer to physical robots through improved exploration and sim-to-real tech-niques [15]. Recent advances have further enabled chal-lenging tasks including grasping, object reorientation [16], and in-hand manipulation [17]. However, most methods still emphasize task success and manipulation performance [18], while safety is typically handled through reward penal-ties [19] or early termination [17]. ReDMan [5] explicitly in-troduces safety costs for dexterous manipulation, but mainly considers a single cumulative constraint. Consequently, ex-isting methods remain insufficient for manipulation scenarios involving multiple heterogeneous safety requirements [20]. 

B. Constrained RL 

Constrained RL incorporates safety requirements into pol-icy optimization through CMDP formulations [6]. A widely used approach is Lagrangian optimization [21], which intro-duces adaptive multipliers to balance reward maximization and constraint satisfaction. Other representative directions include interior-point optimization [22], CVaR-based for-mulations [23], penalty-based methods [9], state augmenta-tion [24], and world-model-based safe RL [25]. Projection-based methods [11] instead decouple performance improve-ment from safety correction by projecting an intermediate policy back into the feasible region. Primal-dual approaches provide another important paradigm by jointly optimizing policies and dual variables, with representative methods including PDO [26] and CVPO [27]. Despite these advances, most existing methods are developed primarily for cumula-tive constraints, motivating more effective optimization for heterogeneous safety requirements. Although constrained RL has achieved considerable suc-cess in safety-critical control, existing safe dexterous manip-ulation studies remain largely limited to single cumulative cost constraints. A straightforward extension to multiple safety requirements is to introduce additional Lagrange mul-tipliers or penalty coefficients, which increases the number of constraint-related hyperparameters and may lead to unstable or overly conservative policy optimization. In particular, cumulative and state-wise safety constraints in dexterous manipulation are inherently coupled: relaxing the cumulative cost constraint may improve task performance, but can simultaneously increase the likelihood of violating the state-wise cost constraint. These limitations highlight the need for more effective multi-constraint formulations for safe dexterous manipulation. III. IV. METHODOLOGY In this section, we present Multi-constraint Dexterous Pol-icy Optimization (MDPO) for safe dexterous manipulation under cumulative and state-wise cost constraints. We first re-formulate the state-wise constraint into a tractable trajectory-level form. We then introduce the two-stage optimization framework of MDPO, consisting of policy performance improvement and joint multi-constraint policy projection. Fi-nally, we present a sample-based first-order implementation for practical policy optimization.

V. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION In this section, we conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of MDPO in multi-constraint safe dexterous manipulation. 

A. Experimental Setup 

Tasks. We evaluate MDPO on the ReDMan [5] bench-mark, which provides standardized safety-constrained RL environments for dexterous manipulation. Five representative tasks are considered in our experiments, including Joint ,

Finger , Armjoint , Armfinger , and Grasp . These tasks cover different dexterous manipulation scenarios involving hand joints, fingers, coordinated arm-hand control, and object interaction. The original ReDMan environments characterize safety violations mainly using binary cost signals, where the cost changes abruptly between safe and unsafe states. To provide a denser safety signal for policy optimization and better reflect the severity of safety violations, we modify the original cost formulation into a continuous cost function while preserving the corresponding safety semantics of each task. The resulting continuous cost ct is used to construct both the cumulative and state-wise safety requirements. The running maximum cost Mt is incorporated into the observa-tion, and the state-wise cost increment Dt is constructed to provide a tractable optimization signal for state-wise safety. For all tasks, we set the cumulative cost threshold ¯c = 35 

and and state-wise cost threshold ¯D = 1 .

Baselines. We compare MDPO with three representative constrained RL methods, including LAG, P3O, and CRPO. Since these methods are originally designed mainly for conventional constrained RL settings, we adapt all of them to the same multi-constraint formulation considered in this work, such that both the cumulative cost constraint and the state-wise cost constraint are explicitly incorporated during policy optimization. Specifically, the multi-constraint LAG baseline introduces independent adaptive multipliers for the cumulative and state-wise safety constraints; P3O is extended by introducing separate penalty terms for the two safety objectives; and CRPO is adapted to account for both constraints when determining whether to optimize task performance or perform safety-oriented policy updates. These adaptations ensure that all baselines operate under the same cumulative and state-wise safety requirements as MDPO. All methods are trained using the same environment interaction budget and evaluated under identical task settings for a fair comparison. 

Implementation Details. The hyperparameters involved in the practical implementation of MDPO are summarized in Table I. The same settings are used across all tasks. 

B. Comparison Experiments 

Fig. 2 compares MDPO with the multi-constraint baselines LAG, P3O, and CRPO on five dexterous manipulation tasks. Overall, MDPO achieves a favorable balance between task performance and safety. Across the evaluated tasks, it reaches competitive or higher success rates and episode rewards while maintaining relatively low cumulative costs and state-wise violation rates, indicating that the proposed method can effectively coordinate manipulation objectives with heteroge-neous safety requirements. From the task-performance per-spective, MDPO exhibits faster and more stable convergence on most tasks. On relatively simpler tasks such as Joint and 

Finger , several baselines can eventually reach comparable final performance, but generally require more interactions or show larger fluctuations during training. The advantage of MDPO becomes more evident on ArmJoint , ArmFinger , and particularly Grasp , where stronger arm-hand coordination and contact-rich interactions make policy learning more challenging. From the safety perspective, MDPO generally keeps the episode cost below or close to the prescribed cu-mulative cost limit while simultaneously achieving low cost rates. In contrast, some baselines can satisfy the cumulative-cost requirement but still exhibit notable state-wise viola-tions, indicating that existing constrained RL methods re-main insufficient for simultaneously handling heterogeneous safety requirements. This result highlights the advantage of MDPO in jointly regulating long-horizon cumulative risks and instantaneous state-wise safety violations during policy optimization. These results validate the proposed two-stage optimization framework. MDPO first obtains a performance-improved intermediate policy and then jointly corrects cumu-lative and state-wise safety risks through adaptive projection. The independently adjusted safety coefficients allow the two constraints to contribute according to their corresponding risk levels, while KL regularization limits excessive deviation from the intermediate policy. Consequently, MDPO achieves more stable multi-constraint satisfaction without sacrificing dexterous manipulation performance.

This result demonstrates the potential of MDPO for transferring multi-constraint dexterous manipulation policies from simu-lation to real robotic systems. VI. CONCLUSION In this work, we propose the Multi-constraint Dexter-ous Policy Optimization for safe dexterous manipulation under both cumulative and state-wise safety constraints. To handle the heterogeneous temporal characteristics of these constraints, we reformulate the state-wise requirement into a tractable trajectory-level objective through a state-wise cost increment formulation. Based on this formulation, MDPO adopts a two-stage optimization framework that first improves task performance and then jointly projects the intermediate policy toward the feasible region defined by both safety constraints. The joint projection incorporates cumulative and state-wise safety signals with independently adapted correction coefficients while using KL regularization to preserve the manipulation capability of the intermediate policy.
