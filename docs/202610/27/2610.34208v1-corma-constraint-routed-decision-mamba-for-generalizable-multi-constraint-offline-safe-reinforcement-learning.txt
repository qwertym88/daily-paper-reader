Abstract 

Offline safe reinforcement learning (RL) learns safety-aware policies from static datasets while satisfying safety constraints during deployment. Motivated by the strong trajectory-modeling capabilities of Transformers, recent Decision Trans-former (DT)-based methods formulate offline safe RL as conditional sequence modeling by conditioning action gen-eration on return-to-go (RTG) and cost-to-go (CTG) to-kens, thereby enabling adaptation to varying safety bud-gets. However, existing DT-based methods are developed for single-constraint tasks. For tasks with multiple con-straints, generalizing learned policies to unseen combina-tions of safety budgets remains challenging due to the con-flicting requirements imposed by heterogeneous constraints. Moreover, the quadratic complexity of Transformer architec-tures significantly increases the computational overhead in multi-constraint tasks, as representing additional constraints requires more CTG tokens. Recently, Mamba has emerged as an efficient sequence-modeling backbone, offering linear computational complexity and strong generalization capabil-ities. In this paper, we propose CORMA, a constraint-routed decision Mamba framework for generalizable multi-constraint offline safe RL. CORMA decomposes policy inference into constraint-aware RTG estimation and adaptive action gener-ation, where constraint-specific experts model feasible RTG distributions and a router-guided product-of-experts mecha-nism fuses these distributions to provide safety-aware guid-ance for a Mamba-based action decoder. A three-stage opti-mization strategy is designed to improve training stability. Ex-tensive experiments on OSRL benchmarks demonstrate that CORMA achieves superior reward-safety trade-offs, reduces inference latency by 37 % compared with DT-based meth-ods, and exhibits stronger zero-shot generalization to unseen safety-budget combinations than offline safe RL baselines.

After pre-training on seen safety-budget regions, Transformer-based sequence models struggle under unseen safety-budget combinations, whereas CORMA generalizes more robustly. through constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs), where policies are optimized subject to predefined safety budgets (Liu et al., 2023a; Zhao et al., 2023). Most existing methods, however, require online interactions that may ex-pose the agent or environment to unacceptable risks. Offline safe RL avoids such unsafe exploration by learning entirely from static datasets without additional environmental inter-actions (Alles et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2025). Existing offline safe RL methods mainly include model-based (Kidambi et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2020), model-free (Kumar et al., 2020; Kostrikov et al., 2021), and conditional sequence modeling approaches (Chen et al., 2021; Xue et al., 2026). Model-based and model-free methods often rely on Bellman backups, making them susceptible to overestima-tion and bootstrapping errors in offline settings. Moreover, they are typically trained for fixed safety budgets and thus generalize poorly to unseen safety budgets during training (Achiam et al., 2017). In contrast, methods such as the Con-strained Decision Transformer (CDT) condition action gener-ation on return-to-go (RTG) and cost-to-go (CTG), enabling arXiv:2610.34208v1 [cs.LG] 25 Oct 2026 zero-shot adaptation to different safety budgets through con-ditional sequence modeling (Liu et al., 2023b). Despite their effectiveness, existing Transformer-based methods are primarily designed for single-constraint tasks and face two major limitations in multi-constraint settings. First, self-attention has quadratic complexity with respect to sequence length, while representing additional constraints requires more CTG tokens, substantially increasing training and inference overhead (Dao et al., 2022; Tay et al., 2022). Second, standard self-attention lacks an explicit mechanism for coordinating multiple CTG conditions and their influence on RTG-guided action generation (Vaswani et al., 2017). Mamba, a selective state space model (SSM), provides a promising alternative. Its parallel scan mechanism captures long-range causal dependencies with linear computational complexity, while its selective state mechanism dynamically filters task-relevant information (Gu and Dao, 2024). Mamba has shown strong performance in conditional sequence mod-eling for unconstrained offline RL (Lv et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024), yet its potential for safe RL with multiple con-straints remains unexplored. This motivates the following question: How can Mamba-based conditional sequence mod-eling efficiently coordinate multiple safety constraints and generalize zero-shot to unseen safety-budget combinations? 

To address this, we propose CO nstraint-Routed deci-sion MA mba (CORMA), a novel Mamba-based condi-tional sequence modeling framework for generalizable multi-constraint offline safe RL. To be specific, CORMA introduces a constraint-aware expert-routing mechanism, where inde-pendent RTG expert networks evaluate diverse safety budgets and model feasible RTG distributions. A gating router as-signs adaptive weight scores to these experts, and fuses their predicted distributions through a product-of-experts (PoE) mechanism. The final RTG is then sampled from the fused distribution and fed into a Mamba-based action decoder. To improve training stability, we design a three-stage optimiza-tion strategy that separately trains the action decoder, RTG experts, and the router. Experimental results demonstrate that CORMA achieves strong reward-safety trade-offs in multi-constraint safe RL tasks and robust zero-shot generalization to unseen safety-budget combinations. The main contribu-tions of this paper are summarized as follows: 

• We identify fundamental limitations of conditional se-quence modeling in multi-constraint offline safe RL : additional CTG conditions substantially increase the se-quence modeling overhead; while directly concatenating multiple CTG tokens is insufficient to achieve robust gen-eralization under unseen safety-budget combinations. 

• We propose CORMA, a novel conditional sequence mod-eling framework for generalizable multi-constraint offline safe RL. CORMA uses constraint-aware RTG experts to model feasible RTG distributions under different CTG conditions, and fuses them through a router-guided PoE mechanism to generate constraint-aware RTG guidance for a Mamba-based action decoder. 

• We conduct extensive experiments on OSRL benchmarks. Results verify that CORMA achieves superior task per-formance while reduces inference overhead by 37% com-pared with Transformer-based baselines, and exhibits stronger zero-shot adaptation to unseen safety-budget combinations than existing offline safe RL baselines.

State Space Model and Mamba 

A continuous-time SSM describes the mapping of an input signal x(t) ∈ RD to an output signal y(t) ∈ RD through a hidden state h(t) ∈ RN . A standard linear SSM is given by 

h′(t) = Ah(t) + Bx(t), y(t) = Ch(t), where A ∈ RN ×N ,B ∈ RN ×D and C ∈ RD×N are the state transition, in-put projection, and output projection matrices. To process discrete-time sequences, the continuous parameters are dis-cretized with a step size ∆. Using zero-order hold (ZOH), we obtain ¯A = exp(∆ A), ¯B = (∆ A)−1(exp(∆ A) − I) · ∆B.

The resulting discrete recurrence can be written as ht =¯Aht−1 + ¯Bxt, y t = Cht. Although traditional SSMs have shown promise in sequence modeling, they rely on linear time-invariant dynamics where the matrices ¯A, ¯B, and C re-main fixed across time (Gu et al., 2022). This time-invariant property limits their ability to dynamically filter task-relevant information. Mamba addresses this limitation by introducing a data-dependent selection mechanism (Gu and Dao, 2023), where B, C, and ∆ are parameterized as functions of the current input xt. This enables Mamba to selectively preserve important contextual information while filtering irrelevant noise. Furthermore, by leveraging a hardware-aware paral-lel scan algorithm, Mamba achieves efficient long-sequence modeling with linear complexity of O(L).

## Investigating Conditional Sequence Modeling in Multi-Constraint Safe RL 

In this section, we investigate two key challenges of applying conditional sequence modeling to multi-constraint safe RL: high computational complexity and limited generalization to unseen safety-budget combinations. 

Computational Complexity Challenges Under Multiple Constraints 

Traditional offline safe RL algorithms, such as Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO) (Achiam et al., 2017), and Con-straints Penalized Q-learning (CPQ) (Xu et al., 2022), rely on trust regions or conservative penalties to satisfy constraints. When extended to multi-constraint scenarios via Lagrange multipliers (Tessler et al., 2018; Stooke et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2022) or separate cost critics (Guo et al., 2025), they suf-fer from gradient conflicts during shared parameter updates, since different constraints may induce inconsistent policy-update directions. This limitation motivates a sequence mod-eling alternative that formulates offline safe RL as trajec-tory generation conditioned on RTG and CTG tokens. How-ever, the representation of multiple constraints requires addi-tional CTG tokens. Due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention, the longer sequences cause computational costs to grow quadratically, which results in significant inference overhead. To demonstrate this, we conducted experiments on an offline safe RL dataset derived from Safety-Gymnasium. We compared the standard CDT model with Constrained Decision Mamba (CDM), a variant that replaces the Trans-former backbone with Mamba. As illustrated in Fig. 2 (a), while CDT suffers from a sharp increase in inference latency as the number of constraints increases, CDM consistently achieves lower latency. Generalization Challenges under Dynamic Combinations of Safety Budgets 

Traditional Lagrangian-based methods optimize policies un-der static safety budgets and require full retraining when safety budgets change. Recent conditional sequence model-ing methods, such as CDT (Liu et al., 2023b), SDT (Guo et al., 2024), and CoPDT (Xue et al., 2026), achieve gen-eralization to unseen safety budgets in single-constraint en-vironments. However, whether these methods can still gen-eralize when multiple safety budgets change simultaneously in multi-constraint tasks remains underexplored. To investi-gate this, we evaluate sequence models across safety-budget combinations with different degrees of deviation from the of-fline dataset. Since different constraints have heterogeneous cost scales, we normalize each cost return Ci by its corre-sponding safety budget κi, and measure overall safety using Cost-N , defined as Cost-N = max i{Ci/κ i}. ## Method 

In this section, we first introduce the constraint-routed RTG prediction module, which models constraint-specific feasible RTG distributions and fuses them through a router-guided PoE mechanism. Then, we present the Mamba-based action decoder for conditional action generation. Finally, we de-scribe the three-stage optimization mechanism for decoupled training of the action decoder, experts, and router. 

Constraint-Routed PoE for RTG Estimation 

Under the conditional sequence modeling framework, policy inference is conditioned on both target return and the remain-ing safety budget, which are represented by the RTG token 

Rt and the CTG tokens {Cit }Ni=1 , respectively. Nevertheless, an inherent tension exists between Rt and Cit . If an unreal-istically high Rt is specified, the model may over-prioritize reward maximization and consequently incur constraint vi-olations. Thus, it is imperative to set an appropriate Rt that balances reward maximization and constraint satisfaction. Moreover, in multi-constraint tasks, different constraints may require conflicting target returns. Satisfying the safety budget of one constraint may contradict that of another. This trade-off significantly reduces the feasible range of Rt. To address this, we design a constraint-routed PoE mechanism for RTG estimation. Experimental Setup 

Tasks and Constraints We evaluate our method on OSRL benchmark tasks built upon Bullet-Safety-Gym (Liu et al., 2023a) and Safety-Gymnasium (Ji et al., 2023). We con-sider four types of robots (Point, Car, Ball, and Drone) and four tasks (Circle, Goal, Push, Button). Task names follow the convention “Robot-Task", e.g., PG denotes “Point-Goal". For each task, we incorporate three heterogeneous con-straints: the boundary/collision constraint, the high-velocity constraint, and the low-velocity constraint. Based on these, we establish two task settings: the -v2 tasks include the two constraints of boundary/collision and high velocity, while the challenging -v3 tasks encompass all constraints.

Comparison and Analysis 

RQ1: As shown in Table 1, CORMA achieves a favorable reward–safety trade-off across most tasks. Conventional of-fline safe RL methods remain competitive on -v2 tasks, but degrade markedly on the challenging -v3 tasks. More impor-tantly, CORDT consistently outperforms CORMA w/o PoE, indicating that the constraint-routed PoE mechanism is the primary source of CORMA’s performance gains. Meanwhile, CORMA-S frequently violates constraints, indicating that a single expert is insufficient for reliable multi-constraint RTG prediction. CORMA w/o PoE generally outperforms CDT-MC and CoPDT-MC, confirming the complementary benefit of the Mamba action decoder. These results demonstrate that CORMA can effectively learn safety-aware policies from of-fline datasets under multiple heterogeneous constraints. 

RQ2: To evaluate CORMA’s adaptability, we first examine its few-shot adaptation to unseen safety-budget combinations on the challenging DC-v3 task. The models are pre-trained on the base dataset and then fine-tuned using a small subset of trajectories. As shown in Fig. 4, CORMA reduces the col-lision, high-speed, and low-speed costs below their budgets with fewer fine-tuning steps. The overall performance follows CORMA > CORDT > CORMA w/o PoE > CDT-MC. The advantage of CORDT over CDT-MC indicates the effective-ness of constraint-routed PoE mechanism, while the gain of CORMA over CORDT shows that the Mamba action decoder improves adaptability. Moreover, we evaluate the zero-shot generalization of CORMA under slight, moderate, and severe safety-budget regions, corresponding to increasing values of 

d(κ, Kseen ). As shown in Fig. 5, CORMA maintains com-petitive rewards across different regions. Fig. 5(b)-(d) further show that CORMA controls all individual costs within their budgets, whereas the baselines exhibit increasing violations. These results demonstrate the robust zero-shot adaptability of CORMA to unseen safety-budget combinations. 

RQ3: We evaluate the computational efficiency of CORMA during training and deployment. Although CORMA adds additional experts and a router, the linear complexity of Mamba significantly reduces sequence mod-eling overhead compared with Transformer-based baselines. As shown in Table 2, this computational efficiency advan-tage offsets the overhead of the additional modules, leading to lower training time than the Transformer-based baselines. Moreover, CORMA achieves 0.005 s/step inference latency, demonstrating its ability to support multi-constraint policy inference with lower deployment latency. 

## Conclusion 

In this work, we study multi-constraint offline safe RL with varying safety-budget combinations. We propose CORMA, a framework that decomposes policy inference into constraint-aware RTG estimation and adaptive action generation. A three-stage optimization mechanism separately trains the ac-tion decoder, experts, and router. Experiments on OSRL benchmarks show that CORMA achieves better reward-safety trade-offs and stronger zero-shot generalization to un-seen budget combinations than existing safe RL baselines. {ξCi }Ni=1 . When confronted with unseen safety-budget combinations, an idealized feasible optimal policy can allocate an appropriate target return RTG based on the current state and remaining bud-gets. However, when handling such unseen constraint conditions, traditional CDT-based methods fail to generate suitable RTGs, leading to a substantial RTG transition discrepancy ξR against the fea-sible optimal policy, which compounds rapidly over time steps. In contrast, by thoroughly learning the intrinsic relationships among states, CTGs, and RTGs from offline expert trajectories, CORMA can dynamically set well-calibrated RTGs when encountering unseen budget combinations. This effectively reduces the RTG transition discrepancy ξR against the feasible optimal policy, thereby tightening the performance-gap bounds and enabling the cumulative return ΛR 

> 1

## C. Related Work 

C.1 Offline Safe Reinforcement Learning 

Offline safe reinforcement learning learns constraint-satisfying policies from static datasets and avoids risky online exploration in real-world environments. Early methods mainly address single-cost constraints. For example, CPO enforces safety by constraining each policy update within a trust-region optimization framework (Achiam et al., 2017). CBPL extends safe policy learning to offline batch settings by combining fitted policy evaluation with constrained policy improvement (Le et al., 2019). CPQ further reduces offline constraint violations by assigning conservative penalties to unsafe or out-of-distribution actions during Q-learning (Xu et al., 2022). However, many practical scenarios require agents to satisfy multiple safety constraints simultaneously. To address the complex challenges of multi-constraint settings, recent studies have developed specific gradient manipulation techniques. For instance, (Yao et al. , 2022) approach multi-constraint safe RL from a Multi-Objective Optimization (MOO) perspective, proposing a Gradient Shaping (GradS) method for Lagrangian-based algorithms that improves training efficiency by eliminating redundant and conflicting constraint gradients. Similarly, (Kim et al. 2023) introduce a trust region-based Safe Distributional Actor-Critic (SDAC) algorithm, which features a gradient integration method that manages infeasibility issues by projecting unsafe policies into a feasible policy set through a quadratic program consisting of all constraint gradients. Nevertheless, due to the inherent trade-offs among different constraints, these methods may still suffer from policy generalization under unseen safety-budget combinations. 

C.2 Generalization in Offline Safe RL 

Recent studies have explored generalization in offline safe RL. For instance, CCAC enables zero-shot adaptation to varying constraint thresholds by modeling the relationship between state-action distributions and safety constraints to regularize critics and policy learning (Guo et al., 2025). CDT improves adaptation to varying constraint thresholds by conditioning action generation on target returns and cost returns (Liu et al., 2023b). CoPDT advances multi-task safety adaptation by using a constraint-prioritized prompt encoder to distinguish different constraints and a dynamic RTG generation mechanism to predict target returns aligned with current safety requirements (Xue et al., 2026). However, existing methods still mainly generalize to threshold changes under a single constraint. They remain insufficient for multi-constraint settings where several safety thresholds vary simultaneously. 

C.3 Mamba in Reinforcement Learning 

Mamba is a selective state space model that enables linear-time sequence modeling through an input-dependent selection mechanism and a hardware-aware parallel scan (Gu and Dao, 2024). Its efficiency in processing long trajectories has motivated several applications in reinforcement learning. Ota et al. replace the self-attention modules of Decision Transformer with Mamba for sequential decision-making (Ota, 2024). Lv et al. further propose a multi-grained Decision Mamba that captures both inter-step temporal dependencies and intra-step relationships among RTGs, states, and actions, achieving improved performance on MuJoCo tasks (Lv et al., 2024). Subsequent studies explore different applications of Mamba in trajectory modeling. Huang et al. introduce Decision Mamba-Hybrid, which combines Mamba-based long-term memory with Transformer-based action prediction and achieves faster inference on long-horizon tasks (Huang et al., 2024). Dai et al. show that a carefully designed Decision Mamba can outperform Decision Transformer with substantially fewer parameters on Atari and MuJoCo (Dai et al., 2024). Nevertheless, existing Mamba-based RL methods mainly focus on unconstrained return maximization and computational efficiency. To the best of our knowledge, Mamba-based conditional sequence modeling has not yet been studied for offline safe RL under varying and unseen safety-budget combinations. 

## D. CORMA Three-Stage Training and deployment algorithms 

In this section, we provide the detailed algorithms of CORMA in multi-constraint scenarios. As described in the main paper, the workflow of CORMA primarily includes two phases: the three-stage offline training and the online policy deployment. The offline training phase consists of three decoupled stages. In Stage I, we pretrain the Mamba-based action decoder backbone θB. We sample length-K multi-constraint trajectories τ−K:t from the offline dataset D. The backbone learns the conditional action policy πθB via minimization of the loss function LB(θB). After this stage, the backbone parameters θB are frozen. In Stage II, we train the constraint-specific experts. For each constraint i, we filter the dataset to obtain a specific subset 

Di. The i-th expert network ϕi processes the constraint-specific trajectory sequence τ i 

> −K:t

> −K:t

> i

DR. The router evaluates the current state st and the normalized remaining safety budget vector ¯Ctto compute the routing weights αt. ## E.Experimental Details 

E.1 Experimental Tasks and Datasets 

Safety-Gymnasium (Ji et al., 2023): Built on the MuJoCo physics simulator, Safety-Gymnasium provides safety-critical continuous-control environments with diverse robot-task combinations. In our experiments, we evaluate Point and Car agents on Button, Goal, and Push tasks, denoted as PB, CB, PG, CG, PP, and CP, respectively. In the Goal task, the agent is required to navigate toward a target location while avoiding unsafe regions. In the Push task, the agent must move an object to a designated goal position, which introduces more complex contact dynamics and longer-horizon planning. In the Button task, the agent needs to reach and activate target buttons scattered in the environment. These tasks involve heterogeneous safety requirements, including collision avoidance, high-velocity limitation, and low-velocity regulation, making them suitable for evaluating policy learning under multiple simultaneous constraints. 

Bullet-Safety-Gym (Liu, 2023a): Built on the PyBullet physics simulator, Bullet-Safety-Gym contains lightweight yet challenging safety-control tasks for different robotic agents. We evaluate three Circle tasks with Ball, Car, and Drone agents, denoted as BC, CC, and DC, respectively. In the Circle task, the agent is encouraged to move along a circular trajectory while satisfying safety constraints related to boundary violation and velocity control. Compared with Safety-Gymnasium, Bullet-Safety-Gym provides shorter and more focused locomotion tasks, which allow us to examine whether an offline safe RL method can efficiently balance task reward and constraint satisfaction across different robot dynamics. For both benchmark suites, we construct two experimental set-tings: the -v2 setting includes boundary/collision and high-velocity constraints, while the more challenging -v3 setting further introduces a low-velocity constraint. Therefore, the evaluated tasks cover both two-constraint and three-constraint scenarios, enabling a comprehensive assessment of multi-constraint safety generalization. The offline datasets used for each task are sourced from OSRL (Liu et al., 2023a). Fig. 1 visualizes some representative tasks of these environments. 

E.2 Baselines 

We provide a more detailed introduction to the baselines used in our experiments in this section. 

• BCQ-Lag is a Lagrangian extension of Batch-Constrained Q-learning (BCQ) for offline safe reinforcement learning. BCQ constrains the learned policy to select actions close to the offline dataset distribution, thereby reducing extrapolation errors caused by out-of-distribution actions. In BCQ-Lag, safety constraints are handled by introducing Lagrangian multipliers into the policy optimization objective. For the multi-constraint setting, we extend the Lagrangian formulation with multiple dual variables, each corresponding to one safety constraint. 

• BEAR-Lag is a Lagrangian variant of Bootstrapping Error Accumulation Reduction (BEAR). BEAR regularizes the learned policy by minimizing the divergence between policy actions and dataset actions, which helps reduce bootstrapping errors in offline reinforcement learning. BEAR-Lag further incorporates safety constraints through Lagrangian relaxation. Similar to BCQ-Lag, we adapt BEAR-Lag to multi-constraint scenarios by using separate Lagrangian multipliers for different constraints. 

• PDCA is a recent primal-dual critic-based offline constrained reinforcement learning method. It estimates the Lagrangian objective with learned reward and cost critics, and alternates between primal policy improvement and dual variable updates. Compared with classical Lagrangian baselines, PDCA provides a more principled primal-dual optimization framework for offline constrained policy learning. In our experiments, we extend PDCA to the multi-constraint setting by assigning an independent dual variable to each constraint. 

• POCE is a recent conservative offline constrained reinforcement learning algorithm designed for multi-constraint settings. It uses conservative value estimation to improve policy learning under safety constraints and reduces the risk of selecting unsafe out-of-distribution actions. Compared with BCQ-Lag and BEAR-Lag, POCE is more directly related to multi-constraint offline safe RL, since it explicitly considers multiple cost signals during policy optimization. 

• OASIS is a recent offline safe reinforcement learning method based on conditional distribution shaping. It aims to improve the quality of offline data by generating or reshaping trajectories toward safer and more rewarding regions. In our experiments, OASIS is adapted to multi-constraint tasks by treating a trajectory as feasible only when all constraint costs are below their corresponding safety thresholds. 

• CAPS is a constraint-adaptive policy switching method for offline safe reinforcement learning. It trains policies with different reward-cost trade-offs and chooses an appropriate policy according to the given safety budget during deployment. This makes CAPS suitable for evaluating constraint adaptation without retraining. Since the original formulation mainly considers scalar safety budgets, we adapt CAPS to the multi-constraint setting by selecting policies according to the maximum normalized constraint violation, i.e., Cost-N, and by requiring all normalized costs to satisfy the target thresholds. 

• CCAC is a constraint-conditioned actor-critic method for offline safe reinforcement learning. It conditions the policy and value functions on safety thresholds, enabling the learned policy to adapt to different constraint budgets at test time. In our experiments, we extend CCAC to multi-constraint tasks by conditioning the policy on the multi-dimensional threshold vector and evaluating safety using the maximum normalized violation cost. 

• BC-Safe is a behavior cloning baseline trained only on safe trajectories from the offline dataset. Specifically, trajectories whose costs satisfy all safety budgets are selected to train a supervised policy. This baseline provides a conservative reference for multi-constraint offline safe RL. • CDT-MC is a multi-constraint adaptation of the Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT). It extends the standard Decision Transformer by incorporating cost-to-go (CTG) as an additional condition, allowing the policy to generate actions according to both the target return-to-go (RTG) and the remaining safety budget. In our multi-constraint experiments, CDT-MC is extended by concatenating multiple CTG tokens corresponding to different constraints. 

• CoPDT-MC is a multi-constraint adaptation of the recent sequence modeling method Constraint Prioritized Decision Transformer (CoPDT). CoPDT is designed to prioritize constraint satisfac-tion during offline policy learning. To handle our settings, CoPDT-MC extends this framework by incorporating multiple cost constraints into its sequence modeling architecture. 

• CORDT is a Transformer-based variant of our framework. It retains the constraint-specific expert routing and Product-of-Experts (PoE) fusion mechanism, but replaces the Mamba-based conditional action decoder with a Transformer backbone. This baseline is designed to isolate the contribution of the Mamba architecture. By comparing CORDT with CORMA, we can evaluate whether the linear-complexity selective state-space model improves sequence modeling efficiency and long-horizon decision-making under multi-constraint conditions. 

• CORMA w/o PoE is an ablation baseline that completely removes the constraint-specific experts and the PoE fusion mechanism. It directly utilizes the empirical RTG and concatenated CTGs as inputs for the Mamba action decoder, without explicitly modeling the feasible RTG distribution. This baseline is used to evaluate the necessity and effectiveness of our constraint-routed RTG estimation module. 

• CORMA-S is an ablation baseline that removes the constraint-decoupled architecture. Instead of using multiple constraint-specific experts, it employs a single monolithic expert to directly model the joint RTG distribution conditioned on all CTG tokens simultaneously. This helps verify whether a single unified network is sufficient to disentangle conflicting safety requirements compared to our decoupled routing mechanism.

## F.More Experimental Results 

F.1 Analysis on Safe Policy Learning under Multiple Constraints 

Tables 4 and 5 report the performance of different algorithms under the seen safety-budget combi-nations contained in the offline datasets. The v2 tasks involve two constraint dimensions, whereas the v3 tasks additionally introduce a low-velocity constraint, resulting in more complex interactions among heterogeneous safety requirements. These experiments evaluate whether each method can adapt to different multi-dimensional safety budgets observed during training while maintaining both constraint satisfaction and competitive task performance. Overall, CORMA achieves the most stable reward–safety trade-off across the evaluated settings. Among the 30 task–budget combina-tions, CORMA satisfies the normalized safety threshold in 26 cases, substantially more than the other sequence-modeling variants. Averaged over all combinations, CORMA achieves a normalized reward of approximately 81 .4 with an average Cost-N of 0.96 . These results indicate that CORMA can adapt its behavior to different safety-budget configurations without relying on a single fixed constraint setting. On the v2 tasks, CORMA maintains Cost-N below 1.0 under all 15 safety-budget combinations. It also achieves the best safety-performance trade-off in six combinations, including S1 and S2 on BC-v2, S1 on CC-v2, and S2, S4, and S5 on DC-v2. Although several conventional offline safe RL methods, such as OASIS, CAPS, and CCAC, also obtain feasible results under some two-constraint settings, their reward–safety trade-offs vary across tasks and budget combinations. In contrast, CDT-MC and CoPDT-MC frequently violate the safety threshold despite attaining relatively high rewards, indicating that directly conditioning a sequence model on multiple CTG tokens is insufficient for reliably coordinating heterogeneous constraints. The three-constraint results further reveal the increased difficulty of multi-constraint policy learning. CORMA remains feasible in 11 of the 15 v3 task–budget combinations and achieves an average Cost-N of approximately 0.98 . F.2 Analysis on Trajectory Stitching Ability 

This subsection evaluates the trajectory stitching ability of different sequence-modeling methods in multi-constraint offline safe reinforcement learning. To construct a more challenging setting, we remove from the offline datasets all complete trajectories that directly satisfy the target safety-budget combinations. Consequently, the policy cannot satisfy all constraints by simply imitating a complete jointly feasible trajectory. Instead, it must learn complementary local behaviors from different trajectories and compose them into a new safe decision sequence during deployment. We compare CDT-MC, CORMA w/o PoE, CORDT, and the full CORMA on Bullet-Safety-Gym and Safety-Gymnasium tasks. The normalized reward measures task performance, while Cost-N

measures the maximum normalized violation among all constraints. As shown in Table 6, CDT-MC and CORMA w/o PoE achieve average normalized rewards of 80 .2

and 78 .9, respectively, but their average Cost-N values reach 1.52 and 1.27 , both clearly exceeding the safety threshold. CDT-MC satisfies all constraints only on BC-v2 and exhibits different degrees of safety violation on the remaining tasks. In particular, although CDT-MC achieves relatively high rewards on three-constraint tasks such as BC-v3, DC-v3, and PB-v3, its Cost-N values reach 2.04 ,

2.16 , and 2.04 , respectively. This indicates that its generated behaviors remain strongly biased toward reward maximization and cannot reliably compose local behaviors that satisfy different constraints. CORMA w/o PoE reduces the average Cost-N to 1.27 , suggesting that the Mamba action decoder provides some benefit for long-horizon behavior modeling. Nevertheless, this variant fails to satisfy the safety threshold on all 12 tasks, indicating that conditioning Mamba only on multiple CTGs and a predefined RTG is insufficient to address multi-constraint behavior composition when jointly feasible trajectories are absent. By introducing constraint-specific experts, a router, and PoE fusion, CORDT further reduces the average Cost-N to 1.11 and satisfies the safety threshold on five tasks: BC-v2, DC-v2, DC-v3, PB-v3, and CG-v3. Compared with CDT-MC and CORMA w/o PoE, these results indicate that explicitly modeling feasible RTG distributions under different constraints helps extract more effective high-level guidance from trajectories with complementary safety properties. For example, CORDT achieves reward–safety results of 84 .8/0.99 and 87 .0/0.99 on DC-v3 and CG-v3, respectively, demonstrating that it can effectively compose safe behaviors from different trajectories in some tasks. However, CORDT still violates the constraints on CC-v2, BC-v3, CC-v3, CB-v3, PG-v3, PP-v3, and CP-v3. This suggests that, with a Transformer-based action decoder, the fused RTG guidance cannot always be translated into temporally consistent safe actions over long horizons. In contrast, CORMA achieves the most stable safety performance while maintaining competitive task rewards. Its average reward is 80 .2, which is comparable to 80 .2 for CDT-MC and 80 .4 for CORDT, whereas its average Cost-N is only 0.97 . More importantly, CORMA is the only method that satisfies the safety threshold on all 12 tasks, with Cost-N consistently ranging from 0.93 to 0.98 .This shows that the safety advantage of CORMA is not limited to a few favorable environments, but remains stable across both two-constraint and three-constraint tasks. In particular, on BC-v3, CC-v3, PG-v3, PP-v3, and CP-v3, all competing methods violate the constraints, whereas CORMA still achieves feasible reward–safety results of 86 .5/0.98 , 64 .2/0.98 , 86 .8/0.95 , 71 .4/0.96 , and 

82 .3/0.98 , respectively.Several tasks also illustrate the adaptive reward–safety trade-off achieved by CORMA. On CC-v3, CDT-MC and CORMA w/o PoE obtain rewards of 70 .7 and 73 .2, respectively, but their corresponding Cost-N values are 1.18 and 1.26 . CORMA obtains a lower reward of 64 .2,but successfully controls Cost-N at 0.98 . Similarly, on DC-v3 and CG-v3, CORDT achieves slightly higher feasible rewards than CORMA, whereas CORMA preserves a larger safety margin. These results demonstrate that CORMA possesses a certain degree of trajectory stitching ability. The constraint-specific experts extract feasible return information for individual constraints from different trajectories, while the router-guided PoE mechanism coordinates these complementary signals according to the current state and remaining safety budgets. The Mamba action decoder then converts the fused high-level guidance into temporally consistent safe action sequences over long horizons. Compared with CDT-MC, CORMA w/o PoE, and CORDT, CORMA does not achieve safety by substantially sacrificing the average reward. Instead, it provides a more stable reward–safety trade-off, supporting its ability to stitch trajectories from incomplete and constraint-conflicting offline data.

F.5 How Critical Is the Three-Stage Training Strategy? 

To evaluate the effectiveness and generality of the training mechanism in CORMA, we conduct an ablation study on four challenging three-constraint tasks: DC-v3, PB-v3, CG-v3, and CP-v3. We compare the full CORMA with four variants: (1) w/o 3-stage Training, which jointly optimizes the action decoder, constraint-specific experts, and router; (2) w/ Mean-Regression Router, which replaces the PoE negative log-likelihood in Stage III with an MSE loss on the fused RTG mean; (3) w/o Balanced Router Sampling, which samples the constraint-specific subsets according to their original data proportions rather than with equal probability; and (4) w/o Expert Freezing, which updates the expert parameters together with the router during Stage III. As shown in Table 9, the full CORMA achieves the most stable reward–safety trade-off across all four tasks, obtaining an average normalized reward of 89 .4 while maintaining an average Cost-N

of 0.94 . More importantly, it is the only variant that satisfies all constraints on every evaluated task.Jointly optimizing the action decoder, experts, and router produces the highest average reward of 90 .4, but increases the average Cost-N to 2.48 . This substantial safety degradation indicates that simultaneous optimization exposes the router to continuously changing expert distributions and introduces interference among the objectives of the three modules.Replacing the PoE likelihood with mean regression reduces the average Cost-N to 1.26 , but still fails to satisfy the safety thresh-old on all four tasks. This result suggests that aligning only the fused RTG mean is insufficient, because the router cannot exploit the predictive variances of the constraint-specific expert distri-butions.Removing balanced router sampling results in an average Cost-N of 1.15 . Although this variant performs better than joint training and mean regression, it remains unsafe across all evaluated tasks. This confirms that sampling the constraint-specific subsets according to their natural frequen-cies may cause constraints with larger feasible subsets to dominate router optimization.Unfreezing the experts during Stage III further degrades both reward and safety, yielding an average reward of 84 .2 and an average Cost-N of 1.38 . Updating the experts together with the router causes the constraint-specific RTG distributions learned in Stage II to drift, thereby creating non-stationary targets for PoE fusion. Overall, these results demonstrate that sequential module specialization, distributional PoE su-pervision, balanced router sampling, and expert freezing are jointly important for achieving stable multi-constraint RTG estimation and constraint satisfaction.
