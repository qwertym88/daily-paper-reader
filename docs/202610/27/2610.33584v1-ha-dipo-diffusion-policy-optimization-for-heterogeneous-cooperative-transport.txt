# HA-DiPO: Diffusion Policy Optimization for Heterogeneous Cooperative Transport 

Xuebin Li 1, Jinpeng Xiao 2, Zong Chen 2, Pierluigi Arpenti 3,Bruno Siciliano 3, Jiebao Sun 1, and Yiqun Li 2,∗

Abstract — Cooperative transport with heterogeneous legged robots requires coordinating different motion capabilities while supporting an unfixed shared payload. We present HA-DiPO (Heterogeneous-Agent Diffusion Policy Optimization), a hier-archical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework under centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE). The framework decouples team-level coordination from embodiment-specific motion control: at the high level, a centralized critic uses team-value estimates to jointly train independent coordination policies, aligning robot-specific action responses with a shared transport objective; at the low level, embodiment-specific robust motion policies track the coordination commands. The high-level diffusion policies offer more flexible action distributions than single-Gaussian parameterizations, allowing potentially multimodal coordination responses to be represented. The coordination policies learn from scratch through reward-driven simulator interaction without demonstrations. At execution, each robot generates coordination commands using only local robot and payload observations, without partner-state inputs or inter-robot messages. Simulation comparisons show that HA-DiPO achieves higher final mean returns and velocity-tracking success rates, as well as lower velocity errors, than the evaluated baselines. Real-world experiments further demonstrate quadrupedal–wheeled-legged and human–robot cooperative transport. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Cooperative transport of long or heavy objects is an important application of multi-robot systems in industrial and field operations [1]. Robots with different motion capabilities can share the load, but must coordinate their motion while maintaining stable support. When the payload rests on the robots without rigid attachment, their actions are coupled through contact: acceleration, slip, or a posture adjustment by one robot can alter the payload state and its partners’ support conditions. Differences in actuation and motion response further complicate coordination, making identical commands insufficient to ensure consistent payload motion. We study how such robots can learn to coordinate through interaction and transport an unfixed payload using local observations.

However, extending existing transport approaches to het-erogeneous teams carrying an unfixed payload remains challenging. First, some transport systems rely on predefined formation geometry or dedicated coupling mechanisms [2], [3]. These approaches embed coordination in particular geometric constraints or mechanical interfaces, which must be reconsidered when support contacts can shift. Other systems rely on external motion capture for state feed-back [4]. Second, continuous-action multi-agent learning commonly uses single-Gaussian policy parameterizations, which offer limited flexibility for representing multimodal coordination behaviors [5]. Diffusion policies offer richer action-distribution representations [6], but demonstration-driven diffusion approaches to cooperative transport remain dependent on the availability and coverage of expert data [7]. Finally, decentralized methods based on parameter sharing or single-robot policy reuse [8], [9] do not explicitly address joint learning of robot-specific policies that accommodate different motion capabilities while coordinating through the shared payload. To this end, we propose HA-DiPO, a hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for heterogeneous loose-contact transport. The framework separates team-level coordination from embodiment-specific locomotion (Fig. 1): high-level policies learn how to move and support the shared payload, while robot-specific low-level policies handle robust command tracking. This division allows coordination to be learned through base-motion commands while retaining con-trollers tailored to each robot’s dynamics and actuation. Under centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE), a centralized critic guides independent diffusion actors through team-value estimates. The high-level policies learn from scratch through reward-driven simulator interaction, without expert demonstrations or imitation pretraining; the pretrained low-level policies remain frozen during coordination learning. Onboard payload estimates enable each robot to execute from local robot and payload observations, without partner-state inputs or inter-robot messages. Within this hierarchy, each actor learns a value-guided response to its current teammates. These responses are shaped by action value and need not be unimodal, motivating diffu-sion actors that offer greater flexibility than single-Gaussian policies in representing distinct coordination responses [6], [10]. A variational objective over diffusion paths makes these policies trainable without explicit action-density evaluation. During training, actors are updated sequentially using the latest teammate policies [11].

Cooperative transport requires coordinating robot motion while maintaining support of a shared payload [1]. Model-based approaches organize this coupled task through explicit control structures, including task-priority optimization [2], layered control [12], and passive-arm model predictive control [3]. Learning-based methods complement these de-signs by acquiring cooperative behavior from interaction or demonstrations. Reinforcement learning has enabled tether-suspended transport and decentralized carrying [8], [13], while demonstration-based learning has produced a joint diffusion policy integrated with whole-body control [7]. Cooperation with heterogeneous robots or humans addition-ally requires adaptation to differing motion capabilities and partner behavior. Physical-interaction feedback provides one basis for this adaptation: force prediction and deformation-agnostic control support human–robot carrying [14], [15], while proprioceptive learning enables human–humanoid co-ordination [16]. Inferring interaction wrenches from propri-oceptive histories further supports the reuse of a transport policy with diverse partners [9]. A complementary approach is to jointly train interacting policies, as demonstrated in heterogeneous human–robot collaboration with trainable humanoid partners [17]. In this setting, CTDE supports team coordination through centralized value learning while retaining local actor inputs at execution [18]; sequential training updates allow each policy to respond to updated teammates [11]. Alongside the coordination mechanism, policy parame-terization determines the action distributions that can be represented. Diffusion policies provide a flexible alterna-tive to single-Gaussian actors, initially demonstrating multi-modal manipulation behavior through imitation learning [6]. Demonstration-based generative action modeling has also been extended to vision–language–action systems through diffusion and flow matching [19], [20]. Reward-driven op-timization provides a complementary route for improving these policies through interaction, encompassing online diffusion-policy learning [10] and policy-gradient fine-tuning of demonstration-pretrained actors [21]. Recent extensions to cooperative multi-agent learning further connect expressive diffusion policies with coordinated exploration [5]. Taken together, these studies motivate jointly learning expressive, robot-specific coordination policies for hetero-geneous loose-contact transport. We separate team-level coordination from embodiment-specific locomotion and train independent diffusion actors through centralized team-value guidance and variational optimization. The coordination policies learn from scratch without demonstrations and execute using local observations. III. D IFFUSION POLICY OPTIMIZATION

The experimental evaluation combines quantitative simu-lation benchmarks, controlled policy-representation analysis, and real-world deployment to address three main questions. First, does HA-DiPO improve cooperative transport perfor-mance compared with existing multi-agent reinforcement learning baselines? Second, do diffusion actors provide a representational advantage over Gaussian policies when coordination requires distinct high-value action responses? Third, can the learned coordination behavior be deployed with heterogeneous robot and human partners while preserving decentralized execution from local observations? For all reported evaluations, HA-DiPO uses three 256-unit hidden layers, GELU activations, and Fourier time embeddings in each diffusion network, with N = 8 sampling steps. Each centralized critic is a ReLU MLP with hidden widths (256 , 256 , 128) , trained with Huber threshold η =10 . Actors and critics are optimized with Adam, while the entropy temperatures are adjusted automatically during training. Training is performed on NVIDIA A40 GPUs using a server equipped with two Intel Xeon Gold 6346 CPUs and 1 TB of RAM. 

A. Cooperative Transport Performance 

Isaac Lab-trained policies transfer directly to MuJoCo for forward, backward, lateral, turning, and payload-disturbance tests (Fig. 3(a)–(f)). Training and independent evaluation use Isaac Lab [25], with a Unitree B2 quadruped and B2W wheeled-legged robot supporting an unfixed cylindrical payload. Other elongated cross-sections can be addressed by adapting perception and training settings. Each coordi-nation actor receives six 28-D frames (168 dimensions) of simulator-provided robot and payload observations, excludes teammate states, and outputs the 5-D command ai =[vx,i , v y,i , ˙ψi, h b,i , θ b,i ] through the hierarchy (Section IV-A). The two HA-DiPO actors share architecture, observation– action interfaces, and bounds but have independent parame-ters; platform heterogeneity arises from distinct actuation and locomotion dynamics. The frozen low-level policies output 12 leg-joint position residuals for B2 and 16 commands for B2W: 12 leg-joint position residuals and four wheel velocities. This work introduced HA-DiPO, a hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for cooperative transport with heterogeneous robots supporting an unfixed shared payload. Robot-specific diffusion policies are coupled during training through a centralized team-value function, while decentralized execution relies only on local observations. A variational diffusion objective enables value-guided policy optimization without explicit action-density evaluation, and sequential updates account for evolving teammate policies. The evaluations show that HA-DiPO can represent distinct high-value action responses more effectively than Gaussian policies and improve cooperative transport performance. In simulation, it achieves the highest mean survival and velocity-tracking success among the evaluated baselines, with a tracking success rate of 0.935 ± 0.074 and a planar-velocity RMSE of 0.0476 ± 0.0036 m/s. The learned policies are also deployed on a heterogeneous quadrupedal–wheeled-legged team and with a human partner using only local robot and payload observations. These results support the use of team-value-guided, robot-specific diffusion policies for coordinating heterogeneous embodiments. Future work will focus on larger teams, broader payload and environment variations, and more extensive quantitative real-world validation. APPENDIX ATHEORETICAL ANALYSIS
