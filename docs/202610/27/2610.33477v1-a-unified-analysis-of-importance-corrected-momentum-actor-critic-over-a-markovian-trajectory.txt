Abstract 

We present a generic actor-critic method that combines a ridge-regularized pathwise least-squares temporal-difference (LSTD) critic with importance-corrected momentum (ICM) actors. The critic solves a ridge-regularized system per frame by Newton-Schulz inversion and needs no stepsize, no knowledge of the mixing time, and no rank condition. Averaging stochastic policy gradients over frames of T consecutive transitions, the ICM actors incur a frame-dependent gradient bias of order 1/T . To our knowledge, we establish the first finite-time bounds for ICM actor-critic methods with normalized and unnormalized steps along a single Markovian trajectory. Up to a stationary-to-discounted distribution mismatch, the averaged gradient norm over K updates is bounded by O(K−1/3 + 1/T ) for normalized steps and by O(K−1/4 + 1/T ) for unnormalized steps. We specialize these bounds to Muon, normalized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum, SGD with momentum, and AdaNorm, and compare them in the nuclear norm. With a dimension-free stepsize, Muon confines the factor min {m, n }1/2 associated with an m×n actor parameter matrix to the noise and correction terms. The comparison also characterizes when orthogonalization guarantees a larger noiseless ascent than normalization: exactly when the gradient’s nuclear rank exceeds the spectral-to-Frobenius curvature ratio. 

Keywords: Actor-critic methods, importance-corrected momentum, least-squares tem-poral difference, Markovian sampling 

1 Introduction 

Actor-critic (AC) methods improve the policy along a policy-gradient (PG) estimate where a learned critic replaces Monte Carlo returns to reduce gradient variance (Konda and Tsitsik-lis, 1999; Sutton et al., 1999; Sutton and Barto, 2018). Convergence analyses of AC methods bound the propagation of critic error into actor update asymptotically (Konda and Tsit-siklis, 1999; Zhang et al., 2020b) and non-asymptotically for double-loop (Qiu et al., 2021; Kumar et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2021), two-timescale (Wu et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2024; Zeng and Doan, 2024), and single-timescale (Olshevsky and Gharesifard, 2023; Chen and Zhao, 2023; Shen and Chen, 2022) settings. The vanilla critic performs semi-gradient temporal-difference (TD) policy evaluation (Bhandari et al., 2021) Moreover, the vanilla critic can be accelerated by adaptive stepsizes and momentum accelerate (Sun et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2026), and freed from knowledge of the mixing

time by multi-level Monte Carlo PG estimators (Dorfman and Levy, 2022; Suttle et al., 2023; Patel et al., 2024; Ganesh et al., 2025) 

Research gaps. While current convergence analyses of AC methods update the actor by unnormalized gradients (Wu et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2020a,b; Qiu et al., 2021; Kumar et al., 2023; Olshevsky and Gharesifard, 2023; Chen and Zhao, 2023), recent actor designs depart from such steps in two ways. First, an importance-corrected momentum (ICM) esti-mator (Huang et al., 2020) can reduce momentum variance by reweighting the previous PG estimator on the current trajectory via importance sampling. Second, the momentum could be normalized (Cutkosky and Mehta, 2020) and orthogonalized (Jordan et al., 2024; Chang et al., 2025; Lion et al., 2026). For example, Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) applies the Newton-Schulz orthogonalization (NSO) (Kelley, 1995) to the momentum matrix and moves the model parameter along an approximation of momentum polar factor. Moreover, a normal-ized step can bound the importance weights without further assumptions (Barakat et al., 2023). To our knowledge, such orthogonalization is absent from policy updates reshaped by Fisher preconditioning (Kakade, 2001; Peters and Schaal, 2008; Agarwal et al., 2021; Kho-dadadian et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2017), mirror descent (Yang et al., 2022; Lan, 2023), trust-region and proximal surrogates (Schulman et al., 2015, 2017), momentum (Huang et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2024), and Adam-type steps (Xiong et al., 2021). Besides, Muon-based reinforcement learning has only been investigated empirically (Ruan et al., 2026; Fan et al., 2026). The current finite-time analyses of normalized and orthogonalized momentum meth-ods assume unbiased independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) gradients, e.g., nor-malized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum (Cutkosky and Mehta, 2020), Muon with finite NSO (Kim and Oh, 2026) or variance reduction (Chang et al., 2025), and linear-minimization-oracle methods (Pethick et al., 2025; Kovalev, 2025; Shen et al., 2025; Riabinin et al., 2026). The requirement of unbiased i.i.d. gradients cannot be met by reinforcement learning tasks along a single trajectory, whose PG estimators exhibit bias and correlated noise: the score-weighted TD-based PG estimator relies on a critic that is still being learned, and the sample trajectory is drawn from a Markov chain induced by the evolving actor. Prior works leave the underlying three aspects unaddressed when normalized and orthogonalized ICM actors learn along a single trajectory that consists of T transitions per consecutive frame. i. Analyses of normalized and orthogonalized momentum are based on unbiased gra-dients (Cutkosky and Mehta, 2020; Barakat et al., 2023; Kim and Oh, 2026; Chang et al., 2025; Pethick et al., 2025; Kovalev, 2025; Shen et al., 2025; Riabinin et al., 2026), analyses of biased stochastic approximation and SGD control the bias only through its norm (Karimi et al., 2019; Ajalloeian and Stich, 2020; Demidovich et al., 2023), and variance-reduced methods with biased estimators assume a mean field that depends on the current iterate alone (Hu et al., 2020; Khanduri et al., 2021), whereas the frame estimates of an AC loop have a bias that depends on the iterate and on a start state generated by the previous frame. ii. Existing finite-time AC analyses are confined to unnormalized steps, whose progress is controlled through second moments (Wu et al., 2020; Olshevsky and Gharesifard, 2023; Chen and Zhao, 2023). Normalized steps, in contrast, necessitate first-moment control of the momentum’s tracking error, and ICM actors are not accommodated.

iii. ICM has been analyzed for i.i.d. samples (Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019) and for restarted episodes (Huang et al., 2020; Barakat et al., 2023),whereas along a single trajectory the kernel evolves with the actor and the importance weight corrects the actions of a frame but not its start state, which couples consecutive frames. 

Our contributions. Different from the AC framework of Kumar et al. (2023) that combines a fixed SGD actor with several critic under i.i.d. sampling, our AC framework averages the PG estimator over a frame attenuates the critic error by the frame length such that the ICM-based actor update determines the convergence rate under a single Markovian trajectory. Therefore, by fixing the critic, the PG estimator, and the ICM, we investigate how different actor updates govern the convergence rate and the dependence on the rank of the actor parameter. The critic is a ridge-regularized pathwise least-squares TD (LSTD) critic, i.e., the pathwise LSTD critic of Lazaric et al. (2012) with a ridge term. By leveraging Newton-Schulz inversion (NSI) that requires only general matrix-matrix multiplications (GEMMs), we approximate the critic parameter while fully exploiting the parallel computing power of graphics processing units (GPUs). Our contributions are summarized as follows. 

• An inverse-free LSTD critic. Our proposed inverse-free critic can approximate the ridge-regularized pathwise LSTD solution with GEMMs alone for every ridge param-eter λ > 0 and every frame length T ; therefore, does not require stepsize, knowledge of the mixing time, and rank condition on the actor parameter. Theorem 4 quantifies both sources of critic error: the NSI error decays superlinearly in the number P1 of NSI recursions, and the mean-square Markovian sampling error is of order τmix /T .

• Generic bounds over a single Markovian trajectory. Conditioned on the start state of each frame and the lagged critic, the noise of the PG estimators forms a martingale-difference sequence across frames, and their bias has two parts: the stationary-to-discounted mismatch (that enters the convergence rate only once due to the cancella-tion of importance correction between consecutive iterates) and the frame bias (which is of order 1/T and varies with the actor parameter at a rate of order 1/T ). With such decomposition, every actor update inherits a finite-time convergence rate in the nuclear norm: an O(K−1/3) transient for normalized actor updates and an O(K−1/4)transient for unnormalized ones (Theorems 9 and 10), plus a floor formed by the mismatch and the frame bias. 

• Instances and comparisons. We apply our theoretical results to normalized actor updates, such as Muon and normalized SGD with momentum (NSGDM), and to unnormalized ones, such as SGD with momentum (SGDM) and an adaptive-stepsize actor (AdaNorm). We establish that the Muon actor update retains at least 78% of the first-order progress of the exact polar step along the momentum without a conditioning assumption after O(log r) NSO recursions with the actor parameter rank 

r ≤ min {m, n }. Comparing the instances, we identify two effects: normalization alone explains the faster rate, and orthogonalization with a dimension-free stepsize confines the factor r1/2 to the noise and correction terms. We also characterize when orthogonalization guarantees a larger noiseless ascent than normalization: exactly

To our knowledge, Theorems 9 and 10 and their instances are the first finite-time con-vergence rates for ICM actors along a single Markovian trajectory with a bootstrapped critic. The floor of our bounds isolates the stationary-to-discounted mismatch of uniformly weighted samples, a bias that practical AC implementations share (Thomas, 2014; Nota and Thomas, 2020; Zhang et al., 2022); every other floor term decays at least as 1/T . 4 Generic Finite-Time Analysis 

In this section, we establish the finite-time analysis of Algorithm 1 when all frames are con-secutive segments of a single continuing trajectory such that the observations are correlated within and across frames. More specifically, Sections 4.1–4.4 bound the critic error, split the bias of the PG estimator, bound the tracking error of the ICM, and turn the tracking error into the convergence rates of the actor, respectively. Wk and Wk−1, the lagged critic vk−1, and the start state sk, 0 are Fk−1-measurable; hence, conditionally on Fk−1, Ok is a T -step trajectory of πk from sk, 0.

5.4 Comparison 

Since the four actors share the critic, the frames, the PG estimator, and the ICM, the differences among the bounds in Table 2 stem from the actor updates up to the evaluation of ¯ σ and ϵbias along the respective iterates and the constant factors, e.g., √2 and 1 + χ, that reflect the proof technique. 

Rate. The bounds decay as O(K−1/3) for Muon and NSGDM and as O(K−1/4) for SGDM and AdaNorm. Since all bounds contain the drift ηD F β/(1 −η) of the frame bias (cf. (25)), they require β = O(1 − η). Moreover, the SGDM and AdaNorm steps, which scale with Mk, additionally require 2 ηC F ¯β ≤ 1− η and 2 T R πRg ¯β ≤ 1 to keep ∥Mk∥F ≤ 2Rg

and the importance weights bounded (Lemma 18), whereas the Muon and NSGDM actors admit any c > 0. Under the proportional schedule, the descent inequalities of normalized actor updates, which are linear in the tracking error, balance ∆1/(βK ) against √1 − η ¯σ at 

K−1/3, whereas those of unnormalized actor updates, which are quadratic in the gradient norm, balance ∆1/(βK ) against (1 − η)¯ σ2 at K−1/2 for the squared norm. Hence, the better exponent in these bounds is due to normalization, which the Muon actor update shares with NSGDM. Note that the coupling 1 − η ∝ β2 behind the complexity O(ϵ−3) of the unnormalized ICM actor with unbiased estimates (Huang et al., 2020) is unavailable here since it lets the drift of the frame bias grow as DF/β. Besides, without the importance correction, both normalized bounds decay only as O(K−1/4) (Remark 1); therefore, the faster rate in these upper bounds requires both normalization and the importance correction.

6 Conclusions 

In this work, we have established the first finite-time convergence rates for ICM actors that learn along a single Markovian trajectory with an inverse-free ridge-regularized pathwise LSTD critic. Up to the stationary-to-discounted mismatch and an O(1/T ) frame bias, the averaged gradient norm decays as O(K−1/3) for normalized actor updates and as O(K−1/4)for unnormalized ones. Among the instances, Muon with a dimension-free stepsize confines the factor r1/2 to the noise and correction terms, and orthogonalization guarantees a larger noiseless ascent than normalization exactly when the nuclear rank of the PG exceeds the spectral-to-Frobenius curvature ratio. Future work includes the sampling protocols that remove the mismatch, the multilevel frames (Dorfman and Levy, 2022; Suttle et al., 2023) that lower the transition cost per update, and the empirical comparisons of the actor updates at equal transition budgets.
