## ABSTRACT 

Several state-of-the-art methods for online reinforcement learning in continuous control improve policies using action gradients of a learned critic. However, critics are typically trained to predict returns, and accurate value predictions do not necessarily yield accurate action derivatives, potentially leading to unreliable policy updates. We propose Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization (FERPO), an on-policy maximum entropy reinforcement learning algorithm that performs policy improvement using critic values without differentiating the critic with respect to actions. FERPO derives an optimal target action distribution from a policy-improvement objective regularized by entropy and Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence. We then fit the actor to this target by minimizing a forward-KL ob-jective, estimated using self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) with actions drawn from the rollout policy. By limiting the target distribution’s deviation from the rollout policy, the KL regularization helps keep these importance weights well behaved. In contrast to reverse-KL objectives, which can favor a subset of the tar-get distribution’s modes, the forward-KL objective encourages coverage of mul-tiple high-value modes and thereby promotes exploration. Experiments and abla-tions on MuJoCo Playground and ManiSkill show competitive performance and sample-efficiency gains. Computational benchmarks also demonstrate faster ac-tor updates than Relative Entropy Pathwise Policy Optimization (REPPO). Our implementation is publicly available. 1

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) learns policies through interaction to maximize cumulative reward. Its successes range from game playing (Silver et al., 2017) to autonomous drone racing (Kaufmann et al., 2023) and robot locomotion (Ha et al., 2025). In robotics, massively parallel simulation has accelerated on-policy learning by collecting trajectories from thousands of environments simulta-neously. This has enabled rapid policy training and successful transfer from simulation to physical robots (Rudin et al., 2022; Zakka et al., 2025). These advances make efficient and reliable policy improvement central to scaling online robot learning. Relative Entropy Pathwise Policy Optimization (REPPO; Voelcker et al., 2026) is a recent state-of-the-art on-policy method for this setting. It learns a soft action-value critic from on-policy tra-jectories and improves the actor by differentiating the critic with respect to actions, while limiting policy changes relative to the rollout policy. This makes pathwise actor updates compatible with massively parallel on-policy learning. However, standard critic training supervises values, whereas these actor updates require accurate action derivatives. Accurate value predictions do not necessarily imply accurate derivatives: critic-gradient error can bias policy improvement (D’Oro & Ja´ skowski, 2020), and learned action gradients can show poor directional agreement with rollout-based esti-mates (Sarafian et al., 2021). In on-policy learning, biased gradients can also interact with changing data distributions and destabilize training (Gurumurthy et al., 2023). These limitations motivate policy improvement that uses critic values without requiring their action derivatives.

## 3 METHOD : FERPO 

Pathwise actor updates require accurate critic action derivatives, although standard critic learning supervises values. Value accuracy alone does not ensure derivative accuracy (D’Oro & Ja´ skowski, 2020; Sarafian et al., 2021). This motivates an actor update that uses critic values without requiring 

∇aQϕ(s, a ). We introduce Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization (FERPO ). FERPO first minimizes the Lagrangian in Eq. 4 over unrestricted action distributions q(· | s) with fixed multipliers, then fits the parametric policy to the resulting target using the forward KL divergence combined with self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS). The temperature and trust-region mul-tiplier are adapted alongside the actor. Controlling importance-weight concentration. Using the optimal regularized distribution q∗ as the forward-KL target tempers the importance weights needed to fit the Boltzmann target pQ,τ di-rectly. For τ > 0, q∗(a | s)/π roll (a | s) ∝ [pQ,τ (a | s)/π roll (a | s)] τ / (τ +λ). For λ > 0, the 4Under review exponent lies below one, reducing log-weight differences and flattening the normalized weights on the same candidate actions. With zero entropy regularization ( τ = 0 , λ > 0), the explicit rollout-density factor cancels entirely, leaving wi(s) ∝ exp( Qϕ(s, a i)/λ ). For positive τ , the residual factor is πroll (ai | s)−τ / (τ +λ). For a fixed critic and rollout policy, increasing τ at fixed λ strengthens this inverse-density dependence and can increase weight concentration, although it also softens the value weighting, so the overall effect need not be monotone. The update of the Lagrange multiplier λ in Eq. 8 uses this flattening effect to limit weight concentration. At the population level, a lower bound on normalized ESS also gives an upper bound on the target’s KL divergence from the rollout policy. The following proposition also transfers this control to the fitted actor in TV when its population fitting loss does not increase. This motivates using ESS to adapt λ and connects it to the policy-proximity motivation in Section 2.

To improve learning of the soft action-value function Qϕ, we follow the critic-training approach of REPPO (Voelcker et al., 2026), combining multi-step temporal-difference (TD) targets with trace parameter λTD , the histogram loss with Gaussian targets (HL-Gauss) (Farebrother et al., 2024), and auxiliary prediction losses. The targets are computed from on-policy rollouts and include the entropy contributions to the soft return. Here λTD is the trace-decay parameter, separate from the actor’s KL coefficient λ. HL-Gauss encodes each scalar soft-return target as a Gaussian-smoothed histogram over value bins and trains the critic’s categorical output using cross-entropy. The scalar Qϕ(s, a ) is recovered as the mean of the predicted histogram. Following REPPO, auxiliary self-prediction trains the critic representation to predict the next transition’s latent representation, providing an additional learning signal alongside the value targets (Schwarzer et al., 2021; Voelcker et al., 2024; Fujimoto et al., 2025). 

## 4 RELATED WORK 

Constrained measure transport (CMT). Related methods use similar constrained target construc-tion and distribution fitting for sampling with normalizing flows (von Klitzing et al., 2026) and dif-fusion models (Blessing et al., 2025). Both adapt the trust-region multiplier λ using KL constraints. The normalizing-flow method additionally regulates temperature through a bound on entropy de-crease. 

Maximum a posteriori policy optimization. MPO is an off-policy method that alternates KL-constrained target construction with forward-KL policy fitting, using replay data and Retrace for critic learning (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018). Using our notation, its variational objective uses the augmented reward r(s, a ) − λ log( q(a | s)/π roll (a | s)) , with unrestricted improvement distribution 

q and frozen reference policy πroll . In MPO, this reference can differ from the behavior policies represented in replay. On-policy MPO would set q = πroll where the log ratio vanishes, so its critic estimates unregularized environment returns. MPO uses a KL constraint to limit the improvement of q away from πroll while this critic is held fixed (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018). Advantage-weighted policy fitting. Advantage-weighted regression (AWR; Peng et al., 2019) and advantage-weighted actor–critic (AWAC; Nair et al., 2020) are off-policy methods that fit policies to actions stored in replay using exponential advantage weights. For τ > 0, FERPO also favors less likely actions at equal critic value, providing an explicit exploration incentive.

Number of SNIS samples. To test whether a larger sampling budget improves learning, we vary the number of rollout-policy action samples per state, K ∈ { 2, 4, 8, 16 }, on G1 flat terrain with adaptive temperature. Mean return improves as K increases, with smaller gains at larger sample counts suggesting diminishing returns. At 50.07M environment steps, increasing K from 8 to 16 raises mean return from 34 .32 ± 0.18 to 34 .81 ± 0.24 (mean ± standard error of the mean, SEM; Figure 3, left), while doubling the number of candidate actions evaluated. 

Temperature. To assess the contribution of entropy regularization, we compare adaptive tempera-ture with τ = 0 at K = 16 using the same G1 evaluation budget. Without entropy regularization, mean return falls to 30 .32 ± 0.27 (mean ± SEM; Figure 3, left), 4.49 points below the adaptive control. FERPO’s maximum-entropy formulation therefore achieves higher mean return than the variant without entropy regularization on this task (see Appendix A.3.1 for details).

Cached V extension. A learned Q function must approximate both the immediate reward and future returns, and may therefore fail to preserve the reward’s shape across actions. Learning this shape can be particularly difficult in sparse reward settings, where few sampled transitions yield nonzero rewards. To mitigate this source of error, we extend the cached Q variant above by replacing its critic with a soft state-value function. For each rollout state, we simulate K candidate actions and, after fitting and freezing V , cache r(s, a ) + γV (s′) as a one-sample Monte Carlo estimate of QV (s, a )

for reuse throughout actor updates. This incorporates the observed immediate reward directly, at the cost of K additional environment calls per rollout state. In massively parallel online RL, these independent one-step simulations can be batched on the GPU (Zakka et al., 2025), and caching amortizes their cost across all actor epochs. ## 6 CONCLUSION , LIMITATIONS , AND FUTURE WORK 

FERPO performs on-policy maximum-entropy policy improvement by fitting an entropy- and KL-regularized target through forward KL. Critic values weight actions sampled from the rollout policy using self-normalized importance sampling, avoiding critic action derivatives. An ESS controller regulates the concentration of these weights. On DMC and ManiSkill, FERPO reaches high performance with fewer environment interactions than REPPO and achieves competitive final results. At K = 16 , combined actor-and-critic up-date time is 6.9% lower than REPPO for uncached FERPO and we also evaluate cached FERPO with a 22.2% actor update speedup. Ablations on G1 flat terrain highlight the benefit of entropy regularization and gains from larger SNIS candidate sets. Limitations include critic-value errors, finite-sample importance-sampling errors, restricted Gaussian policies, and higher GPU memory use. With eight seeds per task, FERPO remains below REPPO on the G1/T1 suite aggregate at the same environment-interaction budget. The gap is concentrated on the G1 tasks; T1 endpoints are similar or slightly higher for FERPO under the recorded evaluation protocols. State-value FERPO combines one-step simulation with a learned state-value critic to score candi-date actions. Its higher returns on AcrobotSwingupSparse and the modest iteration-time overhead measured for its optimized implementation make it a particularly promising direction for future work. Two further directions are FERPO in discrete action spaces and combining FERPO with dif-fusion policies. For small discrete action spaces, target normalization and the forward-KL objective could be evaluated by exact summation over actions and larger ones through sampling. Diffusion-augmented MDPs (DA-MDPs) (Sanokowski & Patil, 2026) offer a promising and relatively straight-forward route to combining FERPO with expressive diffusion policies: FERPO’s forward-KL up-dates could be applied to individual denoising transitions in the augmented MDP. 9Under review

Efficient candidate simulation. We combine Warp graph execution with parallel simulation of all 

K candidates across environments. Candidates are sampled and simulated during rollout, branching from each state without altering the rollout trajectory. Their rewards and next observations remain on the GPU until the critic has been fitted, then serve to construct the score cache. This avoids repeated transfers of simulator states between CPU and GPU. Actor epochs reuse the candidates and scores while updating their SNIS weights. Since observation normalization can change during rollout, we retain the sampling densities and correct these weights for any difference from the frozen rollout reference. 

Timing protocol. We benchmark both methods on the same NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB with Warp graphs enabled. We run three repetitions in separate processes, randomizing method order and averaging five iterations after two warmup iterations. GPU synchronization at iteration boundaries ensures that timings include all simulation and learning work; startup, compilation warmup, evaluation, external logging, and checkpointing are excluded.
