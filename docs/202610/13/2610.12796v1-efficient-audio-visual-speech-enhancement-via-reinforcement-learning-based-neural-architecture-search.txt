# Abstract 

Audio-visual speech enhancement (AVSE) has emerged as a critical technology for enabling reliable human-computer inter-action in challenging acoustic environments such as crowded caf´ es and moving vehicles. However, state-of-the-art AVSE models frequently exceed 22 million parameters, making them unsuitable for real-time deployment on resource-constrained edge devices such as smartphones, wearables, and embedded systems. To address this fundamental deployment challenge, we introduce a reinforcement learning-driven neural architec-ture search (RL-NAS) framework that automatically discov-ers compact yet high-performance AVSE architectures. Our approach constrains the design space to respect the temporal alignment requirements between audio and visual streams while carefully controlling parameter proliferation through a novel re-ward function that jointly optimizes speech-quality improve-ments (measured by SI-SNR gains) and model compactness. By leveraging proximal-policy optimization with constraint-aware action masking, our agent efficiently evaluates only 35 candi-date architectures before converging on a 2.9-million-parameter model achieving 14.5 dB SI-SNR improvement—merely 0.7 dB below the 22-million-parameter baseline, yet with a nine-fold compression ratio. When deployed on commercial mo-bile CPUs, this lightweight network achieves a 4.3× reduc-tion in real-time factor, demonstrating practical viability for on-device speech enhancement. In comparison to uninformed ran-dom search, our RL-driven methodology achieves 92% superior search efficiency while revealing crucial architectural princi-ples, including the preference for shallower but wider temporal-convolutional blocks and the tolerance of aggressive visual-pathway pruning. These discoveries establish new design prin-ciples for efficient multimodal speech systems and demonstrate that hardware-aware NAS, guided by reinforcement learning, can successfully deliver lightweight AVSE models without sac-rificing perceptual quality.

# 1. Introduction 

Audio-visual speech enhancement represents a paradigm shift in robust speech processing, leveraging the complementary in-formation contained in visual lip movements to augment noisy acoustic signals [1, 2, 3]. This multimodal approach consis-tently outperforms audio-only alternatives, particularly in sce-narios characterized by severe acoustic degradation. The field of lip reading has advanced considerably in recent years, with methods like LipNet [4] and broader visual speech recognition frameworks [5] providing strong feature extraction foundations. Recent advances leveraging temporal convolutional networks have substantially improved enhancement quality, with archi-tectures such as AVSEGAN [6] and Conv-TasNet variants [7] achieving remarkable performance metrics. General speech en-hancement techniques have also evolved significantly, with su-pervised approaches [8], speech separation methods [9, 10], and enhancement frameworks [11, 12] all contributing to the knowl-edge base. However, these state-of-the-art systems carry a pro-hibitive computational burden, with model sizes frequently ex-ceeding 22 million learnable parameters. This substantial pa-rameter count creates significant barriers to practical deploy-ment, preventing the adoption of AVSE technology on smart-phones, hearing aids, and other edge devices where computa-tional resources are inherently limited. Neural Architecture Search offers a promising avenue for addressing this efficiency gap. By automating the discovery of optimal network topologies, NAS can explore vast design spaces and identify architectures that balance performance and efficiency far more systematically than manual engineering ap-proaches. Nevertheless, applying NAS to multimodal speech enhancement presents unique and substantial challenges. The interdependencies between audio and visual processing path-ways create an enormous potential search space. Moreover, the requirement to maintain temporal synchronization between the audio and visual streams introduces architectural constraints that must be carefully enforced. Additionally, the training cost associated with evaluating candidate architectures becomes compounded when multiple sensor modalities are involved. To overcome these challenges, we propose a comprehen-sive RL-based NAS framework specifically designed for audio-visual speech enhancement. Our framework incorporates three fundamental innovations: (1) a carefully designed constrained search space that explicitly preserves temporal alignment be-tween modalities while maintaining architectural validity, (2) a sophisticated parameter-performance balanced reward func-tion that elegantly trades off enhancement quality against model size, and (3) a progressive shrinking strategy that efficiently guides the search from high-performance baseline architectures toward increasingly compact solutions. Through extensive ex-perimentation, we demonstrate that our approach discovers a compact 2.9-million-parameter model that retains 95.4% of the baseline performance while achieving a nine-fold reduction in model size, enabling practical deployment on edge devices. Our RL-NAS methodology also reveals important architectural in-sights that challenge conventional wisdom about multimodal network design, including the surprising finding that visual in-formation can be represented in dramatically reduced dimen-sional spaces without substantial performance degradation. arXiv:2610.12796v1 [cs.SD] 8 Oct 2026 2. Related Work 

2.1. Neural Architecture Search Foundations 

Neural Architecture Search has fundamentally transformed the landscape of automated machine learning by enabling the sys-tematic discovery of optimal network architectures without ex-tensive manual engineering. The seminal work by Zoph and Le introduced reinforcement learning as a powerful tool for this purpose [13], where an RL agent learned to sequentially construct network architectures by predicting the configuration of successive layers. Evolutionary approaches to NAS have also proven effective, with regularized evolution methods [14] demonstrating competitive performance without the stability is-sues associated with early RL-based approaches. However, the computational requirements of these initial methods, which ne-cessitated thousands of GPU-days for complete architecture dis-covery, motivated substantial subsequent research into more ef-ficient methodologies. The introduction of differentiable NAS (DARTS) repre-sented a pivotal advancement by reformulating architecture search as a continuous optimization problem amenable to gradient-based methods [15]. This perspective shift dramat-ically reduced the computational cost of architecture search. Subsequent innovations focused on further improving search ef-ficiency through parameter sharing strategies [16], which en-able the RL agent to leverage knowledge accumulated dur-ing the evaluation of previous architectures. Hardware-aware NAS methodologies like ProxylessNAS [17] have emerged to directly optimize for deployment targets. More recent ap-proaches employ predictor-based methods that learn to estimate architecture performance without requiring full model training [18], dramatically accelerating the search process. Training-free NAS methods [19] represent an even more extreme ef-ficiency frontier. Contemporary research increasingly empha-sizes multi-objective optimization formulations that simultane-ously consider multiple competing objectives such as model ac-curacy, computational latency, and memory consumption [20], particularly important for edge deployment scenarios where computational resources are severely constrained. Recent ad-vances in generative NAS [21] and LLM-informed design prin-ciple transfer [22] suggest promising new directions for archi-tecture discovery. 

2.2. NAS Applications in Speech and Audio Processing 

Speech and audio processing tasks present distinctive chal-lenges for neural architecture search due to their inherent tem-poral dynamics and stringent noise robustness requirements. ST-NAS pioneered the application of differentiable architecture search to end-to-end automatic speech recognition [23], demon-strating that NAS-discovered architectures could match or ex-ceed the performance of manually designed systems. Parallel work by Hu et al. introduced NAS-TDNN, which automated the architecture search process for time-delay neural networks and achieved remarkable 96% model compression while pre-serving accuracy [24]. These successes motivated intensive in-vestigation into optimal architectures for speech enhancement specifically. Temporal convolutional networks emerged as par-ticularly well-suited substrates for this task, offering an attrac-tive balance between effective long-range temporal modeling and computational efficiency [7]. The demonstrated success of TCN-based architectures in speech enhancement applications has made them the foundation for numerous subsequent com-petitive systems. 

2.3. Optimal Temporal Convolutional Network Design 

Systematic investigation through neural architecture search has revealed consistent and reproducible patterns in optimal TCN design for speech processing tasks. Research has demonstrated that a subtle but important depth-width tradeoff exists: shal-lower networks with higher expansion ratios (typically 1.5 to 2.0) consistently outperform deeply nested narrower config-urations, contrary to intuitions inherited from computer vi-sion architectures [25]. Temporal convolutional networks have emerged as a powerful paradigm for sequence modeling tasks [26, 27], with particular strength in capturing long-range de-pendencies through dilated convolutions [28]. Furthermore, the dilation schedule employed in dilated convolutional blocks sig-nificantly influences performance. Analysis across multiple pa-pers has shown that linear dilation stride patterns systematically surpass exponential dilation strategies, particularly when the network must maintain robustness to environmental noise [29]. An important architectural principle involves parameter redis-tribution across layers: shifting learnable parameters from later processing stages toward earlier layers creates more robust fea-ture representations and enhances overall noise tolerance [6]. These principles informed the design of more recent competi-tive architectures such as MSGLN, which integrates global self-attention mechanisms with local convolutional operations to ef-ficiently model long-range temporal dependencies while pre-serving computational efficiency [25]. 

2.4. Multimodal and Efficient Neural Architecture Search 

The extension of neural architecture search to multimodal learn-ing scenarios remains an active and challenging research fron-tier. The coordination of cross-modal fusion strategies and the balancing of architectural investments across different modal-ities present substantial technical challenges. Recent work has begun to address these challenges through innovative ap-proaches such as architecture embedding vectors that enable knowledge transfer across diverse vision and audio tasks [30]. Multimodal learning theory and practice [31, 32, 33] provide crucial foundations for understanding how to integrate multiple information streams effectively. Audio-visual research has established several crucial prin-ciples for multimodal architecture design. Notably, visual pro-cessing pathways demonstrate substantially higher tolerance for aggressive parameter reduction compared to audio streams, with studies demonstrating that 50% to 80% reductions in vi-sual pathway complexity frequently yield minimal performance degradation [1]. This asymmetry arises because visual lip movement information is relatively simple geometrically com-pared to the complex spectral and temporal patterns in de-graded audio. Fusion strategy selection also critically impacts efficiency: additive fusion mechanisms typically outperform concatenation-based approaches while simultaneously reduc-ing the overall parameter count by approximately 85% [29]. Encouraging preliminary evidence suggests that sub-3-million-parameter models can maintain performance within 1 dB SI-SNR of 20+ million parameter baselines [6], motivating the search for even more aggressive compression strategies. Model compression and efficiency optimization remain es-sential for practical deployment. Pruning-based approaches [34, 35] and quantization methods [36, 37] have demonstrated substantial parameter reductions. The success of efficient mo-bile architectures like MobileNets [38, 39] and MobileBERT [40] has established design principles for resource-constrained inference. Emerging approaches such as binarized neural archi-tecture search and normalizing flow-based design methodolo-gies [41] represent promising directions for achieving further compression in low-parameter regimes. 

2.5. Generative Approaches to Speech Enhancement 

Recent work has explored generative adversarial networks (GANs) [42] as an alternative paradigm for speech enhance-ment. AVSEGAN represents an important application of GAN-based learning to audio-visual enhancement, leveraging adver-sarial training dynamics. Beyond speech-specific applications, generative approaches like pix2pix [43] have demonstrated ef-fectiveness in image-to-image translation tasks that share simi-lar structural challenges with audio-visual enhancement. How-ever, GANs introduce additional complexity and training insta-bility compared to supervised approaches, making them less amenable to efficient hardware deployment. Our architecture search framework sidesteps these challenges by focusing on dis-criminative learning with explicit efficiency constraints. 

2.6. Research Gaps and Our Contribution 

Despite substantial progress in neural architecture search and speech enhancement, important gaps persist in the current literature. Current multimodal speech enhancement systems typically lack generalization mechanisms to handle variations across different noise types and signal-to-noise ratio conditions [7]. Most systems operate under the assumption of fixed acous-tic conditions rather than adapting dynamically to real-time en-vironmental changes. The intersection of quantization-aware architecture search with speech enhancement remains substan-tially underexplored, despite the critical importance of low-bit representations for edge deployment. Hardware-aware NAS for speech tasks remains an underexplored area, with most efficiency-oriented research focused on vision and NLP do-mains [44]. Our work directly addresses these gaps through sev-eral key contributions. First, we introduce constrained search spaces that explicitly preserve temporal alignment requirements while dramatically reducing the effective search complexity. Second, our parameter-performance balanced reward function elegantly trades off multiple competing objectives through a principled multi-objective optimization approach. Third, our framework incorporates hardware-aware efficiency considera-tions from initial design through final deployment. Finally, our comprehensive analysis reveals reproducible architectural pat-terns and design principles that can guide future multimodal speech-enhancement research, providing a foundation for con-tinued progress in this critical domain. 

# 3. RL-Based NAS Methodology 

Our reinforcement learning-based neural architecture search framework implements a constrained Markov Decision Pro-cess that systematically optimizes audio-visual architectures while rigorously preserving the essential temporal synchroniza-tion between modalities. The methodology extends established hardware-aware NAS principles [20] with novel modality-specific innovations carefully tailored for the unique require-ments of speech enhancement. 

3.1. Constrained Search Space Design 

The search space design process begins with domain insights gleaned from extensive analysis of temporal convolutional net-work optimization studies. Rather than exploring the full com-binatorial explosion of possibilities, we incorporate domain knowledge to enforce three categories of constraints that dra-matically reduce the effective search complexity while ensuring that all discovered architectures remain architecturally viable. The temporal consistency constraint ensures that audio and visual processing pathways maintain perfect temporal align-ment throughout the network: Taudio = Tvisual . This requirement is enforced through the exclusive use of dimension-preserving operations (such as pointwise convolutions and residual connec-tions) that do not alter the temporal resolution of intermediate feature maps. The channel compatibility constraint maintains the mathematical validity of skip connections and fusion op-erations by enforcing B ≤ E, where B denotes bottleneck channels and E represents encoder channels. The compute bounds constraint prevents runaway parameter growth by limit-ing R × B ≤ 24 , where R is the number of TCN repeats and B

is the block dimension, thereby preventing memory exhaustion during training and deployment. Table 1 presents the complete search space configuration. This carefully constrained design reduces the potential search space from O(10 14 ) unconstrained possibilities to merely 324 valid architectural configurations, representing a 99.9997% re-duction in search complexity while maintaining sufficient diver-sity to discover novel and high-performing architectures.

3.5. Progressive Shrinking Strategy 

Algorithm 1 implements our progressive shrinking strategy, which orchestrates a curriculum learning approach that progres-sively compresses the baseline architecture toward the target pa-rameter budget. This strategy incorporates three complemen-tary mechanisms. First, curriculum learning begins the search from the high-performance 22-million-parameter baseline ar-chitecture rather than random initialization, providing a warm start that dramatically accelerates convergence. Second, step-wise dimension reduction modifies only a single architectural dimension per optimization step, maintaining architectural vi-ability and enabling the agent to thoroughly explore the con-sequences of each modification. Third, fidelity extrapolation estimates the full-training SI-SNR performance from interme-diate 10-epoch results using linear regression fit to historical data, dramatically reducing the computational cost of architec-ture evaluation. This systematic approach achieves 92% search efficiency compared to random exploration, requiring only 35 total archi-tecture evaluations to identify high-performing configurations that would require thousands of random trials to discover.

This represents a substantial reduction compared to alterna-tive NAS methodologies: differentiable NAS typically requires 3,000+ GPU-hours, evolutionary NAS demands 2,500+ epochs, and exhaustive random search would necessitate 5,000+ epochs to achieve equivalent architectural coverage. # 4. Experiments and Results 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Our evaluation framework emphasizes realistic audio-visual speech enhancement scenarios. We conduct experiments on the Lip Reading Sentences 3 (LRS3) dataset, which provides 438 hours of high-quality audio-visual speech recordings with syn-chronized audio and video streams [48]. For additional robust-ness analysis, we note that large-scale speech datasets like Lib-riSpeech [49] have become standard for evaluating speech en-hancement and recognition systems. For noise, we employ the noise set from the Clarity Enhancement Challenge 2 (CEC2), which was specifically curated to reflect realistic hearing aid scenarios with signal-to-noise ratios ranging from −5 to 5 dB [50]. The evaluation metric is SI-SNR improvement in decibels, which measures the degree to which the network improves the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio. We also report the real-time factor on Intel i7-1185G7 mobile CPUs, which provides a practical measure of deployment feasibility. Speaker verifi-cation tasks on standard benchmarks like speaker recognition challenges [51] provide complementary evaluation perspectives for multimodal speech systems. The baseline model consists of a 22-million-parameter audio-visual speech enhancement net-work following the architecture described in previous work. 

4.2. Architecture Evolution During Search 

Table 2 presents the progression of architectures discovered dur-ing the search process, showing the top trajectory from our five parallel search workers. The baseline 22-million-parameter ar-chitecture achieves a baseline SI-SNR improvement of 15.2 dB. As the RL agent progressively applies architectural modifica-tions, we observe a carefully managed degradation in enhance-ment quality coupled with dramatic improvements in efficiency. After five optimization steps, the agent reduces the encoder channels from 256 to 192, achieving a 26.5% parameter reduc-tion while maintaining nearly identical SI-SNR performance. Subsequent steps involve bottleneck reduction, TCN depth re-duction, hidden dimension scaling, and finally aggressive visual pathway pruning. Each modification was made with careful at-tention to maintaining the viability of the resulting architecture and preserving adequate capacity for high-quality speech en-hancement. 

4.3. Discovered Optimal Architecture 

The neural architecture search process converged on a 2.9-million-parameter model that achieves excellent efficiency-performance balance. Compared to the baseline architecture, the discovered model incorporates several key modifications. The encoder channels are reduced from 256 to 192 dimensions, representing a 25% reduction in the feature representation ca-pacity at the input stage. The bottleneck, which serves as the compression layer during processing, similarly reduces from 256 to 192 channels. The TCN component, which performs the core temporal modeling, employs only 3 repeats of 6-block con-figurations (43% reduction in temporal processing depth) with an expansion ratio of 2.0 in the hidden dimensions. Most dra-matically, the visual processing pathway is aggressively pruned from 256 output channels to 128, representing a 50% reduction. This aggressive pruning of the visual pathway represents one of the most significant discoveries from the search process, as it suggests that lip movement information can be represented in dramatically lower-dimensional spaces than previously appre-ciated.

# 5. Conclusion 

This paper presents a comprehensive reinforcement learning-based neural architecture search framework specifically tailored for audio-visual speech enhancement. By formulating archi-tecture search as a constrained MDP with carefully designed state representations, action spaces, and multi-objective reward functions, our framework successfully bridges the efficiency gap between state-of-the-art performance and practical deploy-ability on edge devices. The discovered 2.9-million-parameter model maintains 14.5 dB SI-SNR improvement, representing 95.4% of the baseline performance while achieving nine-fold compression, demonstrating efficiency-accuracy tradeoffs that align with emerging paradigms in resource-constrained ma-chine learning [38, 52]. The research contributes several important findings to the multimodal neural architecture community. Our analysis demonstrates that visual lip-movement information can be rep-resented in remarkably reduced dimensional spaces—just 128 channels compared to 256 in baseline designs—without sub-stantial quality loss, a finding that challenges conventional ar-chitectural assumptions about multimodal fusion [31]. The search process revealed that optimal TCN configurations em-ploy three repeats of six-block structures with expansion ratio 2.0, suggesting that shallower but wider networks may represent a fundamental tradeoff in speech processing, consistent with findings in temporal sequence modeling [26]. Parameter redis-tribution patterns discovered through NAS suggest that concen-trating learnable parameters in early processing stages enhances noise robustness compared to conventional designs. The addi-tive fusion approach for multimodal combination outperformed concatenation-based alternatives while simultaneously reducing parameters, aligning with emerging attention-based multimodal fusion mechanisms [53, 54]. The resulting lightweight audio-visual speech enhancement model—comprising 11.7 MB of parameters—enables previ-ously impractical deployment scenarios. Real-time hearing aids equipped with visual augmentation become feasible through the dramatic four-fold reduction in computational requirements. Mobile video conferencing applications can now incorporate audio-visual enhancement without imposing unacceptable bat-tery drain. Always-on voice assistants with visual context awareness move from theoretical possibility to practical real-ity. These practical applications represent the ultimate moti-vation for this research: making powerful multimodal speech enhancement available to users on their personal devices, lever-aging efficient inference techniques established in mobile com-puting [39, 40]. Future research should investigate several promising ex-tensions of this framework. Dynamic architectures that adapt to changing acoustic conditions and noise characteristics could provide further efficiency gains through techniques like adap-tive gating. Quantization-aware neural architecture search, combining this framework with low-bit representation learn-ing [36] and pruning strategies [34, 35], could achieve addi-tional compression beyond what is currently possible. En-hanced cross-modal attention mechanisms specifically designed for synchronizing audio-visual information [53, 55] could im-prove the quality of fusion while maintaining model com-pactness. Integration with self-supervised learning approaches could reduce dependence on carefully annotated training data and extend applicability to diverse real-world scenarios. Explo-ration of generative NAS approaches [21] and hardware-aware optimization strategies [44] will continue advancing the fron-tier of efficient multimodal speech processing. These exten-sions will build upon the principled multi-objective optimiza-tion framework we have established. The RL-NAS framework presented in this work estab-lishes a generalizable blueprint for developing efficient mul-timodal systems that successfully balance high performance with stringent deployment constraints. By leveraging rein-forcement learning to systematically explore constrained ar-chitectural spaces while optimizing multi-objective trade-offs, this approach demonstrates that heavyweight multimodal sys-tems can be compressed without sacrificing fundamental qual-ity. This methodology extends beyond speech enhancement to broader applications in multimodal processing, suggesting that hardware-aware NAS guided by reinforcement learning repre-sents a powerful paradigm for realizing ubiquitous, intelligent voice interfaces.
