# Continuous-Time Estimation-Aware Optimal Control of Mobile Sensors for Target Tracking 

Michael States, Sharveshwaran Umashankar, Minsen Yuan, and Yue Yu 

Abstract — Target tracking is the problem of estimating the state of a target system using measurements collected (often-times) by mobile sensors. Trajectory optimization for mobile sensors must account not only for dynamical and operational constraints, but also for uncertainty in estimating the target system’s state. We propose an estimation-aware optimal control method for target tracking that accounts for the nonlinear dynamics of both the mobile sensor and target system, the nonlinear covariance dynamics in extended Kalman filtering, and nonlinear measurement models. Furthermore, it ensures constraints on the mobile sensor are satisfied in continuous time (within numerical precision). In numerical simulations, the proposed method achieves estimation-aware target tracking while satisfying the mobile-sensor constraints in continuous time, even under sparse time discretization. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Target tracking is the problem of estimating the state of a dynamical target system using measurements. These measurements are often collected by mobile sensors , which are mobile platforms equipped with onboard sensing units (such as aerial vehicles carrying range or bearing sensors). The measurements often depend jointly on the states of the mobile sensor and the target system. As a result, planning the mobile sensor’s trajectory must account for not only the sensor’s dynamics and operational constraints, but also how measurements collected along the trajectory affect estimation of the target system’s state. Target tracking has a wide range of applications including search and rescue, environmental monitoring, surveillance, and site inspection [1]–[4]. Existing work on target tracking uses various metrics to quantify how the mobile sensor’s trajectory affects target state estimation. One class of metrics involves the observ-ability Gramian, which characterizes how well measurements collected along the mobile sensor’s trajectory can distinguish different initial states of the target [5]–[8]. Variants of the ob-servability Gramian instead focus on the distinguishability of the target’s final state [9]. Another direction uses the Fisher information matrix to quantify the amount of information that measurements provide about the target state [10]–[14]. A widely used metric is the state-estimation error covari-ance, which directly quantifies the uncertainty of the state estimate [15]–[26]. Covariance-based metrics are particularly attractive because widely used estimators, like the Kalman filter, provide a covariance that accounts for process noise and measurement noise. These often feature discrete-time [8], [15], [17]–[20], [26] or continuous-time Kalman filters [21]–[25]. One limitation of existing target-tracking methods is that they only constrain the mobile sensor state in discrete time. Constraints, such as keep-out zones, are often safety critical and must be satisfied at all times, but since informative sens-ing often requires aggressive maneuvers that drive the state close to constraint boundaries, constraint violations can occur between discretization points. Existing methods impose such constraints either through explicitly discrete-time formula-tions [10], [16]–[20] or through direct transcription and collocation of continuous-time models [9], [22], [24], [25]. Reducing violations between discretization or collocation points generally requires dense time discretization, which increases the size and computational cost of the resulting trajectory optimization problem. Addressing this limitation by enforcing continuous-time constraints introduces an additional computational challenge for target tracking. Recent continuous-time optimal control methods ensure constraint satisfaction between discretization points by differentiating the underlying continuous-time dy-namics equation [27], [28]. In target tracking, this dynamics equation not only introduces nonlinear coupling between the mobile sensor’s state and target covariance, but also must preserve the covariance matrix’s symmetric structure. Some recent results have developed efficient backpropagation-based methods for differentiating covariance dynamics for discrete-time extended Kalman filtering [18]. However, to the best of our knowledge, efficient differentiation formulas of the continuous-time extended Kalman filter covariance and using them to ensure continuous-time constraints in target tracking has received little if any attention. We propose an estimation-aware optimal control method that jointly accounts for the nonlinear measurements and mobile sensor dynamics, as well as target system covariance dynamics in extended Kalman filtering. It ensures state and input constraints on the mobile sensor trajectory at all times (within numerical precision). To this end, we first transform constraints on the mobile sensor into auxiliary continuous-time nonlinear dynamics with boundary conditions. Next, we provide an efficient formula that exploits the symmetry of the covariance matrix to differentiate the continuous-time nonlinear covariance dynamics in extended Kalman filtering and support efficient gradient-based optimization. Finally, we demonstrate the proposed method with the augmented arXiv:2610.06654v1 [eess.SY] 5 Oct 2026 Lagrangian algorithm with two examples, LTE transmitter localization, and ground vehicle tracking. Compared with off-the-shelf solvers that only enforce constraints at dis-cretization points, the proposed method ensures continuous-time constraint satisfaction and supports sparse time dis-cretization. We presented a method of estimation-aware optimal con-trol that jointly considers the dynamics of the mobile sensor and target covariance, while enforcing constraints in continu-ous time. We then differentiated the covariance dynamics and exploited the symmetry of the covariance matrix to support efficient optimization.
