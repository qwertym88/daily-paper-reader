# Adaptive Whole-Body MPC with Optimal Posture Planning for Humanoid Robots under Unknown Payloads 

Manh Cuong Nguyen †, Duc Cuong Vu † Student Member, IEEE , Viet Anh Vu, Duc Tuan Pham, Toan Nguyen, Minh Hiep Phung, Ngo Anh Vien, Minh Nhat Vu ∗ Member, IEEE 

Abstract — Humanoid robots performing lifting and material-handling tasks must maintain balance despite unknown and varying payloads. However, payload uncertainty significantly affects whole-body dynamics and may destabilize conventional controllers. We propose an adaptive whole-body control archi-tecture that integrates three components: (i) a centroidal model predictive controller (MPC) that generates dynamically feasible reference motions, (ii) an adaptive fuzzy balance controller that compensates for payload-induced model mismatch by gener-ating corrective torques distributed across the supporting-leg joints, and (iii) a waypoint-based optimal posture planner built on a triple-integrator model that produces smooth sit-to-stand and stand-to-sit trajectories satisfying center-of-mass (CoM) stability constraints, joint limits, and whole-body feasibility requirements. Together, these components enable stable low-ering, grasping, lifting, carrying, and placing behaviors under significant payload uncertainty. Extensive simulation and hard-ware experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework improves balance robustness and reduces tracking degradation during posture transitions and lifting tasks compared to non-adaptive baselines. 

Index Terms — Humanoid robots, Adaptive Whole-Body Con-trol, Fuzzy Logic Control, Optimal Trajectory Generation 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In recent years, humanoid robots have attracted significant attention due to their versatility and potential to operate in human-centered environments [1]. Their comprehensive structure, which integrates mobility [2], manipulation [3], and perception [4], enables them to perform a wide range of complex tasks and operate in modern manufacturing systems [5]. Unlike traditional industrial robots, which are typically designed for structured, repetitive tasks, humanoid robots are inspired by human morphology and motion, enabling them to operate in environments originally designed for human work-ers. This anthropomorphic configuration provides remarkable advantages in industrial scenarios that require flexibility, dexterity, and whole-body coordination, particularly in tasks involving physical interaction with the environment. Among these applications, lifting and material handling of unknown payloads represent a critical and challenging problem [6]. Such tasks frequently arise in real-world in-dustrial environments where the object properties, including mass, shape, and inertia, are not precisely known in advance. A. Posture transition for humanoid robot 

Posture transition motions such as sit-to-stand and stand-to-sit are essential capabilities for humanoid robots operating in human-centered environments. These tasks require coor-dinated whole-body motion, multi-contact interaction, and robustness against model uncertainties and payload variations [17]. Recent studies have therefore focused on optimization-based approaches to generate dynamically feasible trajec-tories while explicitly accounting for balance and contact constraints, e.g., [8]. Several works have proposed unified centroidal and whole-body MPC formulations to simultane-ously optimize contact forces, center-of-mass motion, and kinematic trajectories, enabling robust locomotion and pos-ture [18]. Reduced-order models have also been introduced to improve computational efficiency while maintaining stability, allowing real-time implementation in balancing and posture control tasks [19]. Despite these advances, most existing work focuses pri-marily on locomotion or assumes fixed payload conditions. Efficient and real-time trajectory generation for posture transitions under uncertain payloads remains a challenging problem. In particular, integrating waypoint-based trajectory modeling with MPC to generate smooth, dynamically con-sistent motions for posture transitions has received limited attention. 

B. Adaptive Fuzzy Control (AFC) for humanoid robot 

Fuzzy logic has been widely applied in humanoid robotics to address nonlinear dynamics and environmental uncertain-ties. A recent review highlights its effectiveness in balance regulation, gait stabilization, and disturbance rejection when precise models are unavailable [20]. Fuzzy-based controllers have been successfully used to maintain stability during standing and walking by regu-lating CoM motion and ensuring the zero moment point containment under uncertainties [21]. Disturbance rejection performance has been further improved through fuzzy stabi-lization strategies [22], while adaptive fuzzy approaches have enhanced dynamic walking robustness [23]. Fuzzy methods have also been extended to terrain adaptation and compli-ance regulation [24], as well as payload-aware humanoid manipulation [25]. Despite their robustness, conventional fuzzy controllers rely on heuristic rule design and lack predictive optimization and constraint handling. In contrast, MPC enables systematic, constraint-aware torque generation [26], but its performance may degrade under significant uncertainties. To combine the strengths of both methods, we integrate Adaptive Fuzzy Control (AFC) with MPC-based Whole-body Control for the pose transition task of humanoids. The adaptive fuzzy component estimates torque compensation from tracking errors and augments the nominal MPC output, improving robustness while preserving the optimal structure of the predictive control framework. The choice of a fuzzy compensator over classical model-based alternatives, such as disturbance observers (DOB) [27], model-reference adaptive control [28], or robust control [29], is deliberate and follows from the nature of the payload uncertainty considered here. An unknown payload simulta-neously perturbs the robot mass, center-of-mass location, and inertia tensor, so the induced model mismatch is a coupled, state-dependent nonlinearity rather than an additive bounded disturbance. Model-based robust and adaptive laws therefore require either an explicit parametric disturbance model, a reliable uncertainty bound, or online identification of the payload; on a full-scale humanoid these quantities are noisy and slow to converge, and conservative worst-case bounds sacrifice performance. In contrast, the proposed controller is estimation-free: it maps directly measurable balance signals, i.e., the capture-point deviation, the CoM velocity, and its integral, to a bounded compensation moment through an interpretable rule base, without ever estimating the payload mass or reconstructing the disturbance term. This yields three practical advantages. First, it removes the payload-identification loop and its convergence transients, which is critical when the load changes abruptly during grasping and placing. Second, the saturated, rule-bounded output provides an inherent safety envelope on hardware, unlike unbounded adaptive gains that risk parameter drift. Third, the fuzzy law is complementary to, rather than a replacement for, the predictive layer: the MPC retains full constraint-aware optimality for the nominal motion, while the fuzzy term only supplies model-free residual compensation, preserving the MPC structure. In this section, simulations and experiments are conducted to demonstrate the performance of the proposed approach under ideal and real-world conditions. The proposed method is implemented on a Humanoid Robot shown in Fig. 4, which has 28 DoFs (7 for each arm and 6 for each leg). The overall control structure based on the proposed ap-proach is illustrated in Fig. 2. The framework supports two operating modes. The first mode enables manual control via a joystick, combined with a trajectory planner that generates reference velocities for the pelvis. The second mode focuses on posture transition planning, which is handled by the proposed optimization method described in Section IV. The reference pelvis states are then provided to the cen-troidal model MPC, which operates at 50 Hz. Subsequently, the proposed adaptive control framework, running at 500 Hz, generates the desired joint torques. These torque commands are finally executed by a low-level controller operating at a frequency above 1 kHz, ensuring fast and stable torque tracking. 

A. Simulation validation 

Simulation validations were conducted within the MuJoCo physics engine [32] to independently evaluate the effec-tiveness of the proposed modules. As illustrated in Fig. 6, when lifting an unknown payload using a standard Centroidal MPC (left), the lack of an adaptive balancing control leads to uncompensated CoM shifts. This, compounded by non-optimized kinematic trajectories, causes the robot to exceed its stability margin and collapse (Stages 1–3). In contrast, the proposed framework (right) successfully maintains whole-body equilibrium. By integrating the Adaptive MPC with the Posture Transition Planner, the robot dynamically computes the required compensation torques and synthesizes feasible waypoints to proactively lower its center of mass (Stages 2–3). This synergy ensures that the CoM remains rigorously within the stability margin throughout the entire manipula-tion cycle, highlighting the necessity of combining adaptive disturbance rejection with task-optimal posture generation. This keeps the CoM within the stability margin throughout the manipulation cycle. 

B. Experiment implementation 

Two experimental scenarios are conducted. First, a static lifting task is considered to evaluate the robot’s ability to handle payload variations. Second, a dynamic scenario combining lifting and load-carrying during locomotion is im-plemented to further assess the performance and robustness of the proposed control strategy. 

1) Lifting: In the lifting experiment scenario, two pay-loads of 7 kg and 10 kg were implemented. Figure 7 presents the experimental results for the 7 kg payload case. The results include the evolution of the pelvis height and pitch angle, as well as the rotation and torque profiles of the hip, knee, and ankle joints. With the proposed motion planning strategy, smooth transitions in posture and joint states are achieved throughout the task. At approxi-mately 30s, the robot begins to decrease its pelvis height and lean forward in pitch, completing the sitting motion within 3s. During the subsequent phase, the robot grasps the 7 kg object and initiates the lifting process. After successfully lifting the object, the robot stands up during the period from 55s to 80s before finally placing the object back down. For the 10 kg payload, the experimental results are presented in Fig. 8. In this scenario, the object mass is significantly increased, which requires greater control effort during the lifting task. At approximately 20 s, the robot moves into a bowing posture in preparation for lifting. After grasping the object at around 30 s, the robot slightly lifts it while gradually recovering the torso pitch angle. Due to the substantial change in payload mass and the resulting variation in the torso dynamics, the adaptive con-troller increases its control effort to maintain balance. This behavior is reflected by small oscillations in the pelvis height and pitch angle during the initial lifting phase. 2) Lifting and Walking: In the combined lifting and walking scenario, a structured task pipeline is implemented. The carried object weighs 7 kg. The experimental results are presented in Fig. 1 and Fig. 9. The left panel illustrates the evolution of the robot states over time, while the right panel provides a zoomed view of the walking phase. The proposed pipeline consists of the following stages: 1) The robot starts from a stable home position, then bows down and grasps the object (83s); 2) After grasping, the robot lifts the object and returns to the home posture (110s); 3) The robot walks while carrying the load for 15s, from 140s to 155s, before stopping; 4) Finally, the robot places the object on the ground and recovers to the home position. The results demonstrate that, under uncertain payload conditions, the robot is able to walk normally and maintain stable locomotion while successfully completing the assigned task. The fuzzy parameters were not re-tuned for walking, indicating that the tuning is not lifting-specific. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presented an adaptive whole-body control architecture for humanoid robots handling unknown pay-loads by integrating Optimal Posture Planning, Whole-Body MPC, and Adaptive Fuzzy Control. The framework proactively generates dynamically feasible motions while the fuzzy controller reactively compensates for payload-induced torque disturbances, preserving the optimal and constraint-consistent behavior of the MPC. Validations in MuJoCo and on a real-world platform across stationary lifting and dynamic loco-manipulation scenarios demonstrated the sys-tem’s effectiveness in maintaining whole-body balance. To overcome the heuristic and reactive limitations of the current fuzzy logic, future work will focus on integrating learning-based methods to optimize compensation strategies and proactive disturbance observers to further enhance agility in highly dynamic environments.
