# Linear Occupancy Homomorphisms for Discounted Markov Decision Processes 

Yihan Liu, Sarah H.Q. Li 

Abstract — We introduce linear occupancy homomorphisms (LOHs) , a class of Markov decision process (MDP) transforma-tions that defines the MDP homomorphism over the feasible occupancy measure space of MDPs. In contrast to classic MDP homomorphism frameworks, which are defined through mappings over the state and state-action spaces, LOHs map between occupancy measure spaces and are representable as linear transformation matrices that expose important structural properties of the homomorphism itself. For this class of MDP transformations, we derive finite dimensional linear inequality constraints that certify when a candidate transformation matrix is a valid LOH and derive the corresponding transformed MDP. We further establish a connection between LOHs, soft MDP homomorphisms, and factored matrix robust MDPs. When the linear transformation matrix is invertible, we prove that the original MDP is equivalent to a constrained MDP after the homomorphic transformation, and derive necessary and sufficient conditions under which the LOH preserves the optimal value. We illustrate the benefits of LOHs in two settings: improving robust MDP solutions by transforming nonrectangular parameter uncertainty sets into homomorphic rectangular MDPs, and decomposing a large MDP into smaller independent MDPs. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Many decision-making problems formulated as MDPs are hard to solve because their structural features are poorly matched to available solution methods. Examples of this include robust MDP with transition uncertainty correlated across states [1]–[3], interconnected dynamical networks in which weak coupling prevents subsystems from being solved independently [4]–[6], or state-action abstractions that obscure exploitable symmetries and decompositions within the underlying dynamics [7]–[10]. Classical MDP homo-morphisms [11]–[13] address related challenges by mapping an MDP’s state-action space to an alternative representation, typically through state-action pair mappings and aggregation, with the goal of reducing problem complexity or enabling transfer across similar tasks [9], [10], [13]. However, state aggregations and state-action maps are not the only useful transformations. For example, linear combinations of state-action pairs can define new state-action pairs without simply merging existing pairs. In this paper, we ask whether an MDP homomorphism can be directly defined over the feasible MDP solution space, its occupancy measures, and whether such transformations can expose underlying structure that is difficult to exploit in their original state-action representations.

This leads us to introduce LOHs, a class of MDP homo-morphisms defined as linear transformations of the MDP’s occupancy measure spaces. By establishing occupancy mea-sures (instead of state and action spaces) as primitive ob-jects being transformed, LOHs allow the transformation to be designed around desirable structure to expose in the transformed MDP. Our approach enables us to use MDP homomorphisms as a general tool for restructuring state-action representation while maintaining an explicit mapping between the feasible solutions of the original and transformed problems. 

Contributions . We formally define LOH between MDPs with the same state-action cardinality and derive a finite-dimensional certificate that guarantees when a linear matrix is a valid LOH. These conditions lead to an intuitive interpre-tation of LOH as a simultaneously consistent state and state-action transformations of the occupancy measure spaces, and establish a connection between LOHs, soft MDP ho-momorphisms, and factored matrix representations of MDP parameter uncertainty. When a valid LOH is invertible, we show that solving the original MDP is equivalent to solving the transformed MDP with an additional cone constraint, and derive necessary and sufficient conditions under which a LOH preserves the optimal value between its original and transformed MDPs. Finally, we use two illustrative numerical examples in robust MDPs and MDP state decompositions to demonstrate that LOHs can be designed to expose computa-tional structure rather than merely reduce model dimension. II. R ELATED RESEARCH 

MDP homomorphisms transform MDPs while preserv-ing relevant reward and transition structure [11]. Classical approaches define state-action mappings and equivalence relations that enable model minimization, abstraction, and hierarchical reinforcement learning [11], [12], [14], [15]. Extensions of this research consider approximate homomor-phisms and bound the resulting performance loss [16], soft stochastic mappings for transfer between related tasks [13], [17], and homomorphisms for continuous state-action spaces and policy optimization [10]. In contrast, we define transfor-mations over feasible occupancy-measure spaces. The LOH framework has fundamental connections to robust MDPs and MDP state aggregation and decomposition. Standard tractable formulations of robust MDPs rely on rectangular uncertainty sets [18]–[20], motivating extensions to coupled and non-rectangular uncertainty [1]–[3], [21]. Factor-matrix models represent such correlations through shared transition factors [1]. LOHs connect naturally to this representation but arXiv:2610.07441v1 [math.OC] 5 Oct 2026 use occupancy transformations more generally to restructure transition dynamics. MDP state-action abstraction and aggre-gation reduce decision complexity by exploiting sufficient in-formation states or weak coupling within MDPs [6], [8], [9], [22]. LOH differs in that it exposes block decomposability rather than reducing decision complexity. III. P RELIMINARIES

LOH Synthesis . Our results primarily characterize and certify candidate LOHs, while a complementary problem is to synthesize a LOH that induces desired structure in the transformed MDP. We introduced LOH for discounted MDPs over finite state-action spaces. We provide sufficient conditions for when the transformed MDP is homomorphic to the original MDP and when it produces the same optimal value. We demonstrate how the LOH framework is useful to optimize robust MDP under nonrectangular transition uncertainty sets.
