Abstract — Flow-based policies provide an expressive frame-work for continuous robot control, but their iterative ODE integration incurs substantial inference cost. Naively reducing the integration budget can severely degrade control, since policies optimized under full-step execution are not explicitly constrained to remain reliable under coarse numerical integra-tion. We refer to this mismatch as the few-step discretization gap . To address this problem, we introduce RFPO, a flow-policy optimization framework for reliable few-step execution. Reward-aware online Reflow rectifies student-induced transport paths during on-policy learning, making the resulting policy more robust to coarse integration. A frozen Gaussian PPO controller supplies complementary action-space supervision at full and intermediate integration budgets, while the deployed policy remains a single flow student executed with one Euler step. Across Unitree Go2, Boston Dynamics Spot, Unitree H1, and Unitree G1, RFPO consistently preserves full-step control performance under one-step execution, with one-step returns remaining within 2.4% of their corresponding 64-step values across both zero and random initialization. On Unitree Go2, one-step execution retains 98.5% of the 64-step reward while reducing onboard mean inference latency from 4.39 ms to 

0.08 ms, yielding a 54.9 × speedup. Real-robot experiments further validate stable one-step locomotion. Code: https: //github.com/AIGeeksGroup/RFPO . Website: https:// aigeeksgroup.github.io/RFPO 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Learning expressive and efficient continuous-control poli-cies is a central problem in robotics. Conventional reinforce-ment learning typically parameterizes actions with simple distributions such as diagonal Gaussians, which are easy to optimize but can be restrictive when multiple control strategies are valid under the same observation. Generative policies provide a more expressive alternative and have shown strong performance in visuomotor and geometry-aware robot control [3], [33]. In particular, flow match-ing [12] and rectified flow [13] model action generation as an ODE that transports simple noise distributions into continuous actions. Flow-based policies have since been adopted for efficient robot manipulation [34] and large-scale vision-language-action models such as π0 [1], highlighting their potential as an expressive policy class for robot control. Beyond demonstration-supervised learning, recent work has increasingly optimized generative policies directly from environment rewards. Diffusion-based approaches such as DPPO [21], DIME [2], and GenPO [4] extend reinforce-ment learning to iterative generative action distributions. For flow-based policies, ReinFlow [35] enables online fine-tuning through stochastic flow dynamics, while Flow Q-Reward-Optimized Flow Policy Our Method

Learning [18] and SAC Flow [36] explore offline and off-policy optimization. FPO [16] constructs an on-policy sur-rogate directly from conditional flow-matching losses, and FPO++ [32] further improves this formulation with per-sample flow ratios and asymmetric trust-region updates. These advances make reward-driven optimization of expres-sive flow policies increasingly practical. However, high-reward flow policies are not necessarily reliable when the numerical integration budget is aggres-sively reduced. Existing flow-policy RL objectives optimize control return under the training integration budget but do not explicitly constrain the resulting vector field to remain numerically reliable under coarse integration. Consequently, reducing the number of Euler steps can substantially change the generated action and lead to severe control degradation, as illustrated in Fig. 1. We refer to this mismatch between full-step optimization and low-budget execution as the few-step discretization gap . Although Reflow [13], consistency learning [27], fast ODE solvers, and policy distillation [19], [34] have enabled efficient few-step generation, these tech-niques are primarily developed for generative modeling, imitation learning, or post-hoc policy acceleration rather than jointly with on-policy reward optimization. This motivates directly shaping reward-optimized flow policies for reliable few-step execution. To address this problem, we introduce RFPO, a flow-policy optimization framework for reliable few-step control. The key mechanism is reward-aware online Reflow, which repeatedly constructs student-induced full-step endpoints and rectifies their transport paths during on-policy learning, making the learned vector field more amenable to coarse numerical integration. A frozen Gaussian PPO controller supplies complementary action-space supervision at full and arXiv:2610.10453v1 [cs.RO] 7 Oct 2026 intermediate integration budgets, anchoring the quality of the learned actions without directly supervising one-step execu-tion. The original on-policy objective is retained throughout training, and all training-only components are removed at deployment, leaving a single flow student executed with one Euler step. We evaluate RFPO across two quadruped and two humanoid embodiments: Unitree Go2, Boston Dy-namics Spot, Unitree H1, and Unitree G1. Across all four embodiments and both zero and random initialization, one-step execution remains within 2 .4% of the corresponding 64-step return, whereas FPO++ and distillation-only baselines exhibit substantial degradation under aggressive step reduc-tion. On Unitree Go2, RFPO retains 98.5% of its 64-step reward while reducing onboard mean inference latency from 4.39 ms to 0 .08 ms, yielding a 54.9 × speedup . Real-robot experiments further demonstrate stable one-step locomotion across forward, lateral, and yaw commands. Our contributions are threefold:  

> •

We identify the few-step discretization gap in reward-optimized flow policies, where aggressive reduction of the numerical integration budget can severely degrade control despite strong full-step performance.  

> •

We introduce RFPO, which uses reward-aware student-induced online Reflow to regularize transport geometry during on-policy learning, together with complementary multi-budget action-space supervision from a frozen Gaussian PPO controller.  

> •

Extensive evaluations across two quadruped and two humanoid embodiments demonstrate robust one-step execution. On Unitree Go2, RFPO retains 98.5% of 64-step reward with a 54.9 × onboard inference speedup

Generative and flow policies for robot control. Genera-tive policies provide an expressive alternative to Gaussian actors for modeling multimodal robot behaviors. Diffusion Policy [3] and 3D Diffusion Policy [33] demonstrate strong visuomotor and geometry-aware manipulation performance using iterative generative action models. Flow matching [12] and rectified flow [13] provide an ODE-based alternative and have been adopted by robot policies such as RF-Policy [6], FlowPolicy [34], and ManiFlow [31], as well as large-scale vision-language-action models such as π0 [1]. Recent work has further accelerated iterative robot policies through consistency learning and policy distillation [19], [30]. These methods are predominantly trained from demonstrations or supervised robot data, whereas our work studies few-step ex-ecution of flow policies optimized directly from environment rewards. 

Reinforcement learning with generative policies. Genera-tive models have increasingly been incorporated into plan-ning and reinforcement learning. Diffusion-based methods have been used for trajectory planning and expressive offline policies [8], [29], [9], while Q-score matching connects diffusion-policy scores with reward-derived action gradi-ents [20]. DPPO [21], DIME [2], and GenPO [4] further op-timize iterative generative policies with reinforcement learn-ing. For flow-based policies, ReinFlow [35] studies online fine-tuning, while Flow Q-Learning [18] and SAC Flow [36] consider offline or off-policy optimization. FPO [16] and FPO++ [32] instead develop on-policy objectives directly from conditional flow-matching losses. These methods pri-marily optimize control return, while our work addresses the complementary problem of preserving policy behavior when the numerical integration budget is aggressively reduced. 

Few-step generative policy acceleration. Reducing the in-ference cost of iterative generative models has motivated both solver-based and learning-based acceleration. Training-free approaches such as DDIM [25] and DPM-Solver [15] reduce the number of numerical integration steps, while learning-based approaches include progressive distillation [23], con-sistency models [27], consistency trajectory models [10], improved consistency training [26], and Shortcut Models [5]. For flow-based models, Reflow [13] and InstaFlow [14] reshape transport trajectories to support coarse or one-step generation. Related ideas have been transferred to robot policies through Consistency Policy [19], One-Step Diffusion Policy [30], FlowPolicy [34], and ManiFlow [31]. These approaches largely target generative modeling, imitation learning, or post-hoc policy acceleration. In contrast, RFPO performs student-induced Reflow during on-policy reward optimization, directly regularizing the transport geometry encountered by the evolving policy. A frozen deployable controller provides complementary action-space supervision at full and intermediate integration budgets rather than direct one-step supervision. 

Learning-based legged locomotion and sim-to-real. Deep reinforcement learning has enabled agile legged locomotion through simulation-based training, robust policy learning, and massively parallel simulation [7], [28], [22]. Subsequent work has improved real-world robustness through rapid adap-tation and perceptive locomotion [11], [17]. These studies primarily address dynamics mismatch, terrain variation, and online adaptation, whereas our work focuses on a comple-mentary deployment bottleneck: preserving the behavior of reward-optimized flow policies under aggressive reductions of the numerical integration budget. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

Environment and task. We evaluate RFPO on velocity-control tasks spanning two quadruped and two humanoid embodiments: Unitree Go2, Boston Dynamics Spot, Uni-tree H1, and Unitree G1 (29 DoF). Each policy is trained and evaluated using the corresponding embodiment-specific observation space, reward function, and low-level control interface. For each embodiment, the PPO teacher and all flow-policy variants use the same task definition and domain-randomization protocol. For the real-robot study, we use the official Unitree-Go2-Velocity task. The Go2 policy receives a 45-D proprioceptive observation and outputs continuous joint-control targets through the official low-level PD inter-face. The observation does not include base linear velocity. 

Compared methods. We compare RFPO with the Gaussian PPO controller [24] and FPO++ [32]. We also evaluate KD-only , which uses PPO action supervision without Reflow. For the detailed Go2 ablation, we additionally consider uni-form Reflow , advantage-weighted Reward-aware Reflow , and 

Reflow+KD . The complete RFPO uses reward-aware online Reflow, multi-budget action distillation, auxiliary zero-start training, and adaptive compute regularization. 

Integration and evaluation protocol. The standard full integration budget is N = 64. For the cross-embodiment comparison, we evaluate both 64-step and one-step Euler execution. For the detailed Go2 analysis, we additionally sweep N ∈ { 64 , 32 , 16 , 8, 4, 1} without retraining for in-dividual solver budgets. We evaluate flow policies under both zero initialization ( ε = 0), which matches deterministic deployment, and random initialization ( ε ∼ N (0, I)). PPO uses a single deterministic policy forward pass and there-fore has no integration budget. Unless otherwise specified, evaluation uses 256 parallel environments with one episode collected per environment, yielding 256 evaluation episodes per setting. All reported flow-policy evaluations use the EMA checkpoint (decay 0 .95 with a 500-step warm-up). 

Metrics and real-robot evaluation. We report average episodic reward for all embodiments. The detailed Go2 study additionally reports the mean absolute forward-velocity tracking error |vx − vcmd

Onboard inference efficiency is measured using single-thread ONNX Runtime on the Go2 onboard CPU after 30 warm-up it-erations and 100 timed evaluations; we report mean and p99 latency. For hardware validation, command trajectories recorded on the real robot are replayed under matched inputs in simulation, and we report joint-position correlation and mean absolute error (MAE).

D. Onboard Inference Efficiency 

We evaluate inference latency on the Unitree Go2 onboard CPU using single-thread ONNX Runtime. As shown in Tab. IV, reducing the solver budget from 64 steps to a single Euler step decreases mean latency from 4 .39 ms to 0.08 ms, corresponding to a 54.9 × speedup . The p99 latency simultaneously decreases from 20 .06 ms to 0 .10 ms; the former is approximately the period of a 50-Hz control loop, whereas one-step execution leaves substantial computational headroom. Together with the preserved one-step return in Tab. I, these results demonstrate the practical deployment benefit of few-step flow control.

This anal-ysis evaluates matched-command joint-position consistency rather than full state-level sim-to-real equivalence. V. L IMITATIONS 

Although RFPO is evaluated across two quadruped and two humanoid embodiments in simulation, our current real-robot validation focuses on Unitree Go2 locomotion. The hardware evaluation covers a limited set of motion com-mands, and our matched-command analysis measures joint-position consistency rather than full state-level sim-to-real equivalence. Broader real-world evaluation on humanoid platforms and more contact-rich tasks would further establish the generality of the approach. In addition, the deployment efficiency of RFPO comes at the cost of additional com-putation during training. Online Reflow requires full-budget student rollouts to construct student-induced endpoints, while action distillation relies on a separately trained frozen PPO controller. Reducing this training overhead and dependence on an external action-space teacher is an important direction for improving scalability. Finally, our current deployment uses a fixed one-step Euler solver, while adaptive compute regularization is used only during training. Extending RFPO to select the integration budget online according to the current control state may provide a better accuracy–efficiency trade-off for highly dynamic or contact-rich behaviors. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented RFPO, a framework for reliable few-step execution of reward-optimized flow policies. By combin-ing reward-aware online Reflow with complementary multi-budget action supervision, RFPO reduces the discretization gap between full-step optimization and low-budget deploy-ment while preserving the original reward-driven objective. Across two quadruped and two humanoid embodiments, one-step execution closely matches the corresponding 64-step policy, demonstrating robust few-step control across different robot morphologies. On Unitree Go2, RFPO retains 

98.5% of the 64-step reward while reducing onboard mean inference latency from 4 .39 ms to 0 .08 ms, yielding a 54.9 ×

speedup . Real-robot experiments further validate stable one-step locomotion across multiple motion commands. Overall, these results show that explicitly shaping reward-optimized flow policies for coarse numerical execution provides apractical path toward expressive and efficient robot control.
