# Closing the Horizon Gap in Policy Optimization for Adversarial MDPs 

Mingyi Li ∗ Taira Tsuchiya †

October 8, 2026 

Abstract 

We consider policy optimization for online episodic tabular Markov decision processes (MDPs) with adversarial losses and bandit feedback. Policy optimization updates the policy locally at each state and avoids optimization over the occupancy-measure polytope, but its existing regret bounds are larger by a factor of the horizon H than those of occupancy-measure-based algorithms. We close this gap by using regularized Q-functions, which allow us to control the stability of the local updates jointly over all state-action pairs rather than separately at each state. The resulting algorithm attains high-probability regret bounds of eO(pHS (H + A)T ) for known transitions and eO(HS √AT ) for unknown transitions, where S

is the number of states, A the number of actions, and T the number of episodes. Both bounds improve the horizon dependence of existing policy optimization bounds, and the latter matches the best-known bound. We further extend the algorithm to adversarial linear-mixture MDPs and obtain the same improvement in the horizon dependence. 

# 1 Introduction 

We study policy optimization for online learning in episodic tabular Markov decision processes (MDPs) with adversarial losses and bandit feedback. Policy optimization is widely used in reinforcement learning and has achieved empirical success ( e.g., Schulman et al., 2015, 2017). In each of T episodes, the environment chooses a loss function, and the learner chooses and executes a policy, observing losses only at the visited state-action pairs. The learner aims to minimize the regret, the gap between the cumulative loss of the learner and that of the best fixed policy in hindsight. Algorithms for online learning in episodic tabular MDPs typically use either global optimization or policy optimization . Global optimization solves an optimization problem over the occupancy-measure polytope in each episode. With this approach, Zimin and Neu (2013) achieve minimax-optimal regret eO(√HSAT ) for known transitions, and Jin et al. (2020) achieve the best-known regret bound of eO(HS √AT ) for unknown transitions, where H is the horizon, S the number of states, and A the number of actions. However, global optimization can be computationally demanding for large MDPs. Policy optimization is a computationally efficient alternative, which updates the action distribution locally at each state (Shani et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2021). The best-known regret bounds for policy optimization are 

eO(√H3SAT ) for known transitions and eO(H2S√AT ) for unknown transitions (Luo et al., 2021). These bounds have an extra factor of the horizon H compared with those of global optimization. Later work on

Is the extra horizon dependence intrinsic to policy optimization, or can it be improved? 

1.1 Contributions of this paper 

We answer this question by showing that the extra horizon dependence can be improved. We prove that policy optimization achieves high-probability regret bounds of eO(pHS (H + A)T ) for known transitions (Theorem 5.1) and eO(HS √AT ) for unknown transitions (Theorem 5.2). In both settings, we improve the horizon dependence of the regret bounds of Luo et al. (2021). For unknown transitions, the leading term improves by a factor of H and matches the best-known regret upper bound for this setting. Table 1 compares these bounds with existing results. To obtain these bounds, we exploit regularized Q-functions (see (2) and (3)) to construct and analyze our policy updates. Replacing the ordinary Q-functions with regularized ones yields more optimistic Q-function estimates while retaining local policy updates at each state. The regularized value at each state depends recursively on the regularized values in the next layer, and thus optimism propagates backward across layers. Our algorithm updates the policy by exponential weights with regularized Q-functions and covers both known and unknown transitions. Regularized Q-functions allow us to control the stability of the algorithm jointly over all state-action pairs 

(s, a ). Existing analyses of policy optimization first use the performance difference lemma (Kakade and Langford, 2002) to express the regret as a weighted sum of local regrets, one for each state. They then control the stability of each local policy update separately. Since Q-function estimators aggregate losses over the remaining horizon, separate stability bounds can repeatedly count the same downstream losses. This repeated 2accounting leads to the extra horizon dependence in the resulting regret bounds. In contrast, we decompose the regret jointly over all state-action pairs, as in the occupancy-measure approach. The regularized Q-function connects the local policy updates across layers, allowing us to analyze their stability together. The overall stability term then reduces to the difference between the ordinary and regularized value functions at the initial state. We bound this difference using the expected square of the cumulative estimated loss along a trajectory. We relate the regularized Q-function to existing policy optimization and global optimization methods (Sec-tions 4.1 and 4.2). The optimism of the regularized Q-function can be interpreted as an implicit exploration bonus in the local policy update, connecting our method to policy optimization with exploration bonuses (Luo et al., 2021). We also interpret our algorithm’s policy update as optimization over occupancy measures with a regularizer induced by negative conditional entropy, and thus relate it to global optimization methods (Zimin and Neu, 2013; Jin et al., 2020). In Section 6, we extend the same algorithm to linear-mixture MDPs and obtain a high-probability regret bound of eO(√HSAT + dS 3/2√HT ) (Theorem 6.3), where d is the dimension of the feature map. This improves the horizon dependence of the policy optimization bound of Cheng et al. (2026) by a factor of H and matches the best-known bound of Li et al. (2024). Table 2 compares this bound with existing results for linear-mixture MDPs. 

# 2 Related work 

Adversarial MDPs. Adversarial tabular MDPs were studied in the early work of Even-Dar et al. (2009); Yu et al. (2008), and later in the episodic setting. For known transitions and bandit feedback, Zimin and Neu (2013) use global optimization and achieve regret eO(√HSAT ), which matches the lower bound Ω( √HSAT )

up to logarithmic factors. For unknown transitions, global optimization achieves regret eO(HS √AT ) under full-information feedback (Rosenberg and Mansour, 2019) and bandit feedback (Jin et al., 2020), leaving a √S gap from the lower bound Ω( H√SAT ) (Jin et al., 2018; Domingues et al., 2021). For policy optimization under bandit feedback, Shani et al. (2020) establish a regret bound with eO(T 2/3)

dependence on T . Using dilated exploration bonuses, Luo et al. (2021) improve this dependence to eO(√T ) and obtain regret bounds of eO(√H3SAT ) for known transitions and eO(H2S√AT ) for unknown transitions. Both bounds incur an extra factor of H compared with the corresponding bounds for global optimization. Under full-information feedback and unknown transitions, this horizon gap can be closed by a policy optimization algorithm with regret eO(HS √AT ) (Li et al., 2026b). Aiming instead to improve the dependence on S,Tiapkin et al. (2025) use policy optimization and achieve regret eO(H3√SAT ) in the same setting. 

The horizon gap in policy optimization. The same horizon gap appears in the regret bounds of pol-icy optimization in many other settings. Under delayed bandit feedback, where losses from an episode are revealed only after a delay, global optimization achieves regret eO(√HSAT + H(SA )1/4√D) and 

eO(HS √AT + H(SA )1/4√D) for known and unknown transitions, respectively, where D denotes the total delay (Jin et al., 2022). The corresponding policy optimization bounds are eO(√H3SAT + H3√T + D)

and eO(H2S√AT + H3√D) (Lancewicki et al., 2023). Under preference feedback, where the learner ob-serves comparisons between arms rather than numerical losses, global and policy optimization achieve regret 

eO(( H2S2K)1/3T 2/3) and eO(( H6SK 5)1/3T 2/3), respectively, for adversarial preferences with Borda-score rewards, known transitions, and K arms (Tsuchiya et al., 2025). Under aggregate bandit feedback, the learner observes only the total loss of each trajectory rather than the 3losses at the visited state-action pairs. In contrast to the settings above, policy optimization is well suited to this feedback and achieves regret eO(√H3SAT + H3√T ) for known transitions and eO(H2S√AT ) for unknown transitions (Lancewicki and Mansour, 2025). For known transitions, this bound matches the lower bound Ω( √H3SAT ) and the best-known bound eO(√H3SAT ) of global optimization (Ito et al., 2025) up to logarithmic factors when SA ≥ H3.Other studies develop best-of-both-worlds policy optimization algorithms, which achieve polylog( T ) regret for stochastic losses and eO(√T ) regret for adversarial losses without knowing the type of losses (Dann et al., 2023), and some of which further achieve data-dependent regret bounds that exploit the structure of the loss sequence (Li et al., 2026a,b). Under bandit feedback, their leading worst-case terms remain eO(√H3SAT )

for known transitions and eO(H2S√AT ) for unknown transitions, which are the same as those of Luo et al. (2021). These methods therefore have the same extra factor of H relative to global optimization. Linear-mixture MDPs assume that the transition kernel is linear in a known d-dimensional feature map, allowing the learner to exploit this structure to generalize across states and actions (Ayoub et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2021). For adversarial linear-mixture MDPs with unknown transitions and bandit feedback, global optimization achieves regret eO(√HSAT + dS 3/2√HT ) (Li et al., 2024). In the same setting, policy optimization achieves regret eO(√H3SAT + dS 3/2√H3T ) (Cheng et al., 2026). This bound also incurs an extra factor of H in both the loss-estimation and transition-estimation terms compared with the global optimization bound. 

Regularized Q-functions. Regularized Q-functions have been studied in stochastic control and reinforce-ment learning. In stochastic optimal control, policy improvement with regularized Q-functions converges to the optimal cost (Rawlik et al., 2012). Similarly, in reinforcement learning, a regularized Q-function formulation yields dynamic policy programming for finding an optimal policy in discounted MDPs (Azar et al., 2012). Beyond these specific algorithms, a general theory of regularized MDPs analyzes how approximation errors affect policy performance (Geist et al., 2019). These regularized policy updates are also connected to occupancy measures. Neu et al. (2017) relate conditional-entropy regularization of occupancy measures to policy updates and interpret the finite-horizon update of Rawlik et al. (2012) as mirror descent. Unlike these works, we consider adversarial losses and use regularized Q-functions to analyze the stability of policy optimization under bandit feedback in (non-regularized) episodic MDPs.

We introduced a new policy optimization framework based on regularized Q-functions that improves the horizon dependence of regret bounds while retaining local policy updates. For tabular MDPs with unknown transitions, our algorithm closes the horizon gap between policy optimization and the best available regret bounds achieved by global optimization. We believe that this framework can also reduce the horizon dependence of policy optimization under delayed feedback and in best-of-both-worlds settings, and leave these extensions for future work.
