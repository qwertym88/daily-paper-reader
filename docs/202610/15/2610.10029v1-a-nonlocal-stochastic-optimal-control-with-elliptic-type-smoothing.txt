# A Nonlocal Stochastic Optimal Control with Elliptic-Type Smoothing 

S, tefana-Lucia Anit , a∗† Luca Di Persio ‡

Abstract 

We study a nonlocal stochastic optimal control problem in which the con-trol acts through the elliptic smoothing operator S = ( I − ∆) −1 on Rd. The state is described by the law of a controlled diffusion, equivalently by a con-trolled Fokker-Planck equation with coefficients depending on Su. We prove the existence of an optimal control by the direct method in the calculus of variations. We then derive a Pontryagin-type maximum principle by spike variations, relying on sharp properties of the operator S. The result yields a pointwise minimisation rule for the optimal control. We discuss the meaning of the optimal control problem in the case of population dynamics. 

Keywords: Stochastic differential equation, Stochastic optimal control problem, Fokker-Planck equation, Pontryagin maximum principle, Population dynamics. 

MSC: 49J20, 49K20, 49N45, 65N21, 92D25.

A possible continuation of the present work is the treatment of time-dependent controls u = u(t, x ), with elliptic smoothing performed at each time level through 

η(t, ·) = Su(t, ·), which would require compactness in both time and space for the 18 admissible controls, and the Pontryagin principle would yield a pointwise minimi-sation rule in (t, x ). Moreover, a further substantial extension is the treatment of matrix-valued non-scalar diffusions, since, in that case, the adjoint equation involves the full Hessian D2p, and the Fokker-Planck equation contains second-order deriva-tives of qij ρ, hence requiring a separate global Sobolev regularity analysis for the non-divergence and divergence formulations with matrix-valued coefficients. A further direction is to replace the resolvent S = ( I − ∆) −1 by fractional elliptic smoothing operators (I −∆) −θ, θ > 0. Indeed, in the latter setting, the spike analysis then depends on the exact singular behaviour of the fractional Bessel kernel at the origin. Identifying the sharp range of θ for which the weak spike asymptotics still yields a Pontryagin principle would connect the present model with a broader class of nonlocal interaction mechanisms.
