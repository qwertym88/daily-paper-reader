Abstract —Physically connected humanoids need consistent dy-namics for internal actuator loops, inter-robot forces, and chang-ing ground supports. We extend RoboCompiler with member-wise dynamics assembly, compiling reduced dynamics from rigid bodies, moving actuator parts, and reflected rotor inertias. Com-piled body Jacobians and analytic constraint curvature yield an explicit Coriolis factor satisfying the power identity for floating-base quasi-velocities. A two-level reduction retains reusable robot models while composing their supports and hand connections; local inertial updates have rank at most six, and prescribed-acceleration constraint wrenches are affine in actuator efforts. These interfaces support actuator-aware tracking, coupled force allocation, payload updates, and support-mode switching for five hand-linked humanoids. Across 264 feasible states in eight humanoid configurations, normalised acceleration discrepancies remain within 1.6×10 −10 against a hard-constraint reference and 

1.4 × 10 −10 against a compiler-independent reference; MuJoCo simulations achieve 0.73 mm root-mean-square (RMS) pelvis error in the ring dance and complete side steps through 20 support-mode changes. (a) (b)

Their dynamics must consistently account for loop closure, actuator transmission, and constraint curvature [1], while retaining each robot’s inertial structure as payloads and support modes change. The 

Springer Handbook of Robotics identifies synchronization and physical interaction as central issues in cooperative multirobot systems [2, Sec. 53.10.3]. Slipping interfaces add contact-mode and frictional-force requirements [3], motivating a model that separates persistent mechanical connections from chang-ing environmental contacts. Partial velocities and coordinate reduction establish dynamics in independent speeds and the recovery of constraint forces [4]–[6]. Constraint embedding or-ganises loop-constrained submechanisms for recursive dynam-ics [7], [8], while body-Jacobian formulations provide explicit Coriolis factors [9], [10]. Rigid-constraint libraries [11], [12] and regularised simulators [13] implement complementary constrained dynamics. The integration task is to expose addi-tive member contributions and reusable robot models directly from a physical record. RoboCompiler [14] generates closure differentials, rank-checked velocity lifts, power-conjugate actuator maps, and projected tree dynamics from physical records; its original implementation evaluates constraint curvature by finite dif-ferences. The member-wise formulation of [15] expresses dynamics through additive body contributions and supports local inertial changes when the coordinate-reduction map is supplied. The present extension connects these interfaces: it compiles the member Jacobians and their analytic rates, includes moving actuator bodies and reflected rotor inertias, and separates internal robot reduction from the composition of supports and inter-robot connections. A. Implementation 

A physical record lists bodies with inertias, joints with both attachment frames, actuator ports with effort bounds, drive data (rotor inertia, transmission, damping, friction) and named sole and palm frames; no joint is marked as a cut and no closure map is supplied. Our implementation of the compiler of [14] selects the tree, keeping requested independent joints, actuated joints and joints with drive data in it, forms the de-pendency modules and acquires a feasible configuration with the PACDM solver [16] of the RoboCompiler repository, used unchanged. During reference generation, hand-link dependent coordinates are reselected when the corresponding arm Jaco-bian becomes poorly conditioned. The resulting changes of representation follow the covariance relation in [14, Eq. (22)]; execution uses the prepared coordinate selections of the active support mode. Poses, screws and their rates are computed level by level on stacked arrays, and structurally identical modules of one layer are solved as one stacked system. One evaluation returns 

(Γ , C, g, D, L ), the robot modules, the composer lift and its rate. The Python/NumPy extension adds routines for analytic rates, member-wise dynamics assembly, composition, inertial updates, and wrench recovery in ipd/ , with record-based backend exporters and reproducible benchmark workflows. The executable model and benchmark definitions specify the geometry, inertias, drive parameters, contact settings, and reference motions. Dependency versions are pinned, and result summaries record package versions and a source-code hash. MuJoCo policy comparisons use the common sensor-noise seed; reported tracking errors are trajectory RMS values for this fixed seed. The same compiled arrays generate (i) the assembled dynamics model, (ii) an MJCF model in which tree joints carry their armature, damping and friction, chords are equality constraints and soles touch the floor through native contact, and (iii) a Pinocchio model with rigid constraint models for all chords. The views share the physical record and use distinct dynamics algorithms. The first reference solves a hard-constraint Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system in 50-digit ball arithmetic [19], assembled from double-precision Pinocchio tree terms and physical constraint rows. The realistic plant denotes the MuJoCo simulation [13] generated from the same records and stepped at 1000 Hz, with regularised equality constraints for loops and hand links and unilateral frictional contact under the soles (rubber on wood: friction coefficient 0.8, torsional friction, elliptic cones, 5 ms contact time constant). Foot–ground contacts can slip or separate, while hand links retain the spherical connection topology. The plant also includes the following drive and sensing effects: Coulomb friction in every drive and passive bearing (rod-end pins and ball joints are ideal), a first-order lag of 2 ms of every applied effort, the torque–speed limit of every motor (full effort up to 50% of the no-load speed), and zero-mean Gaussian noise on the measured state (standard deviations 0.1 mrad and 10 mrad/s for rotary joints, 5 μmand 1 mm/s for screws, 0.25 mm and 0.25 mrad for the base pose, 5 mm/s and 5 mrad/s for its twist). The ideal MuJoCo plant disables joint Coulomb friction, actuator lag, speed-dependent effort limits, and sensor noise, while retaining viscous damping, regularised constraints, and frictional sole contacts; the Pinocchio plant integrates the rigid constrained dynamics [11], [12] with ideal sole welds and Baumgarte position and velocity gains of 400 s −2 and 40 s −1. Noise is sampled independently at each control update; root poses are perturbed through the SE (3) exponential map. Cut-joint chart coordinates are reconstructed from the measured body poses. The controller selects the active mode’s independent measured speeds and reconstructs dependent model speeds through the compiled lifts. In MuJoCo executions, ideal support anchors are set to the current measured sole poses at each evaluation, while slip and separation are governed by the plant’s unilat-eral contacts. Reference generation applies closure correction; online evaluation uses a nominal constrained model at the measured configuration, with dependent speeds reconstructed through the compiled lifts. The analytic identities hold under the stated rank and closure assumptions. The controller updates at 2 ms intervals of simulated time, while MuJoCo advances in 1 ms steps. The proposed algorithm compiles composable dynamics from physical records, preserving coupled dynamics through analytic curvature and two-level composition.
