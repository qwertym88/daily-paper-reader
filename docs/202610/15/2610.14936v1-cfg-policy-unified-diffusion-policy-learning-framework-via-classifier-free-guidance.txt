## ABSTRACT 

Diffusion policies are increasingly used in robotic control and manipulation, vision-language-action models, and world-model-based agents. Their ability to repre-sent multimodal action distributions offers rich opportunities for exploration in reinforcement learning (RL). However, optimizing diffusion policies with RL can reduce the behavioral diversity and further limit their exploration capabil-ity. In RL post-training, directly fine-tuning a pretrained policy may also dis-rupt its acquired capabilities, while action-space residuals may lack the expres-sive power for multimodal exploration. Together, these issues reveal a common challenge across online and offline-to-online diffusion RL: improving task per-formance while retaining the behavioral diversity and capabilities needed for continued adaptation. We introduce CFG-Policy, a unified, plug-and-play frame-work that separates exploration and exploitation into two generative policies and combines their score or velocity fields during sampling through classifier-free guidance. For online RL, a frozen exploration branch sustains behavioral diver-sity while an exploitation branch learns from task rewards; for offline-to-online RL, a pretrained exploitation branch remains frozen while a trainable branch steers the composed behavior. CFG-Policy preserves the underlying RL objec-tives and improves performance with QSM and QVPO on 5 MuJoCo tasks and with Flow-SDE+PPO on 4 RoboTwin manipulation tasks. Project page is at

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion policies have become an expressive model class for sequential decision making. Their ability to represent complex, multimodal action distributions has supported robotic control and manipulation (Chi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025), vision-language-action policies (Chen et al., 2025a), and world-model-based policy adaptation (Chandra et al., 2025). Related flow-matching policies generate actions through continuous velocity fields and are increasingly used in reinforcement learning (RL) (Lipman et al., 2022; Park et al., 2025; Ding et al., 2026a; Hu et al., 2026). Beyond fitting demonstrations, these generative policies offer a rich space of plausible actions to explore when learning from rewards. However, the diversity that makes diffusion policies attractive for exploration can decline as reward optimization concentrates the policy on currently successful behaviors. Prior work has observed mode collapse and training instability when diffusion models are fine-tuned with RL (Barcel´ o et al., 2024). In online diffusion RL, methods such as Q-Score Matching and QVPO improve task performance by updating a single generative policy (Psenka et al., 2023; Ding et al., 2024); continued improvement then depends on that same policy still producing sufficiently diverse actions. In RL post-training, direct optimization of a pretrained diffusion or flow policy (Ren et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026) may disturb capabilities learned before RL. Residual policy methods can preserve a base policy, but action-space corrections may provide less expressive exploration than modifying the generative process itself (Johannink et al., 2019; Yuan et al., 2025). To address these challenges, we propose a unified, plug-and-play framework called CFG-Policy 

that separates exploration and exploitation into two generative policies. Inspired by classifier-free guidance (CFG) (Ho & Salimans, 2022), it combines their score or velocity fields at each step of action generation. Given two fields vA and vB , the field of behavior policy is induced by 

vw = vA + w(vB − vA) (1) where w controls their relative contribution. Both fields act on the same intermediate sample, so the combination steers the entire generative trajectory rather than interpolating completed actions. Because this composition replaces only the behavior sampler, it can be added to different diffusion RL algorithms without redefining their actor or critic objectives. The same mechanism handles two complementary learning regimes by changing which branch is frozen. In online RL , a separately trained exploration policy remains fixed while the exploitation policy learns from task rewards; the composed sampler maintains an exploratory source throughout training. In offline-to-online RL post-training , a pretrained exploitation policy remains fixed while a second, trainable branch steers the composed policy toward higher-return behaviors. The framework thus preserves exploration while learning exploitation in the first regime, and preserves an acquired policy while learning adaptive behavior in the second. We test this formulation with QSM and QVPO on five MuJoCo tasks and with Flow-SDE+PPO on four RoboTwin manipulation tasks, finding improvements in both regimes. ## 2 RELATED WORK 

Classifier-Free Guidance. Guidance was originally introduced in diffusion models to steer gen-eration toward desired conditions. Classifier guidance uses gradients from an auxiliary classifier to modify the reverse diffusion process (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), while classifier-free guidance (CFG) instead combines conditional and unconditional model predictions during sampling (Ho & Salimans, 2022). The guidance scale controls the strength of this steering and has been widely used in text-to-image systems such as GLIDE and Imagen (Nichol et al., 2021; Saharia et al., 2022). Beyond conditional generation within one model, composable diffusion models combine separately modeled concepts through their generative scores (Liu et al., 2022). Subsequent work shows that successful score composition also depends on the sampler and studies MCMC-based corrections for compositional generation (Du et al., 2023). 

Generative Reinforcement Learning. Offline RL established diffusion policies as expressive models of multimodal behavior: Diffusion-QL combines behavior modeling with Q-value maximiza-tion (Wang et al., 2022), while IDQL reweights actions sampled from a diffusion behavior policy using critic estimates (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023). Online diffusion RL must improve policies through interaction rather than samples from a fixed dataset (Ding et al., 2026b). QVPO uses a Q-weighted variational objective (Ding et al., 2024), and SDAC uses reweighted score matching to fit value-induced maximum-entropy policies (Ma et al., 2025). DIPO improves sampled actions with ∇aQ before refitting the policy (Yang et al., 2023), whereas QSM aligns the policy score with the critic gradient (Psenka et al., 2023). DACER, DIME, and MaxEntDP further study actor–critic updates and entropy-aware optimization for diffusion policies (Wang et al., 2024; Celik et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025). ExDM explores diffusion models for unsupervised reinforcement learning by modeling state visitation distributions and deriving intrinsic rewards from distributional novelty, encouraging agents to discover rarely visited states (Ying et al., 2026). Separately, CFGRL treats CFG as a controllable improvement operator for extracting a policy from offline data (Frans et al., 2025). These methods illustrate different ways to optimize or guide a generative policy, but generally leave exploration and exploitation coupled within the policy being improved. 

VLA and Generative Policy RL Post-Training. Large vision-language-action (VLA) policies such as π0 use flow matching to generate robot actions from visual and language inputs (Black et al., 2024). RL post-training seeks to improve such pretrained behavior through reward feedback. DPPO and ReinFlow develop policy-gradient methods for diffusion and flow policies (Ren et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026), while πRL applies online RL fine-tuning to flow-based VLA models (Chen et al., 2025a). RIPT-VLA uses sparse binary success rewards and interactive rollouts to post-train pretrained VLA models (Tan et al., 2025), and Flow Policy Optimization addresses policy-ratio estimation for online fine-tuning of flow-matching VLAs (Lyu et al., 2025). An alternative is residual adaptation, which freezes a base policy and learns an action-level correction (Johannink et al., 2019; Silver et al., 2018; Yuan et al., 2025). However, online diffusion RL methods can lose exploratory diversity as the policy converges toward high-reward behaviors, while RL post-training needs a more stable and sample-efficient way to explore beyond a pretrained policy. The proposed CFG-Policy addresses these distinct challenges by composing separate exploration and exploitation policies throughout diffusion or flow sampling. In online RL settings, a frozen exploration branch continues to supply diverse behavior as the exploitation branch improves. In offline-to-online RL post-training, the pretrained policy remains frozen while a trainable generative branch explores and steers the composed policy toward higher returns. This design preserves the selected behavioral capability, enables adaptation throughout the generative trajectory rather than only through final-action residuals, and can be plugged into compatible RL backbones without changing their optimization objectives. Notably, CFGRL uses guidance as a policy-improvement operator learned from fixed offline data (Frans et al., 2025), whereas CFG-Policy combines two behaviorally distinct policies and optimizes one through RL interaction, as summarized in Table 1.

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate CFG-Policy in the two complementary regimes introduced in the Method: online RL and RL post-training. Our experiments study whether (i) CFG-Policy can be applied to different generative RL algorithms without changing their original objectives, (ii) the same compositional mechanism is effective in both learning regimes, and (iii) adaptive generative composition is preferable to fixed guidance or simple action-space blending. 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Online RL. We evaluate on five MuJoCo tasks: HalfCheetah-v4, Walker2d-v4, Ant-v4, Humanoid-v4, and Hopper-v4. CFG-Policy is instantiated with QSM (Psenka et al., 2023) and QVPO (Ding et al., 2024). The original actor and critic objectives are unchanged. The exploitation branch is inherited from the base algorithm, while a separately pretrained RND exploration policy (Burda et al., 2018), which we use it for the exploration branch, is kept frozen during RL training. We report mean performance and sample standard deviation over 5 runs. 

RL post-training. We evaluate on four RoboTwin tasks (Chen et al., 2025b): Adjust Bottle, Pick Dual Bottles, Rotate QR Code, and Grab Roller. For both π0 and π0.5 (Black et al., 2024; Intelligence et al., 2025), the pretrained policy is frozen as the exploitation branch, while a second branch initialized from the same SFT checkpoint is optimized with RL. We compare against direct Flow-SDE+PPO fine-tuning and report task success rates. We provide introduction of the tasks of benchmark and further implementation details in Appendix A and B.4. 4.2 ONLINE REINFORCEMENT LEARNING

We investigate two design choices of CFG-Policy: (1) adaptive guidance coefficient selection and (2) generative-space composition compared with action-space interpolation. 

Adaptive versus fixed guidance. Figure 4 compares adaptive guidance with fixed coefficients 

w ∈ { 0.8, 0.98 } on HalfCheetah-v4. For both QSM and QVPO, adaptive guidance achieves higher final training returns than the tested fixed coefficients. This indicates that dynamically adjusting the exploration–exploitation trade-off can provide a more flexible alternative than using a single manually selected guidance coefficient throughout training. 

Generative composition versus action-space blending. We further compare CFG-Policy with directly interpolating actions sampled from the exploration and exploitation policies. As shown in Table 4, action-space blending consistently underperforms the original QVPO policy, even when using a large exploitation coefficient. In contrast, CFG-Policy combines generative fields during the sampling trajectory, allowing both policies to influence the denoising process. The results highlight the advantage of composing policies in the generative space rather than interpolating independently generated actions. 

## 5 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATION 

In this paper, we introduced CFG-Policy to address exploration–exploitation challenges across online diffusion RL and offline-to-online RL post-training. By composing separate generative policies during diffusion or flow sampling, it preserves one behavioral capability while the other is optimized using the original RL objective. In online RL, a frozen exploration branch sustains behavioral diversity as exploitation improves; in post-training, a frozen pretrained policy retains its acquired capabilities while a trainable branch steers behavior. With QSM and QVPO on 5 MuJoCo tasks, CFG-Policy achieves higher final returns and faster convergence, improving sample efficiency. CFG-Policy for Online Reinforcement Learning. We consider a fixed exploration policy πE ,a trainable exploitation policy πX , a critic Q, a replay buffer B, an underlying RL optimizer A,and a guidance coefficient w. We first initialize πX , Q, and B, while keeping the parameters of πE

frozen throughout training. At each environment step, both policies evaluate the same intermediate generative state conditioned on the current observation st, and their generative fields are composed as 

vw = (1 − w)vE + wv X .

The original sampler is otherwise left unchanged, and the resulting composed policy πw is used to generate the behavior action at. After executing at, the transition (st, a t, r t, s t+1 ) is stored in B. The optimizer A then updates the critic and the exploitation policy using its original RL objective, with gradients blocked through πE . Thus, CFG-Policy only modifies the sampling interface while preserving the optimization rule of the underlying RL algorithm. After training, the learned exploitation policy πX is returned.

Exploration branch pre-training. We pretrain a separate exploration policy using the intrinsic reward produced by Random Network Distillation (RND). The RND prediction error provides a novelty signal, while the exploration policy generates actions. This pretraining is conducted separately from reward-based training, and its environment steps are excluded from the formal training budget. ϵcfg (xt, t ) = w ϵ exploit (xt, t ) + (1 − w) ϵexplore (xt, t ), (56) and use the combined prediction in the original DDPM reverse update. Guidance therefore acts throughout the generative trajectory, rather than interpolating two completed actions. 

Guidance coefficient. The coefficient w ∈ [0 , 1] is a global scalar controlling the relative influence of the two branches: larger values favor exploitation, whereas smaller values favor RND-driven exploration. In the adaptive variant, w is updated during training to improve estimated action value while maintaining a target divergence from the pure exploitation branch. We initialize the base policy from a task-specific SFT checkpoint and keep it frozen throughout RL post-training. After loading the SFT weights, we copy its action decoder and action projections to create an independently trainable exploration expert. Both experts share the frozen vision–language backbone and its observation features, but maintain separate action-expert parameters. Consequently, they produce the same velocity before RL updates. At each denoising step, we combine their predictions as 

vCFG = (1 − w)vbase + w v exp , (57) where w is the guidance scale. Only the exploration expert and value head receive gradient updates. We use the standard Flow-SDE and clipped PPO training procedure. During action generation, one of the five denoising steps is randomly selected for a stochastic Flow-SDE transition, while the remaining steps use ODE transitions. PPO updates use the recorded transition log probability and GAE advantages. The policy predicts 50 action steps, executes the first 25, and then observes the environment again. Each run trains for 50 rounds and is evaluated every 10 rounds on 64 fixed scenes. We test guidance scales of 0.5, 0.8, and 0.98 ; the original single-policy Flow-SDE + PPO method is trained separately as the baseline. For detailed implementation parameters, see Table 6.
