## ABSTRACT 

Reinforcement learning with general utilities optimizes nonlinear functionals of policy-induced occupancy measures. We develop a natural actor-critic method for concave RLGU objectives admitting an exact variational dual representation, with explicit constructions for a broad class of separable occupancy utilities. Rather than estimating the occupancy measure and then forming the policy-gradient pseudo-reward, our method learns this pseudo-reward directly as a function of the state-action pair using a neural critic. The critic is trained by projected stochastic updates, while multilevel Monte Carlo combines outputs across training horizons to control its bias at logarithmic expected cost. Our analysis separates condi-tional actor bias from estimator second moments and accounts explicitly for dual optimization, critic approximation, and neural linearization errors. Under policy-transfer, dual-stability, and local critic-regularity conditions, the method achieves 

eO(T −1/2) global convergence above policy, critic-class, and finite-width approx-imation errors using eO(T ) expected environment transitions and reference sam-ples. For a fixed critic representation, this yields eO(ϵ−2) sampling complexity above the approximation floor, covering infinite state-action spaces and occupancy utilities beyond fixed finite vectors of returns. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Standard reinforcement learning (RL) maximizes an expected discounted sum of rewards, an objec-tive linear in the policy-induced occupancy measure. Many applications, however, depend on the occupancy distribution itself rather than on a fixed linear reward. For example, imitation learning seeks to match the learner’s occupancy measure to an expert’s target distribution, while exploration may encourage high visitation entropy (Ho & Ermon, 2016; Hazan et al., 2019). Reinforcement learning with general utilities (RLGU) provides a unified formulation: 

J(θ) = F (λθ ),

where λθ is the normalized discounted state-action occupancy measure and F is a utility functional. We focus on concave utilities and generally parameterized policies. Although J is generally non-concave in θ, concavity of F in the occupancy measure provides the global comparison structure used in our analysis (Zhang et al., 2020; Barakat et al., 2025). The main statistical challenge is the policy-dependent pseudo-reward rθ = ∇λF (λθ ). Holding this reward fixed, the policy gradient takes the form 

∇θ J(θ) = (1 − γ) ∇ϑV πϑ (rθ )|ϑ=θ . (1) For small finite spaces, one can estimate the occupancy measure directly and then evaluate rθ . In larger spaces, however, occupancy approximation becomes an additional learning problem. In this work, we study concave RLGU objectives admitting an exact variational dual representation, and give explicit constructions for a broad class of separable occupancy utilities. Instead of first estimating λθ and then forming rθ , we directly learn the dual pseudo-reward as a function of (s, a )

using a neural critic. This places the approximation requirement on the reward function actually needed by the actor, rather than on the full occupancy density, and allows the framework to extend naturally to infinite state-action spaces. Our method and analysis hinge on two technical ideas. 

Technical novelty: learning the dual reward. Building on Fenchel duality and variational policy gradients (Zhang et al., 2020; Zahavy et al., 2021), we learn the pseudo-reward through stochastic neural dual optimization (Section 2.2). Our finite-time analysis uses dual curvature and feature conditioning to establish a stable recursion toward the constrained dual minimizer, separating dual-class approximation from neural linearization error. Table 2 in Appendix E gives reference-sampling estimators for the required dual gradients. 

Technical novelty: controlling the bias of a trained critic. The critic’s O(H−1/2) root-mean-square error above its approximation floor would require long training if used directly as actor bias. Adapting MLMC and neural actor-critic analyses (Blanchet & Glynn, 2015; Ganesh et al., 2025a; Naskar & Aggarwal, 2026), we apply randomized corrections to critic output functions at dyadic horizons, matching the longest run’s mean at logarithmic expected training cost (Lemma 4). Here MLMC controls a learned dual reward rather than finite scalarization coefficients. Separating actor bias from second moments in the KL-potential bound (Lemma 1) and adjusting the actor stepsize preserves the eO(T −1/2) statistical rate with explicit finite-width error. 

Contributions: Our key contributions are as follows: • We develop a neural dual approach to concave RLGU that learns the utility-induced pseudo-reward directly, avoiding explicit occupancy estimation. For separable utilities, we derive explicit dual objectives and sampling formulas (Table 2, and Proposition 1 in Appendix E). • We introduce a function-valued MLMC correction across critic-training horizons that in-herits the bias of a long critic run at logarithmic expected training cost. We establish critic bounds and a natural-gradient analysis separating conditional actor bias from estimator second moments (Lemmas 3, 4, and 2). • Combining these ingredients, we obtain eO(ϵ−2) expected sampling complexity above an explicit approximation floor for a fixed critic representation, including infinite state–action spaces (Theorem 1). Table 1 contrasts this guarantee with existing RLGU results in repre-sentation, scope, and complexity. 21.1 RELATED WORKS :

General-utility and convex reinforcement learning: Hazan et al. (2019) study concave visitation objectives through planning, and Zhang et al. (2020) develop the variational policy-gradient and Fenchel-dual formulation. Zahavy et al. (2021) express convex RL as a policy-cost game, while Geist et al. (2022) connect it to mean-field games and Frank-Wolfe updates. We build on these objective and duality foundations, but analyze a trained neural pseudo-reward. 

Large-state RLGU and occupancy approximation: Barakat et al. (2023) provide variance-reduced tabular methods and a linear occupancy approximation method. Barakat et al. (2025) obtain global guarantees using maximum-likelihood occupancy estimation. Table 1 distinguishes their rep-resentation assumptions and guarantees. Our dual-reward approach instead imposes approximation and stability conditions on the utility gradient rather than the occupancy density. 

Concave utility RL. Finite-objective concave utility RL (CURL) optimizes a concave function of finitely many expected returns (Agarwal & Aggarwal, 2023; Agarwal et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022). For finite state-action spaces, it is equivalent to concave RLGU: M = |S||A| indicator rewards recover the occupancy vector through normalized returns (Ganesh & Aggarwal, 2026). In infinite spaces, general RLGU is strictly more expressive, since finitely many occupancy integrals cannot represent every occupancy utility. Proposition 2 in Appendix F gives the finite reduction and a continuous-state counterexample. 

Variational and Fenchel-dual approaches: Our stochastic dual learning also relates to varia-tional divergence estimation (Nowozin et al., 2016). The actor analysis adapts approximate NPG arguments from Agarwal et al. (2021); Ganesh et al. (2025a;b), while the critic-output correction adapts MLMC ideas from Blanchet & Glynn (2015); Naskar & Aggarwal (2026). Ganesh & Aggar-wal (2026) obtain the ϵ−2 exponent for finite-objective concave scalarization; our extension treats a function-valued dual reward and accounts for its optimization, approximation, and sampling errors.

## H ADDITIONAL RELATED WORKS 

Variational duality and policy-gradient analysis. Our analysis builds on the general-utility policy-gradient identity and concave-dual representation of Zhang et al. (2020); Section 2.2 fixes the reward convention. Lemma 5 combines the classical performance-difference identity with the supporting hyperplane inequality for concave utilities. Appendix A adapts approximate NPG anal-ysis with transferred errors (Agarwal et al., 2021; Ganesh et al., 2025a), isolating the conditional actor bias needed for the multilevel estimator. 

Occupancy approximation in large-state RLGU. Barakat et al. (2023) study linear occupancy fitting, while Barakat et al. (2026) use maximum-likelihood occupancy estimation. Their represen-tation assumptions concern distributions; ours concern the population minimizer of a dual-reward problem, with sufficient approximation conditions given in Proposition 3. The improved sampling exponent in Table 1 depends on dual stability, reward approximation, and the stated sampling access; it does not imply that either representation class uniformly dominates the other. 

Finite-objective CURL. Agarwal & Aggarwal (2023); Agarwal et al. (2022); Bai et al. (2022) de-velop finite-objective CURL through model-based and policy-gradient analyses. Since CURL also denotes occupancy-functional objectives (Geist et al., 2022), our comparison specifically concerns the fixed-finite-return formulation. Ganesh & Aggarwal (2026) apply MLMC to a finite vector of scalarization derivatives, achieving the ϵ−2 sampling exponent. Proposition 2 connects this result to finite RLGU, with objective-count and regularity dependence inherited from the reduction. In infinite spaces, our function-valued formulation accommodates utilities beyond finite moment sum-maries. 

MLMC estimation and neural actor-critic analysis. MLMC is an established debiasing tech-nique (Blanchet & Glynn, 2015). Neural actor-critic analyses use local linearization and bias-sensitive NPG bounds (Ganesh et al., 2025a; Satheesh et al., 2026), while Naskar & Aggarwal (2026) combines multilevel estimation with neural critics for average-reward constrained problems. We specialize these tools to stochastic concave-dual optimization with reset discounted trajectories, explicitly counting dual-reference samples and retaining neural linearization error in the MLMC sec-ond moment and actor stepsize. Our fixed-representation guarantee also tracks feature conditioning through (50) and (25). 20
