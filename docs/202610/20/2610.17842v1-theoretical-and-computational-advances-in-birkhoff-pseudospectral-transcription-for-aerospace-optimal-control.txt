# Theoretical and Computational Advances in Birkhoff Pseudospectral Transcription for Aerospace Optimal Control 

Riccardo Minnozzi ∗ and Marco Sagliano †

University of Bologna, Via L. Montaspro 97, Forlì (FC), Italy, 47121 

This paper develops theoretical and numerical extensions of Birkhoff Pseudospectral (PS) transcription for aerospace Optimal Control Problems (OCPs). Building on the recently proposed body of knowledge a Birkhoff integration operator is derived for flipped-Legendre-Gauss-Radau (fLGR) grids, drawing connections to regular Legendre-Gauss-Radau (LGR) grids and to the favorable conditioning properties that make this PS approach particularly appealing. We also propose the enforcement of differential constraints via PS operators, and its primal and dual equivalence to the state augmentation approach is established for control-rate limits. The numerical developments include the formulation and solution of heterogeneous multiphase OCPs, parameterized non-affine time mappings, composition of affine scaling and equilibration methods, and a hybrid derivative strategy to combine linear PS operators with numerical derivatives for nonlinear functions. These methods are implemented in the Rapid Pseudospectral Trajectory Optimization and Robustification (RAPTOR) transcription framework and assessed through feature-oriented benchmarks: a one-dimensional landing problem, a planar orbit raising scenario, an atmospheric entry problem, and a multiphase Reusable Launch Vehicle (RLV) problem with simultaneous booster-recovery and upper-stage-insertion trajectories. 

## I. Introduction 

Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) phases represent some of the most demanding trajectory-design problems encountered in aerospace applications. From planetary exploration vehicles to reusable launch systems, their design requires the simultaneous satisfaction of nonlinear vehicle dynamics, operational and path constraints, and terminal conditions, while optimizing mission-specific performance objectives. Such trajectory-design problems are naturally formulated as Optimal Control Problems (OCPs), in which the state and control trajectories are selected to minimize a prescribed performance metric subject to the governing dynamics and constraints [1]. Numerical approaches to optimal control are commonly classified as indirect or direct methods. Indirect methods apply the necessary conditions of optimality to reformulate the OCP as a multi-point boundary-value problem [2], whereas direct methods discretize the

continuous problem and transcribe it into a finite-dimensional parametric optimization problem [3]. Direct methods have become widely adopted for aerospace trajectory optimization thanks to their robustness in enforcing nonlinear dynamics, operational constraints, and complex mission architectures [3–5]. Among them, Pseudospectral (PS) methods approximate the continuous state trajectories using high-order polynomial representations and enforce the system dynamics at a set of nonuniform collocation nodes, thereby providing algebraic approximations of the differential and integral operators [6]. For sufficiently smooth solutions, these methods exhibit spectral spatial convergence with increasing polynomial order, meaning that complex trajectories can be accurately captured with low-order discretizations [4]. Moreover, appropriate covector-mapping theorems enable the reconstruction of the continuous-time costates from the multipliers of the discretized problem, allowing the reconstruction of dual information from the converged solutions [7, 8]. Different PS formulations arise from both the selected node distribution and the interpolation representation of the states. Classical methods are commonly based on Lagrange interpolation at Gauss-type nodes, while more recent formulations based on Birkhoff interpolation employ mixed function- and derivative-value information to construct alternative discretizations [6, 9]. The literature on PS optimal control is extensive. Ref. [6] provides a general treatment of the numerical foundations of pseudospectral approximation, while Ref. [4] reviews their development and flight applications. Aerospace applications range from low-thrust trajectory optimization [10–12] to atmospheric entry, powered descent, and reusable-launch-vehicle trajectory design [13–17]. Pseudospectral discretizations have traditionally been coupled with Nonlinear Program (NLP) solvers and have more recently also been incorporated into convex trajectory-optimization frameworks [18]. Despite their favorable convergence properties, high-order Lagrange PS discretizations exhibit an important numerical limitation: the conditioning of differentiation-matrix-based formulations deteriorates approximately as O( 𝑁 2)

with the number of discretization nodes 𝑁 [9]. Multi-interval and ℎ𝑝 -adaptive approaches mitigate this limitation by distributing the approximation among multiple lower-order polynomial segments; nevertheless, the deteriorating conditioning of the underlying differentiation formulation limits the practical use of very high-order discretizations. Birkhoff-based PS formulations provide an alternative approach to this problem. By constructing the state approximation from mixed boundary-value and derivative information, the differential transcription can be recast in an integration-matrix form whose conditioning can remain essentially independent of the discretization order for relevant boundary-condition configurations [9]. An extensive theoretical framework on universal Birkhoff methods is introduced in Refs. [9, 19], covering the construction and covector mapping of Birkhoff PS methods built on Legendre and Chebyshev grids for Gauss, Radau, and Lobatto node families. Motivated by these promising conditioning properties, this work introduces several theoretical and numerical advancements in the context of Birkhoff PS methods, and implements them within the Rapid Pseudospectral Trajectory Optimization and Robustification (RAPTOR) ∗ framework, a modular MATLAB transcription engine for multi-phase

The theoretical contributions of this manuscript are the following: first, we derive the Birkhoff integration formulation for flipped-Legendre-Gauss-Radau (fLGR) grids and relate it to existing results on Legendre-Gauss-Radau (LGR) grids and Birkhoff methods in general. Second, we introduce a direct approach to enforce differential constraints of nonlinear variables, and derive its primal-dual equivalence to the state-augmentation approach for the specific case of control-rate constraints. The work complements these theoretical advancements with numerical contributions, including support for heterogeneous multiphase transcription, non-affine time mapping for targeted/adaptive mesh-resolution, and the composition of affine scaling methods, enabling a unified implementation of Birkhoff PS optimal control for generic problems. The theoretical and numerical aspects previously introduced are then demonstrated on four benchmark problems: a one-dimensional landing problem for non-affine time mapping and differential constraints, a dense-grid orbit-raising problem for conditioning comparisons between Lagrange and Birkhoff formulations, an atmospheric-entry problem to evaluate the composition of different scaling techniques, and an end-to-end launcher mission coupling upper-stage insertion with booster Return To Launch Site (RTLS) to showcase a relevant multiphase OCP with phase branching. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II summarizes PS integration methods and illustrates the Birkhoff formulation for fLGR grids. Section III describes direct differential-constraint enforcement and its equivalence to state augmentation. Section IV focuses on the numerical advances that led to the unified transcription framework implemented in RAPTOR, including non-affine time mappings and the composition of affine scaling techniques. Section V presents the corresponding landing, orbit-raising, atmospheric-entry, and multiphase Reusable Launch Vehicle (RLV) demonstrations.

## IV. Numerical Advancements 

The theoretical developments in Secs. II and III are integrated into a common numerical framework that enables heterogeneous multiphase PS transcription. Each phase is transcribed according to its independently defined node family, polynomial order, and underlying global interpolant (Lagrange or Birkhoff), while endpoint and linkage conditions couple the phasewise discretizations into a single NLP. Within this setting, we introduce two numerical developments: parameterized non-affine time mappings, allowing targeted redistribution of temporal resolution of the PS discretization, and composable affine transformations, for scaling and equilibrating the transcribed problem. The methodology is implemented in RAPTOR, which provides the computational framework used for the numerical assessments in Sec. V.

D. Hybrid Jacobian Assembly and Solution Recovery 

Problem-specific functions such as dynamics, costs, and constraints are typically arbitrary nonlinear functions of the state, control, time, and parameters. Conversely, the PS operators that define the transcription are linear. The transcription process implemented in RAPTOR exploits this separation by combining exact derivatives of the linear PS operators with numerically differentiated derivatives of the nonlinear problem-specific functions. This hybrid approach allows the known structure and accuracy of the PS operators to be retained while avoiding the computational costs and potential inaccuracies of blindly applying numerical differentiation techniques to the entire transcription. For a Lagrange phase, the differentiation matrix enters the Jacobian directly through the defects in Eq. (7). For a Birkhoff phase, the integration matrix enters through the reconstruction constraints in Eq. (12), while the dynamics equations in Eq. (14) remain node-local. The same separation applies to the objective gradient, for which derivatives of the running cost are combined with the quadrature weights. This hybrid assembly avoids differentiating the complete transcription numerically and preserves the known structure of its PS operators. At a given collocation node, the derivatives of f, g, h, and 𝐿 depend only on the state and control values at that node, 18 together with the applicable times and parameters. When the nodal quantities are assembled over a phase, their state and control derivatives therefore form block-diagonal Jacobian contributions. Derivatives with respect to phase times, phase parameters, and shared parameters instead form block columns, as these variables spread their dependence across all nodes through the time dilation factor and direct dependence in problem-specific functions. The node-local derivatives are evaluated simultaneously across the collocation grid and combined with the exact operator blocks to form the constraint Jacobian and objective gradient. ## V. Numerical Results 

We now introduce four targeted benchmark problems to assess and verify the theoretical and numerical contributions presented above: a one-dimensional landing problem examines non-affine time mapping and the direct treatment 19 of thrust-rate limits, a planar orbit-raising problem compares the conditioning and dense-grid behavior of the Lagrange and Birkhoff formulations, a dimensional atmospheric-entry problem evaluates individual and composed scaling strategies, and a five-phase RLV problem demonstrates multiphase capabilities in a branching mission with simultaneous first-stage recovery and upper-stage insertion. Computations were performed in MATLAB R2023b on an AMD Ryzen 5 5600H processor with 16 GB of DDR4 memory. The RAPTOR source code is available at 

https://github.com/raptor-solver/raptor-matlab.git . While the RAPTOR framework interfaces with NLP solvers Interior Point OPTimizer (IPOPT) [33], Sparse Nonlinear OPTimizer (SNOPT) [34], and We Optimize Really Huge Problems (WORHP) [35], results adopt the IPOPT solver unless stated otherwise.

## VI. Conclusions 

This manuscript introduced theoretical and numerical extensions of Birkhoff PS transcription for aerospace OCPs. The Birkhoff integration operator was constructed directly for fLGR grids, pairing the Birkhoff-𝑎 formulation with the noncollocated initial endpoint. The formulation replaces the high-order differentiation defects of a Lagrange transcription with integration-based reconstruction constraints and node-local dynamics equations. Its conditioning follows the anchor-dependent behavior established for Birkhoff PS transcriptions: retaining the anchor-state column in the reconstruction system produces O( √𝑁 ) growth, whereas eliminating a prescribed anchor state places the remaining PS-specific reconstruction block in the O( 1) regime [19, 21]. Both behaviors remain favorable relative to the approximately O( 𝑁 2) growth of the Lagrange differentiation operator. These rates characterize the linear PS-operator blocks rather than the complete NLP or KKT system. Differential constraints on nonlinear trajectory quantities were formulated directly through the PS operators. For control-rate limits, eliminating the auxiliary rate control from the state-augmentation formulation recovers the direct discrete constraints under identical problem collocation. The multiplier difference associated with the lower and upper rate bounds also satisfies the auxiliary costate equation of the augmented formulation. The direct approach therefore preserves the primal and dual structure of state augmentation while avoiding its additional rate-control variables and collocation equations. The numerical developments complement these theoretical results within a heterogeneous multiphase transcription. Variable-dependent non-affine time mappings allow the redistribution of PS nodes in physical time while consistently transforming the dynamics, differential constraints, and integral cost. The ordered composition of affine scaling and equilibration methods permits each strategy to act on the coordinates produced by the preceding transformations, reproducing pre-existing scaling approaches (e.g. PJRN [14]) and allowing the design of potentially improved problem-tailored scaling. Exact derivatives of the linear PS operators are combined with node-local numerical derivatives of the nonlinear functions, retaining the known transcription structure without numerically differentiating the complete NLP. These methods are implemented in RAPTOR, which provides the common computational framework used for their assessment. The benchmarks isolate the role of each presented development. For the landing problem, a non-affine time mapping improves the coarse-grid representation of the thrust switch without increasing the NLP dimension. Direct enforcement of the thrust-rate limit reproduces the primal and dual solutions of state augmentation while reducing the number of 32 decision variables and the mean solution time. The dense-grid orbit-raising study recovers the expected O( 𝑁 2) and 

O( √𝑁 ) conditioning trends for the Lagrange differentiation matrix and the unreduced Birkhoff reconstruction system, respectively, with the Birkhoff formulation exhibiting shorter solution times for this benchmark. For atmospheric entry, applying static Ruiz equilibration after PJRN reduces the effective initial-guess Jacobian condition number and the mean solution time, while recovering the same solution. Finally, the five-phase reusable-launch-vehicle problem demonstrates nonlinear branching linkages that couple a shared ascent to simultaneous booster-recovery and upper-stage-insertion trajectories.
