Abstract 

We characterize the randomized component-oracle complexity of finite-sum nonconvex–concave optimization with a bounded dual domain. For Φ( x) = max y∈Y n−1 ∑ni=1 fi(x, y ), the goal is to find an ε-stationary point of the Moreau envelope Φ 1/(2 L) with probability at least 2 /3 and a fixed query budget. Let A = L∆/ε 2, B = LD Y /ε , and ¯B = max {1, B }, where ∆ bounds the initial primal gap and DY bounds the dual diameter. For A above a universal constant, the worst-case complexity is

The bounds agree within constants for B ≤ n1/4 and within one logarithm for larger B. Upper bounds require only mean-squared averaged smoothness and concavity of the average in the dual variable; lower bounds already hold under individual smoothness. The lower constructions cover adaptive component selection, arbitrary query norms, private randomization, and outputs that were never queried. A cone construction gives the small-diameter bound, while a serial branch construction and balanced replication give the remaining two regimes. On the upper side, a persistent estimator with primal averaging complements accelerated proximal dual steps equipped with an explicit primal certificate. The characterization takes the worst case over admissible known dual domains and finite dimensions. 

Keywords: finite-sum optimization; nonconvex–concave minimax optimization; oracle complexity; randomized lower bounds; Moreau stationarity.

# 7 Scope and Further Questions 

The result concerns exact component-query complexity. It does not bound arithmetic time, bit complexity, storage, or the cost of projecting onto an arbitrary known convex domain. The lower-bound dimension is finite but may grow exponentially with the serialized branch length. Obtaining comparable bounds in a fixed or polynomially bounded dimension remains a separate question. The equality of IS and AS rates uses average dual concavity. Imposing dual concavity on every component gives a smaller class, and the branch lower construction does not establish the same characterization there. The common-interaction refinement in (19) also indicates that additional structure can change the diameter dependence. Removing the remaining logarithm for the general large-diameter finite-sum class, and determining sharp constants in the transition from zero-query to nontrivial accuracy, are further questions. No empirical performance or practical tuning claim is needed for the oracle characterization.

This section does not treat a general strongly monotone operator as a gradient. A simple projected finite-sum solver is used only on strongly convex–strongly concave saddle subproblems. Acceleration is applied to a genuine convex dual objective, and a primal averaging certificate is proved explicitly. In particular, a small dual objective gap alone is not substituted for a small primal proximal gap.

For a current primal center c set
