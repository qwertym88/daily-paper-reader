Abstract 

This paper develops a polynomial parametric Koopman operator (PPKO) framework for stochastic model predictive control (SMPC) of nonlinear systems with parametric uncertainty and time-varying stochastic disturbances. The Koopman operator is parameterized by polynomial chaos expansions (PCEs), enabling data-driven nonlinear dynamics to be integrated with PCE-based uncertainty propagation and control. We show that the polynomial parameterization introduces no irreducible approximation gap beyond PCE truncation. We then derive a condensed deterministic SMPC reformulation that handles both time-invariant parametric uncertainty and time-varying disturbances while retaining an online optimization dimension that depends only on the control horizon and input dimension, independent of the lifted-state and PCE dimensions. The resulting formulation yields convex programs with expectation and second-moment constraints that can be solved using standard optimization methods. Numerical studies on the Duffing oscillator and the electrothermal cooling system demonstrate the accuracy, scalability, and closed-loop performance of the proposed framework. 

Key words: Koopman operators; Polynomial chaos expansions; Stochastic model predictive control; Nonlinear stochastic systems. 

1 Introduction 

Many real-world control systems operate under signif-icant uncertainties arising from uncertain physical pa-rameters and varying operating conditions. Some com-mon examples include chemical reaction systems with uncertain kinetic and heat transfer coefficients [32], me-chanical systems with uncertain mass or stiffness pa-rameters [24], and fluctuations in ambient temperatures in building control [16]. In such scenarios, stochastic model predictive control (SMPC) has emerged as a prin-cipled approach to handle uncertainty by explicitly in-corporating probabilistic models of uncertain parame-ters and stochastic disturbances into the predictive con-trol framework [25]. tation of SMPC is the need to propagate uncertainty through the system dynamics and evaluate expecta-tions appearing in the cost and constraint functions. When the underlying system dynamics are nonlinear, the resulting SMPC problem is typically formulated as a nonlinear program (NLP), which must be solved at each sampling time. Such NLPs are generally computation-ally expensive and difficult to integrate with real-time optimization in practical applications. In contrast, for stochastic systems with linear dynamics, a rich set of computationally efficient methods exists in the litera-ture, including approaches based on polynomial chaos expansions (PCEs) [35,10,4,26,27,36]. These PCE-based methods approximate the stochastic dynamics by pro-jecting onto a set of orthogonal polynomials, which results in a deterministic expanded system of PCE coef-ficients that can be controlled with deterministic MPC methods. Although PCE-based SMPC methods are widely used, they have several notable drawbacks. First, stochastic Galerkin projections (SGPs) do not necessarily preserve stability, even for linear systems, and introduce trunca-

Preprint submitted to Automatica 20 October 2026 arXiv:2610.24105v1 [eess.SY] 16 Oct 2026 tion errors that can propagate over the prediction hori-zon [30,12]. Second, PCE-based methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality, as the number of basis polyno-mials for a fixed degree increases combinatorially with the uncertainty dimension [6]. This issue is further exac-erbated in the time-varying disturbance setting, where the uncertainty dimension increases linearly with the SMPC horizon, further worsening the curse of dimen-sionality [5,7]. On the other hand, in the deterministic MPC literature, the MPC problem dimension can be made to depend only on the horizon and control dimen-sions through a technique known as model condensation 

[31,19]. This process algebraically eliminates the states by recursively substituting the dynamical equations into the prediction model. Altogether, these observations mo-tivate the use of condensing techniques in PCE-based SMPC to ensure that the online optimization problem dimension scales only with the horizon and control di-mensions, rather than with the number of PCE basis polynomials. Recently, a popular data-driven approach to improve the computational tractability of nonlinear determinis-tic MPC is to learn a linear high-dimensional represen-tation of the dynamics with Koopman operator meth-ods [21,29,3,34,22,37]. These methods enable the refor-mulation of nonlinear MPC optimization problems as compact QPs after condensation to remove the high-dimensional state dependence [40]. To address model-ing errors arising from the finite-dimensional approxima-tion of the Koopman operator, robust tube-based MPC formulations have been proposed that ensure closed-loop stability under bounded additive disturbances [43], while stochastic MPC has been combined with Koopman models to handle approximation uncertainty probabilis-tically through chance constraints [20]. However, exist-ing approaches do not jointly incorporate time-invariant parametric uncertainty and time-varying stochastic dis-turbances directly into the Koopman representation in a form that enables PCE-based uncertainty propaga-tion and condensed SMPC. Parametric Koopman oper-ators have been proposed to extend Koopman represen-tations to systems with time-invariant or time-varying inputs [14,37], which implicitly suggests parameterizing the Koopman operator by a PCE. This approach en-ables the application of linear SMPC methods to nonlin-ear systems, which is the gap addressed by our proposed Polynomial Parametric Koopman Operators (PPKO) framework. 

1.1 Contributions 

The main contributions of this article are as follows. 

• We propose a PCE-based parametric Koopman operator framework for nonlinear dynamical sys-tems with time-invariant parametric uncertainty and time-varying stochastic disturbances. 

• We show that restricting the parameter dependence of the Koopman operator to a finite-order PCE in-troduces no irreducible approximation gap beyond polynomial truncation; under the stated assump-tions, this truncation error vanishes as the polyno-mial order increases. 

• We derive a condensed deterministic reformula-tion of the SMPC problem for systems with both time-invariant parametric uncertainty and time-varying stochastic disturbances. Exploiting the IID disturbance structure, the required first- and second-order moments are propagated recursively and computed offline, yielding an online convex optimization problem whose dimension scales only with the control horizon and input dimension, in-dependent of the number of Koopman observables and retained PCE terms. 

• We validate the framework on both ODE and PDE examples, including the Duffing oscillator and the electrothermal cooling system. This article extends the previously presented results in [18] by generalizing the framework to include time-varying stochastic disturbances, a rigorous analysis of the approximation gap when parameterizing Koop-man operators with PCEs, and an additional numerical study using the electrothermal cooling system as a high-dimensional PDE–ODE example.

In deterministic nonlinear MPC, Koopman operators have been used to lift the nonlinear dynamics in (2b) to a higher-dimensional space of observables in which the lifted states evolve linearly. This allows the nonlinear MPC problem to be reformulated as a linear MPC prob-lem. With quadratic stage costs and linear constraints, the resulting optimization is a convex quadratic pro-gram (QP). On the other hand, there is a robust literature on SMPC frameworks based on PCEs, which enable uncer-tainty propagation into the prediction model in a struc-tured and computationally tractable manner, thereby facilitating the evaluation of stochastic objectives and moment-based constraints [26,25]. This implicitly sug-gests combining these two methodologies into a single framework for SMPC of nonlinear stochastic processes.

4 Polynomial Parametric Koopman Operators for Stochastic Systems 

This section develops an extension of conventional Koopman models for stochastic systems, which we refer to as Polynomial Parametric Koopman Operators (PP-KOs). As discussed in the introduction, the uncertain nonlinear dynamics in (2b) generally cannot be effi-ciently represented by a single uncertainty-independent Koopman operator. This motivates a family of Koop-man operators parameterized by the joint uncertainty variables ξ = ( w, θ ). Parametric Koopman models were introduced in [14] to construct Koopman approxima-tions of dynamical systems with unknown parameters. 5 Condensed Deterministic SMPC Reformula-tion 

Once a PCE LPV model such as (14) is identified, the conventional approach for deterministic SMPC refor-mulation is to apply Stochastic Galerkin Projections (SGPs) to (2), which results in a deterministic expanded system for the PCE coefficients. The conventional SGP approach increases the state dimension proportionately with the number of retained PCE polynomials, which increases the computational burden of the determinis-tic reformulation of SMPC. SGPs also induce additional truncation error during uncertainty propagation and the errors may propagate along the prediction horizon. In this section, inspired by condensing methods in MPC, we propose a deterministic reformulation approach that explicitly avoids the use of SGPs, which eliminates trun-cation errors and yields an online optimization whose decision vector is the stacked input sequence, with di-mension depending only on the control horizon and in-put dimension.

5.2 Computing the Condensed Matrices 

Although the resulting optimization problem is deter-ministic, the remaining computational challenge lies in evaluating the expectations over the parametric un-certainty and stochastic disturbances (θ, w) required to construct the condensed matrices {S, ¯Gt, Mt, ct}.A straightforward approach is to approximate these expectations by sampling many realizations of (θ, w).However, since the dimension of the disturbance tra-jectory w = ( w0, . . . , w H−1) grows linearly with the prediction horizon H, this approach can become com-putationally burdensome for long horizons. To address this challenge, we propose an offline recursive moment-condensation algorithm that uses Gaussian quadrature to integrate over the parametric uncertainty θ and ex-ploits both the IID structure of {wt} and the PCE structure of the PPKO model to efficiently compute the required condensed matrices. In particular, condi-tional on θ, the current realization wt is independent of the past and hence independent of zt, which allows the required conditional expectations to be propagated re-cursively using averaged one-step operators rather than by explicitly parameterizing the full disturbance path.

6 Numerical Examples 

This section presents numerical results demonstrating the performance of the proposed PPKO-SMPC frame-work on two stochastic nonlinear dynamical systems: a Duffing oscillator subject to random forcing and a high-dimensional stochastic electrothermal cooling sys-tem governed by a coupled ODE–PDE model. These two distinct examples are selected because they exhibit nontrivial closed-loop regulation challenges. In particu-lar, the electrothermal cooling system provides a high-dimensional case study that highlights the computa-tional advantages of our condensed SMPC reformula-tion. In each case study, we compare the open-loop and closed-loop performance of four different MPC frame-works: (1) Deterministic EDMD-DL (2) Scenario-based SMPC (3) Intrusive SGP-based SMPC (4) PPKO-SMPC (our framework) The PPKO-SMPC framework and the numerical re-sults can be reproduced using the code available at https://github.com/wallytgy/Koopman-NN . All computational examples were carried out on a Dell Latitude 7440 laptop equipped with a 13th Gen Intel Core i7-1365U processor and 32 GB of RAM, running Microsoft Windows 11 Enterprise. Reported solve times include online prediction/assembly and optimization, excluding offline setup and Monte Carlo audits. 

6.1 Stochastic Duffing Oscillator 

Consider the controlled Duffing oscillator:

6.1.1 Model Identification for PPKO and Baselines 

Data generation. The continuous-time dynamics in (73) were discretized with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme using sampling interval ∆t = 0 .02 , over 200 time steps per trajectory. For model identification, we gen-erated 400 stochastic trajectories, each with indepen-dently sampled uncertain parameters and disturbance realizations. For each trajectory, the initial condition was sampled from U[−1.5, 1.5) × U [−1.5, 1.5) , and the inputs were sampled i.i.d. from N (0 , 0.62) and applied with zero-order hold over each control interval. The dataset was split at the trajectory level into 280/60/60 train/validation/test trajectories. 

Koopman observables. For both PPKO and the de-terministic EDMD-DL baseline, Koopman observables were learned with EDMD-DL using a 28-dimensional lift: one constant observable, two coordinate observ-ables, and 25 learned observables. The feature map was parameterized by a two-hidden-layer fully connected network with 64 neurons per hidden layer and tanh 

activations. 

PPKO identification. For PPKO, the polynomial operator maps Ap(θ, w ) and Bp(θ, w ) were repre-sented with a joint total-degree-two Legendre–Hermite polynomial-chaos basis, using Legendre polynomials for the parametric uncertainty and Hermite polynomials for the disturbances. PPKO was trained by alternating minimization: the neural observable parameters were updated by Adam on minibatches, and the Koopman coefficient blocks {Aα, B α} were then updated once per epoch via closed-form ridge least squares on the full training set. Here, a batch size of 2048 refers to 2048 one-step transition samples (xk, u k, θ, w k, x k+1 ) per Adam update, rather than 2048 trajectory segments. Train-ing used early stopping based on validation prediction error, and the final selected ridge parameter was 10 −6.14 Baseline models. For the deterministic EDMD-DL baseline, a separately learned dictionary of the same architecture was used, and Koopman operator matri-ces {A, B } were fit by ridge regression. For the in-trusive SGP-SMPC baseline, we used a total-degree-1 Legendre–Hermite expansion and solved the corre-sponding deterministic expanded PCE system. The intrusive SGP-SMPC and scenario SMPC baselines are equation-based and therefore do not require neural-network training. Scenario SMPC used 64 fixed param-eter/disturbance paths during closed-loop regulation; its NLP and the SGP NLP were solved with IPOPT, while EDMD-DL used CLARABEL. 

6.1.2 Open-loop Performance 

To assess the accuracy of the proposed PPKO model in open-loop prediction, the uncontrolled Duffing oscilla-tor was propagated over a horizon of H = 10 steps with 

∆t = 0 .02 from a representative initial condition. The PPKO mean and second moment were compared to Monte Carlo (MC) ground truth ( Nmc = 5 ,000 samples) and the baseline models. Fig. 1 shows that the PPKO predictions closely match the MC reference in both the mean and second-moment trajectories, which is crucial for our SMPC formulation since the constraints use second-moment predictions. Moreover, Fig. 2 shows that the PPKO state distributions for H = 10 also closely match the MC histograms, confirming that the learned Koopman model accurately captures the stochastic open-loop dynamics. In this case study, the deterministic EDMD-DL base-line fails to capture both the first and second moments accurately, demonstrating the importance of incorpo-rating uncertainty for accurate open-loop prediction. The intrusive SGP baseline also accurately captures the state second moments and empirical distributions. However, the expanded system of PCE coefficients produces a large nonlinear system: the degree-one ex-pansion contains 24 coefficients per state, resulting in a 48-dimensional system for the open-loop horizon considered here. This expansion makes the resulting closed-loop SMPC problem computationally expensive, as discussed in the next section.

6.3 Discussion 

The two distinct numerical case studies demonstrate that our proposed PPKO-SMPC framework provides a favorable tradeoff between stochastic modeling accu-racy and online computational efficiency. In both case studies, PPKO captures the first- and second-order statistics of the nonlinear stochastic dynamics with substantially better accuracy than the deterministic EDMD-DL model and with comparable accuracy to the intrusive SGP and Monte Carlo baselines. The closed-loop regulation simulations show that PPKO-SMPC achieves performance comparable to the stochastic baselines, with online computation substantially below the equation-based stochastic baselines, although above deterministic EDMD-DL for the electrothermal QCQP under the common solver. This computational advan-tage becomes particularly pronounced for the high-dimensional electrothermal PDE–ODE example, which illustrates the benefit of the condensed SMPC formu-lation as the number of state dimensions, lifted dimen-sions, and PCE basis functions increases. Furthermore, deterministic EDMD-DL may violate stochastic con-straints during closed-loop operation, which motivates the use of SMPC formulations in stochastic settings. Overall, these results indicate that the proposed frame-work can incorporate PCE-based uncertainty propa-gation and Koopman operators into nonlinear SMPC without inheriting the curse of dimensionality typically associated with conventional stochastic formulations. 

7 Conclusion 

In summary, we developed the PPKO framework, in-tegrating Koopman operator theory and PCEs for SMPC of nonlinear systems with parametric uncer-tainty and stochastic disturbances. We showed that PCE-parameterized Koopman matrices introduce no irreducible approximation gap, with the additional er-ror vanishing as the PCE degree increases. We also developed a recursive offline condensation scheme that computes the required moment matrices and yields a deterministic SMPC problem whose online dimension depends only on the horizon and control dimension. The framework is validated on numerical examples spanning different dynamics and problem sizes. Several important research directions remain beyond the scope of this article. These include analyses of recur-sive feasibility [16], closed-loop stability [8], adapting the parametric uncertainty distribution online [42], and handling temporally correlated disturbances, which are all directions that require further investigation. We de-liberately focused here on developing the PPKO frame-work, including its approximation capabilities and con-densed deterministic SMPC formulations, leaving these next theoretical developments as future work.
