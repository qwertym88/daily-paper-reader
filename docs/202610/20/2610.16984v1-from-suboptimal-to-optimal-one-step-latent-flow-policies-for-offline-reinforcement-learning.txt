## ABSTRACT 

Offline reinforcement learning aims to learn effective policies from fixed datasets without environ-ment interaction. Although generative models such as diffusion and flow matching enhance policy expressiveness, they typically start from uninformative Gaussian noise and generate actions through multi-step iteration, leading to high computational costs and failing to effectively leverage suboptimal trajectories in datasets. We propose MeanFlow2Better (MF2B), which reformulates offline RL as generative decision chunk optimization from current performance status to better performance status in a high-level latent decision space. MF2B maps trajectory chunks to high-level latent decision representations through an encoder, placing generation starting points within a structured decision space sculpted by behavioral data. The model learns a shared conditional mean velocity field in this space, constructing two flow mappings: from Gaussian-Induced Decision Sources toward positive behavioral decision regions, and from suboptimal decisions toward high-value decision regions. Both flow mappings complete decision refinement through one-step ODE integration over the unit time interval ∆t = 1 , requiring neither multi-step iterative sampling nor backpropagation through time. Furthermore, we propose hierarchical chunk-level advantage to guide the flow direction toward high-return regions, where the high-level decisions cover multiple low-level actions, leading to sequence length compression from T to K ≈ T /H and significantly lower computational overhead. On the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark, MF2B achieves an average score of 90.91, matching or surpassing strong baselines. 

## 1 Introduction 

Reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved remarkable progress across diverse domains, including robotics[Zhu et al., 2023, Hwangbo et al., 2019], games[Schrittwieser et al., 2020, Perolat et al., 2022], and fundamental science[Jumper et al., 2021, Ren et al., 2018]. In many real-world settings such as robotics[Zhu et al., 2023, Ma et al., 2026], healthcare[Yu et al., 2021a], and autonomous driving[Li et al., 2023], however, online interaction is expensive, unsafe, or infeasible. Offline RL addresses this by learning solely from pre-collected datasets[Huang et al., 2024], but faces two persistent challenges: limited policy expressiveness as behavioral distributions grow complex and multimodal[Kang et al., 2023], and the underutilization of suboptimal trajectories that constitute the majority of practical datasets. Generative policies based on diffusion[Wang et al., 2022] and flow matching[Park et al., 2025] improve expressiveness, yet share two structural limitations. First, they generate behaviors through multi-step denoising or ODE integration, incurring high inference costs. Second, they model policies directly in raw action space, treating trajectories of different qualities as homogeneous samples[Fujimoto and Gu, 2021, Kostrikov et al., 2021] and offering no mechanism to lift suboptimal behaviors toward high-value regions. We argue that suboptimal trajectories are not pure noise but starting points for improvement. Drawing on the neural null-space mechanism in motor cortex [Stavisky et al., 2017], we design Gaussian-Induced Decision Sources as a semantic and value null-space origin from which structured flow learning sculpts task-relevant decision representations.

## 2 Related Work 

Offline Reinforcement Learning. Reinforcement learning is inherently an online paradigm[Sutton et al., 1999b], and its online learning characteristics present limitations in scenarios where online data collection and learning cannot occur simultaneously, such as autonomous driving. Offline reinforcement learning enables agents to learn from pre-collected fixed datasets without collecting new data during the learning process[Fujimoto et al., 2019, Kumar et al., 2020, Yu et al., 2021b, Kumar et al., 2019, Hu et al., 2022, 2023]. 

Diffusion Policies in Offline RL. Offline RL aims to learn effective policies from fixed datasets without environment interaction [Levine et al., 2020], with early methods addressing distributional shift via conservative objectives such as CQL [Kumar et al., 2020], TD3+BC [Fujimoto and Gu, 2021], and IQL [Kostrikov et al., 2021]. However, these approaches often rely on unimodal Gaussian policies, which struggle to model complex, multi-modal action distributions. To address this, recent approaches have adopted diffusion models [Ho et al., 2020, Song et al., 2020] for offline RL. Diffusion Q-learning (DQL) [Wang et al., 2022] replaces Gaussian policies with diffusion models, modeling complex multimodal action distributions through multi-step denoising. IDQL [Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023] and EDP [Kang et al., 2023] improve upon DQL but still share the critical limitation of high sampling costs from multi-step denoising. 

Flow Matching and Flow Policies. Flow matching is a generative modeling approach that learns time-varying velocity fields through regressing target vector fields, which define probability paths transporting samples from simple prior distributions to target data distributions, characterized by simple training and fast inference[Lipman et al., 2023] (see Section 3 for details). Recently, flow matching has attracted attention in reinforcement learning due to its strong expressiveness and faster inference. FQL [Park et al., 2025] uses flow matching policies to approximate behavior policies and distills flow policies for efficient policy learning and inference. FlowQ [Alles et al., 2025] learns a conditional velocity field for flow policies by modeling energy-guided probability paths as Gaussian trajectories. However, these methods typically rely on simple Gaussian priors and multi-step sampling from flow policies, limiting representation richness and bringing additional computational overhead. To address computational bottlenecks from multi-step generative policies, MeanFlowQL [Wang et al., 2026] reformulates MeanFlow [Geng et al., 2025a] as a residual mapping that directly transforms noise into actions. SSCP [Koirala and Fleming, 2026] uses enhanced flow matching objective functions to predict direct completion vectors from intermediate samples, achieving one-shot action generation. Unlike the above methods, this paper introduces MF2B, achieving paradigm shifts in three dimensions. First, at the representation space level, existing flow policies operate directly in raw action space[Park et al., 2025, Alles et al., 2025], requiring learning highly nonlinear mappings from Gaussian noise to complex action distributions. MF2B compresses trajectory chunks into high-level latent decision representations through an encoder, reformulating flow matching as short-range approximately linear flow mappings within decision space, thereby establishing foundations for one-step generation. Second, at the generation mechanism level, FQL[Park et al., 2025] and FlowQ[Alles et al., 2025] 2One-Step Latent Flow Policies for Offline RL A P REPRINT 

learn marginal velocity fields requiring multi-step ODE integration, while MF2B learns mean velocity fields[Geng et al., 2025a] directly predicting residual directions from starting points to endpoints, achieving one-step high-fidelity genera-tion. Third, at the optimization objective level, existing methods indiscriminately fit dataset behavior frequencies, while MF2B introduces a dual-branch flow architecture: the Source-to-Positive Alignment Mean-Flow Branch establishes baseline mappings from Gaussian-Induced Decision Sources to dataset decisions, and the Quality-Uplift Mean-Flow Branch achieves value-guided flow mapping from suboptimal decisions to high-value decisions through hierarchical chunk-level advantage weighting, with both branches sharing the same conditional mean velocity field parameters. This design enables the model to extract flow mapping patterns leading from suboptimal trajectories to optimal policies, breaking through dataset quality ceilings. To our knowledge, this is the first offline reinforcement learning method combining mean flow matching with value-guided dual-branch flow mapping in high-level latent decision space to exploit suboptimal-to-optimal data capabilities, achieving unity of expressiveness and computational efficiency.

3.3 Flow Matching 

Flow matching is a generative modeling approach that adopts simple and intuitive training objectives by regressing target vector fields, which define desired probability paths[Lipman et al., 2023]. Flow matching learns a time-varying vector field by defining an ordinary differential equation (ODE), whose solution transports samples from a simple prior distribution to the target data distribution. Given a data distribution p(x), the goal is to learn a time-varying velocity field vθ (xt, t ) : Rd × [0 , 1] → Rd

## 4 MeanFlow2Better Method 

MF2B reformulates offline reinforcement learning as a quality improvement problem in high-level latent decision space. Trajectory chunks are compressed into high-level latent decision representations through a Chunk-Level Latent Decision Encoder qϕ, a shared Conditional Latent Decision Mean-Flow Field vθ learns conditional mean velocities across two supervisory branches, and a Hierarchical Action Decoder pψ recovers continuous action sequences from refined decisions. A Hierarchical Chunk-Level Advantage Critic Cη provides value guidance during training only. Throughout this section, we fix t0 = 0 and t1 = 1 as the start and end of the unit flow interval. ## 8 Conclusion 

This paper proposes MF2B, a one-step policy optimization method for offline reinforcement learning in high-level latent decision space. MF2B reformulates offline RL as a generative policy optimization problem from suboptimal to optimal, mapping trajectory chunks to high-level decision representations through a Chunk-Level Latent Decision Encoder, and learning a shared conditional mean velocity field in this space. The method completes decision refinement through a dual-branch flow mapping mechanism: the first branch transports Gaussian-Induced Decision Sources toward positive behavioral decision regions, establishing task-relevant decision structures. The second branch transports suboptimal decisions toward high-value decision regions, distilling superior behavioral patterns from existing experiences. Hierar-chical chunk-level advantage estimation further guides the flow mapping toward high-return regions. Unlike multi-step denoising diffusion policies and flow matching policies requiring multi-step ODE integration, MF2B directly predicts conditional mean velocity over the unit time interval ∆t = 1 and completes decision refinement through one-step Euler integration, requiring neither iterative sampling nor backpropagation through time. On 9 Gym-MuJoCo tasks from the D4RL benchmark, MF2B achieves an average score of 90.91, surpassing FlowQ (85.6) and SSCP (87.9), and significantly outperforming FQL (79.2). The high-level latent decision representation compresses sequence length from original action steps T to K ≈ T /H , significantly reducing computational overhead in long-horizon tasks. The suboptimal-to-high-value transport mechanism effectively utilizes low-quality trajectories for policy improvement, making MF2B particularly effective in long-horizon continuous control tasks. Future research can extend MF2B to goal-conditioned RL and multi-task RL scenarios, learning conditional mean velocity fields to support diverse goal-oriented behaviors and further advancing the applicability of offline RL in real-world domains. Nevertheless, the high-level latent decision representations of MF2B are constrained by the diversity of the offline dataset, and excessively narrow behavioral support may limit the quality of the learned decision space. Addressing this through meta-learning initialization, data augmentation, or VLM-grounded latent priors remains an open direction, as does extending the framework to goal-conditioned RL and multi-task RL settings to support diverse goal-oriented behaviors.
