# ABSTRACT 

Learning continuous-time dynamical systems from data is an active area of re-search. The Koopman formalism is often used here, because it is a flexible ap-proach that is informative about the system’s fundamental properties. The formal-ism represents dynamical systems through linear operators acting on a suitable function space. A practical difficulty arising in the numerical approximation of Koopman operators is the choice of a “dictionary”, meaning an effective finite-dimensional representation of the function space. In this paper, we focus on learn-ing dynamical systems in continuous-time, and so we consider numerical approx-imations of the Koopman generator instead of the Koopman operator. Our main contribution is a data-driven random-feature method to obtain a parsimonious and effective function representation for Galerkin approximation methods such as gEDMD. The representation we construct is data-informed, which allows us to improve upon data-agnostic methods. We empirically demonstrate the suitability of our method for forecasting tasks and system analysis. The numerical experi-ments show that our approach is accurate, and faster than iterative optimization. Further, we prove convergence of our approximation of the vector field and the re-sulting trajectories. In addition to the numerical results, we also make theoretical contributions, including a general convergence criterion for the semigroup gener-ated by the gEDMD approximation and a counterexample showing that gEDMD does not generally preserve exponential boundedness. With our contribution, we advance the frontier of efficiently learning continuous-time dynamics from data, towards interpretable and mathematically robust modelling based on the Koopman framework. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Identifying and inferring fundamental properties of complex nonlinear dynamical systems with an unknown analytical expression using only sparse or irregularly sampled observations is an impor-tant but difficult problem in scientific machine learning. One viable framework for such analyses is Koopman theory (Koopman, 1931), which has received much attention in recent years due to ben-efiting greatly from advancements in data-driven methods (Mezi´ c, 2005; Budiˇ si´ c et al., 2012). The Koopman framework shifts the perspective from the evolution of states in physical coordinates to the evolution of functions of the state, called observables (Budiˇ si´ c et al., 2012). This enables the study of the underlying system through linear operators acting on the space of observables rather than directly handling the nonlinear dynamics in state space. The central operators of study are the semigroup of Koopman operators and its associated generator, which capture most properties of the semigroup of flow maps and vector field. The operators are linear on the space of observables of the system, which 1arXiv:2610.39897v1 [cs.LG] 29 Oct 2026 makes them amenable to powerful tools from spectral theory and which can help to reveal global system behaviour, such as stability, attractors and constants of motion (Mezi´ c & Banaszuk, 2004). For general dynamical systems, this linearisation is only possible when the space of observables is chosen to be sufficiently large, typically infinite-dimensional, thus turning the problem of approx-imating an unknown finite-dimensional nonlinear map into approximating an infinite-dimensional but linear map. Given a dataset of trajectories, common methods for approximating these operators are the EDMD (Williams et al., 2015a) and gEDMD (Klus et al., 2020) algorithms, which learn the evolution of the observables projected onto a finite-dimensional subspace. This subspace is spanned by a chosen set of functions, often called a “dictionary”. Rather than targeting a single member of the operator semigroup, it is useful to learn its generator, which represents the instantaneous change rather than a fixed time increment and makes the whole semigroup accessible via its spectrum. The performance of EDMD and gEDMD depends heavily on selecting a dictionary capable of capturing the dynamics of the underlying system, a task that is often difficult without sufficient prior knowl-edge of the system. A solution to this problem is to construct the dictionary from the available data using data-driven methods, a task which is commonly referred to as dictionary learning. For in-stance, Li et al. (2017) proposed training a neural network as a dictionary for EDMD by minimising a suitable loss function. This has later been extended to an analogous algorithm for learning the Koopman generator using gEDMD (Buzhardt et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). As alternatives to neural networks, researchers have also investigated using random features as dictionary functions (Tabish et al., 2025; Sprink et al., 2026), which are computationally efficient by avoiding the iterative op-timisation of training a neural network. In this work, we employ data-driven random-features to learn parsimonious and effective dictionaries for gEDMD . The random feature distribution is constructed using the SWIM algorithm (Bolager et al., 2023), an approach in which training data prescribes the choice of random features. This approach allows us to combine the performance of a learned dictionary with the efficiency of random-feature methods to approximate the Koopman generator from continuous-time data. 

Figure 1: A schematic representation of our method. We construct all terms in orange from the training data. Chaotic dynamical systems present a particular challenge to data-driven methods, because prediction is only possible for a limited time range. In the paper, we discuss this challenge with respect to dictionary learning for gEDMD, and contrast it to adjoint-based methods such as Neural ODEs. Our key contributions in this paper are the following. • We propose a method for learning linear representations of continuous-time dynamical systems (see Figure 1, Section 2). • We provide theoretical guarantees for the convergence of our method given sufficient data and dictionary functions (Theorem 1, Theorem 2). • We extend the convergence theory of gEDMD (Theorem 2, Theorem 3). 2• We investigate challenges of learning chaotic continuous-time systems from data and con-trast it to Neural ODEs (Theorem 4). • We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in forecasting and characterizing the long-term behavior of dynamical systems (Section 4). • We provide an implementation of this work publicly accessible via https://gitlab. com/fd-research/dragons .We include a comprehensive review of related works in Section A.

For the results presented in Section 3.1, we assume the following. • The measure μ has an everywhere positive and bounded density ρ w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure λ on X , where 0 < α ≤ ρ(x) ≤ β < ∞, ∀x ∈ X .• The data is sampled i.i.d. from μ.• The Koopman operators Kt form a C0-semigroup. In this section, we briefly highlight what we can and cannot theoretically guarantee about how the spectrum of our approximant relates to that of the true generator. In general, spectral convergence is a challenging problem within the Koopman community (Colbrook & Townsend, 2024), that has as of yet not been fully resolved. Llamazares-Elias et al. (2026, Theorem 6.3) show the almost sure convergence of eigenpairs of ˆLN M ′ 

> N

to some (nontrivial) eigenpairs of L when taking an appropriate subsequence of (N, M N ) pairs, where the convergence of the eigenfunctions is weak. By Lemma 3, this result also applies to our method. However, it is worth highlighting the limitations of this result. Firstly, the result holding only for a subsequence means that increasing the dictionary size and training data can potentially increase the error on the spectral approximation, even when reducing the pointwise error for the generator over F. Secondly, the result explicitly guarantees convergence 6only to a subset of the true spectrum. This is an inherent limitation of gEDMD, as it cannot resolve continuous spectra, which commonly arise in sufficiently complex dynamical systems (Mezi´ c, 2022; Giannakis & Valva, 2024). To account for this limitation, in addition to the computed spectra, we also approximate the associated residual according to Colbrook & Townsend (2024) and report it in all of our experiments. Chaotic systems are particularly susceptible to this limitation. 4.1 FORECASTING 

To assess the predictive performance of DRAGONS, we choose three test problems: a Van der Pol oscillator, the chaotic Lorenz 63 system, and a mean-field model of cylinder flow. Details on the datasets, experimental setup and hyperparameter search are given in Section F. We compare our approach to state-of-the-art dictionary learning methods using an Adam optimizer (gEDMD-DL), 7and against data-agnostic random features (Gaussian) and common dictionary choices such as radial basis functions (RBF-Dict) and polynomials (Poly-Dict). For the chosen systems, a polynomial dictionary is sufficiently expressive and invariant, thus the Poly-Dict sets the standard for a good dictionary for all of these problems. The results are given in Table 2, where we report the test error, dictionary size and training time. DRAGONS shows excellent performance, either on par with or better than existing methods in terms of error. The fit time is reasonably low when considering the dictionary size, indicating that the computation is dominated by the linear least squares solver. For chaotic systems in particular we achieve a lower error with DRAGONS compared to the Gaussian and the Adam-trained neural network dictionary. This is an noteworthy observation because chaotic systems have continuous spectra, which pose a challenge for gEDMD (see Section 3.2). DRAGONS is thus a robust choice for forecasting tasks on irregular time intervals. Further details and insights from these experiments can also be found in Section F. Koopman Mode Analysis: Flow around a cylinder In this experiment, we demonstrate that spectral properties of a system’s Koopman generator can be extracted accurately with DRAGONS. Specifically, it allows us to interpret the underlying system dynamics on a global scale for the widely used model of the flow around a cylinder. Figure 4a shows the eigenvalues of the generator and their corresponding spectral residuals. Nine eigenvalues with large negative real parts are omitted for vi-sualization. The real part of an eigenvalue determines the growth or decay rate of the corresponding mode, while the imaginary part determines its oscillation frequency. The two modes of a complex-conjugate pair typically represent one oscillatory pattern. Representative spatial Koopman modes corresponding to three selected eigenvalues are shown in Figure 4b and Figure 4c. 8(a) Eigenvalues of ˆL. (b) Non-oscillatory modes. (c) Oscillatory mode. 

Figure 4: Generator spectrum and representative spatial modes for the flow around a cylinder. Several modes with positive real parts are present in the learned spectrum, which may reflect the transient dynamics away from the unstable steady solution. Although such modes are associated with growth, we do not observe unstable predictions near the limit cycle, probably because their net contribution to the reconstructed dynamics remains small in this region. In addition, the relatively large residuals of some growing modes may indicate that they are only locally accurate near the unstable state rather than globally valid along the entire trajectory. 

Molecular Conformation Dynamics: Alanine Dipeptide In this final experiment, we demon-strate that our approach is applicable to high-dimensional data sets. Specifically, we show that the generator approximation can be used to extract distinct molecular conformations and metastable states of alanine dipeptide purely from long and high-dimensional trajectory data. For molecular dynamics, an evolution in finite time does not suffice for an understanding of the sys-tem’s long term behaviour; typically high-dimensional systems exhibit a few metastable states deter-mined by a potential energy landscape. The global conformation pattern of alanine dipeptide can be represented using two dihedral angles at the central backbone denoted as φ and ψ (Ramachandran et al., 1963). We use trajectory data from N¨ uske et al. (2017) and perform a molecular conformation analysis similar to Klus et al. (2018) and Herwig et al. (2025). We have shown that data-driven random features provide a robust dictionary for the approximation of the Koopman generator. Our method is supported by empirical results on forecasting problems, demonstrating high accuracy without the high computational costs typically associated with iterative dictionary learning. We also demonstrate how our method can be used to gain insight into the system’s spectral properties. Our theoretical results affirm the validity of our method and extend the existing convergence theory for gEDMD. We also extend known limitations of iteratively trained neural networks from discrete-time to a continuous-time setting. 

Several limitations remain for future work. Firstly, our convergence results are existence state-ments without an expression relating the error to the required number of data points or dictionary functions. This could be alleviated by combining results from Llamazares-Elias et al. (2026) and Rudi & Rosasco (2017). Secondly, our theoretical guarantees for spectral convergence are bound to the limitations of gEDMD, which is why future work should look into combining our dictionary with other generator estimation algorithms with improved guarantees. Finally, since our algorithm involves solving a potentially large linear system, in the case of sizeable datasets the user needs to carefully choose an efficient linear solver or subsampling the dataset. Related works in this direction have primarily focused on learning effective dictionaries for Koop-man operator approximation. Ideally, the observables span a compact approximately invariant sub-space, but constructing such representations often requires strong prior knowledge or restrictive assumptions (Folkestad et al., 2020). Data-driven approaches therefore learn the mapping from the state space to observables directly from data (Dietrich et al., 2020). Neural-network dictionaries are a common choice (Li et al., 2017), including methods that learn Koopman eigenfunctions (Lusch et al., 2018), jointly optimize the dictionary and operator (Yeung et al., 2019), or construct more structured representations using time delays, invertible mappings, or residual information (Val´ abek et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2024; Coote & Colbrook, 2026). Although expressive, these methods gener-ally require iterative gradient-based optimization and can therefore be substantially more expensive than the final linear Koopman fit. A computationally attractive alternative is to construct nonlinear features without iteratively training the hidden representation. Random-feature methods fix or directly sample the nonlinear mapping and reduce learning to a linear estimation problem (Rahimi & Recht, 2007; Rudi & Rosasco, 2017). In Koopman approximation, this idea has been explored using random Fourier features (Bak et al., 2022; N¨ uske & Klus, 2023; Sprink et al., 2026), randomized approximations of Koopman eigen-functions (Liao, 2026), and reservoir computing (Gulina & Mauroy, 2021). We discuss these directions in detail below. 

Explicitly finding a governing equation. Learning governing dynamics directly from data has long been an important goal in data-driven modeling, particularly for systems whose analytical equations are unknown or only partially available. One approach is symbolic regression, which searches over candidate analytical expressions to identify compact representations of the underlying dynamics (Bongard & Lipson, 2007; Schmidt & Lipson, 2009). Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) instead assumes that the governing equations admit a sparse representation in a predefined library of candidate functions and identifies the active terms through sparse regres-sion (Brunton et al., 2016). This sparse-regression framework has also been extended to the discov-ery of partial differential equations through PDE-FIND (Rudy et al., 2017). 

Koopman operator approximation. The Koopman operator provides a linear, although generally infinite-dimensional, representation of nonlinear dynamical systems by describing the evolution of observable functions rather than the state itself. Data-driven methods approximate this operator on a finite-dimensional subspace spanned by a dictionary of observables. In particular, Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) constructs a finite-dimensional approximation from snap-shot pairs once such a dictionary has been specified (Williams et al., 2015a). The quality of the resulting approximation, however, depends strongly on this choice. Simple predefined dictionar-ies, such as monomials, radial basis functions, or other problem-specific features, can be effective for low-dimensional systems but may grow prohibitively large as the state dimension or required approximation order increases. 

The dictionary-selection problem. Selecting an effective dictionary is therefore a central challenge in Koopman operator approximation. Ideally, the observables would span a compact Koopman-invariant, or approximately invariant, subspace and thus provide coordinates in which the nonlinear dynamics evolve linearly. Koopman eigenfunctions constitute a natural choice for such coordinates, but their construction generally requires significant prior knowledge or restrictive assumptions on the underlying dynamical system (Folkestad et al., 2020). Conversely, choosing an excessively rich generic dictionary increases both the computational cost and the amount of data required to identify the projected operator. Data-informed dictionaries seek to mitigate this trade-off by constructing representations adapted to the observed dynamics and thereby reducing the number of features re-quired for an accurate approximation (Dietrich et al., 2020). 

Kernel-based observable spaces. Kernel methods provide one route toward richer observable spaces without explicitly enumerating a large dictionary. Kernel DMD represents observables im-plicitly through the feature map associated with a prescribed kernel, making it possible to operate in very high- or infinite-dimensional feature spaces while carrying out the main computations in terms of kernel evaluations (Williams et al., 2015b). This alleviates the explicit feature-growth problem of conventional EDMD, particularly for high-dimensional systems. Nevertheless, the kernel type and 17 its hyperparameters remain design choices, and the computational cost typically scales unfavorably with the number of snapshots. More recently, kernel selection itself has therefore also been formu-lated as a dictionary-learning problem, in which combinations of kernels and their parameters are optimized from data (Bolager et al., 2026b). Thus, although kernel methods reduce the need to man-ually specify individual observables, the broader problem of identifying a representation appropriate for the dynamics remains. 

Neural dictionary learning. A widely studied alternative is to parametrize the dictionary directly using neural networks. Early EDMD dictionary-learning approaches jointly adapt a neural represen-tation and the associated Koopman approximation to the observed dynamics (Li et al., 2017). Deep-Koopman similarly learns approximate Koopman eigenfunctions from data, demonstrating that non-linear coordinate transformations can be constructed without prescribing the observable functions beforehand (Lusch et al., 2018). Related architectures combine an encoder, approximately linear latent dynamics, and a decoder to learn low-dimensional representations in which the evolution is close to linear (Otto & Rowley, 2019). Other formulations directly optimize both the neural-network dictionary and the projected Koopman operator (Yeung et al., 2019), use time-delay embeddings to incorporate temporal information (Val´ abek et al., 2025), or employ invertible neural networks to obtain an explicit inverse transformation between the state and observable spaces (Jin et al., 2024). These approaches substantially increase the flexibility of the dictionary and can yield compact rep-resentations for systems for which suitable analytical observables are not known. 

Neural ODE-based dictionaries and joint Koopman optimization. Several recent works extend this basic neural dictionary-learning paradigm. Neural ODEs have been used to construct parameter-efficient EDMD dictionaries by interpreting the feature transformation as the flow of an auxiliary continuous-depth network (Terao et al., 2021). Connections between Koopman dictionary learning and neural state-space models have subsequently been studied in more detail. In particular, repeat-edly projecting the linearly evolved lifted state back to the physical state space, as commonly done in practical EDMD implementations (Van Goor et al., 2023; Junker et al., 2022), reintroduces a non-linear state-space evolution and creates a close relationship between projected EDMD models and neural ODE or neural flow-map representations (Buzhardt et al., 2025). Automatic differentiation through the EDMD solution has likewise been used to optimize the dictionary and Koopman ap-proximation simultaneously rather than alternating between the two problems (Constante-Amores et al., 2024). 

Structure- and residual-aware dictionaries. The representation can also be adapted to properties beyond short-term prediction accuracy. Trainable dictionaries have, for example, been combined with Residual DMD by incorporating spectral residuals of approximate Koopman eigenvalues and eigenfunctions into the learning objective (Coote & Colbrook, 2026). This explicitly targets the spectral consistency of the learned representation rather than relying solely on reconstruction or trajectory errors. Related work constructs approximately invariant observable subspaces through optimization on the Grassmann manifold, allowing part of the observable space to be constrained by prior knowledge while the remaining subspace is adapted to the data (Schurig et al., 2025). For parametrized dynamical systems, both the dictionary and a parameter-dependent family of projected Koopman operators can be represented by neural networks and learned jointly, permitting the opera-tor itself to vary with static or time-dependent parameters (Guo et al., 2025). Bai & Ding (2025) use the Koopman framework to evolve the parameters of a Neural ODE, although they do not perform dictionary learning, making them an exception to this type of learning. 

Neural Network approximation of generators for continuous spectra. Neural approaches have also been developed directly at the generator level. KoopGen, for instance, represents structured state-dependent Koopman generators and is designed to accommodate systems with broadband or continuous spectral components without assuming that the relevant dynamics admit a simple global finite-dimensional eigendecomposition (Su et al., 2026), however since it does not learn dictionaries, the approach is not directly comparable to our method. 

Computational cost of trainable dictionaries. These developments highlight the expressive power of neural dictionary learning, but this flexibility comes at a computational cost. The parameters defining the observables must typically be determined through iterative gradient-based optimiza-tion, often jointly with operator, decoder, or reconstruction parameters. Consequently, dictionary construction can require many passes over the training data and can become substantially more expensive than the final linear least-squares problem underlying classical EDMD. The result may 18 additionally depend on network architecture, initialization, learning rates, regularization, and other optimization hyperparameters. This distinction is important: once a fixed dictionary has been constructed, the corresponding Koopman approximation is essentially a linear regression problem, whereas learning the dictionary can turn the overall procedure into a large non-convex optimization problem. 

Convergence and spectral reliability. Care is also required when discussing convergence of such approximations. For a fixed sequence of sufficiently rich approximation spaces and appropriate sam-pling assumptions, convergence results are available for EDMD; in particular, finite-dimensional projected operators converge to the Koopman operator in suitable operator senses as the number of samples and dictionary functions increases (Korda & Mezi´ c, 2018). This does not, however, imply that every eigenvalue of a finite EDMD matrix provides a reliable approximation of the spectrum of the infinite-dimensional Koopman operator. Finite-dimensional truncations can exhibit spectral pollution, particularly when the underlying operator has continuous or otherwise complicated spec-tral components. Residual DMD addresses this distinction by computing operator-level residuals that permit a posteriori verification of candidate spectral quantities and, under suitable assumptions, provide rigorous convergence guarantees for spectra, pseudospectra, and spectral measures (Col-brook & Townsend, 2024). Recent residual-guided dictionary-learning methods build directly on this observation by incorporating such residuals into the representation-learning objective (Coote & Colbrook, 2026). 

Random-feature alternatives. A conceptually different approach is to retain expressive nonlinear representations while avoiding iterative optimization of the hidden mapping altogether. Random-feature methods follow precisely this strategy: rather than learning all parameters of a nonlinear feature map through gradient descent, the hidden parameters are sampled or otherwise constructed once, after which only a linear output model is fitted (Rahimi & Recht, 2007; 2008b). Such mod-els possess strong approximation properties for broad classes of functions, including Barron-type functions (Barron, 1993) and functions in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (Rahimi & Recht, 2008a). Conventional random-feature methods typically draw their parameters from a fixed, data-independent distribution; random Fourier features, for example, sample frequencies according to the spectral distribution associated with a shift-invariant kernel (Rahimi & Recht, 2007). Despite their simple training procedure, random features provide favorable trade-offs between approxima-tion accuracy, feature dimension, and computational complexity (Rudi & Rosasco, 2017). From the perspective of Koopman approximation, this separation is particularly natural: the nonlinear fea-tures define a candidate observable space, while the action of the Koopman operator on this space can subsequently be estimated through linear regression. 

Data-dependent sampling with SWIM. Rather than sampling features from a prescribed, data-independent distribution, Bolager et al. (2023) introduced Sampling Where It Matters (SWIM), a data-driven procedure for constructing the weights and biases of shallow and deep neural networks without gradient-based optimization. SWIM samples pairs of training points and constructs individ-ual neurons from their relative positions, assigning higher sampling probability to pairs exhibiting large changes in the target function relative to their distance in feature space. In this way, features are preferentially placed in regions where the target function varies strongly. After the hidden layers have been constructed, only the final linear layer is fitted. The resulting sampled networks retain uni-versal approximation properties and, in the experiments of Bolager et al. (2023), achieve accuracy comparable to conventionally trained networks while requiring substantially less training time. A closely related combination of SWIM and Koopman operator approximation was recently pro-posed by Bolager et al. (2026a) for constructing Koopman-informed recurrent neural networks .There, SWIM is used to generate a data-adapted nonlinear lifting without backpropagation, while EDMD determines a finite-dimensional Koopman operator governing the evolution in the lifted space. A recurrent update is then obtained by repeatedly lifting the current state, evolving it linearly through the learned Koopman operator, and projecting it back to the state space. Consequently, both the nonlinear feature representation and the recurrent dynamics can be constructed without gradient-based training. This work demonstrates that SWIM-generated observables can be successfully cou-pled with Koopman approximations for dynamical-system learning and provides a particularly close precursor to the dictionary-construction strategy considered here. 

Random Fourier features for Koopman learning. Randomized feature construction has conse-quently appeared in Koopman methods in several forms. Random Fourier features approximate 19 translation-invariant kernels with an explicit finite-dimensional feature map, avoiding the construc-tion and factorization of the full kernel matrix. Importantly, their use in EDMD predates recent neural dictionary-learning approaches: random Fourier features and Nystr¨ om approximations were proposed as scalable approximations of kernel EDMD specifically to reduce the computational cost associated with large datasets (DeGennaro & Urban, 2019). Random Fourier dictionaries have since been applied to Koopman approximation in different settings (Bak et al., 2022; N¨ uske & Klus, 2023; Tabish et al., 2025; Sprink et al., 2026), and randomized shallow feature models have also been used directly to approximate Koopman eigenfunctions (Liao, 2026). These approaches replace the nonlinear optimization of a neural dictionary by the generation of a fixed collection of nonlinear observables followed by the usual linear operator fit. 

Reservoir-based dictionaries. Reservoir computing follows a closely related philosophy. A ran-domly initialized recurrent network maps the input into a high-dimensional nonlinear dynamical representation, while its internal parameters remain fixed and only a linear readout is trained (Bollt, 2021; Gauthier et al., 2021). The resulting optimization is therefore typically reduced to a least-squares problem. In a Koopman context, reservoir states can themselves serve as observables, al-lowing the reservoir to provide a nonlinear dictionary without backpropagating through its internal weights. Reservoir-based methods have explicitly exploited this idea for Koopman operator approx-imation and dictionary construction using linear convex optimization (Gulina & Mauroy, 2021). The recurrent structure additionally provides an implicit representation of temporal history, distinguish-ing reservoir features from purely state-dependent random mappings. 20 B KOOPMAN THEORY 

B.1 KOOPMAN OPERATOR 

The Koopman operator provides a linear representation of potentially nonlinear dynamical systems by describing the evolution of observable functions rather than the state itself (Budiˇ si´ c et al., 2012). Consider a dynamical system on a state space X ⊆ Rd equipped with a measure μ, where zt ∈ X 

denotes the state at time t. Its evolution is governed by 

ddt zt = F (zt), (7) where F : X 7 → Rd is the vector field describing the instantaneous velocity at each state. Assuming that each initial state in X admits a unique solution that remains in X for all t ≥ 0, the flow map 

φt : X 7 → X maps an initial state to its state at time t:

zt = φt(z0). (8) The system can therefore be described by the triplet (X , T , φ ·), where T ⊆ R is the time domain. For forward-time evolution, the flow maps {φt}t≥0 form a semigroup, satisfying 

φ0 = Id X , φt1 ◦ φt2 = φt1+t2 . (9) Let F, typically a Hilbert space, be a space of scalar-valued functions g : X 7 → C called observables ,chosen such that g ◦ φt ∈ F for every g ∈ F and t ≥ 0. The Koopman operator Kt : F 7 → F is defined by 

Ktg = g ◦ φt, (Ktg)( z0) = g(φt(z0)) = g(zt). (10) Thus, evolving the state for a time t and then evaluating g gives the same result as applying Kt to g

and evaluating the resulting observable at the initial state. Although the underlying flow map may be nonlinear, the Koopman operator is linear in the observable: 

Kt(αg 1 + βg 2) = αKtg1 + βKtg2, g1, g 2 ∈ F , α, β ∈ C. (11) The flow’s semigroup property is inherited by {K t}t≥0, called the Koopman semigroup .This framework trades nonlinear evolution in a finite-dimensional state space for linear evolution in a typically infinite-dimensional function space, enabling the use of linear spectral methods to analyze nonlinear dynamics. A nonzero observable χ ∈ F is an eigenfunction of Kt with eigenvalue λ if 

(Ktχ)( z0) = χ(φt(z0)) = λχ (z0), ∀z0 ∈ X . (12) When a vector-valued observable admits an eigenfunction expansion, it can be expressed as a lin-ear combination of eigenfunctions with vector coefficients called Koopman modes. These modes specify fixed directions in the observable’s output space, while the eigenfunctions determine their state-dependent amplitudes. For numerical approximation, a finite dictionary of observables is selected to span a finite-dimensional subspace of F. Such a subspace is generally not invariant under the Koopman op-erator: evolving an observable in the subspace may produce a function outside it. Representing the evolution within the chosen subspace therefore introduces an approximation. B.2 GEDMD ALGORITHM

The simulation data for the flow past a cylinder at Reynolds number Re = 160 are taken from the Computer Graphics Laboratory at ETH Zurich (Popinet, 2004; G¨ unther et al., 2017). The corre-sponding unstable steady-state solution is computed by enforcing symmetric boundary conditions at

The oscillatory modes ϕ1 and ϕ2 are obtained as the first two POD modes of the velocity fluctuations around the mean limit-cycle flow (Sirovich, 1987). The shift mode ϕshift represents the change from the unstable steady flow to the mean limit-cycle flow.
