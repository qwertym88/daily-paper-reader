Abstract 

Many applications of multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) require policies that balance conflicting objectives while satisfying safety constraints. We study safe MORL with zero constraint violation throughout learning, covering both cumulative and stepwise constraints. A key challenge is to explore sufficiently under unknown dynamics and con-straint costs without violating the constraints. For a given preference over objectives, we develop an algorithm to learn a safe Pareto optimal policy by combining optimistic reward estimates with pessimistic cost bounds. With high probability, every policy executed over 

T episodes incurs zero constraint violation. The algorithm returns a safe Markov policy induced by the averaged occupancy measure and achieves an eO(1 /√T ) suboptimality gap under smooth Tchebycheff scalarization. We further develop a preference-free algorithm that first explores safely without a prespecified preference and then computes a safe policy for any given preference without further interaction. Over T exploration episodes and K

planning iterations, it retains the same safety guarantee and achieves an eO(1 /√T + 1 /√K)suboptimality gap. Both algorithms require knowledge of a policy that strictly satisfies the constraints and its costs. We then relax these requirements, incurring at most con-stant cumulative violation. To our knowledge, we provide the first sample-efficient MORL algorithms that guarantee safety for every executed policy. 

Keywords: Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning, Safe Reinforcement Learning, Con-strained Markov Decision Processes, Smooth Tchebycheff Scalarization

1 Introduction 

Reinforcement learning (RL) studies sequential decision-making through interaction with an environment to maximize cumulative rewards (Sutton and Barto, 1998). In many real-world applications, however, the learner must simultaneously optimize multiple, potentially conflicting objectives such as efficiency, energy consumption, and fairness. This motivates 

multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) (Roijers et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2014), where the learner seeks policies that perform well under different trade-offs among objectives. Since a single policy that maximizes all objectives rarely exists, MORL commonly targets Pareto optimal policies, i.e., policies for which no objective can be improved without degrading another (Van Moffaert and Now´ e, 2014; Qiu et al., 2024). For a given preference over the objectives, the goal is to learn a Pareto optimal policy that reflects the desired trade-off. In 

preference-based learning, the preference is fixed before exploration. Learning under a dif-ferent preference may require additional environment interaction. Preference-free learning removes this need by exploring the environment once without a prespecified preference and then computing a policy for any given preference from the collected data (Wu et al., 2021). In safety-critical applications of MORL, the learner must balance multiple objectives while satisfying explicit safety requirements, such as avoiding collisions in autonomous driv-ing, respecting resource and actuation budgets in robotics, or keeping operating tempera-tures within prescribed limits in industrial control (He and Lv, 2023; Tang et al., 2023). Existing safe MORL methods can be broadly classified into two categories. The first treats safety as an additional objective, or a set of objectives, augmenting the reward vector with costs and learning trade-offs via scalarization or preference learning (Huang et al., 2022). This strategy does not in general guarantee safety, since whether the learned policy satisfies the safety requirements depends on the chosen preference or scalarization (Achiam et al., 2017). The second category imposes safety as explicit constraints while optimizing multi-objective performance, leading to constrained MORL frameworks (Kim et al., 2025; Park et al., 2026). Under cumulative safety, these constraints limit the expected total cost over an episode, whereas under stepwise safety, they limit the cost at each step. A safe Pareto optimal policy is Pareto optimal within the class of policies satisfying these constraints. The objective value vectors of such policies form the safe Pareto front . However, existing con-strained MORL methods may still violate the constraints during learning (Gu et al., 2025). In constrained RL, prior work has established sublinear or constant bounds on cumulative constraint violation (Qiu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Stradi et al., 2025). Some methods further assume knowledge of a strictly safe policy, which satisfies every constraint with strict inequality. Under this assumption, they ensure that every executed policy satisfies the con-straints with high probability, yielding zero constraint violation throughout learning (Liu et al., 2021; Bura et al., 2022; Stradi et al., 2025). However, the literature on constrained MORL still lacks a unified, sample-efficient framework that guarantees zero constraint vi-olation throughout learning under either cumulative or stepwise safety constraints. This leads to the following question: 

Can we design provably sample-efficient algorithms for safe MORL that traverse the safe Pareto front while guaranteeing zero constraint violation? 

This problem involves three main challenges. First, safety requirements conflict with the need for exploration when costs and dynamics are unknown. The learner may not know

whether an exploratory policy is safe before execution, yet every policy executed during learning must satisfy the safety constraints. If the learner executes only policies already known to be safe, the resulting data may be insufficient to identify safe Pareto optimal policies. Second, the set of safe Pareto optimal policies cannot generally be obtained by removing unsafe policies from the unconstrained Pareto optimal set. A policy can be Pareto optimal among safe policies even when it is dominated by an unsafe policy. To obtain a safe Pareto optimal policy for a given preference, we adopt the smooth Tchebycheff scalariza-tion (Lin et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2024), which measures each objective relative to its optimal value over the safe policy class. These optimal values are unknown, and estimating them requires solving an auxiliary constrained RL problem for each objective while maintaining safety throughout learning. Finally, it is even more challenging to explore the environment only once and then compute a safe Pareto optimal policy for any subsequently specified preference. Since no preference is available during exploration, the collected data must suffice for every preference. At the same time, these data should be collected only by safe policies, which restrict the state-action pairs that the learner can visit. To address the above challenges, we propose CSTRL (Constrained Smooth Tchebycheff Reinforcement Learning) for preference-based learning. Its mirror-ascent updates use opti-mistic reward estimates to encourage exploration, while pessimistic cost bounds are used to maintain safety. We further develop PF-CSTRL (Preference-Free CSTRL ), which uses reward and transition estimation uncertainty to guide safe exploration and collect data for planning under any subsequently specified preference. Both algorithms require prior knowledge of a strictly safe policy and its constraint costs. We also develop extensions that relax these requirements. 

Contributions. Our main contributions are as follows. (i) We propose CSTRL and 

PF-CSTRL for preference-based and preference-free safe MORL, respectively. To our knowl-edge, these are the first sample-efficient MORL algorithms that guarantee zero constraint violation for every policy executed during learning. Both algorithms combine optimistic mirror-ascent updates of occupancy measures with pessimistic cost bounds under either cumulative or stepwise safety constraints. (ii) We establish that, over T learning episodes, 

CSTRL guarantees zero constraint violation with high probability under either safety for-mulation for every executed policy. It returns a safe Markov policy induced by the primal average of the occupancy measures of these policies and achieves a suboptimality gap of 

eO(1 /√T ) under smooth Tchebycheff scalarization. Using T exploration episodes followed by K planning iterations, PF-CSTRL maintains the same safety guarantee throughout both stages and achieves a suboptimality gap of eO(1 /√T + 1 /√K). (iii) We extend both al-gorithms under weaker assumptions about prior knowledge. When a strictly safe policy is known but its costs are unknown, we estimate its costs conservatively while preserving zero constraint violation. When both the policy and its costs are unknown, we learn a strictly safe policy and a conservative estimate of its costs in an estimation phase and incur only constant cumulative violation. 

Related Work. MORL studies sequential decision-making problems with multiple, po-tentially conflicting objectives and seeks policies that represent different trade-offs among them (Roijers et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2014; Hayes et al., 2022). A substantial line of work characterizes the Pareto front using scalarization-based formulations, including linear 

> 3Li, Shu, Gao, Qiu and Jiang

and Tchebycheff scalarizations, and develops methods for learning Pareto optimal policy sets (Roijers et al., 2013; Van Moffaert and Now´ e, 2014; Hayes et al., 2022; Kubo et al., 2026). More recent work studies preference-based methods that learn policies for given preferences and preference-free methods that compute policies for any preference specified after exploration (Yang et al., 2019; Qiu et al., 2024; Hu and Yu, 2026). However, most existing MORL approaches are developed for unconstrained environments and therefore do not provide explicit safety guarantees (Hayes et al., 2022; Qiu et al., 2024). Constrained reinforcement learning studies sequential decision-making under explicit safety or resource constraints, most commonly formalized as constrained Markov decision processes (Altman, 2021). Classical approaches enforce expected cumulative constraints over an episode via Lagrangian or primal-dual methods, providing asymptotic or averaged feasibility guarantees but allowing violations during learning (Achiam et al., 2017; Tessler et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2020). To obtain stricter guarantees, recent works adopt conservative or pessimistic designs that achieve zero or bounded cumulative constraint violation, often by tightening constraints or assuming access to a strictly safe policy (Liu et al., 2021; Vaswani et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2023; Ni and Kamgarpour, 2026). Other works incorporate optimism in the face of uncertainty to enable efficient exploration under safety constraints (Qiu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Ding and Lavaei, 2023; Wendl et al., 2026), and further investigate stepwise safety (Xiong et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). However, existing methods are largely limited to single-objective settings and do not address the challenges of multi-objective or preference-free learning. 

2 Preliminaries 

We consider a finite-horizon constrained multi-objective Markov decision process. It is defined by the tuple ( S, A, H, P, r, c, ρ ), where S and A are the finite state and action spaces. The horizon H is the number of steps in each episode. The transition kernel is 

P := {Ph}Hh=1 with Ph : S × A × S 7 → [0 , 1], and Ph(s′|s, a ) denotes the probability of transitioning to state s′ ∈ S from state s ∈ S after taking action a ∈ A at step h ∈ [H]. The reward function is vector-valued with m objectives, denoted by r := ( r1, . . . , r m), where 

ri := {ri,h }Hh=1 for every i ∈ [m]. Each ri,h : S × A 7 → [0 , 1] maps a state-action pair to the reward for the i-th objective at step h. Moreover, the constraint cost function is vector-valued with n constraints, denoted by c := ( c1, . . . , c n). We further define cj := {cj,h }Hh=1 ,where each cj,h : S × A 7 → [0 , 1] maps a state-action pair to the cost for the j-th constraint at step h. The initial state distribution is denoted by ρ. The learner follows a policy 

π := {πh}Hh=1 , where πh(a|s) represents the probability of taking action a ∈ A in state 

s ∈ S at step h ∈ [H]. Let Π denote the class of all stochastic Markov policies. For any 

π ∈ Π, we define the value of π for the i-th objective as V ri (π) := E[PHh=1 ri,h (sh, a h)|ρ, π, P ]and the value of π for the j-th constraint as V cj (π) := E[PHh=1 cj,h (sh, a h)|ρ, π, P ]. In each episode t ∈ [T ], the learner follows the policy πt and generates a trajectory 

{(sth, a th)}Hh=1 . At each step h ∈ [H], the learner observes a stochastic reward rti,h ∈ [0 , 1] for each objective i ∈ [m] and a stochastic constraint cost ctj,h ∈ [0 , 1] for each constraint j ∈ [n]. These observations satisfy E[rti,h |sth, a th] = ri,h (sth, a th) and E[ctj,h |sth, a th] = cj,h (sth, a th). The reward function r, the constraint cost function c, and the transition kernel P are unknown.

Safety Constraints and Safe Policies. To formulate the safety constraints, we collect the constraint cost functions defined above into a matrix C ∈ [0 , 1] H|S||A|× n, whose j-th column is ( cj,h (s, a )) h∈[H], (s,a )∈S×A . We use d ∈ { 1, H } to denote the number of time steps to which each safety constraint applies. For each constraint j ∈ [n], the learner knows the 

d time steps to which the constraint applies, and we set cj,h ≡ 0 at all other steps h ∈ [H]. When d = 1, each constraint applies to a single time step, which gives stepwise safety. When 

d = H, each constraint applies to all H steps, which gives cumulative safety. Accordingly, we have τ ∈ [0 , d ]n. In both cases, a policy π ∈ Π is safe if C⊤θP,π ≤ τ . The corresponding safe policy class and occupancy measure set are Π safe := {π ∈ Π : C⊤θP,π ≤ τ } and Θsafe := {θP,π : π ∈ Πsafe }. Pareto Optimality. In unconstrained MORL, the standard goal is to identify the Pareto optimal policy set, that is, the collection of policies for which no objective can be improved without degrading another (Qiu et al., 2024). When explicit safety constraints are imposed, policies that are Pareto optimal in the unconstrained setting may violate them. We therefore consider Pareto optimality relative to the safe policy class. 

Definition 4 (Safe Pareto optimal policy) For any two policies π ∈ Πsafe and π′ ∈

Πsafe , we say that π′ dominates π if and only if V ri (π′) ≥ V ri (π) for all i ∈ [m] and there exists at least one i′ ∈ [m] such that V ri′ (π′) > V ri′ (π). A policy π ∈ Πsafe is a safe Pareto optimal policy if and only if no other policy π′ ∈ Πsafe dominates π. We denote the set of safe Pareto optimal policies by Π∗

5 Preference-Free Safe Multi-Objective RL 

We note that CSTRL is preference-based, since its preference vector λ is fixed before explo-ration and a new exploration process is required whenever λ changes. To avoid repeated environment interaction across preferences while maintaining safety under either cumulative or stepwise constraints, we propose PF-CSTRL . The algorithm consists of a safe exploration stage followed by a preference-dependent planning stage. Its exploration stage collects a dataset using safe policies without a prespecified preference. The planning stage then computes a safe policy for any λ ∈ ∆m from this dataset without further environment interaction. The exploration stage collects one trajectory per episode, and the counts and estimators in (3)–(5) are computed from these trajectories. 8 Conclusion 

We develop a unified framework for safe multi-objective reinforcement learning that covers both cumulative and stepwise safety constraints. Pessimistic cost bounds ensure safety throughout learning, while averaging the resulting occupancy measures produces a single safe Markov policy. Separating exploration from planning also allows the learner to compute a policy for any given preference from the collected data, without further interaction with the environment. In both preference-based and preference-free settings, zero constraint violation can be guaranteed when a strictly safe policy is known but its costs are unknown. When the strictly safe policy itself is also unknown, the cumulative constraint violation remains bounded by a constant.
