# A Behavioral approach to Constrained Linear Quadratic Control: Feasibility and Optimality 

Soutrik Bandyopadhyay, Debasattam Pal and Shubhendu Bhasin 

Abstract — This paper develops a constrained lin-ear–quadratic (LQ) optimal control framework within the behavioral approach. Unlike classical formulations that rely on state–input representations, our method treats both the system dynamics and the constraints as trajectory-level objects. The system is modeled through a kernel repre-sentation, while inequality constraints are captured using behavioral inequalities defined over infinite-dimensional convex cones. Using quadratic differential forms (QDFs) to encode performance indices, we formulate the constrained LQ problem as a convex variational optimization over trajectories. By employing Gˆ ateaux differentials, we de-rive the stationarity and complementarity conditions and show that they admit a representation as an augmented behavioral system whose unique decomposition into au-tonomous and controllable sub-behaviors yields a complete parameterization of all optimal constrained trajectories. The resulting conditions constitute a behavioral analogue of the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) framework. A numerical example illustrates the effect of inequality constraints on the optimal behavior. The framework provides a general, trajectory-level representation for constrained optimal con-trol and opens avenues for closed-loop and data-driven extensions. 

I. Introduction A fundamental way of analyzing dynamical systems is the behavioral approach, where a system is defined as the set of trajectories following some underlying relation among variables. Unlike input-output descriptions, the behavioral approach does not presuppose a partition of variables into states, inputs, or outputs; instead, it characterizes a system directly through the laws satisfied by its measurable trajectories. Championed by Jan C. Willems [1], [2] in the 1980s, behavioral systems theory has gained traction in recent years in data-driven control, signal processing, robust control, and other areas (cf. [3] and the references therein). Within this framework, optimal control problems can also be formulated without resorting to state-space rep-resentations. A key tool is the theory of quadratic dif-ferential forms (QDFs) [4], which encode performance functionals and Lyapunov-like expressions using two-variable polynomial matrices, thereby generalizing the

This has enabled developments in opti-mal control including LQ problems for multidimensional and singular systems [7]–[9]. While behavioral formulations for unconstrained LQ control are well understood, the incorporation of con-straints remains comparatively underdeveloped, espe-cially when constraints must themselves be enforced on trajectories rather than on pre-assigned input or state variables. Real-world control problems often involve uni-lateral or inequality-type restrictions, such as saturation limits, safety constraints, physical contact constraints, or sign constraints. While classical constrained optimal control approaches have been reasonably successful in the literature [10]–[14], such approaches typically rely on state-input models, making them incompatible with the behavioral viewpoint. Alongside these model-based approaches, data-driven methods, most notably Data-enabled predictive control (DeePC) [15], [16] solve constrained predictive control problems directly from data, with extensions addressing robustness [17], reachability [18], and invariance [19]. However, all the approaches mentioned above assume a-priori partitioning of states, inputs and outputs and are typically expressed in the state-space representation. One promising approach in describing constraints in the behavioral setting can be traced back to A. A. ten Dam’s thesis [20], which introduced Behavioral Inequal-ities, a geometric formulation of unilateral constraints on trajectories. By characterizing restricted behaviors via convex cones and contact conditions, this line of work provided early insight into how inequality-type dynami-cal constraints can be expressed without assigning roles to variables, including the contact problem for linear continuous-time dynamical systems [21]. The feasibility of such behavioral inequalities had been conjectured in [22], but the author claimed that such analysis is an open problem owing to the fact that matrices are generally non-invertible over the polynomial ring. In parallel, there has been recent interest in convex processes [18], [23], [24] for describing dynamical systems with inequality constraints. However, to the best of our knowledge, con-strained optimal control has not yet been fully integrated with these behavioral constraint formulations. arXiv:2610.40542v1 [math.OC] 29 Oct 2026 In our recent work [25], static behavioral inequalities were analyzed to establish geometric feasibility con-ditions for unconstrained trajectory sets. The present paper builds upon [25] by extending those algebraic inequality foundations into a dynamic optimal control setting. Specifically, while [25] focuses on static feasi-bility checks, this work formulates variational trajectory optimization under behavioral inequality constraints, de-rives stationarity and complementarity conditions, and constructs an augmented behavioral KKT system for constrained LQR synthesis. In this paper, we bridge this gap by developing a constrained linear quadratic optimal control framework within the behavioral approach. The system dynamics and the constraints are both treated as behavioral objects: the system is described by a kernel representation, while inequality constraints are modeled via behavioral inequalities defined over infinite-dimensional convex cones. This enables constraints to be imposed directly on trajectories, without distinguishing between variables, and expressed through general poly-nomial matrix relations. Our main contribution is to show that constrained LQ optimal control can be cast as a convex varia-tional problem over trajectories and solved using tools from convex optimization in infinite-dimensional vector spaces. Leveraging the structure of QDFs and the latent-variable image representation of controllable behaviors, we derive the stationarity and complementarity con-ditions through Gˆ ateaux differentials and construct an augmented behavioral system encoding the optimality conditions. We show that this augmented system admits a decomposition into autonomous and controllable sub-behaviors, enabling explicit parameterization of all opti-mal solutions. This yields a behavioral analogue of the KKT conditions and a constructive path to computing optimal constrained trajectories, while preserving the generality of constrained optimal control problems.

VI. Conclusions 

In this paper we proposed a framework for constrained linear-quadratic optimal control within the behavioral approach, treating system dynamics and constraints as fundamental trajectory-level objects instead of relying on state-space or input-output representations. Such a formulation allows a more general treatment of complex systems where the distinction between inputs and outputs may not be fixed. We provided necessary and sufficient conditions for the feasibility of behavioral constraints and the optimality of the constrained optimal problem. The numerical results validated the effectiveness of the proposed method, showing how the optimal trajectories are modified when hitting constraints compared to the unconstrained case. While the present method solves the open loop problem, the closed loop and data-driven extensions of the present work may be attempted in a future work.
