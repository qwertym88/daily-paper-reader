# ABSTRACT 

Fast inference and strict constraint satisfaction are essential for learning-based decision-making. Existing methods often rely on constraint-specific projections or feasible-set parameterizations, while iterative repair can be costly. We introduce Convex-SKM-Net, a convex constraint-satisfaction layer that forms linearization halfspaces from residual and (sub)gradient oracles without constraint-specific in-equality projectors. It applies fixed-depth dual projected-gradient descent (dual PGD) to minimum-displacement block problems using GPU-friendly matrix op-erations. We jointly learn the predictor and a positive-definite mobility, adapting the correction geometry to improve objective quality. We prove convergence of the outer iterates at fixed dual depth and linear convergence in distance under Slater’s condition and bounded subgradients. All our test outputs on three second-order-cone model-predictive control (SOC-MPC) horizons and six power-system convex relaxations satisfy a 10 −3 feasibility tolerance. Comparisons with the lat-est constraint-satisfaction methods show inference speedups of up to 144 .3× on SOC-MPC and 7.7× on a convex quadratically constrained quadratic program-ming (QCQP) benchmark with joint training. On QCQP, the mean objective gap is 0.012% , with full test feasibility at 10 −4. Learning mobility reduces the five-seed affine-task mean gap by 22 .4% and improves gaps in matched power-system comparisons. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Learning-based decision-making in power systems, model-predictive control, and wireless resource allocation requires fast predictions that satisfy physical constraints and retain good objective val-ues (Donti et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2018; Alizadeh & Tabassum, 2023). Neural predictions can violate constraints even when violations are penalized during training (Zeng et al., 2024; Donti et al., 2021). Constraint layers enforce feasibility through feasible-set parameterizations, optimiza-tion, or iterative repair. Their use depends on the operations available for each constraint family: parameterizations may require interior points (Tordesillas et al., 2026), and splitting methods re-quire component projections (Grontas et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026). Residual-based repair works directly with constraint values and derivatives, but repeated correction can remain costly (Donti et al., 2021; Nguyen & Donti, 2025; Chu et al., 2026). Repair also changes the predicted decision, so its direction affects the final objective. We therefore design both the correction computation and its directional cost. Convex-SKM-Net uses convexity to give different inequalities a common local representation. A residual and a subgradient define a linearization halfspace containing the feasible set, allowing con-

After satisfying the affine equalities, we formulate minimum-displacement problems over selected blocks of these halfspaces. Fixed-depth dual projected-gradient descent (dual PGD) computes block updates with GPU-friendly matrix op-erations, without per-block factorizations or linear solves. Several block corrections reuse one lin-earization. On second-order-cone model-predictive control (SOC-MPC), inference is 80 .7–144 .3×

faster than fixed-400 -step Πnet. On the 300-bus semidefinite relaxation, postprocessing a fully fine-tuned predictor is 7.7× faster than LMI-Net, with a lower mean gap on identical raw predictions. We also learn how displacement is measured during repair. Euclidean distance assigns the same cost to equal-length moves, although their effects on the task objective can differ. A positive-definite mobility changes these directional costs while preserving the feasible set. Joint training adjusts both the predictor’s initial output and the geometry of its subsequent corrections. The same layer supports postprocessing with a prescribed mobility, including the identity. Learning mobility reduces the five-seed mean gap on the affine task by 22 .4% . In matched joint-training comparisons, it also reduces mean gaps by 11 .0% and 8.1% on the 300-bus conic and semidefinite relaxations. Our contributions are: 

(1) General convex constraint satisfaction. We introduce a layer using residual and (sub)gradient oracles without constraint-specific inequality projectors, supporting both postprocessing and joint training. 

(2) Block corrections with dual PGD. Our GPU-friendly block updates use fixed dual depth with-out per-block factorizations. We establish convergence of the outer iterates and linear convergence in distance under Slater’s condition and bounded subgradients. 

(3) Learnable mobility. We learn directional correction costs jointly with the predictor, improving objective gaps on affine and nonlinear convex tasks. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

We review neural constraint layers and the numerical methods underlying our corrections. 

Feasible-set parameterization. Linear-constraint constructions use polyhedral generators, gauge maps, or decision rules (Frerix et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023; Constante et al., 2025); other approaches calibrate constraints or restrict network maps over prescribed input regions (Zhao et al., 2023; Balestriero & LeCun, 2023). RAYEN uses an interior point and ray boundaries, computing the point offline for fixed constraints (Tordesillas et al., 2026). Radial transformations, invertible maps, and interior-point predictions also construct constrained outputs (Konstantinov & Utkin, 2023; Liang et al., 2024; Liang & Chen, 2025; Schneider & Kuhn, 2026). 

Differentiable solver layers. Affine repair uses closed-form updates or subconstraint decomposi-tions (Min & Azizan, 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). Optimization layers differentiate through constrained solutions during training (Amos & Kolter, 2017; Agrawal et al., 2019; Min & Azizan, 2025). Linear constraints admit generalized Sinkhorn and entropy-regularized dual iterations, with linear repair also used in tabular generative models (Wang et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2024; Stoian et al., 2024). 

Πnet and LMI-Net use component projections in operator splitting (Grontas et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026); Dykstra’s algorithm and alternating projections also support constrained prediction (Chen et al., 2018; Cristian et al., 2023). Among residual-based layers, DC3 combines equality completion with violation gradients, and FS-Net minimizes violation (Donti et al., 2021; Nguyen & Donti, 2025). Other updates use adap-tive repair, damped linearization, optimality conditions, or regularized Newton steps (Lastrucci & Schweidtmann, 2025; Goertzen et al., 2026; Iftakher et al., 2026; Chu et al., 2026). Sampling Kaczmarz–Motzkin (SKM) corrects the most violated sampled row (De Loera et al., 2017); nonlinear SKM handles nonlinear equations and variants with convex constraints (Zhang et al., 2023). T-SKM-Net unrolls row repair; AT-SKM-Net uses graph-guided sampling for dynamic linear constraints (Zhu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). Linear block methods project onto equalities or fit boundaries (Needell & Tropp, 2014; Zhang & Li, 2021; 2023; Briskman & Needell, 2015); subgradient, parallel, block-iterative, and minibatch methods also address convex inequalities (dos Santos, 1987; Kiwiel, 1995; Bauschke & Combettes, 2017; Necoara & Nedi´ c, 2021). 23 METHOD : C ONVEX -SKM-N ET
