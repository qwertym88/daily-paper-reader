# FORGE: Feasible Online Reduced-Order Reference Generation and Whole-Body Execution for Agile Humanoid Locomotion 

Adrian B. Ghansah 1, Yujin An 2, Jeeseop Kim 3, Soon-Jo Chung 1

Abstract — This paper presents a two-stage learning frame-work for versatile and agile humanoid locomotion that couples online reduced-order model (ROM) reference generation with whole-body tracking control. In the first stage, we train a command-conditioned whole-body reference generator using single rigid body dynamics combined with full humanoid kine-matics. In the second stage, the frozen ROM policy is rolled out online to generate references for training a whole-body tracking policy on the full-order dynamics model. Learning a kino-dynamically consistent reference generator in the simplified ROM space enables us to generate references over a broad command space and over multiple locomotion strategies using a single unified framework. We demonstrate the approach on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving longitudinal velocities from −2.0 m /s to 2.8 m /s, lateral velocities up to ±1.0 m /s,angular velocities up to ±3.0 rad /s, one-legged hopping, and continuous transitions across the commands, all using one unified reference policy and one whole-body tracking policy. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent advances have enabled humanoid robots to perform individual dynamic behaviors with impressive reliability. However, unifying these behaviors within a single control framework that is both agile and versatile remains chal-lenging due to the high-dimensional, underactuated, and nonlinear hybrid dynamics of humanoids. Model-based controllers often address this challenge by decomposing locomotion into two subproblems: generating reference trajectories and whole-body tracking. For reference generation, full-order model (FOM) trajectory optimization employs the complete dynamics, yielding trajectories that are dynamically consistent with the full-order system. However, using the complete dynamics may make the optimization problem computationally expensive [1]–[3]. Many frame-works therefore perform trajectory optimization offline or update the planner at a lower rate than the tracking controller [1], [4], which may limit their applicability in online settings. Reduced-order models (ROMs) offer an alternative ap-proach in which the robot is represented by a simplified model capturing its dominant dynamics. Because this low-dimensional representation involves fewer states and con-straints, the associated optimization is less expensive, en-abling ROM-based planners to generate references online,

How-ever, a trajectory that is feasible for the ROM may not be feasible for the full-order system, as the low-dimensional representation does not account for full-body kinematic con-straints and limb dynamics [6], [7]. Learning-based controllers provide an alternative approach for handling the complex dynamics of humanoid locomotion. Rather than relying on an explicit trajectory optimizer or a ROM planner to compute control actions, reinforcement learning (RL) trains policies that map observations directly to actions by maximizing a reward function. Massively parallel simulation allows these policies to accumulate large amounts of experience through training, and end-to-end policies trained in this manner have demonstrated remark-able robustness and task performance [8]–[10]. Locomotion frameworks that learn distinct skills via separately trained policies have been explored [11], yet, training a single policy that covers multiple tasks or broad command ranges remains difficult. Beyond task rewards alone, a complementary strategy is to supply reference trajectories during training, providing structured objectives for RL training [12], [13]. Whole-body tracking policies trained in this way have demonstrated highly agile behaviors [13]–[15]. However, this agility de-pends on high-quality reference trajectories [13]–[15], which remain challenging to obtain over a broad command space, let alone generate online [12], [13]. This work addresses the challenge of generating and track-ing high-quality whole-body reference trajectories online for versatile and agile humanoid locomotion over broad command spaces. To this end, we propose a two-stage frame-work. In the first stage, we train a command-conditioned whole-body reference generator based on single rigid body (SRB) dynamics combined with full-order humanoid kine-arXiv:2610.37974v1 [cs.RO] 27 Oct 2026 matics. In the second stage, we integrate this reference generator into the training of a whole-body tracking policy. We show that the proposed framework generates high-quality whole-body reference trajectories over a broad com-mand space and leverages them to train a whole-body track-ing policy. We further validate the framework on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Our contributions are:

A. Reduced-Order Models for Humanoid Locomotion 

ROMs are attractive for humanoid locomotion control be-cause they provide simplified representations of the complex full-order humanoid dynamics. These models are commonly employed to plan foothold locations and to generate the ground reaction forces and moments (GRFMs) required to stabilize the robot via real-time control. One of the canonical ROMs is the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) model [16], [17], which represents the robot as a point-mass moving in the horizontal plane at a fixed center of mass (COM) height, rotating about a passive pivot point at the foot. Related ROMs capture additional features of different locomotion behav-iors: the Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP) model [6] incorporates the hybrid nature of legged locomotion, the Angular Linear Inverted Pendulum (ALIP) model [18], [19] accounts for angular momentum about the stance foot, and the Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) model [20] permits variation in vertical velocity. These pendulum models, however, are primarily concerned with the regulation of step length and step timing; centroidal models instead incorporate both linear and angular momentum dynamics, providing a richer representation of the robot system. One such model is the SRB model [21], which is commonly coupled with Raibert-style footstep planners [22], and to which model predictive control (MPC) is often applied to compute GRFMs that are then mapped to feedforward torques through the contact Jacobian [7], [21], [23]. ROMs have also been employed to guide the training of RL policies, often through objectives derived from ROM outputs, such as step-length targets. In [24], the LIP model was used to generate rewards based on desired footstep targets, whereas in [25], [26], the ALIP model was used to generate step-length rewards that regulate the angular momentum for traversing constrained footholds. The HLIP model was employed in [27] to generate step-length rewards for achieving multigait locomotion and high-speed running. Common to these works is that the ROMs guide the policy during training rather than generating whole-body reference trajectories for the robot to track. Offline-generated ROM trajectories have also been explored for policy training. In [28], gait libraries based on the SLIP model were generated offline via trajectory optimization, and the resulting refer-ences were used during training to provide COM velocity and foot location rewards. Similarly, in [29], trajectory libraries based on the SRB model were generated offline via optimiza-tion for various dynamic maneuvers and were subsequently used as expert references during policy training to generate COM linear velocity, angular momentum, and foot position rewards. The direction most closely related to the proposed work is [30], in which LIP-based whole-body references are generated online. However, whereas [30] focuses on whole-body reference generation for various terrain setups, our work proposes a two-stage framework that broadens the com-mand space, enabling whole-body locomotion that ranges from agile maneuvers to high-speed running while also accommodating seamless transitions between commands. 

B. Humanoid Reference Generation 

In humanoid locomotion, providing motion references, such as kinematic and joint-torque trajectories, as a prior for imitation-based RL has proven crucial for robust policy learning of online tracking controllers. Model-based tra-jectory optimization serves as a foundation of such ref-erence generation by formulating motion synthesis as aconstrained optimization problem that enforces physically grounded dynamics to produce high-quality, dynamically feasible trajectories. These optimization-based approaches range from formulations built on ROMs [29] to those built on full-order-dynamics [31]. However, classical optimiza-tion pipelines typically rely on precomputed gait libraries and separate per-task policies, which limits versatility and hinders transitions between behaviors. Human motion data offers an alternative source for the reference generation, yet retargeting motions from human to robot morphologies often yields kinematically or dynamically infeasible references. Consequently, obtaining high-quality references for diverse downstream tasks remains an important open problem [12], [13], [15]. 

C. Whole-Body Motion Tracking 

Whole-body motion tracking for humanoids aims to trans-form kinematic references into dynamically feasible motions, enabling humanoids to operate in real environments through imitation-based RL. The control policy is trained to mini-mize the discrepancy between the reference motion and the executed motion, which allows the humanoid to reproduce diverse behaviors while satisfying physical constraints. Ref-erence motions are typically drawn from large-scale human datasets. This paradigm was first demonstrated for humanoid character animation in physical simulation [32], [33], and has since been extended to teach real humanoid robots skills Fig. 2: An overview of the two-stage training architecture. Real-time human imitation via teleoperation further broadens deployment to a wide range of environments [35]–[37]. To move beyond skills confined to fixed environments and toward real-time control in dynamic settings, a generator–tracker hierarchy has recently been pro-posed [38]–[40], where a diffusion-based motion generator produces future whole-body trajectories conditioned on task inputs and a tracker converts them into physically executable actions. However, all of these paradigms fundamentally rely on large-scale human motion datasets, which remain scarce and expensive to acquire. III. B ACKGROUND

IV. W HOLE -B ODY REFERENCE GENERATION 

A. Overview 

The objective of the Stage 1 illustrated in Fig. 2, is to develop a whole-body reference generator capable of realizing a broad range of locomotion commands through a single policy. Learning multiple skills within one policy while ensuring smooth transitioning remains challenging. We therefore employ the SRB dynamics in (1) and incorporate the full humanoid kinematics information into the training process, ensuring that all generated motions respect the kinematic capabilities of the robot. The Stage 1 training is conducted in a numerical ROM simulator, as described in Section IV-B. The output of the Stage 1 is a command-conditioned whole-body reference generator, which is sub-sequently employed by the Stage 2 whole-body tracker. B. Training Environment 

Stage 1 training is conducted in a proposed numerical ROM simulation environment that combines SRB dynamics with full humanoid kinematics using simplified contact kine-matics. Because the environment is formulated directly at the reduced-order level, it exposes the quantities that ROM-based control acts upon, specifically the end-effector states governed by task-space accelerations and contact timings as well as applied GRFMs. This formulation therefore provides immediate access to the command inputs without mediation by an external physics or contact engine. The environment instead advances the system states through numerical evalu-ation that encompasses both contact and collision detection, with MuJoCo [41] employed solely for visualization. 

1) Kinematics and Dynamics: In our simulator, only the robot base is modeled dynamically, while the end effectors are represented kinematically. The inertial properties of the physical robot are lumped into a single rigid body and the simulated physics evolves according to the SRB dynamics in (1). The external forces and moments acting on the system are computed such that the GRFMs are included only when the corresponding foot is in contact with the ground. The end effectors of the ROM comprise the feet poses and twists together with the hand positions and velocities, and they are driven kinematically through end effector task space accelerations. Given the ROM end effector poses and the SRB pose, the generalized coordinates of the full robot,

nj denotes the number of joints. The joint velocities and accelerations are subsequently computed through numerical differentiation of the joint positions. 2) Contact Kinematics: Each foot is modeled as an ankle with four contact points, two at the front and two at the back of the foot, matching the geometry of the robot’s foot. When a foot contact point is active, it is fixed in space, while the foot remains free to rotate about that point. We choose to constrain the foot roll to zero rotation to avoid brittle side contacts. As a result, the two heel contact points are always in the same contact state, and the same for the two toe contact states. We evaluate the versatility and agility of the proposed two-stage framework, which couples online ROM-based refer-ence generation with whole-body tracking control, through both simulation studies and hardware experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Both the ROM reference gen-erator and whole-body tracking controller operate at 50 Hz.

In simulation, we verify that the ROM-guided reference generator produces whole-body references that the Stage 2 policy can track while following the commanded velocity. Particular emphasis is placed on continuously time-varying longitudinal commands and concurrent hopping commands, both of which are handled by a single unified policy. The velocities of Unitree G1 are measured from its onboard IMU and odometry estimator. The measured velocities follow the commanded signals closely across both channels, and the robot transitions continuously between commands, including sign reversals through zero, without switching between separately trained skills. We emphasize that all results are produced by a single policy with no per-task tuning, no gait-specific gain scheduling, and no mode-switching logic: lateral stepping and in-place turning emerge from the same ROM-guided reference generator that produces forward and backward walking. These results indicate that the whole-body refer-ences supplied by the Stage 1 generator remain consistently trackable by the Stage 2 policy across a broad command space, and that this property is preserved under transfer to hardware without additional tuning.

VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work we presented a framework for realizing versa-tile and agile locomotion on humanoid robots. We trained a command-conditioned whole-body reference generator based on ROM control, where SRB dynamics were combined with the full humanoid kinematics to synthesize the reference motions. Integrating this reference generator into a whole-body tracking framework enabled a single policy to realize a broad range of twist commands and multiple modes of locomotion. We demonstrated the proposed approach on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving longitudinal speeds from −2.0 m/s to 2.8 m/s, lateral speeds up to ±1.0 m/s, turning rates up to ±3.0 rad/s, and the ability to hop on one leg.
