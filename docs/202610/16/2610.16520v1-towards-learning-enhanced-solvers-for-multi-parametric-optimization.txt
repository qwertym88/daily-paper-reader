# Towards learning-enhanced solvers for multi-parametric optimization ∗

## Dieter Teichrib, Fabian K¨ onig, and Moritz Schulze Darup 

Abstract . Neural networks (NNs) have been employed in various ways to accelerate the solution of multi-parametric optimization problems (OPs), which prominently arise in model predictive control (MPC). Early approaches focused on approximating the parametric solution (e.g., the optimal control law) directly by NNs. More recently, learning-based techniques have been used to emulate or enhance solver it-erations. For the direct approximation of the parametric so-lution, it has been demonstrated that exploiting structural properties of the underlying OP facilitates the design of suit-able NN architectures. In this paper, we show that struc-tural analysis can also be leveraged to systematically inte-grate learning into classical optimization algorithms, thereby enhancing their performance. We formalize our concept for the case of multi-parametric quadratic programs (mpQPs), which frequently arise in linear-quadratic MPC, as a representative application. More precisely, we demonstrate that tailored NNs enable aparameter-dependent fine-tuning of the step sizes in a stan-dard dual projected gradient scheme. The design of the NNs is guided by the observation that optimal step sizes, which permit obtaining the exact optimal solution in finitely many iterations, are piecewise constant functions over the parame-ter space (e.g., the state space in MPC). We establish key the-oretical properties of the proposed approach and illustrate its effectiveness in a numerical case study. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The computational complexity of multi-parametric optimization problems (OPs) remains a key challenge in applications that re-quire real-time solutions. As is the case in, e.g., model predictive control (MPC) [1], where a multi-parametric OP, with the system state as a parameter, has to be solved at every time step within the system’s sampling period to obtain the control input. To mit-igate the challenges associated with the online solution of multi-parametric OPs, several approaches have been proposed. A non-machine-learning-based method is to solve the multi-parametric OP offline for all feasible parameter values and store the asso-ciated solutions. For cases where the multi-parametric OP is a multi-parametric quadratic program (mpQP), it is known that the resulting explicit optimizer is a piecewise affine (PWA) function

But even eval-uating the explicit PWA optimizer may be intractable in some cases, since the number of polyhedral sets grows in the worst case exponentially with the number of constraints in the OP [2]. Therefore, neural networks (NNs) have been applied in var-ious ways to accelerate the solution of multi-parametric OPs. The earliest applications of NNs in this context were primar-ily focused on directly approximating the explicit solution, i.e., training NNs to represent explicit optimizers [3–6] or opti-mal value functions (OVFs) [7–9]. More recently, learning-based techniques that aim to emulate or enhance solver itera-tions [10, 11], often referred to as ”Learn-to-Optimize” (L2O) frameworks [12–14], were introduced. Now, an insight from the research on the direct approximation of solutions for multi-parametric OPs is that the efficacy of these approximations can be significantly enhanced when the NN topology is designed to exploit structural properties of the underlying OP. More specifi-cally, the PWA and PWQ structure of the optimal control law and the OVF, respectively, in MPC for linear systems with quadratic costs have been used to derive NN topologies that yield low-error approximations [4, 15, 16]. In this paper, we demonstrate that a rigorous analysis of clas-sical optimization algorithms can also be used to systematically integrate structural knowledge into NNs that are used to approx-imate solver iterations. We formalize this concept for the case of mpQPs. Specifically, we show that tailored NNs can be used to enable parameter-dependent fine-tuning of step sizes within a standard dual projected gradient scheme. Our approach builds on the following results that we derived from a theoretical analy-sis of dual projected gradient solvers for mpQPs. First, we show that there exist optimal step sizes that, in principle, permit ob-taining the exact optimal solution in a finite number of iterations (Theorem 5 and Theorem 10). Moreover, we prove that these optimal step sizes are piecewise constant (PWC) functions over the parameter space (e.g., the state space in the context of MPC). By designing NNs that structurally match this PWC mapping, we enhance the performance of the iterative dual projected gra-dient solver compared to a solver with a parameter-independent fixed step size. We first state the key theoretical results for this approach and demonstrate its effectiveness through a numerical case study. The paper is organized as follows. In the remainder of this sec-tion, we state some basic notation. In Section II, we briefly sum-marize relevant background on mpQPs, dual projected gradient solver, optimal solutions derived from the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, and NNs. In Section III, we introduce some preliminary results that allow us to derive optimal step sizes for a dual projected gradient solver that exactly solves mpQPs under arXiv:2610.16520v1 [math.OC] 12 Oct 2026 certain technical assumptions. Subsequently, we show in Sec-tion IV that it is possible to satisfy these assumptions for almost all feasible parameters of the mpQP. Finally, in Section V, we combine all theoretical results to derive a learning-enhanced dual projected gradient solver. Moreover, we illustrate our method and evaluate its performance in Section V.A.

L EARNING OPTIMAL STEP SIZES

Based on a rigorous theoretical analysis of DPGD solvers for mpQPs in Section III and Section IV, we derived NN topologies that allow us to enhance the standard DPGD solver by approx-imating optimal parameter-dependent step sizes. The theoreti-cal analysis reveals several new insights into DPGD solvers for mpQPs. We showed that the dual of mpQPs and thereby the mpQPs themselves can be solved exactly by these solvers when choosing parameter-dependent optimal step sizes. Moreover, we showed that these optimal step sizes are PWC functions of the mpQPs parameter.
