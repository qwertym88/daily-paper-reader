# Task-Space Motion-Force Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for Precise and Compliant Control under Uncertainties 

Quang-Nam Nguyen 1 and Ludovic Righetti 1,2

Abstract — In Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), the control law is implicitly determined by the solution of an Optimal Control Problem (OCP). Consequently, OCP design is often tailored to specific platforms and tasks, demanding extensive expert knowledge and tuning effort. To address this, we propose a unified NMPC framework that directly plans and controls the robot joints subject to task-space motion and force requirements and is capable of seamlessly generalizing across multiple robotic platforms and diverse tasks. Further-more, optimization-based methods often treat interactions as rigid contacts, making the torque solution sensitive to the modeling errors of the robot dynamics and the environment. We study the intrinsic task-space impedance of our proposed controller, its partial contraction for differential stability and differential passivity under unmodeled interactions. As a result, our framework enables closing the force-feedback loop in the high-level planning layer to achieve precise motion-force tracking performance, addressing the long-standing challenge of force-feedback NMPC. Finally, we validate our framework on physical hardware, demonstrating hybrid motion-force control on a redundant manipulator and compliant locomotion on a quadruped, while preserving core NMPC advantages such as hard constraint satisfaction, optimal redundancy exploitation, and real-time planning under unforeseen interactions.

To realize the motion on real robots, the optimal trajectory is then used as reference for faster tracking controllers such as joint-space linear/nonlinear MPC [4] or reinforcement learning (RL) tracking policies [5]. Recent efforts have made whole-body NMPC possible by carefully tailoring the OCP to specific platforms and tasks [6] or making strong assumptions about contacts, either via analytical model [7] or in simulation [8], rendering these approaches often brittle to unexpected interactions. These reasons can explain why the community tends to prefer policy learning methods (e.g. RL) [9], [5] which remove all the issues of real-time decision making. However, such approaches might not be suitable for all contexts, for example when hard constraint satisfaction guarantees are dictated by the application, or when the environment model is inaccurate or unavailable, leading to large sim-to-real gap. Furthermore, incorporating force/torque sensors to achieve motion-force tracking performance of state-of-the-art force control techniques has been a challenge for both optimization and learning methods [10]. In this paper, we propose a task-space NMPC framework to generate in real-time robot motion that guarantees passive impedance in the task space for various robots. Indeed, a diverse set of robotic tasks can be achieved with platform-agnostic task-space targets, then a generic OCP featuring end-effector reaching costs can generalize to multiple robot platforms. Moreover, based on theoretical analysis, we re-move unnecessary rigid contact assumptions and instead choose appropriate end-effector cost features to regulate the robot’s task-space impedance and its overall movement in a generic manner while increasing its robustness to distur-arXiv:2610.17069v1 [cs.RO] 12 Oct 2026 bances and model errors. As a surprising result, this frame-work also enables us to utilize a simple and modular way to close the force-feedback loop in NMPC through admittance control. We validate our approach for multiple motion-force control tasks on a 7-DOF manipulator and achieve the same tracking precision as state-of-the-art force control methods while preserving the additional advantages of NMPC such as optimal redundancy resolution and real-time predictive planning under hard constraint satisfaction and unmodeled human-robot interactions. We also run NMPC on a real quadruped to stress-test the robustness to unknown environ-ments and modeling/sensory uncertainties, while achieving customizable compliance at the robot’s feet and center of mass (COM). We open-source our framework which seam-lessly controls diverse types of robots for different tasks, utilizing Pinocchio for our dynamics model and Mujoco for simulations to mimic the model-to-real gap. 

A. Related work 1) Control by optimization: In recent years, the robotics community has largely moved from closed-form methods such as operational-space control [11], inverse dynamics control [12], [13], to optimization-based methods aiming to resolve an OCP, either online through MPC or offline with RL. Optimizing a cost function under constraints, i.e. an OCP, is equivalent to a control law, for example, in the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR). However, it is not always pos-sible to obtain an analytical form of the control law because the difficulty levels between solving differential equations and solving optimization directly may differ for different problems, especially when the dynamics is nonlinear. In this paper, we would like to recover the underlying control law resulting from a generic OCP under nonlinear dynamics with simple joint velocity/torque regularization and end-effector costs, which can be used in diverse tasks such as pick-and-place, goal reaching, drawing, polishing, walking, etc. Classical methods often prefer to decouple complex tasks into task-space planning followed by joint-space planning and control. Similarly, recent efforts in learning-based meth-ods also aim to remove joint-space tele-operation data, which are expensive and hard to scale, and instead col-lect demonstration data in task space [14]. Following this paradigm, we propose a generic NMPC architecture (see Fig. 2) that is applicable to a wide range of robots and replaces Inverse Kinematics (IK), Inverse Dynamics (ID), Proportional-Derivative (PD) control with receding-horizon optimal control. 

2) Impedance and Admittance: Numerous methods have been developed to address hybrid motion-force control, in-cluding impedance [15] and admittance [16] which aim to establish a mass-spring-damper relationship for end-effector contacts but differ in whether the end-effector is force-controlled or position-controlled. For locomotion, COM ad-mittance and foot damping can be modeled via an admittance control law [17] or a PD control law [18] and eventually mapped to joint state and torque using IK and ID solvers [19]. The relationship between impedance and admittance is known to be complementary, and many works have proposed unifying impedance and admittance, such as mode switching [20] or through hardware design [21]. Unlike in closed-form methods which specify spring-like and damper-like constants [22], we can obtain impedance stiffness that can be scaled by the weight matrices in nonlinear OCP. 

3) Force-feedback MPC: There are two approaches in force-feedback MPC which originate in force control: direct and indirect. Some direct force-control MPC methods may impose the rigid contact constraint on the end-effectors [7], [23], which over-constrains the robot while requiring accu-rate contact timings and locations. Moreover, contact forces and control torques are algebraically coupled due to the over-constrained dynamics. Although some works have proposed breaking the algebraic loop by introducing delay [7] or using soft contact models [10], these methods introduce task-specific contact assumptions, making it difficult to implement for different tasks and sensitive to actual contact state. In comparison, we do not apply rigid contact constraints to interactions except standing foot of legged robots. We show that the mismatch between our predictive model and the actual interaction helps characterize task-space impedance, and afford reasoning about contacts in task space instead without modifying the dynamics model. We follow the indirect approach which has shown promis-ing results in linear and nonlinear MPC. For example, [24] proposed to adjust reference end-effector trajectory to regulate contact forces, i.e. an admittance control scheme, in a position-controlled mobile manipulator using sequential linear quadratic MPC. In comparison, our method uses an admittance loop to regulate impedance instead of relying only on the actual robot movements. Recently, [25] combined admittance control with NMPC for aerial robots, updating the reference trajectory using the impedance model’s predictions. In contrast, our method does not use interaction forces as an optimization variable nor in the OCP’s dynamics model, keeping our formulation generic and avoiding the need for force derivative, which is commonly set to zero [25] or estimated indirectly with a contact dynamics model [10]. II. METHODOLOGY OVERVIEW Our OCP formulation using end-effector costs allows to solve tasks in two stages, i.e. the task-space motion-force planning and the generic optimal control, resulting in a uni-fied architecture (see Fig. 2) that is generalizable to different types of robots in various tasks. The high-level task-space planning can be solved using any planning or learning-based methods that output end-effector plans, either discrete or continuous. For example, locomotion only requires a discrete plan for contact placements and timings. Manipulation tasks such as pick-and-place can be solved with discrete reaching targets while some tasks, e.g. drawing and polishing, dictate that the end-effector keeps a continuous path. Finally, end-effector plans are inputs to a generic OCP formulation that computes optimal joint and torque trajectories. A. Clarifications on dynamics model with contacts 

Denote q the robot configuration and u the control torque. The dynamics of fixed-base and floating-base robots is: 

M (q)¨ q + C(q, ˙q) ˙ q + τg (q) = ST u + JTc Fc + JTe Fe (2) where M (q) is the robot’s inertial matrix; τg (q) is the gravity term, C(q, ˙q) ˙ q is Coriolis and centrifugal forces, and S is a selection matrix (for fixed-base robots, S = I). We would like to clarify the distinction between rigid contact wrench 

Fc and external interaction wrench Fe. Rigid contact is associated with holonomic scleronomic constraint [2], [13]: 

Jc(q) ˙ q = 0 ⇒ Jc(q)¨ q+ ˙Jc(q) ˙ q = 0 and its contributed torque 

JTc Fc is derived from Gauss’s principle of least constraint, where the Lagrangian multiplier is also the contact wrench 

Fc. In contrast, external interaction wrench Fe does not require end-effector constraints. For example, the gravity term τg (q) is the contribution of gravity applied to the center of mass (COM), which can be written as τg (q) = JTCOM mg 

but no constraint is imposed. Therefore, Fe may include gravitational forces due to modeling errors. In this work, we only enforce rigid contact constraint on the standing feet in floating-base robots, while the fixed-base robot’s end-effector and floating-base robot’s swing feet are not subjected to contact constraints and their force contributions JTe Fe are not included in our prediction model even when they interact with the environment, i.e. 

M (q)¨ q + C(q, ˙q) ˙ q + τg (q) = ST u + JTc Fc (3) which is valid for a number of reasons. Firstly, contacts may not occur as planned and are generally not rigid contacts, so predicting exact contact timings and force evolution is prone to errors. Secondly, we will show that the mismatch between our prediction model (3) and the full dynamics (2), together with our cost function, characterize a task-space impedance which can be regulated and is robust to unknown interactions.

D. Experiment 4: Compliant locomotion for a quadruped in an unknown, unsteady environment with limited sensing 

In this experiment, a quadruped Go2 must walk through unknown, unsteady obstacles (Fig. 1d) without terrain model or visual perception. Importantly, to stress test the system and validate the task-space impedance, we remove contact detec-tion and translational position/velocity odometry feedback from the controller (floating-base position is set to [0 , 0, 0] 

and velocity to the commanded velocity [Vx, V y , 0] ). We only use joint and gyroscope feedback. The task is therefore quite difficult due to limited feedback. There are instances when the obstacles are moving unexpectedly (see Fig. 1d and the video), the robot is still able to compliantly adapt and robustly compute optimal trajectories to recover balance. Our I-NMPC updates at 33 Hz , solving a constrained OCP with joint state and torque bound constraints for a horizon of 10 to 12 nodes × 0.03 s. The mim solvers [3] spends less than 10 ms to converge. Since Go2 utilizes a low-level PD controller, we send a bundle of the optimal torque, joint position and velocity with KjP = 50 .0N m/rad × I12 ×12 

and KjD = 5 .0N ms/rad × I12 ×12 .

E. Experiment 5: Quadruped Go2 carries human on a chair 

The advantage of our method is that the walking gait can be customized for different purposes by simply changing hyper-parameters, e.g. step height, step duration; and the robot’s morphology can adapt to unknown terrains thanks to the impedance at its feet and COM. Based on the same controller in experiment 4, we simply increase the weight matrix of the COM task cost and set the desired COM further ahead. With those few parameter changes, the robot is able to pull a chair with human sitting on it (Fig. 1e). This experiment not only validates the task-space impedance resulting from our cost function design, but also shows the ability to customize robot’s behaviors in our framework without any re-formulation or re-training. In comparison, a recent work [31] proposes a teacher-student RL framework that achieves a similar compliant behavior by training a teacher policy with an explicit compliant law and privileged external force information which was then re-moved from the student policy training. We achieve a similar result using passive impedance without force feedback. VII. CONCLUSIONS In this paper, we proposed a generic NMPC framework that solves motion-force requirements in the task space and generates optimal joint trajectories to control the robot in real-time with an intrinsic task-space impedance. Based on our theoretical analysis of the task-space impedance of a generic OCP formulation with end-effector cost features, we are able to remove unnecessary rigid contact constraints and instead regulate contact forces by adapting the weight matrices and goal poses of the end-effector costs. This results in a versatile way to reason about contacts with differential stability and passivity guaranties. We validated our method through compliant locomotion tasks with a blind quadruped carrying unknown weights in unknown environment and hy-brid motion-force control tasks with a redundant manipulator under constraints and unforeseen human-robot interactions. Within the scope of this paper, the impedance control law is analyzed for the unconstrained OCP. It would be interesting to study the effect of active constraints that may occur such as joint limits, collision avoidance, friction cone, etc. These topics involve additional analysis into designing or learning terminal cost functions and constraints capable of approximating long-horizon value function and reachability, which we leave for future investigations. In addition, our architecture can generate in real-time smooth robot trajec-tories for any task-and-motion-planning (TAMP) planners or learning policies that output task-space targets, while adding constraint satisfaction guarantee, compliance and force-feedback capabilities. In practice, the main limitation of our method is that the computation time grows as the DOFs and horizon increase, which introduces delay and limits control frequency. For repetitive tasks such as walking, our framework could be used as an expert demonstrator to train behavior cloning policies in simulation, thus replacing online computation by offline memorization.
