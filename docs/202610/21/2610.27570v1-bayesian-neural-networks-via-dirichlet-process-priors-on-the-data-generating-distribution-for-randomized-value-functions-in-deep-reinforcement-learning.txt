Abstract 

Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) offer a principled framework for quantifying epistemic uncertainty. However, standard weight-space priors yield complex, high-dimensional neural network posteriors that are computationally prohibitive to sample from, and popular ap-proximate inference schemes often fail to capture epistemic uncertainty effectively—a failure mode particularly acute in reinforcement learning (RL) (Osband et al., 2018). To resolve this challenge, we introduce a new class of BNNs that quantify uncertainty by placing Bayesian nonparametric Dirichlet Process (DP) priors directly on the underlying data-generating distribution rather than on network parameters. Building on this foundation, we develop DP-DQN, a reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages DP-BNNs to maintain posterior distributions over value functions and perform efficient Thompson sampling. By grounding randomized value functions in Bayesian nonparametrics, DP-DQN unifies data-driven pos-terior reweighting (via the Bayesian Bootstrap) with generative prior hypotheses injected directly through the DP base measure, enabling decisive “deep exploration” with a single neural network without the overhead of multi-network ensembles. 

1 Introduction 

In reinforcement learning (RL), maximizing cumulative return requires navigating the fundamental exploration-exploitation dilemma: balancing current best estimates (exploitation) with exploratory actions that resolve epistemic uncertainty. Solving this trade-off efficiently is challenging because delayed rewards require actions to be coordinated across extended multi-step decision sequences—a property known as deep exploration (Osband et al., 2016; 2019a; Oren et al., 2025)—rather than perturbed independently at each individual state. 

Thompson Sampling (Thompson, 1933; Kaufmann et al., 2012; Russo et al., 2018; Durand et al., 2018; Riquelme et al., 2018; Maillard, 2019) provides an elegant approach that tackles the exploration-exploitation dilemma by maintaining a posterior distribution over models and selecting actions in proportion to the probability that they are optimal. While these models are conventionally drawn from parametric distribution families (e.g., exponential families), Thompson sampling can in principle be applied to any statistical model admitting tractable posterior sampling (Riquelme et al., 2018). A natural choice for such statistical models is Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs), owing to their universal function approximation and rich representation learning capabilities. In contrast to standard neural networks that provide only point estimates, conventional BNNs specify a prior distribution over parameters θ. Given n training observations z1: n, one infers a posterior distribution over θ, which in turn captures epistemic uncertainty in function predictions. In deep RL, one can represent value functions via BNNs and drive exploration via Thompson sampling over value-function predictions. However, exact posterior inference over neural network weights is notoriously intractable, prompting extensive research into approximate Bayesian methods. These range from Monte-Carlo (MC) dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and variational inference (Blundell et al., 2015) to stochastic mini-batch Markov Chain Monte Carlo (Welling & Teh, 2011). Unfortunately, deep RL algorithms built on 1arXiv:2610.27570v1 [stat.ML] 20 Oct 2026 Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) these weight-space approximate Bayesian methods struggle on hard-exploration tasks because they fail to capture function-space epistemic uncertainty faithfully (Osband et al., 2018). This deficiency stems largely from the difficulty of designing meaningful priors directly in parameter space (Arbel et al., 2023): while practitioners frequently possess domain knowledge regarding the data-generating process (e.g., transition dynamics and reward scales), mapping such insight into high-dimensional, isotropic parameter priors on over-parameterized neural weight vectors θ ∈ Rd (d ≫ 1) is rarely straightforward and often causes posterior uncertainty collapse. To resolve these limitations, we introduce Bayesian Neural Networks via Dirichlet Process priors (DP-BNNs), a paradigm that quantifies epistemic uncertainty by placing a nonparametric DP prior directly on the underlying data-generating distribution rather than on network parameters. Under this formulation, posterior inference over the data distribution decomposes into two complementary components (Ferguson, 1973; Vashishtha & Maillard, 2025; Vashishtha, 2026): an empirical reweighting of observed data (the Bayesian Bootstrap) and an explicit prior base measure. In reinforcement learning, optimizing an action-value network on random measures sampled from this DP posterior induces a principled functional posterior distribution over value functions. Crucially, whereas existing randomized value function approaches require training an ensemble of multiple neural networks to represent functional uncertainty (Osband et al., 2016; 2018), our approach achieves temporally coordinated deep exploration with a single neural network by freezing the value function sampled for each episode across the entire rollout. We summarize our primary contributions as follows: • DP-BNN Formulation and Sampling: We introduce Bayesian Neural Networks via Dirichlet Process priors (DP-BNNs), a framework that quantifies epistemic uncertainty by placing a nonpara-metric DP prior on the data-generating distribution rather than on network weights. Using the Sethuraman stick-breaking construction, we develop a tractable posterior sampling algorithm for deep architectures and validate its epistemic uncertainty quantification on spatial 2D regression. • Deep Reinforcement Learning via DP-BNNs (DP-DQN): We instantiate DP-BNNs for randomized value functions in deep Q-learning, establishing the DP-DQN algorithm. By optimizing an action-value DP-BNN on random measures sampled from the DP posterior via episodic bound-ary warmstarts, DP-DQN realizes principled Thompson sampling with exact multi-step temporal consistency using a single neural network. • Comprehensive Empirical Benchmarking: We evaluate DP-BNNs across diverse decision-making benchmarks, demonstrating that they resolve hard exploration in DeepSea across extensive horizons evaluated up to N = 50 (2 50 ≈ 10 15 trajectory search space with empirical O(N 1.45 )sample complexity), RiverSwim-6, deceptive N -Chain, continuous MountainCar-v0, and contextual Wheel Bandit, matching or outperforming multi-network ensembles while strictly maintaining a single-network footprint. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 surveys related work. Section 3 reviews Dirichlet Processes and their stick-breaking construction. Section 4 introduces Bayesian Neural Networks via Dirichlet Process priors (DP-BNNs) and details their posterior sampling algorithm. Section 5 presents DP-DQN, applying DP-BNNs to randomized value functions in deep reinforcement learning, and evaluates its performance on challenging exploration benchmarks. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper. 

2 Related Works 

Randomized Value Functions and Exploration in Deep RL. Our work connects directly to deep reinforcement learning algorithms that explore via randomized value functions, replacing heuristic ϵ-greedy exploration with principled posterior sampling (Thompson Sampling) (Thompson, 1933; Kaufmann et al., 2012; Ghavamzadeh et al., 2015; Osband et al., 2016; Russo et al., 2018). In deep RL, the most direct approach to Thompson sampling is to maintain a Bayesian neural network over action-values. However, exact posterior inference over network weights is intractable (MacKay, 2003), and mini-batch MCMC schemes 2Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) scale poorly to online RL training regimes (Welling & Teh, 2011). Consequently, practitioners have relied on approximate inference methods such as Monte-Carlo dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and mean-field variational inference (Blundell et al., 2015). Unfortunately, these weight-space approximations struggle to maintain functional uncertainty in unvisited regions, leading to exploration failure in challenging environments (Osband et al., 2018). 

Bootstrap Ensembles and Prior Mechanisms. To approximate posterior sampling over function space without exact weight posteriors, Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017) proposed Deep Ensembles, relying on random parameter initializations across independently trained networks. In sequential decision-making, Osband et al. (2016) introduced Bootstrapped DQN, which approximates posterior sampling using an ensemble of K

neural networks trained on nonparametrically resampled data (see also Kveton et al. 2019 for bootstrapping exploration in bandits). In sparse-reward environments, however, empirical bootstrapping alone provides limited guidance in regions devoid of data: because all ensemble heads are trained on unrewarded transitions, their value estimates tend to align across unvisited states. To address this and sustain directed exploration, Osband et al. (2018) introduced Bootstrapped DQN with Randomized Prior Functions (BootDQN-RP), which augments each trainable head fθk with a fixed, randomly initialized prior network pk. While highly effective in practice, this formulation substantially increases computational and memory demands, as it requires training and maintaining an ensemble of K distinct neural networks alongside their corresponding prior networks. In another approach, Bayesian Deep Q-Networks (BDQN) (Azizzadenesheli et al., 2018) applies Bayesian linear regression to the final layer representations; however, its capability on deep-exploration benchmarks has remained largely unexplored in the literature, and our empirical evaluations on DeepSea show that BDQN does not resolve the task. Theoretically, restricting Bayesian inference strictly to the final linear readout cannot account for epistemic uncertainty in the underlying feature representations on unvisited states (see Appendix D.3 for a detailed theoretical analysis). 

Our Approach: Dirichlet Process Priors on Data Distributions. Rather than placing priors on network parameters (Blundell et al., 2015) or relying on multi-network ensembles with additive priors (Osband et al., 2018), our approach formulates epistemic uncertainty nonparametrically on the data-generating distribution. Building upon DP posterior sampling techniques developed in Vashishtha & Maillard (2025) and Vashishtha (2026), DP-BNNs connect Rubin’s classical Bayesian Bootstrap (Rubin, 1981)—recovered in the non-informative limit α → 0—with an informative prior base measure F0. Crucially, whereas empirical bootstrapping can only resample observed data, the base measure populates unvisited regions of the domain, sustaining non-vanishing epistemic uncertainty where experience is absent. This enables directed exploration with a single neural network while avoiding the parameter and memory footprint of multi-network ensembles.

Because DP-BNNs represent uncertainty via the DP posterior over data distributions, training a single neural network on a draw from this posterior directly provides the required sample. Consequently, an agent can perform Thompson sampling with a single neural network, eliminating the heuristic multi-network ensembles required by previous methods. 5 Reinforcement Learning with DP-BNNs 

We now extend the DP-BNN framework to reinforcement learning, developing DP-DQN: a deep Q-learning algorithm that achieves directed, temporally consistent exploration via posterior sampling over action-value functions.

5.3 Empirical Evaluation on Hard-Exploration Benchmarks 

To evaluate whether DP-BNN posteriors provide effective randomized value functions with a single neural network, we examine DP-DQN across the scalable DeepSea benchmark (Osband et al., 2019a), the canonical diagnostic domain for deep exploration. Specifically, we evaluate: (i) whether a single neural network equipped with a DP posterior matches the sample efficiency of multi-network ensembles on canonical DeepSea-20; (ii) how empirical sample complexity scales as grid size expands up to N = 50; and (iii) how the computational and memory footprint of DP-DQN compares with multi-network ensemble baselines. 

The DeepSea Exploration Benchmark. DeepSea requires an agent to execute N consecutive coordinated actions across an episode of length N to collect a solitary terminal reward ( r = +1 .0) at ( N − 1, N − 1). It challenges exploration through three distinct mechanisms: (i) an unknown action permutation per row masks the optimal direction; (ii) a continuous step cost of −0.01 /N penalizes exploration along the optimal path and biases myopic policies toward the unrewarded boundary; and (iii) any suboptimal step irreversibly routes the agent into zero-reward absorbing states. Consequently, dithering exploration strategies (such as ϵ-greedy) require an expected Ω(2 N ) episodes to discover the goal (Osband et al., 2016; 2019a)—scaling from 2 20 ≈ 10 6

episodes at N = 20 to 2 50 ≈ 10 15 episodes at N = 50. We benchmark on canonical DeepSea-20 (Figure 2a) and evaluate scaling behavior across 30 grid sizes up to N = 50 (Figure 2b). 

Base Measure for DP-DQN. To ensure exploration is driven without privileged environment knowledge, DP-DQN specifies an uninformative product base measure over transitions, F0(s, a, r, s ′, d ) = F0s(s)×F0a(a)×

F0r(r) × F0s′ (s′) × F0d(d). State and next-state components are sampled uniformly over the N 2 canonical one-hot vectors (˜ s, ˜s′ ∼ Uniform( Sone-hot ⊂ { 0, 1}N 2

)), actions uniformly ˜ a ∼ Uniform( {0, 1}), and continuing flags ˜d = 0, encoding zero information about transition graph topology or goal coordinates. For rewards, setting F0r = U(0 , R max ) (with Rmax = 1 .0) naturally induces stochastic optimism (Osband & Van Roy, 2017; Agrawal & Jia, 2017), sustaining positive expected value on unexplored states to overcome continuous 10 Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) step costs without artificial reward shaping. We set concentration parameter α = 3 .0 and truncation level 

T = 50; complete hyperparameter specifications, support conditions, and base measure ablations are detailed in Table 2 and Appendices B and D. 

DeepSea-20 Exploration Performance. Figure 2a presents comparative results on DeepSea-20 across 10 independent random seeds per algorithm over 10,000 episodes (mean cumulative regret with shaded ±1standard deviation). All agents employ an action-value architecture with a single hidden layer of 20 units (MLP-20), matching the network capacity in Osband et al. (2018). For DP-DQN, Layer Normalization (Ba et al., 2016) is included to stabilize gradient variance across heterogeneous stick-breaking weights, with concentration parameter α = 3 .0 and truncation level T = 50 (Table 2). Equipped with the uninformative uniform base measure, DP-DQN achieves a 100% solve rate (10/10 seeds), discovering the terminal goal at episode 397 ± 154 and reaching policy convergence in 602 ± 104 episodes, with cumulative regret plateauing at 418 ± 94. In comparison, Bootstrapped DQN with randomized priors (BootDQN-RP, 20 ensemble heads + 20 prior networks) (Osband et al., 2018) requires 5 ,210 ± 604 episodes with 4 ,922 ± 582 regret. Notably, DP-DQN consolidates into the optimal policy within ≈ 200 episodes after initial discovery (397 ± 154 → 602 ± 104). Because the high Bellman surprise of the newly discovered goal sharply increments the information counter 

Nstat , the prior factor Wprior is rapidly attenuated in favor of empirical Bellman targets (Eq. (17)), stabilizing greedy execution without the inter-head variance or conflicting prior biases that can impede policy consolidation in heuristic ensembles. Conversely, standard DQN, BDQN (Azizzadenesheli et al., 2018), and DP-DQN in the Bayesian bootstrap limit ( α → 0; Section 3, Eq. (3)) fail to discover the goal across all seeds (0/10 seeds solved), incurring linear regret ( > 9,900). In this Bayesian bootstrap limit where the prior concentration parameter vanishes ( α → 0), the agent samples purely from empirical replay transitions without synthetic prior augmentation ( Wprior = 0). Because early trajectories observe only zero-reward transitions, pure empirical resampling provides no exploratory optimism toward unvisited states, confirming that the prior base measure F0 is essential for driving deep exploration (Appendices C and D.3). 

Scalability Across DeepSea Grid Size N ∈ [10 , 50] . To evaluate scalability as the exploration horizon expands, we benchmarked DP-DQN across 30 grid sizes up to N = 50 (6 independent random seeds per size, error bars denote ±1 standard deviation; Figure 2b and Table 1). To accommodate the expanding state space (|S| = 2 ,500) and trajectory space (2 50 ≈ 10 15 paths), the action-value network uses two hidden layers of 64 units each (64 × 64 MLP with Layer Normalization). Following standard benchmark convention (Osband et al., 2019a), an environment run is deemed solved when the agent achieves consistent policy convergence, operationalized as maintaining a rolling mean episode return > 0.9 over 20 consecutive episodes (relative to the optimal return 1 .0 − 0.01 = 0 .99), corresponding to the point where cumulative regret permanently flatlines. DP-DQN consistently solves DeepSea-50 in 1 ,925 ± 184 episodes (Table 1). Across grid sizes from 

N = 10 to N = 50, empirical sample complexity scales as O(N 1.45 ), outperforming the empirical O(N 3)scaling of BootDQN-RP (Osband et al., 2018) and remaining well below the Ω(2 N ) lower bound of dithering heuristics. 

Computational and Memory Efficiency. A foundational advantage of DP-DQN is that it eliminates the severe computational overhead of multi-network ensembles. While BootDQN-RP ( K = 20) must instantiate, store, and train K separate action-value networks alongside K prior networks and target networks (O(Kd ) parameters and optimizer buffers), DP-DQN operates on a single active neural network (O(d)parameters), matching the parameter footprint of standard DQN. The non-parametric mechanism introduces two computational overheads relative to standard DQN: (i) sampling Dirichlet process stick-breaking weights requires O(T ) scalar operations once per episode for prior atoms and O(B) per warmstart step for empirical mini-batches, totaling an asymptotic stick-breaking complexity of only O(T + W · B) scalar operations per episode ( O(T +W B ) ≪ W (B +T )d, negligible compared to network tensor arithmetic); and (ii) evaluating the composite loss (Eq. (17)) across the T prior transitions in addition to the empirical mini-batch of size B scales gradient backpropagation by a factor of 1 + T /B . Profiling on canonical DeepSea-20 on a standardized CPU core confirms this efficiency: DP-DQN incurs an amortized execution latency of 0 .43 ms/step (comparable to standard DQN’s 0 .38 ms/step, and 2 .4× faster than BootDQN-RP’s 1 .05 ms/step). Combined with superior sample efficiency (602 ± 104 vs. 5 ,210 ± 604 episodes), DP-DQN converges in only 5 .16 s of total CPU time 11 Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) compared to 103 .26 s for BootDQN-RP—achieving a 20 .0× wall-clock speedup while using 20 × less memory (Table 4, Appendix G). 

Generality Across Diverse Decision Tasks. To evaluate whether the exploratory advantages of DP-BNNs generalize beyond DeepSea, we benchmarked the framework across diverse decision-making tasks (Table 1 and Appendix E). Across deceptive sequential corridors ( N -Chain; Strens 2000), environmental stochasticity and reward traps (RiverSwim-6; Strehl & Littman 2008), continuous control under gravity constraints (MountainCar-v0; Sutton & Barto 1998), and deep contextual bandits (Wheel Bandit; Riquelme et al. 2018; Figure 6), DP-BNN posteriors consistently sustain directed exploration, matching or exceeding competitive multi-network baselines while operating with a single network. Complete experimental protocols and tabular comparisons are detailed in Appendix E. 

6 Conclusions and Future Work 

We introduced Dirichlet Process Bayesian Neural Networks (DP-BNNs), which quantify epistemic uncertainty by placing a nonparametric prior on the data-generating distribution rather than on network weights. We illustrated the application of DP-BNNs to deep reinforcement learning by developing DP-DQN for randomized value functions. By drawing an action-value hypothesis for each episode rollout, DP-DQN realizes temporally consistent Thompson sampling with a single neural network, resolving hard exploration challenges while avoiding the memory and computational overhead of multi-network ensembles. Several promising research directions emerge from this paradigm. On the theoretical side, establishing formal finite-sample regret bounds for DP-DQN that explicitly capture the dependence on the DP prior hyperparameters—namely the concentration parameter α, the base measure F0, and the truncation level 

T —presents a vital avenue of inquiry to rigorously characterize how prior mass and truncation accuracy govern exploration efficiency and asymptotic convergence (Osband & Van Roy, 2017). More broadly, designing structured base measures to incorporate domain-specific inductive biases presents an exciting future direction (Klissarov & Machado, 2023; Machado et al., 2023), which is notoriously difficult to achieve in weight-space Bayesian neural networks (Wilson & Izmailov, 2020). Finally, extending DP-BNN posteriors to continuous actor-critic architectures holds strong practical promise to eliminate the multi-critic ensembles commonly required to mitigate value overestimation or estimate uncertainty (Haarnoja et al., 2018), enabling principled, sample-efficient continuous control with a single neural network.

B.1 Base Measure Support Condition

D Stochastic Optimism and Deep Exploration: DP-BNN vs. Ensembles vs. BDQN 

A foundational question in Bayesian reinforcement learning is whether exploration requires explicit, determin-istic optimism (such as heuristic upper confidence bounds Q + cσ or engineered reward bonuses) or whether it arises naturally from posterior sampling (Osband et al., 2013; Osband & Van Roy, 2017). In this section, we formalize the theory of stochastic optimism, contrast the exploratory mechanisms of randomized prior ensembles (BootDQN-RP) and neural-linear Bayesian Deep Q-Networks (BDQN) against DP-BNNs, and provide empirical ablations across prior base measure geometries. 

D.1 The Principle of Stochastic Optimism in Deep Exploration 

To make the distinction between exploration paradigms mathematically precise, we define both deterministic and stochastic optimism: 

Definition 1 (Deterministic Optimism (Brafman & Tennenholtz, 2002; Auer et al., 2008)) . A value-function estimator ˜Q : S × A → R satisfies deterministic optimism if, for every state-action pair (s, a ) ∈ S × A , the estimate dominates the optimal action-value function: 

˜Q(s, a ) ≥ Q∗(s, a ) with probability 1 (or with high probability ≥ 1 − δ). (33)

Uniform Prior ( U(0 , 1) ). Draws reward atoms uniformly across the support [0 , 1] for all state-action pairs without encoding any target location coordinates (prior mean μ = +0 .5). 

Zero-Mean Standard Normal Prior ( N (0 , 1) ). Uninformative prior with zero expected return everywhere (μ = 0 .0, σ = 1 .0), providing zero target bias or privileged goal information.

DeepSea Scaling Protocol ( N × N Exploration DAG). The empirical scaling and sample complexity results for DeepSea ( N = 10 to N = 50) are presented in the main text (Section 5, Figure 2) and illustrated in Figure 5a. Formally, DeepSea is defined as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) MDP with state space 

S = {(h, c ) : 0 ≤ h < N, 0 ≤ c ≤ h} representing vertical depth h and horizontal position c, with initial state s0 = (0 , 0) and episode length N . At each time step h, the agent chooses between two discrete actions 

A = {0, 1}. Under the standard bsuite protocol (Osband et al., 2019a), an unknown row-specific action mapping hides the true rightward direction behind an independent random permutation per row, preventing heuristic action bias. Stepping rightward advances the agent to ( h + 1 , c + 1) and incurs a minor deterministic step penalty of −0.01 /N ; stepping leftward transitions to ( h + 1 , max(0 , c − 1)) with zero penalty. The environment provides a single positive terminal reward r = +1 .0 located exclusively at the bottom-right corner (N − 1, N − 1), which can only be reached if the agent takes the designated rightward action at every single step along the trajectory. Any single leftward deviation irreversibly commits the agent to zero-reward terminal leaves. Consequently, a uniform random policy discovers the goal with probability exactly (1 /2) N = 2 −N

(9 .5 × 10 −7 for N = 20, and 8 .8 × 10 −16 for N = 50), rendering myopic dithering methods (such as ϵ-greedy and standard DQN) asymptotically incapable of exploration ( O(2 N ) sample complexity). A run is recorded as solved at the earliest episode where the rolling average return over 20 consecutive episodes exceeds 0 .9(matching optimal return 1 .0 − 0.01 = 0 .99), after which cumulative regret flatlines. In contrast, DP-DQN’s non-parametric DP prior generates temporally correlated, optimistic value functions that drive directed exploration straight to the optimal terminal goal, solving DeepSea-20 in 602 ± 104 episodes and DeepSea-50 in 1 ,925 ± 184 episodes (Table 1). 

Deep Exploration on Canonical RiverSwim-6 (Uninformative Prior). The RiverSwim environ-ment (Strehl & Littman, 2008; Osband et al., 2013) tests an agent’s ability to overcome an immediate sub-optimal reward trap in the presence of strong environmental stochasticity (Figure 5b). The MDP consists of N = 6 linearly arranged states S = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, starting at the downstream state s0 = 0 with horizon 

H = 50. The agent has two actions A = {a0, a 1}, where a0 corresponds to swimming downstream (with 26 Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) the current) and a1 corresponds to swimming upstream (against the current). Downstream swimming is deterministic: taking a0 at state s > 0 transitions to s − 1 with probability 1 .0, while taking a0 at s0

stays at s0 and yields an immediate deterministic reward r(0 , a 0) = 0 .05. In contrast, swimming upstream against the current is highly stochastic: from any intermediate state s ∈ { 1, 2, 3, 4}, taking a1 successfully advances to s + 1 with probability 0 .6, remains at s with probability 0 .3, and slips downstream to s − 1with probability 0 .1. At s0, a1 transitions to s1 with probability 0 .6 and remains at s0 with probability 0 .4. At the upstream terminal state s5, swimming right succeeds with probability 0 .6 and yields the maximal terminal reward r(5 , a 1) = 1 .0 (falling back to s4 with probability 0 .4). The minor reward at s0 acts as an exploration trap: because r = 0 .05 is immediately accessible, standard ϵ-greedy exploration quickly reinforces the myopic policy of idling at s0 and fails to reach s5. For DP-DQN, the factorized base measure 

F0(s, a, r, s ′, d ) = F0s(s) × F0a(a) × F0r (r) × F0s′ (s′) × F0d(d) samples discrete states uniformly over the chain ˜s, ˜s′ ∼ Uniform( {0, . . . , 5}), actions ˜ a ∼ Uniform( {a0, a 1}), prior rewards from an uninformative standard normal distribution ˜ r ∼ N (0 , 1.0) everywhere (providing zero target bias or privileged goal information), and continuing terminal flag ˜d = 0. Driven purely by functional posterior variance via stick-breaking draws, single-network DP-DQN (single MLP-20 with LayerNorm) solves RiverSwim-6 across 100% of random seeds in only 27 .4 ± 8.1 episodes, achieving a cumulative return of 474 .2 ± 68 .2 (Table 1). 

Deceptive Exploration on N -Chain. In the deceptive N -Chain benchmark ( N = 10, episode length N ;Figure 5c), an agent must traverse a sequential corridor S = {0, 1, . . . , N − 1} from start state s0 = 0. At state s0, choosing action a0 yields an immediate minor lure reward ( r = 0 .01) but prematurely terminates the episode (or resets to s0). Choosing action a1 advances to state s1 with zero intermediate reward ( r = 0). At intermediate states s ∈ { 1, . . . , N − 2}, choosing a1 advances to s + 1 ( r = 0), while choosing a0 slips backward to s − 1 ( r = 0). Only upon successfully traversing all intermediate states to reach sN −1 does choosing a1 yield the maximal terminal reward ( r = 1 .0). Standard exploration algorithms suffer severe deception because the immediate reward at s0 reinforces the greedy policy of exiting immediately. Furthermore, ensemble methods such as Bootstrapped DQN with randomized priors (Osband et al., 2018) can suffer from bootstrap head collapse when all ensemble heads prematurely fixate on the sub-optimal left branch. In contrast, DP-DQN equips the single network with an exploratory DP base measure on transitions: states are sampled uniformly ˜s ∼ Uniform( {0, . . . , N − 1}), actions ˜ a ∼ Uniform( {a0, a 1}), and next states ˜ s′ = min(˜ s + 1 , N − 1) for ˜a = 1 (and max(˜ s − 1, 0) for ˜ a = 0), with prior rewards ˜ r ∼ N (1 .0, 0.22) for a = 1 and N (0 .0, 0.12) for 

a = 0. This maintains global functional support across value functions and discovers the optimal rightward policy on Episode 1 across all seeds (100% discovery rate), reducing cumulative regret by 77 .5% relative to BootDQN-RP (153 .2 ± 18 .4 vs. 681 .4 ± 92 .6; Table 1).

Absence of Warmstart Steps in Contextual Bandits ( H = 1 ). Action Inference Complexity per Step. At each environment step t, the agent selects an exploratory action. For BootDQN-RP, evaluating the value function requires either querying all K heads to compute posterior voting statistics or indexing the active head and adding the forward evaluation of its randomized prior network, requiring O(K · d) operations. In contrast, DP-DQN requires only a single forward pass through the frozen network θk: at = arg max a Q(st, a ; θk), scaling strictly as O(1 · d) ( O(1) network latency). 

Exploration Complexity: Posterior Sampling vs. Local Dithering. In MDPs requiring deep multi-step exploration over horizon H, exploration efficiency is fundamentally governed by the temporal consistency of the agent’s policy. Local dithering schemes (such as ϵ-greedy in DQN or Gaussian action noise in continuous control) make independent exploratory perturbations at each step. In environments where optimal credit requires an extended sequence of coordinated actions (such as traversing H steps along the diagonal in DeepSea), the probability that independent dithering selects the optimal path by chance decays exponentially as (1 /|A| )H . Consequently, any local dithering heuristic incurs an exponential sample complexity lower bound: Sample Complexity Dithering = Ω (|A| H ) . (40) In stark contrast, by freezing an action-value function sampled from a functional posterior satisfying stochastic optimism ( P(Q ≥ Q∗) ≥ pmin > 0) (Agrawal & Goyal, 2012; Agrawal & Jia, 2017; Osband et al., 2013), posterior sampling achieves provably polynomial sample complexity O(poly( H, |S| , |A| )) in tabular MDPs. DP-DQN preserves this polynomial deep-exploration capability nonparametrically with a single neural network, bypassing both the exponential sample wall of dithering heuristics and the K-fold compute penalty of physical network ensembles. 31
