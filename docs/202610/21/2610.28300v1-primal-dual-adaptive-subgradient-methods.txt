Abstract 

We propose adaptive primal-dual subgradient methods for constrained convex optimization. Our first method applies an AdaGrad-style step-sizes to the dual problem and recovers primal solu-tions by averaging the associated Fenchel-type oracle outputs. It achieves eO(T −1/2) convergence for the dual objective residual, primal objective residual, and feasibility gap in the nonsmooth regime, while automatically improving to O(T −1) when the dual objective is smooth. We fur-ther develop an accelerated variant, which retains the eO(T −1/2) nonsmooth rate while achieving 

O(T −2) convergence in the smooth regime under mild assumptions. These guarantees require no prior knowledge of the underlying regularity. We demonstrate the empirical performance of the proposed methods on optimal transport and semidefinite relaxation of the Max-Cut problem. 

1. Introduction 

Consider convex constrained optimization problems of the form 

min  

> x∈X

f (x) s.t. Ax − b ∈ K , (P) where f : Rp → R ∪ { +∞} is proper, closed, and convex; X ⊆ Rp and K ⊆ Rn are convex and compact sets; A ∈ Rn×p and b ∈ Rn. Primal-dual subgradient methods provide a simple and scalable approach to this class of problems, as their iterations typically rely only on inexpen-sive first-order and Fenchel-type oracle evaluations. Beyond their low per-iteration arithmetic cost, they are also amenable to memory-efficient implementations based on sketching and dimensionality reduction when primal recovery is performed through averaging [20]. A central challenge in practice for subgradient methods is the choice of step-size. Classical convergence guarantees typically rely on a decreasing step-size schedules designed for the worst-case nonsmooth regime. Nonsmooth convex optimization is a broad class however, and individual instances can exhibit substantially more favorable regularity than the worst-case model. Adaptive methods such as AdaGrad [4, 10], instead determine their step-sizes from the observed gradients and can therefore exploit additional smoothness when present, achieving faster convergence rates without prior knowledge of the underlying smoothness degree. This motivates the central question of this paper: 

Can AdaGrad-style step-sizes be used in the dual space while still providing adaptive guarantees for the recovered primal objective residual and feasibility gap? 

Our answer is affirmative, for both basic and accelerated primal-dual constructions. We apply AdaGrad-type step-sizes to the unconstrained dual formulation of (P), while recovering primal iter-

Related works. Duchi et al. [4] introduced AdaGrad, pioneering adaptive step sizes based on accumulated gradient norms. While originally developed for online and stochastic optimization, this mechanism has subsequently been used to obtain universal methods that adapt to different smoothness and noise regimes [10]. In particular, AcceleGrad [11] combines AdaGrad-style adap-tation with acceleration, attaining guarantees for both smooth and nonsmooth convex optimization without prior knowledge of the smoothness regime or its constants. AdaGrad-type adaptation has also been extended to constrained optimization using projection and proximal operations. Kavis et al. [9] proposed UniXGrad for constrained convex optimization, while Bach and Levy [3] developed a universal Mirror-Prox method for variational inequalities. Both methods use extra-gradient-type updates and adapt to smooth and nonsmooth regimes as well as deterministic and stochastic or-acles. Antonakopoulos et al. [2] further developed adaptive extra-gradient methods for min-max optimization and games. Ene et al. [5] developed universal adaptive methods for constrained con-vex optimization based on adaptive projected updates, including accelerated variants, while Wang and Yurtsever [13] developed universal adaptive methods for composite objectives. In contrast, our methods address constrained problems through their unconstrained dual and require only Fenchel-type oracles, rather than projections onto or proximal updates over the constraint set. A complementary approach to universality originates with Nesterov [12]. His universal gradient methods adapt to unknown H¨ older smoothness regime and constants through an inexact line-search strategy. Yurtsever et al. [17] extended this approach to constrained convex optimization using a primal-dual framework with Fenchel-type oracles, providing guarantees for both the objective residual and feasibility gap. Their methods, however, rely on an inexact line-search strategy that requires objective evaluations and can be costly or impractical when such evaluations are expen-sive. Moreover, they require a target accuracy to be specified in advance and converge only to a neighborhood determined by this accuracy, making it difficult to run them to arbitrary accuracy without prior knowledge of the convergence regime. Our methods retain the same primal-dual ora-cle framework while avoiding line searches, objective evaluations, and a prescribed target accuracy, and adapt automatically to the underlying smoothness regime.

3. Experiments 

We compare our methods with HCGM [18] and CGAL [19], using its decreasing-step (dcr) and fixed-step (cst) variants. These methods similarly rely on inexpensive Fenchel-type oracle eval-uations. UniPD and AccUniPD [17] are omitted because they require a target accuracy ε in ad-vance. For each baseline, we tune its single parameter over powers of 10 . Since each method performs one Fenchel-type oracle evaluation per iteration, their per-iteration computational costs are comparable. We therefore report performance against the number of iterations. The experi-mental code and data are publicly available at https://github.com/KarthikPrakhya/ adapd-experiments .

4. Conclusion 

We introduced adaptive primal-dual subgradient methods that achieve optimal convergence guaran-tees for both dual and the recovered primal solutions, automatically adapting between nonsmooth and smooth regimes of the dual space. Our numerical experiments demonstrate the potential of the proposed methods. This is ongoing work, and promising directions include diagonal and matrix-valued adaptive step-sizes, which may better exploit the geometry of ill-conditioned dual problems. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS
