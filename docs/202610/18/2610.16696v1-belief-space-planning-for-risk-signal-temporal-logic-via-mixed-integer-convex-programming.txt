# Belief Space Planning for Risk Signal Temporal Logic via Mixed-Integer Convex Programming 

Shaohang Han, Joris Verhagen, Kaier Liang, and Jana Tumova 

Abstract — Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifies robot tasks with spatio-temporal requirements, and its robustness semantics allow planning by trajectory optimization. In practice, robots estimate their state from noisy measurements, and when the information gained from a measurement depends on the state, the robot can actively reduce its uncertainty by planning in the belief space. The resulting problem is, however, generally non-convex, for which gradient-based methods are neither complete nor globally optimal. In this paper, we consider Risk STL (RiSTL) planning in Gaussian belief space and show that an information-form parameterization of the belief, together with a convex relaxation of the belief dynamics, yields a mixed-integer convex program (MICP). We prove that the MICP is sound and complete, and that the relaxation preserves the globally optimal input trajectories. We validate the effectiveness of our method in numerical simulations. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Complex robot tasks with spatio-temporal requirements can be specified in Signal Temporal Logic (STL) [1]. For example, we may require an underwater robot to reach each inspection target within 60 seconds and hover there for 10 

seconds, to come to the surface for a GPS fix between minutes three and four, and to slow down whenever it is close to a target . Beyond specifying these requirements, STL also admits quantitative semantics through the notion of 

robustness , which measures the degree to which a trajectory satisfies a specification [2]. This allows us to formulate a trajectory optimization problem that maximizes robustness against spatial perturbations, which is a common approach to STL planning. A common assumption in STL planning relies on perfect knowledge of the state, but real-world robots estimate their state from noisy and incomplete sensor measurements. An effective approach is Bayesian filtering, which maintains a 

belief : a probability distribution over possible states [3]. Several extensions of STL quantify this uncertainty and synthesize motion plans with satisfaction guarantees [4]–[7]. In these methods, the uncertainty evolves independently of the plan. In many settings, however, the information gained from a measurement depends on the state, and the robot can 

actively reduce its uncertainty by moving to where its sensors are informative. Capturing this coupling requires planning over the belief dynamics, with the uncertainty itself as a

Existing belief space temporal logic planning finds satis-fying trajectories by search over finite abstractions [8]–[10], and does not optimize robustness. In contrast, [11] follows the trajectory optimization formulation of STL and lifts the risk predicates of RiSTL [5] to the Gaussian belief space, where the belief is represented by its mean and covariance. It maximizes robustness directly in the continuous belief space. The resulting problem is, however, a non-convex nonlinear program (NLP) solved with gradient-based methods, which optimize a smooth reformulation of the robustness [12], [13]. Such solvers are sound but neither complete nor globally optimal. Depending on the initialization, they may converge to a poor local optimum, or fail to find a feasible solution even when one exists. In state space, for linear systems with linear predicates, mixed-integer encodings avoid this limitation. They represent the logical and temporal operators using binary variables [2], [14], and result in a mixed-integer convex program (MICP) that is sound, complete, and solvable to global optimality. In belief space, however, the problem remains non-convex even when dynamics, measurements, and predicates are all linear. There are two reasons. First, the covariance evolves through a nonlinear update. Second, the risk predicates depend nonlinearly on the covariance. Our key finding is that both challenges can be addressed by parameterizing the belief in information form [3], which replaces the covariance by its inverse, the precision matrix. This parameterization allows us to turn the risk predicates into convex constraints. When the measurement information is a piecewise concave function of the mean, we show that the belief dynamics admit a convex relaxation that preserves the optimal input trajectories. The planning problem then becomes an MICP, which can be solved with off-the-shelf solvers. Our contributions are threefold. First, we derive an MICP for RiSTL planning in Gaussian belief space, based on the information-form parameterization and a convex relaxation of the belief dynamics. Second, we prove that our method is sound, complete, and globally optimal. Third, our simulation experiments demonstrate that the planner achieves meaning-ful spatio-temporal tasks over polygonal regions, and that information gathering emerges from the specification, as the robot detours toward informative regions to satisfy RiSTL predicates without an explicit exploration objective. arXiv:2610.16696v1 [cs.RO] 12 Oct 2026 II. B. Implementation Details 

In general, the block constraint (17) and the predicate constraints for (11) and (12) are linear matrix inequalities in Λt, so (20) is a mixed-integer semidefinite program (MISDP). Since At, Wt, Λinit , and It are diagonal in our setup, the belief dynamics (8) preserve diagonal precision matrices, and we restrict Λt in (20) accordingly. The block constraint (17) and the predicate constraints for (11), (12), and (13) then admit second-order cone (SOC) representa-tions [19], so (20) becomes a mixed-integer second-order cone program (MISOCP). We encode the SOC constraints as convex quadratic constraints and the selection of predicate constraints in Cφ with big-M constants. We set α = 0 .01 

and solve the MISOCP with Gurobi [20] on an Intel Core i7-13700H laptop. We then propagate (8) with the optimal inputs and report the robustness of the exact belief trajectory. As a baseline, we solve Problem 1 as an NLP following [11], with the smooth robustness of [12] and IPOPT [21] through Drake [22]. We use eight restarts initialized with RRT* paths to the goal regions and their perturbations, and report the best local optimum.

We showed that RiSTL planning in Gaussian belief space can be formulated as an MICP. Parameterizing the belief in information form makes the risk predicates convex, and relaxing the belief dynamics to a convex constraint preserves the feasible and globally optimal input trajectories. The planner is therefore sound, complete, and globally optimal.
