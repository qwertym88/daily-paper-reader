# Connection between MP and DPP for Mean-Field Stochastic Optimal Control Problems 

## Guangzhi Cui 1 Tianyang Nie 1,* Guangchen Wang 2

Abstract 

This paper deals with the optimal control problem for the general mean-field stochastic differential equations, in which the coefficients depend on both the state process as well as on its law. We focus on the connection between the general maximum principle and the dynamic programming principle for such a control problem. We establish the relationship between the value function of the mean-field optimal control problem and the adjoint equations in the maximum principle. We also give a general mean-field stochastic verification theorem. Some examples are solved explicitly to illustrate our results. 

MSC: 49K45, 49L20, 60H10, 93E20. 

Keywords: Mean-field optimal control, Maximum principle, Dynamic programming principle, Verifica-tion theorem. 

# 1 Introduction 

There are generally two methods to study the optimal control problem, one is the Pontryagin’s maximum principle (MP), the other one is the dynamic programming principle (DPP). The connection between MP and DPP of the deterministic optimal control problem can be found in Fleming and Rishel [20]. They considered the classical case that the value function is smooth, and obtained that ψ(t) = −Vx(t, ¯x(t)) and 

Vt(t, ¯x(t)) = H(t, ¯x(t), ¯u(t), ψ (t)), where ψ, H, V , ¯ u(t) and ¯ x(t) represent adjoint variable, Hamiltonian function, value function, optimal control and optimal state respectively. In the framework of the viscosity solutions, Barron and Jensen [3] first used the viscosity solution to derive the MP from DPP. Zhou [38] further employed the notions of subdifferential and supdifferential to reveal their relationship. For stochastic optimal control problems, Bensoussan [4], Yong and Zhou [36] described the connection between DPP and MP for the classical case, and Zhou [37, 39] further elaborated the relevant results under the framework of viscosity solutions. For stochastic recursive optimal control problem, where the cost functional is described by the solution to a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE), we revealed the connection between DPP and MP both under classical framework and viscosity solution framework in our paper [31]. In this paper, we consider a class of stochastic control problems for the state processes governed by general McKean-Vlasov equations (also known as mean-field stochastic differential equations) in which the coefficients depend nonlinearly on both the state process and its law in the following form:

The McKean-Vlasov equations arise naturally when one tries to describe the behavior of many symmetric agents that interact through an empirical distribution of their states. Furthermore, mean-field games arise when the concept of Nash’s non-cooperative equilibrium is employed, while mean-field control problems are related to Pareto optimality where players can be identified with a single “representative agent”. Indeed, these two problems have subtle differences, which were thoroughly investigated by Carmona, Delarue and Lachapelle [14]. It’s worth noting that Bensoussan, Frehse and Yam [5], Carmona and Delarue [13] provide an overview of recent developments on these two issues. Our goal is to study the optimal control of McKean-Vlasov equations and to establish the connection between MP and DPP. As is well known, there is much literature about the optimal control problem of McKean-Vlasov equations solved through DPP or MP in recent years. For example, Andersson and Djehiche [1], and Buckdahn, Djehiche and Li [9] studied the MP for the mean-field optimal control problem where the state and the cost functional depend on the expectation of the state process, respectively, with convex control domain and with non-convex control domain. Then, Buckdahn, Li and Ma [10] extended the results to the general case with non-convex control domain, where the coefficients depend on the law of the state process rather than its expectation, and obtained the corresponding MP. Recently, we studied mean-field partial information non-zero sum stochastic differential games and obtained the MP by modifying the related second-order adjoint equation in our paper [32]. For DPP method, due to the nonlinear dependency with respect to the law of the process, the problem is actually time-inconsistent, and Bellman’s optimality principle does not hold. However, although the problem itself is time-inconsistent, some form of DPP can be restored by changing the state space to density function space or probability measure space. Lauri´ ere and Pironneau [27] first implemented the idea by assuming the existence at all times of a density for the marginal distribution of the state process and reformulated the problem as a density control problem with a family of deterministic controls. Later, Bensoussan, Frehse and Yam [5, 6, 7] developed this method further to investigate the well-posedness of the master equation. Under this reformulation they managed to prove a DPP and deduce a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation in the space of density functions. Without the density assumption, most of the current research took the distribution of state process as the variable of value function to achieve the DPP, see e.g., Pham and Wei [34], Cosso and Pham [17]. In the case with common noise, Pham and Wei [33] proved a DPP for the case where the control process is adapted to the common noise filtration. Then, Djete, Possama¨ ı and Tan [18] investigated the path-dependent mean-field optimal control problem and established the DPP under both strong and weak formulations. Later, Cosso et al. [15] proved the DPP in the infinite-dimensional, non-Markov case. Due to the different technologies used in the MP and DPP for the mean-field optimal control prob-lem, their research and development are relatively independent. There is little research on the connection between the two approaches. To our knowledge, only Carmona and Delarue used some connection in the heuristic sense between DPP and MP of the mean-field optimal control problem in their monograph [13], where they assumed that the diffusion term σ does not depend on the control. Through a combination of probabilistic and analytic approaches, they transformed the mean-field control problem into a stan-dard stochastic control problem by introducing nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equation and the functional derivative. Remark 3.8. In the mean-field control and mean-field games theory, the well-posedness of classical solu-tion to HJB equations and master equations remains a challenging problem. The key results can be found in [2, 8, 21, 30]. It should be noted that the classical solution of HJB equation requires sufficient smooth-ness of the value function, so it is more practical to introduce the viscosity solution. For the definition and well-posedness of the viscosity solution to HJB equations on the space of probability measures, one can refer to [16, 17, 33, 34, 35].

# 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we develop the connection between the MP and DPP for general mean-field optimal control problems. We emphasize that the diffusion term of the state equation in this paper can depend on the control. We establish the relationship between the value function and the first- and second-order adjoint equations via the BSDE comparison theorem, and obtain the general verification theorem. As shown in Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, we need to assume that the value function has sufficient smoothness, which is not always satisfied. Hence, in the future, on the one hand, we hope to study the well-posedness of classical solution to HJB equation (3.6). On the other hand, we shall investigate the nonsmooth versions of the connection between the MP and DPP.
