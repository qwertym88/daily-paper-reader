# Embodiment-Aware Inference-Time Search for Human Reaction Selection in Humanoid Retargeting 

Yongpeng Cao 1,∗, Masahiro Hirano 1, and Yuji Yamakawa 2

{cao, mhirano, y-ymkw }@iis.u-tokyo.ac.jp 

Abstract — Training humanoid robots to learn human be-haviors requires expensive data collection, and human–robot interaction data can be even more difficult and costly to collect. Training a specialized reaction model for each humanoid embodiment can also be time-consuming and resource-intensive. Human-to-humanoid motion retargeting provides a more scal-able alternative, allowing human–human reaction data to serve as a common motion representation for different humanoid embodiments. However, due to morphological and kinematic differences between humans and humanoid robots, a reaction preferred in human motion space may not be the most suitable one for execution on a particular robot embodiment. In this work, we propose an inference-time reaction search framework that leverages stochastic sampling from a gener-ative reaction model and jointly considers reaction quality and humanoid embodiment feasibility. Given the observed interaction, our framework generates multiple plausible re-action candidates. Each candidate is retargeted to the tar-get humanoid and evaluated using complementary interaction and embodiment-specific feasibility criteria, allowing candi-date selection under the physical constraints of the target embodiment. Since the reaction generator remains frozen, the framework can adapt to different humanoid embod-iments at inference time without retraining. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework maintains overall human reaction quality while improving the feasi-bility of retargeted humanoid reactions across different em-bodiments. The proposed pipeline improves humanoid re-targeting feasibility by 1.61% . Moreover, human-space and G1 best-candidate selections disagree in 78 .98% of samples, demonstrating substantial embodiment dependence. Code will be released at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ Runtime-Humanoid-Reaction-Searching-2BF2 .

I. I NTRODUCTION 

As humanoid robots move beyond structured industrial settings into human-centered environments, the ability to interact naturally and responsively with people becomes a key challenge. To achieve this, humanoids must be able to generate plausible reactions to human actions. However, training humanoids to learn human behaviors requires ex-pensive data collection, while human–robot interaction data can be even more complex and costly to collect. Training a specialized reaction model for each humanoid embodiment can therefore be time-consuming and resource-intensive. Human motion data, including reactions collected from human–human interactions, are comparatively more acces-sible and scalable to collect and have been used as motion

Existing works leverage human motion data to train humanoid behaviors by adapting human motions to the target humanoid and subsequently learning control policies from the resulting motions [1], [2]. Human motion can therefore serve as a common intermediate representation that can be transferred to different humanoid embodiments. However, due to morphological and kinematic differences between the human body and humanoid robots, a motion that is plausible as a human reaction may not necessarily be the most suitable one for retargeting and execution on a particular embodiment. Consequently, the reaction preferred in human-motion space and the reaction most suitable after humanoid retargeting may correspond to different candidates. For example, differences in limb proportions and joint ranges can make a human reaction easy to reproduce on one humanoid but difficult on another. Different humanoid em-bodiments may therefore prefer different reaction candidates according to their embodiment-specific constraints. Human reactions are inherently one-to-many: given the same observed interaction, multiple future reactions can be plausible. Stochastic generative models can capture these plausible outcomes and produce diverse reaction candidates through inference-time sampling. We exploit this capability by sampling multiple reaction candidates from a frozen generator and evaluating them after retargeting according to the requirements of the target embodiment. Nevertheless, inference-time selection among reaction candidates based on their suitability after humanoid retargeting remains relatively underexplored. In this work, we propose an inference-time reaction search framework that jointly considers human reaction quality and humanoid embodiment feasibility. Given an observed inter-action, our framework generates multiple causal reaction can-didates, retargets each candidate to the target humanoid, and evaluates them using complementary interaction-preservation and embodiment-specific feasibility criteria. Rather than treating retargeting as a downstream post-processing step, we incorporate post-retargeting evaluation into reaction se-lection. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework maintains overall human-reaction quality while improving the feasibility of retargeted humanoid reactions across different embodiments, including a 1.61% retargeting feasibility gain on the Inter-X test set. A. Human Interaction and Reaction Generation 

Human–human reaction generation has received increasing attention as a critical extension of human interaction synthe-sis, where the interaction of multiple people must remain spatially, temporally, and semantically coordinated. Recent approaches such as InterGen [3] and InterMask [4] ex-plore joint interaction generation, including reactions, using DiT and VQ-VAE architectures. Human–human interaction datasets such as InterHuman [3] and Inter-X [5] further provide diverse motions and benchmarks for learning and evaluating interactive motion generation. A topic more closely related to our work is action-to-reaction generation, where the motion of one agent is gener-ated conditionally on the motion of another. InterFormer [6] introduces an interaction-aware formulation for human re-action generation. ReGenNet [7] further achieves online reaction generation without access to future motion. Recent methods such as Think-Then-React [8] introduce LLM-based semantic reasoning to generate appropriate reactions. Ready-to-React [9], ARMFlow [10], and ReMoGen [11] explore real-time online reaction synthesis using efficient models and training strategies. Human-X [12] extends reaction genera-tion with physically plausible humanoid reaction synthesis. Despite these advances in model architectures and training strategies, most existing methods adopt a fixed inference procedure. Comparatively less attention has been paid to inference-time search to refine the generated reactions. In this work, we instead take the pretrained reaction generator as a proposal model and select among multiple candidate reactions based on embodiment requirements. 

B. Human-to-Humanoid Motion Retargeting 

Learning generalizable humanoid behaviors typically re-quires large and diverse motion datasets. Collecting robot-native motion trajectories at such scale, however, remains substantially more expensive than obtaining human motion data. Consequently, retargeting existing human motion data to humanoids has become a labor-saving and promising approach for constructing training references for humanoid control. Existing retargeting methods address the human– humanoid embodiment gap using optimization-based and learning-based hybrid approaches. GMR [13] converts human motions into robot joint configurations while explicitly considering the target humanoid morphology. NMR [14] instead learns the temporal human-to-humanoid mapping with optimization-based methods to reduce artifacts. Other methods such as CoRe [15] and Human2Humanoid [16] further incorporate morphology constraints, contact, and unified representations to improve cross-embodiment transfer. Following retargeting, physics-based tracking frameworks such as ProtoMotions [17] and TWIST [18] use retar-geted human motion as reference trajectories to learn physi-cally valid humanoid behaviors. Interactive systems such as Human-X also condition the tracking policy on the observed actor to adapt to deviations in the actual interaction. These approaches primarily address how a given hu-man reference should be transferred to a target humanoid. However, when there are multiple possible reactions and a reaction generator can provide multiple plausible human reactions, the choice of the reference reaction itself becomes an additional degree of freedom. Our work therefore con-siders reaction selection jointly with humanoid embodiment compatibility rather than only retargeting a predetermined re-action. This formulation allows the system to select a reaction that simultaneously preserves the intended interaction and provides a more favorable reference for humanoid execution. 

C. Inference-Time Search and Embodiment-Aware Motion Selection 

Inference-time sampling and optimization complement learning-based methods to improve model performance. In human motion generation, DNO [19] optimizes the noise input of a frozen diffusion model based on task-specific objectives, while PhysDiff [20] guides the diffusion process by introducing physics-based projection to adapt to practical scenarios. Diffusion-ES [21] uses generated trajectory pro-posals for repeated evaluation and refinement, and sampling-based methods evaluate candidate trajectories according to related task costs. PointWorld [22] similarly evaluates future states to select and refine robot action candidates. Recent humanoid control frameworks such as BeyondMimic [23] further demonstrate that task-specific guidance can be ap-plied at inference time to adapt motion behaviors to different tasks without retraining the generation model for each new task. These methods demonstrate that additional inference-time computation can improve the task suitability of motions. Re-action generation allows multiple plausible reactions, while retargeting imposes embodiment-dependent constraints on which reactions are suitable for robot execution. However, existing motion-search and guidance methods generally op-timize motions directly in human or task space, while humanoid retargeting approaches usually assume that the human reference trajectory has already been selected. The integration of these two components remains underexplored. Our work addresses this gap through an inference-time search framework that samples multiple causal human reac-tions from a frozen generator, retargets each candidate to the target humanoid, and selects among them using robot-side kinematic and interaction criteria. This formulation allows reaction selection to depend on the target embodiment while leaving the human reaction generator unchanged. III. M ETHODOLOGY

The key idea is to use the reaction generator as a proposal generator: it first generates diverse candidate reactions, while embodiment-aware criteria determine which candidate provides a more suitable motion reference for the target humanoid without degrading the intended interaction semantics. A. Experimental Setup 

In our experiments, we evaluate the proposed inference-time search framework on the Inter-X benchmark [5], which provides a diverse set of human–human interactions. We compare our pipeline against the ReMoGen single-sample generation baseline and evaluate both human reaction qual-ity and humanoid retargeting feasibility. In addition, we conduct ablation studies to analyze the contributions of different components in our framework. We also evaluate the inference-time search framework’s generalization across different humanoid embodiments, retargeting models, and human interaction datasets. Unless otherwise specified, the ReMoGen generator is frozen and K = 16 candidates are sampled at inference time, with candidate 0 serving as the single-sample baseline. GMR and Unitree G1 [26] are used as the primary retargeter and embodiment, respectively. Main comparisons are reported on the official test split, whereas parameter selection, ablations, and diagnostic analyses are performed on the validation split. B. Results

Starting from independently sampled ReMoGen candidates, the highest-ranked candidates satisfying the semantic constraint are used as perturbation centers, and perturbations are applied to their ReMoGen latent representations. Previously generated candidates are retained at each stage to preserve promising candidates discovered in each stage, and perturbation cen-ters are selected from the aggregated candidate pool. The one-stage variant starts from 32 independent samples and generates 8 perturbations for each of the top 4 candidates, resulting in 64 candidates in total. The two-stage variant starts from 16 independent samples, adds 8 perturbations to each of the top 4 candidates in the first stage, and then adds 8 perturbations to each of the top 2 candidates selected from the candidate pool, resulting in 64 candidates. We further evaluate a four-round strategy that starts from 32 independent samples, with 8 perturbations applied to the top 4, top 4, top 2, and top 2 candidates, respectively, resulting in 128 candidates in total. Each variant is compared with candidate 0. The results show that iterative refinement consistently improves robot-side optimization over matched-budget flat sampling, indicating that refining promising candidates can exploit additional search headroom. D. Practical Evaluation 

For the practical deployment of the proposed inference-time search framework, we evaluate the independent physics tracking and runtime performance. Here we use TWIST [18] as the physics tracking tool to evaluate the retargeted hu-manoid motion. The results are summarized in Table VI. We observe that the proposed method maintains comparable tracking success and root RMSE while slightly improving joint RMSE and wrist RMSE.
