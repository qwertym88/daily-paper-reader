Policy representations in reinforcement learning have expanded beyond Gaussian policies to diffusion and flow-matching based generative policies. However, existing RL methods remain representation-specific, lacking a unified optimization interface for emerging generative policies. In particular, extending likelihood-based objectives to multi-step generative policies couples optimization to their internal generation process, where accumulated likelihood ratios can weaken the update signal. Meanwhile, generative-policy-specific methods often rely on multi-candidate generation and critic-based selection, improving overall return while providing limited improvement to the policy itself. We introduce DR-APO (Decoupled-Representation Advantage-Guided Policy Optimization) , which decouples policy improvement from policy representation through advantage-guided target improvement and 

representation-compatible direct action fitting , providing a shared optimization principle for Gaussian, diffusion, and flow policies. Across five MuJoCo-v5 tasks, DR-APO remains competitive with SAC for Gaussian policies and achieves the best aggregate policy-only performance for both diffusion and flow-matching policies. NavDP post-training improves the Success Rate (SR) from 70.33% to 89.55% in simulation and from 25% to 65% in real-world navigation. These results demonstrate a unified policy-improvement principle across Gaussian, diffusion, and flow policies, from continuous-control learning to embodied-policy post-training. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Mainstream RL policy-optimization methods are largely developed for specific policy representations, most notably Gaussian policies. Recent advances in diffusion and flow matching [1], [2] have enabled expressive generative policies, creating a growing need for RL methods that generalize across heterogeneous policy representations. However, existing opti-mization interfaces remain representation-specific and transfer poorly to multi-step generative policies. One line of work extends likelihood-based optimization to generative policies. While PPO-style likelihood ratios are straightforward for Gaussian policies [3], diffusion and flow policies require optimization over their internal generation processes [4]–[7], coupling policy updates to multi-step generation and likelihood-ratio aggregation. Another line exploits generative sampling to produce mul-tiple candidates for critic-based evaluation and selection [8]– [10]. Although effective, execution performance can depend on inference-time critic selection. These limitations moti-vate a unified policy-optimization framework that improves the underlying policy across representations without explicit policy-likelihood evaluation or inference-time multi-candidate selection. We introduce DR-APO (Decoupled-Representation Advantage-Guided Policy Optimization) , a unified framework that decouples policy improvement from the underlying policy representation. DR-APO consists of 

representation-decoupled policy optimization , advantage-guided target improvement , and representation-compatible direct action fitting . It first reformulates policy improvement as target optimization independent of policy parameterization, then constructs locally improved targets from advantage information, and finally realizes these targets through representation-compatible fitting. This enables a unified policy-improvement principle across Gaussian, diffusion, and flow policies without relying on likelihood-ratio actor updates or inference-time multi-candidate critic selection. We evaluate DR-APO on continuous control and closed-loop embodied navigation. Across five MuJoCo-v5 tasks, DR-APO remains competitive with Gaussian policies, achieves the best result in 9/10 diffusion comparisons, and ranks first in all 10 /10 flow comparisons, without inference-time multi-candidate critic selection. DR-APO further improves pretrained NavDP navigation success from 70 .33% to 89 .55% 

in simulation and from 25% to 65% on a DEEP Robotics M20 quadruped robot, a real-world gain of 40 percentage points. Our contributions are threefold:

A. Conventional Policy Optimization 

Continuous-control policy optimization has been extensively studied with parametric actors. TRPO and PPO optimize stochastic policies through trust-region or clipped likelihood-ratio objectives [3], [11], whereas DDPG and TD3 update deterministic actors through learned critic gradients [12], [13]. SAC combines off-policy value learning with maximum-entropy optimization [14], while MPO and AWR per-form non-parametric improvement, projection, or advantage-weighted regression [15], [16]. Related weighted-fitting methods include RWR, AWAC, and CRR [17]–[19]. These paradigms are effective for conventional policies, but are generally developed around specific policy representations. 

B. Generative Policy Optimization 

Diffusion and flow models provide expressive multimodal policies for actions and trajectories [1], [2], [20]–[22]. Their iterative generation processes, however, make conventional optimization interfaces difficult to transfer directly. Exist-ing methods mainly address this mismatch through internal-process policy optimization, value-guided learning or selec-tion, and critic-driven action refinement. One line adapts policy-gradient optimization to the genera-tive process. DPPO treats diffusion denoising as an auxiliary decision process [4], while ReinFlow and πRL introduce stochastic formulations for flow policies [6], [7]. FPO de-velops a PPO-style optimization interface for flow matching [23], while GenPO and ADPO further study on-policy or adaptive optimization of diffusion policies [24], [25]. These methods remain coupled to representation-specific denoising, flow dynamics, or surrogate objectives. Another line incorporates learned value information into policy learning or execution. QVPO and RFM combine gen-erative policies with critic-guided candidate selection [8], [10], while other approaches inject critic information through score matching, reverse diffusion, or value-reweighted objec-tives [5], [26], [27]. When multiple candidates are evaluated by a critic, execution performance additionally depends on inference-time selection. Critic information can also refine actions before policy fitting. DIPO applies multi-step Q-gradient ascent before re-training a diffusion policy [28], while PA-RL combines critic-based reranking, local action optimization, and policy distil-lation [29]. Related flow-based methods transfer critic guid-ance into native vector-field objectives [30]. Repeated critic-gradient refinement can induce substantial target shifts, making the resulting supervision sensitive to critic estimation errors. Overall, existing approaches remain tied to representation-specific optimization, inference-time critic selection, or critic-driven action refinement. A shared policy-improvement mech-anism across heterogeneous policy representations remains less explored. 

C. Embodied Generative Policies and Post-Training 

Generative policies are increasingly used in embodied con-trol and navigation. Diffusion Policy applies diffusion models to visuomotor control [1], while NavDP generates multi-modal local trajectories for visual navigation [31]. Recent work further improves pretrained navigation policies through interaction, including reward-guided self-imitation [32], Q-reweighted post-training [33], online imitation [34], and GRPO-based fine-tuning [35]. These studies demonstrate the effectiveness of embodied-policy post-training and motivate evaluating whether a general policy-improvement framework can transfer from continuous control to pretrained embodied policies. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

We evaluate DR-APO across Gaussian, diffusion, and flow policies on five MuJoCo-v5 tasks, ablate its key components, and assess NavDP post-training in simulated and real-world navigation. 

A. Experimental Setup a) Continuous control: We train policies from scratch for 

1M steps on five MuJoCo-v5 environments [38]: HalfCheetah (HC), Hopper (Hop), Walker2d (W2), Swimmer (Swi), and Humanoid (Hum). Policies are evaluated every 20 k steps over five random seeds. Gaussian policies are compared with PPO and SAC; diffusion policies with AWR, FPO, QVPO, and LSEVPO; and flow policies with FPO, Flow-SDE, ReinFlow, and RFM. B. Cross-Representation Policy Learning 

Fig. 3 and Table I summarize the results across five MuJoCo-v5 environments. Under Gaussian policies, DR-APO achieves the best average AUC@1M rank while remaining competitive with SAC in Final Return. For diffusion policies, DR-APO achieves the highest mean AUC@1M on all five tasks and the highest mean Final Return on four, leading in 9 of the 10 environment–metric comparisons. For flow policies, DR-APO ranks first on both metrics across all five tasks. These results show that the same target-level policy-improvement rule transfers across Gaussian, diffusion, and flow representations without requiring representation-specific likelihood optimization over their internal generation pro-cesses. Moreover, all DR-APO results are obtained by di-rect policy execution, without inference-time multi-candidate critic selection. Together, the results support representation-decoupled policy improvement while retaining strong within-family performance for both generative policy classes.

For flow policies, DR-APO further outperforms RFM Best-of-32 on four of the five tasks, with HalfCheetah being the exception. For diffusion policies, the comparison is task dependent: DR-APO surpasses LSEVPO Best-of-64 on Hopper, Walker2d, and Swimmer, while trailing it on HalfCheetah and Humanoid.

Native Only fits refined clean actions through the native diffusion/flow objectives, whereas Direct Only supervises behavior-anchored action readouts toward the same targets. V. C ONCLUSION 

DR-APO decouples advantage-guided improvement from action fitting without likelihood-ratio updates or inference-time critic selection. On five MuJoCo-v5 tasks, it is com-petitive with Gaussian SAC and achieves the best aggregate policy-only results for diffusion and flow policies.
