Abstract 

Risk-sensitive reinforcement learning (RL) addresses decision-making under uncertainty and adverse outcomes. Learning risk-sensitive policies from fixed datasets is important when additional interaction with the environment is costly. Existing work on risk-sensitive RL focuses on online settings or specific risk measures, leaving a general theoretical under-standing of its offline setting underdeveloped. In this work, we investigate efficient offline risk-sensitive RL using the optimized certainty equivalent (OCE), a general class of risk measures. We consider two formulations: dynamic-OCE RL, which applies OCE recursively at each step, and static-OCE RL, which applies a single OCE to the cumulative rewards. Under the tabular setting, we develop pessimistic value-iteration algorithms for both for-mulations and establish tight sublinear suboptimality bounds, explicitly capturing how risk sensitivity, encoded by utility functions, affects learning difficulty. Moreover, we establish minimax suboptimality lower bounds to characterize the problem’s fundamental limits. To handle large state spaces, we further develop pessimistic least-squares value-iteration algo-rithms that incorporate the general OCE risk criterion into linear function approximation. We establish utility-dependent sublinear guarantees with explicit dependence on the fea-ture dimension d. To our knowledge, we provide the first offline algorithms along with the first upper and lower suboptimality bounds for both dynamic- and static-OCE RL. 

Keywords: offline reinforcement learning, risk-sensitive reinforcement learning, opti-mized certainty equivalent, general risk measures, function approximation

1 Introduction 

Classical reinforcement learning (RL) aims at maximizing the expected cumulative reward by optimizing the policy in the unknown environment through learning (Sutton et al., 1998), typically modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) (Puterman, 2014). How-ever, in many real-world applications, agents are often required to manage the trade-off between risk and reward. This has motivated the development of risk-sensitive reinforce-ment learning (risk-sensitive RL) frameworks, which incorporate various risk measures into the learning objective. Risk-sensitive RL is widely used by a variety of risk-sensitive in-dustries, ranging from finance (Hambly et al., 2023), self-driving (Kamran et al., 2020), to wireless networks (Khalifa et al., 2019). In risk-sensitive RL, the agent aims to opti-mize certain risk-sensitive reward metrics such as mean-variance risk measure (Sood et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2022), entropic risk (Hau et al., 2023), and conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) (Hakobyan et al., 2019). Despite the progress in risk-sensitive RL, the majority of existing works are confined to the online setting, in which the agent learns through ongoing interactions with the environment, simultaneously balancing exploration and exploitation. However, the risk-sensitive nature of real-world applications makes this process costly, mo-tivating a line of work on offline risk-sensitive RL (Ma et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024), in which the agent only has access to a pre-collected dataset and cannot further interact with the environment. Different risk measures are tailored to different applications and scenarios. Therefore, it is crucial to develop a general framework that can flexibly incorporate a broad class of risk measures. However, existing offline risk-sensitive RL studies often focus on a single risk measure, and there is no algorithm that is provably efficient for offline risk-sensitive RL with general risk measures. In recent years, the optimized certainty equivalent (OCE) framework, introduced by Ben-Tal and Teboulle (2007), has emerged as a suitable candidate for risk-sensitive RL research due to its ability to unify commonly used risk measures such as CVaR, entropic risk measure, and mean-variance. Previous research on risk-sensitive RL can be broadly categorized into two paradigms based on how the risk measure is incorporated. The first paradigm introduces the risk measure at each step of the decision process, as in Chen et al. (2024) and Xu et al. (2023). This approach integrates risk locally at every stage of learning, leading to a modified Bellman equation and resulting in a dynamic formulation of the objective. And the second paradigm formulates risk-sensitive RL as a two-stage optimization problem, where the reinforcement learning component acts as a subroutine within a larger risk-aware objective, such as in Wang et al. (2023) and Wang et al. (2024). This static perspective evaluates risk based on the total return, rather than stage-wise rewards, and facilitates the use of history-dependent policies, offering greater flexibility in algorithm design. Although these works are sufficiently general in terms of risk metrics considered, they only consider the online setting, with little guidance on how to best utilize a pre-collected dataset. The gap in research highlights an intriguing question: 

Can we design offline risk-sensitive RL algorithms that are provably efficient for the general OCE risk measure? 

Answering this question requires addressing four challenges. First, while pessimism is well understood in risk-neutral offline RL (Levine et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2021;

Rashidinejad et al., 2021; Nguyen-Tang et al., 2023), it is unclear how pessimistic esti-mators can be constructed in offline risk-sensitive RL with general OCE risk measures, as earlier research relied on the mathematical properties of specific risk measures (Zhang et al., 2024). Second, dynamic and static risk formulations pose distinct challenges for algorithm design and theoretical analysis. Dynamic OCE entails stagewise propagation of nonlinear risk evaluations, whereas static OCE evaluates the total return through a single risk func-tional and requires additional machinery to recover a recursive formulation. Developing algorithms and establishing theoretical guarantees for each formulation therefore require distinct technical treatments, yet a systematic investigation of both in offline RL remains lacking. Third, to the best of our knowledge, minimax suboptimality lower bounds for of-fline risk-sensitive RL under general OCE risk measures have not been established. Finally, extending the offline OCE-RL framework to settings with linear function approximation introduces further technical challenges. In the dynamic formulation, the nonlinear utility transformation and auxiliary OCE optimization require uniform estimation guarantees over a richer value-function class, while the static formulation requires augmenting the state with a budget variable and controlling the resulting discretization error. Although linear function approximation has been studied for specific risk measures, theoretical guarantees for offline RL under general OCE risk measures remain lacking for both dynamic and static formulations. 

Our Contributions. We make the following four main contributions as we derive a uni-fying framework for offline risk-sensitive RL with general risk measures. 

• We develop provably efficient offline algorithms for both dynamic-OCE and static-OCE formulations. These algorithms provide a common algorithmic foundation for both two major paradigms of risk-sensitive RL and clarify how pessimism can be designed in each case. 

• We prove sublinear suboptimality bounds for the proposed algorithms, which can recover to risk-neutral RL and various risk-sensitive RL as special cases. These results are the first suboptimality upper bounds for offline risk-sensitive RL with general risk measures, showing that our algorithms are provably efficient. 

• We establish the first information-theoretic lower bound for offline risk-sensitive RL with general risk measures, which holds for multiple types of offline risk-sensitive RL with the property of OCE. The bound identifies the intrinsic statistical difficulty of the problem and shows how risk sensitivity fundamentally influences the sample complexity. 

• We generalize our results to the linear function approximation setting, which is the first provably efficient risk-sensitive RL algorithm with linear function approximation for OCE. This extension broadens the scope of our theory beyond tabular MDPs and improves its relevance to large-scale applications. Taken together, these contributions form a coherent theoretical framework for offline risk-sensitive RL with OCE. They directly address the central question raised above by establish-ing provably efficient algorithms, matching upper and lower bound analyses, and extending to linear function approximation.

1.1 Related Works 

This work builds upon a growing body of research on offline risk-neutral RL, where the central goal is to identify optimal policies using only pre-collected datasets, without addi-tional interaction with the environment (Levine et al., 2020). In such a setting, the agent is required to infer the optimal policy exclusively from the dataset with no direct access to the underlying transition dynamics. A series of recent studies have investigated this challenge from multiple aspects, leading to a rich line of results (Chen and Jiang, 2019; Jin et al., 2021; Rashidinejad et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2022; Nguyen-Tang et al., 2023; Madhow et al., 2025; Xiong et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2026; Qiao et al., 2026, 2025; Hao et al., 2026; Jiang and Xie, 2025; Ye et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025). Our work is closely related to a long line of research on risk-sensitive RL. For the entropic risk measure, Fei et al. (2020) proposed an online algorithm in the tabular MDP setting, which was later extended to the function approximation regime in Fei et al. (2021). For iterated CVaR, Du et al. (2022) introduced a tabular algorithm, while Chen et al. (2024) extended this framework to incorporate function approximation. Xu et al. (2023) developed a dynamic-OCE-based algorithm for online tabular MDPs. In the offline setting, Zhang et al. (2024) proposed a linear function approximation method with entropic risk. In terms of static-OCE risk formulations, Wang et al. (2023) studied the online tabular CVaR-RL problem and further extended their framework to the more general OCE measure in Wang et al. (2024). Beyond these, a number of works have laid theoretical foundations for risk-sensitive RL (Osogami, 2012; Shen et al., 2013; B¨ auerle and Rieder, 2014; Prashanth, 2014; Shen et al., 2014; B¨ auerle and Ja´ skiewicz, 2024; Liang and Luo, 2024; Ma et al., 2025; Ghosh and Moharrami, 2025; Deng et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2026; Mortensen and Talebi, 2026; Essakine and Vernade, 2026). There are also a number of works that focus on linear function approximation, which are closely related to our work. Zhang et al. (2024) introduced a linear function approximation method for offline RL under the entropic risk metrics, which, however, did not address the more general and challenging OCE setting. Our algorithmic design is further motivated by a broader set of advances in function approximation (Cai et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2020, 2021; Wang et al., 2020; Agarwal et al., 2020; Zanette et al., 2021; Qiu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Modi et al., 2024; Di et al., 2024; Golowich and Moitra, 2024; Qiu et al., 2024; Du et al., 2025; Lee and Oh, 2025).

5 Linear Function Approximation for Offline RL with OCE 

In large state spaces, the proposed algorithms under the tabular setting would incur a large suboptimality bound, according to Theorems 6 and 7. A standard approach to mitigating this dependence is to use function approximation. While function approximation has been extensively studied in RL, developing a theoretically justified algorithm for RL under the OCE risk measure (let alone the offline setting) remains limited. In this section, we take an initial step in this direction by studying offline RL with OCE under linear function approximation, a practical and widely used implementation of function approximation, and we develop learning algorithms for both dynamic-OCE RL and static-OCE RL.

7 Conclusion 

Since the majority of existing research on risk-sensitive RL focuses on online settings or spe-cific risk measures, we study the offline risk-sensitive RL with OCE as a general risk measure. We develop provably efficient offline RL algorithms for both dynamic-OCE and static-OCE formulations and establish corresponding suboptimality upper bounds. Additionally, we establish the first minimax suboptimality lower bound for offline RL under general OCE risk measures, applicable to both formulations. Finally, we extend our framework to linear function approximation and develop provably efficient algorithms for both dynamic-OCE and static-OCE RL, together with corresponding suboptimality upper bounds.
