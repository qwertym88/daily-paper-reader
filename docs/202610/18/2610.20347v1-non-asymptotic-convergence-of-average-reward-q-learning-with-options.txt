## Abstract 

We study the finite-sample convergence of Q-learning algorithms for the average-reward formulation of Reinforcement Learning (RL) with options. While options allow temporarily extended and hier-archical actions, the average-reward formulation incorporates continual tasks beyond discounted and episodic RL. We establish the first ˜O(1 /iterations ) convergence guarantees, in the mean-square sense, for average-reward option Q-learning algorithms in both the inter and intra-option settings. First, we prove that both inter- and intra-option Bellman operators satisfy multi-step span contractions under unichain assumptions. Then, we propose synchronous estimators of these multi-step option Bellman operators leading to two variants of option Q-learning. Finally, we extend the stochastic approximation framework to prove convergence rates of these two algorithms under the minimal assumptions used in the literature. As a result, we obtain the first non-asymptotic convergence rates for Q-learning algo-rithms in average-reward RL with hierarchies and reward machines. Our experimental results across RL environments corroborate the theoretical findings.

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

In Reinforcement Learning (RL), an agent learns a behavioural strategy (aka policy ) while sequentially interacting with an environment by taking a sequence of actions , collecting new observations regarding the environment, and goodness of the actions (also known as rewards ). The aim is to find a policy optimising the cumulative utility of the agent’s actions (Sutton and Barto, 1998). In practice, we encounter RL problems in which the agent has to solve complex tasks and predict the consequences of its actions over multiple levels of abstraction. For example, hierarchical RL (HRL) decomposes a complex high-level task into several low-level tasks, which are often simpler to solve (Barto and Mahadevan, 2003). In RL with reward machines, the agent has access to a reward machine, i.e. an automaton whose states compactly represent the interaction history and in turn determines the reward signal (Icarte et al., 2020). In these settings, the agent has to take temporally extended actions and learn their consequences to optimise the long-term utilities. RL with options framework (Precup et al., 1998; Sutton et al., 1999) allows learning and planning with such temporally extended actions (aka options). An option is a set of initial states, an internal policy for primitive actions, and a termination condition, yielding a stochastic holding time of the option. 

Example 1 (Four Rooms (Sutton et al., 1999)) . An agent navigates through the four points A, B, C, and D situated at the corners of four rooms connected by hallways. The agent’s primitive actions are going left, right, up, or down, while the options are going from one hallway to the other and following a room-specific policy to take primitive actions inside it. Here, an RL agent can take two approaches (Sutton et al., 1999). (a) Inter-option: 

Learn and plan by treating options as actions, and update the estimates on goodness of an option only after its termination. (b) Intra-option: Learn and plan by taking primitive actions, and update the estimates for the options after each action. 

Options allow hierarchical learning and planning (Mitchener et al., 2022; Ravindran and Barto, 2002), skill sharing across tasks (Klissarov and Precup, 2021; Ramesh et al., 2019), and faster exploration via multi-step actions (Klissarov and Precup, 2021). RL with options was originally proposed for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs: a model of RL interaction) with discounted cumulative rewards as utilities (Bacon et al., 2017; Sutton et al., 1999; Tiwari and Thomas, 2019). The framework was later extended to the average-reward setting (Wan et al., 2021), in which the agent aims to maximise the rate of reward obtained per-step over an infinite number of possible interactions. It allows control of the long-term behaviour of continuing RL algorithms outside the episodic setting (Ghavamzadeh and Mahadevan, 2007). Thus, we focus on the average-reward setting of RL with options. But convergence analysis of average-reward RL is often more challenging than its discounted counterpart, in part due to the absence of standard norm contractions. While the asymptotic analysis of average-reward RL with primitive actions is well-developed (Abounadi et al., 2001, 2002; Yu et al., 2026), fewer theoretical guaran-tees are available for its finite-time analysis. Recently, finite-time convergence guarantees were established for average-reward Q-learning with primitive actions (Chen, 2025; Chen et al., 2026). However, there is no finite-time convergence guarantee available for average-reward option-based Q-learning as the existing results are only asymptotic (Wan et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026). In this paper, we bridge this gap .

Our Contributions. With four axes, we theoretically and numerically answer the question:     

> How do the expected errors in inter- & intra-option Q-learning, i.e. the discrepancies between an estimate ˆQkyielded by algorithms and the optimal Q-function Q∗, decrease with the number of iterations k?

1. The convergence of Q-learning algorithms depends on the contraction of the option Bellman operator in a compatible seminorm. We establish J-step contraction for inter- and intra-option Bellman operators with respect to span seminorm extending results for MDPs (Puterman, 1994). 2. We develop new J-step estimators for synchronous inter- and intra-option Q-learning. An unbiased estima-tor would require intermediate greedy option selections based on unavailable greedy finite-horizon Bellman values. We replace these oracle decisions by approximately greedy choices constructed from empirical Bell-man operators, and develop a perturbation analysis to control the resulting bias. The inter-option setting introduces an additional source of bias because the Bellman updates depend on quantities determined by the random option durations and rewards, which must also be estimated online from past interactions. Our analysis jointly controls both sources of bias and further rectifies the estimators and corresponding rates for average-reward Q-learning with primitive actions (Chen et al., 2026; Naskar et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2021). ## 3 J-STEP CONTRACTION OF OPTION BELLMAN OPERATOR 

Contraction is a key property of Bellman operators that ensure convergence of Q-learning algorithms. While contraction is immediate in the discounted setting, it is often unavailable in the average-reward setting (Puterman, 1994; Zhang et al., 2021). Thus, even for classical MDPs, convergence analysis opens with proving the contraction of the J-step Bellman operator, i.e. the J-fold composition HJ , in the span seminorm (Chen et al., 2026). We prove that both J-step inter- and intra-option Bellman operators are contractions .

Comparison with asymptotic analysis. Wan et al. (2021, 2024) conduct asymptotic analysis of their pro-posed 1-step inter- and intra-option differential Q-learning algorithms with RVI-type recentering. The analysis differs in a couple of ways. First, they do not need to construct a J-step estimator of the option Bellman operators since the contraction property of the Bellman operators is not required in the asymptotic analysis. Rather, asymptotic analysis requires only that the option Bellman operators are Lipschitz– a property which the 1-step option Bellman operators satisfy, and hence, they use the 1-step option Bellman operators. Second, their analysis for their inter-option algorithm employs a coupled Stochastic Approximation (SA) scheme to estimate option lengths whilst simultaneously updating the Q-values. This results in a single-timescale SA with vanishing perturbations. This is similar to our derivation of finite-time rates in both the settings, which are modelled as single-timescale SA with decaying perturbations.

## 6 RL WITH HIERARCHICAL REWARD MACHINES 

Prior works on average-reward HRL (Ghavamzadeh and Mahadevan, 2007) derive the average-reward Bellman equations and corresponding Q-learning algorithms, but do not establish convergence of the proposed updates, leaving a gap between the fixed-point characterization and the algorithmic behaviour. Our proposed algorithms are the first to exhibit non-asymptotic convergence to hierarchically optimal policies. Specifically, the analysis establishes convergence of the proposed algorithms to the solutions of (AOE) with options, and closes this gap. 

Hierarchical Reward Machines (HRMs) to options. RM reward is Markovian on X = S × U , where U

is RM’s states. Every transition in RM ⟨u, u t⟩ induces an option that starts in state u and terminates with a change in the RM state. Therefore, ¯Hinter , Hintra on both J-step algorithms defined in Section 4 are consistent with the new state space X . In this case, the option holding times are the hitting times of RM transitions which are not in general bounded. Our analysis allow this by only requiring their second moments to be bounded (Assumption 1). 

Finite-time guarantees. Under Assumptions 2 and 3 and the assumption on the cross-product space (As-sumption 4), both intra- and inter-option operators are J-step span contractions (Theorem E.2). Therefore the 

eO(1 /k ) bounds from Theorem 5.1 and 5.2 hold but with |S||O| replaced by |S|| U ||O| . The intra-option and inter-option bounds vary logarithmically and linearly respectively with RM size |U |.

Hierarchical optimality. Our analysis guarantees convergence of the Q-values, up to an additive constant, to the optimality class Q∗ of (AOE) for the option SMDP on X . Under standard assumptions on options (e.g., non-zero probability of option termination, which in HRMs is equivalent to non-zero probability of satisfying the logical formula associated with an outgoing RM transition), the policies obtained by option Q-learning are hierarchically optimal with respect to the given option policies (Ghavamzadeh and Mahadevan, 2007). Specif-ically, they are optimal among all policies that select between the HRM options. Optimality over all primitive policies requires the option policies to be optimal for their sub-tasks, which depends on the options rather than algorithms. A detailed reduction of MDPs with HRMs to options, and implications of our convergence guarantees is in Appendix E.

## 8 DISCUSSIONS 

We design two variants of option Q-learning algorithms. We derive the first non-asymptotic sublinear convergence for these option Q-learning algorithms. The proof proceeds by (a) J-step contraction for inter- and intra-option Q-learning, (b) designing J-step option Bellman operator estimates and Q-update steps with proper learning rates, (c) tackling the effect of only second moment bounded stochastic holding times for options, and (d) controlling the bias introduced by approximately greedy option selection and online estimation of reward rate and inverse mean holding times. Our results establish the first non-asymptotic convergence bound for Q-learning with hierarchical RMs. We analyse the single timescale framework for synchronous option Q-learning. The natural next step is to analyse the asynchronous setting, where only a subset of the Q-estimates are updated at each iteration. Another direction is the analysis of two-timescale option Q-learning (Manenti and Iannelli, 2025). Second, an important open question is whether the unichain assumptions can be relaxed to more general multichain settings for options, in line with recent developments in average-reward RL (Lee and Ryu, 2025, 2026). Finally, understanding the role of function approximation in the options framework, in terms of stability and convergence, remains an open direction.

## B ADDITIONAL RELATED WORK 

Average-reward Q-learning. Classical algorithms for average-reward Q-learning include Relative Value Iter-ation (RVI) Q-learning and stochastic shortest path (SSP) Q-learning, whose asymptotic convergence properties were established in (Abounadi et al., 2001) using tools from asynchronous stochastic approximation (Borkar, 1998). These results were subsequently extended under weaker assumptions and more general settings in (Abounadi et al., 2002; Wan et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025, 2026). However, these works focus primarily on asymptotic convergence and stability, and do not provide finite-time guarantees. 

Asymptotic stochastic approximation for options. The options framework (Sutton et al., 1999) provides a principled approach to hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL). While most prior work in HRL has focused on the discounted setting (Barto and Mahadevan, 2003; G¨ urtler et al., 2021; Vezhnevets et al., 2017), comparatively less is understood in the average-reward regime, which is particularly relevant for continuing tasks (Ghavamzadeh and Mahadevan, 2007). Existing work (Wan et al., 2024) establishes asymptotic convergence guarantees for RVI-style (Puterman, 1994) option-based Q-learning algorithms, but does not address finite-time analysis. Moreover, the assumptions and proof techniques used for asymptotic stochastic approximation differ substantially from those required for finite-time guarantees. 

Finite-sample analysis for stochastic approximation. Finite-time analysis of stochastic approximation (SA) depends critically on the contraction properties of the underlying fixed-point operator and the behaviour of the noise in the underlying stochastic process. Finite-time guarantees for SA with contractive operators have been extensively studied in (Chen et al., 2020, 2024), while more recent works have extended these techniques to seminorm contractive operators (Chen et al., 2026). However, these results are not directly applicable to average-reward RL with options. In particular, they assume contraction properties that must first be established for the Option Bellman operator (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2), and often rely on almost sure bounds on the noise. In the SMDP setting, such assumptions would require the holding times to be almost surely bounded, which is overly restrictive. Instead, we assume only bounded second moments of the holding times (Assumption 1), which aligns naturally with assumptions commonly used in asymptotic analyses (Yu et al., 2025). 

Options. Options have been shown to have extensive applications in deep reinforcement learning (Bacon et al., 2017; Klissarov and C. Machado, 2023; Machado, 2025; Machado et al., 2023, 2017, 2018). Options had been introduced as a general framework for planning by using temporally extended actions (Sutton et al., 1999). It allows hierarchical learning and planning (Mitchener et al., 2022; Ravindran and Barto, 2002), skill sharing across tasks (Klissarov and Precup, 2021; Ramesh et al., 2019), and faster exploration via multi-step actions (Klissarov and Precup, 2021).

## D ANALYSIS OF SYNCHRONOUS OPTION ALGORITHMS 

In this section we show that the synchronous inter- and intra-option algorithms outlined in Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 respectively can be written as stochastic approximation schemes driven by J-step Bellman operators. In section D.1, we formulate the problem setup and construct the estimators of the J-step Bellman operators and analyze their properties. Section D.2 analyzes synchronous intra-option Q-learning: we first establish an almost-sure growth bound for the iterates, then control the bias induced by empirically approximately-greedy intermediate option selection, and finally combine these bounds with the generalized Moreau-envelope framework of Chen et al. (2026) to obtain the finite-time rate. Section D.3 follows the same analysis for synchronous inter-option Q-learning, with one additional first-order perturbation arising from the online estimation of the reward rates and inverse mean holding times.

## E APPLICATION: AVERAGE-REWARD Q-LEARNING WITH REWARD MACHINES 

In this section we show that the J-step span contraction (Theorem 3.1 and 3.2) and the finite-time bounds for synchronous intra- and inter-option Q learning (Theorems 5.1 and 5.2) hold for average-reward reinforcement learning in Reward Machines (Icarte et al., 2020). Reward machines (RMs) are a type of finite state machine, which take as input an interaction history and outputs a reward function. RMs are used to formalise tasks specified by linear temporal logic, sub-goal sequences or regular languages, in which reward is non-Markovian. Our results show the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for average reward Q-learning for hierarchical RMs.
