# Enhancing Latent World Models with Associative Memory

## Abstract 

World models trained via latent dynamics offer a scal-able path for embodied planning, yet existing methods suffer from representational collapse, computational over-head, and limited long-horizon capability. We introduce a memory-enhanced latent world model that augments JEPA-style prediction with a content-addressable memory bank. Our approach learns high-level representations directly from observations, operating at multiple temporal scales within a shared latent space, and conditions predictions via adaptive layer normalization. To prevent collapse and en-sure effective memory usage, we design two complementary losses that encourage slot diversity and balanced temporal utilization. Experiments on PushT, Tworoom, Reacher, and OGBCube demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance with a lightweight architecture, outper-forming conventional latent world models on long-horizon planning tasks. 

## 1. Introduction 

World Models (WMs) are a powerful family of approaches that learn to predict the consequences of actions, enabling agents to plan and improve entirely within an internal model of the world. Recent advances in representation learning and latent-space modeling have further improved scalabil-ity, allowing world models to be trained directly from high-dimensional inputs. As a result, planning with learned world models has become a powerful framework for em-bodied decision-making. Given the initial observation, the agent imagines the entire future trajectory without feed-back, demanding a tight coupling of perception, prediction, and decision-making. However, conventional approaches solely rely on visual targets and thus fail to exploit the richer guidance that the environment provides. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)-style world models [1–4], on the other hand, learn to com-press observations into a compact, low-dimensional latent space and model temporal dynamics by predicting the la-tent representations of future observations. However, using JEPA for world models while preventing collapse where the model maps all inputs to nearly identical represen-tations—remains a central challenge. LeWorldModel [5] tackles this problem with a lightweight latent prediction framework using the SigREG loss to prevent representa-tional collapse. DINO-WM [6] employs a pretrained DI-NOv2 model to extract observation embeddings, addressing collapse at the cost of high computational overhead and re-liance on external pretraining. While LeWM is efficient, it cannot match the performance of pretrained encoder-based models. In practice, such strategies, which rely on heuris-tic regularizations, often introduce additional instability or substantially increase training complexity. Another misconception in visual planning is that extend-ing the rollout horizon is sufficient to elicit long-horizon behavior. In practice, simply increasing the maximum step count only causes the agent to jitter uselessly around the same goal. It cannot test true long-horizon planning, be-cause the bottleneck lies in the representation of tempo-ral structure rather than in the number of steps. The ef-fective approach is to layer high-level temporal abstraction and semantic structure on top of the environment’s low-level contact dynamics. A classic strategy for long-horizon control is to introduce content-addressable memory into the decision-making structure. By reasoning at multiple tempo-ral scales, agents can plan coarsely over extended horizons while maintaining fine-grained control at shorter ones. Ex-isting studies, such as hierarchical world models [7], intro-duce a temporal hierarchy that captures dynamics at multi-ple timescales while doubling the parameter count. To overcome these limitations, our associative memory-enhanced latent world model is built on a content-addressable memory bank that supplies reusable high-level subgoals, a multi-scale shared latent space couples these subgoals to low-level contact dynamics, and two comple-mentary losses that keep this structure from collapsing. Crucially, our approach operates within a shared latent space enabling direct coupling between levels without sac-rificing model efficiency. We introduce a specifically de-signed memory bank that learns high-level representations directly from observations and uses them to condition pre-dictions via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), com-plementing the low-level action-based prediction. Finally, arXiv:2610.19683v1 [cs.LG] 14 Oct 2026 we devise two memory loss functions to encourage both structural diversity across memory slots and balanced slot usage across time steps in agent planning. We conduct extensive experiments on a wide range of benchmarks, including PushT, Tworoom, Reacher, and OG-BCube, along with comprehensive ablation studies on the proposed associative memory mechanism and its adaptabil-ity to a more complex long-horizon task. Our method outperforms conventional latent world models, achieving the best performance while maintaining computational ef-ficiency. It substantially surpasses the latest LeWorldModel method while remaining simpler and more interpretable than the Hierarchical World Model. Overall, our main contributions are three-fold: (1) We propose associative memory-enhanced latent world models for improving long-horizon action sequence prediction, marking the first work to incorporate long-term memory into the JEPA architecture (Section 3.2). (2) To prevent memory slots from collapsing into iden-tical representations, we introduce two complementary loss functions that encourage diversification and balanced uti-lization of memory slots (Section 3.3.2). (3) Comprehensive experiments including ablation stud-ies across a wide range of tasks in 2D and 3D environments demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms ex-isting approaches while remaining computationally efficient (Section 4). 

## 2. Related work 

This section provides a summary of the most relevant work on world models including the reconstruction-based meth-ods and the latent world model methods. We investigate the relatedness and limitations of these studies comparing with our memory-enhanced method. 

Reconstruction-based world models Reconstruction-based world models learn latent environment dynamics by optimizing objectives that reconstruct observations from compressed latent states, encouraging representations to re-tain sufficient information for predicting future outcomes. Early approaches such as World Models [8] combined vari-ational latent representations with recurrent dynamics and pixel reconstruction to enable imagined rollouts for control. Subsequent methods, including PlaNet [9], Dreamer [10], DreamerV2 [11], and DreamerV3 [12], leveraged recurrent state-space models trained with observation reconstruction and latent transition objectives to learn compact world mod-els. While these methods have demonstrated competitive empirical performance, reconstruction losses may bias rep-resentations toward low-level perceptual details limiting ab-straction and greatly increase computational cost. 

Latent world models Joint-Embedding Predictive Archi-tecture (JEPA) [1–4] is a self-supervised learning method for world modeling that learns predictive representations in latent space rather than reconstructing low-level pixels. For instance, DINO-WM [6] leverages spatial patch features pre-trained with DINOv2 in latent space, enabling more precise planning and control. PLDM [13, 14] learns rep-resentations end-to-end using VICReg [15] at the cost of training instabilities and scalability limitations. LeWorld-Model (LeWM) [5] is a compact JEPA-style world model that predicts next-frame embeddings stably, but its perfor-mance degrades over long horizons. Hierarchical World Models (HWM) [7] address this with separate short- and long-horizon modules, doubling parameters. In contrast, our method uses a flexible memory bank of high-level pri-ors, jointly learning long-horizon contexts, which improves performance without extra prediction modules. 

## 3. Method 

In this section, we present memory-enhanced latent world models. We begin by detailing the joint embedding pre-dictive architecture (JEPA), then introduce a new content-addressable memory component that learns diverse memory slot representations for long-horizon conditioning. The pre-dictor leverages both the current state and action, along with semantically meaningful representations retrieved from the memory bank, to forecast the next latent state. Finally, we employ model predictive control during inference to enable effective action sequence rollouts.

3.2. Associative memory for long-horizon world modeling 

Conventional latent world models including LeWorldModel struggle with long-horizon prediction tasks. One reason is that they learn latent dynamics solely through action-conditioned transitions. Training only on short-horizon in-formation sabotages the model’s planning performance. To overcome this limitation, an intuitive idea is to induce a long-term memory. We propose learnable associative mem-ory in latent world models to accumulate historical latents and further disentangle them into diversified conditioning information. This high-level conditioning information is then fed into a predictor along with the action to enhance long-horizon prediction of the latent world model. 

3.2.1. Associative Memory Structure 

Humans leverage associative memory to predict surround-ing worlds constantly. The contents-addressable ability of associative memory has been employed for enhanced long-range input association in modern transformer architecture [16]. Leveraging such learnable memory disentangles infor-mation across various timesteps and inputs, enabling latent predictions that combine both short-horizon actions with long-horizon memory contexts for enhanced agent planning ability. In particular, an associative memory bank is com-prised of M learnable low-rank slots Γ ∈ RM ×D . Each memory slot diversifies into different general assumptions about observations and environment dynamics, such as var-ious planning stages including moving towards the object, pushing the object, and moving to a specific location. We elaborate on the contents retrieval and long-horizon condi-tioning processes in the remainder of this section. 

3.2.2. Learnable memory bank and contents retrieval 

The learnable memory slots Γ are paired with a set of learn-able keys Ξ ∈ RM ×D that serve as indexes for writing to the memory bank, with each key and memory slot forming a pair. We decouple the training of keys from that of memory slots, ensuring that learning the keys does not substantially interfere with the memory update. These learnable keys are utilized to compute cross-attention with the observation la-tents, which assigns different weights to each memory slot accordingly. ## 4. Experiments 

In this section, we present the settings and extensive exper-imental results on various 2D and 3D planning tasks. We compare MemWM with a broad range of conventional la-tent world models, including comprehensive ablation stud-ies on the effectiveness of the associative memory mech-anism, the memory diversity losses, and the conditioning mechanism. We further provide detailed visualizations of planning trajectories and memory slot usage distributions, demonstrating the interpretability of memory slot roles in various stages of the planning tasks. 

Datasets We evaluate on a diverse set of tasks including navigation, motion planning and manipulation. All datasets are from the stable-worldmodel [17] framework. In particu-lar, PushT is a robotic manipulation task where the goal is to push a T-shaped block to target positions using top-down vi-sual observations. TwoRoom is a 2D navigation task where an agent moves through two connected rooms to reach a specified location. Reacher is a continuous control task in-volving a robotic arm that moves its end-effector to ran-domly appearing target points. OGBCube is a 3D manipu-lation task requiring an agent to perform tasks like pushing or picking a cube. 

Settings For the reimplementation of all baseline meth-ods, we employed the author-recommended settings and hy-perparameters. For the proposed MemWM model, we em-ployed a batch size of 128, a memory slot number of 10, and a dimension of 192 for each memory slot. We initialized the learnable memory slots and keys with random values scaled by 0.02 and employed a query projector with two dense lay-ers and an intermediate dimension of 192. The encoder is a vision transformer and the observation embedding zt is constructed from the [CLS] token from the last layer of the vision transformer. The predictor is a 6-layer transformer with 16 attention heads. Please refer to Appendix A for the complete model settings and hyperparameters. 

Baselines We compare our method to the following con-ventional latent world models: • PLDM [13, 14]: learns representations end-to-end with additional regularization terms, at the cost of training in-stability and scalability limitations. • DINO-WM [6]: employs pretrained DINOv2 as the fea-ture encoder and additional proprioceptive inputs to miti-gate representation collapse in latent space training. • LeWM [5]: is a compact JEPA-style world model that predicts next-frame embeddings. It is the most competi-tive baseline we consider. Ablating the associative mem-ory from MemWM recovers the LeWM architecture. 

Latent planning performance Given an initial observa-tion and a goal, the agent model aims to output a sequence of actions to reach the goal. The predictor rolls out future latents with a selected action sequence updated with model predictive control (MPC), where a cost between the final predicted latent and the goal embedding guides a solver to optimize actions. Moreover, we assume the goal is reach-able within 25 steps and employ a maximum planning bud-get of 50 steps for all tasks. We report the mean success rate across three random seeds, evaluated on the same set of 50 trajectories (Table 1). MemWM achieves the highest aver-age success rate (89.9%), surpassing DINO-WM (89.3%), LeWM (85.8%), and PLDM (79.5%). DINO-WM performs best in Two-Room (100%) and OGBCube (86%). While no single baseline dominates all tasks, MemWM exhibits the most consistent and well-rounded performance. Our method not only achieves superior performance over the various baselines but also uses fewer parameters than PLDM and DINO-WM (Table 1), owing to its efficient memory-based conditioning mechanism. Note that DINO-WM employs a pretrained DINOv2 encoder besides the 75M parameters, whereas our method learns an encoder with much less parameters through the end-to-end training. Both DINO-WM (75M) and the Hierarchical World Model (100M) employ the pretrained DINOv2 as the encoder, which has 1.1 billion parameters. Furthermore, LeWM uti-lized a large evaluation budget in the TwoRoom task [5]. Using the same 50-step budget as the other methods gen-erally degrades its performance. Discussion on conditioning methods To better under-stand the design choice of the conditioning method in our architecture, we conduct ablation studies on how short-horizon action conditioning and long-horizon memory con-ditioning are combined within the predictor. We compare our default sequential design against two alternative strate-gies: • Reverse: We reverse the order of long-horizon mem-ory conditioning and short-horizon action conditioning in AdaLN while maintaining the parameter sharing method. • Separate: We employ two sequential AdaLN mod-ules with corresponding predictors. We then process short-horizon and long-horizon conditioning information through the two separate predictors, where the first pre-dictor’s output serves as input to the second. This is equivalent to the case of not using shared parameters. 

## 5. Conclusion 

We presented an associative memory-based latent world model that enhances JEPA-style models’ performance while alleviating computational overhead. Our method augments latent dynamics with a content-addressable memory bank operating at multiple temporal scales within a shared la-tent space, conditioned via adaptive layer normalization. Two complementary memory losses were proposed to en-courage memory slot diversity and balanced temporal uti-lization, effectively preventing collapse while maintaining a lightweight architecture. The extensive experiments on PushT, Two-Room, Reacher, and OGBCube demonstrate that our approach achieves the best overall performance across the diverse benchmarks, exhibiting diversified mem-ory slot usage patterns at various planning stages. 

Limitations Training becomes unstable and performance degrades in latent world models as the planning horizon in-creases. Our method alleviates this to a large extent, how-ever it requires action-labeled observations for end-to-end training, which incurs a cost in collecting labels. A better solution might be training a policy network that learns dif-ferent actions within the latent space of the world model, eliminating the need to collect each action during planning. Learning such actions through model training could be chal-lenging due to both the prediction of next state of environ-ment and the action could be unstable, leading to collapse of model learning.
