NEAR-OPTIMAL PURE SINGLE-LOOP EXTRAGRADIENT METHOD FOR STRONGLY CONVEX–STRONGLY CONCAVE MINIMAX OPTIMIZATION 

MINHAO ZHANG ∗ AND ZI XU †

Abstract. We study smooth strongly convex–strongly concave minimax optimization with general nonlinear coupling in the deterministic unconstrained setting. We propose a pure single-loop damped extragradient method with fixed parameters and two new full-gradient evaluations per iteration after one initialization query. The method uses an auxiliary feedback recursion and requires no inner solves, accuracy schedules, or staged restarts. We establish last-iterate linear convergence and show that reducing the squared Euclidean distance to the saddle point to an ε fraction of its initial value requires O(√κxκy log(2 κxκy /ε )) full-gradient queries, where κx = L/μ x and κy = L/μ y . This bound attains the optimal condition-number order up to logarithmic factors through fixed explicit updates. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the method. 

Key words. minimax optimization, strongly convex–strongly concave problems, pure single-loop algorithms, extragradient methods, optimal complexity

1.1. Our contributions. Our contributions concern the update structure, the mechanism underlying its analysis, and the resulting last-iterate guarantee. 

A fixed-form damped extragradient method. The method maintains three variables, (xt, y t, v t). We order the variables so that μx ≥ μy ; the auxiliary variable vt then modifies the dual direction, which has weaker curvature. Each iteration predicts a primal–dual point, corrects it using the gradient at the predictor, and updates the auxiliary variable using the gradient at the corrected point. All coefficients depend only on L, μ x, μ y and remain fixed throughout the iteration. Caching the endpoint gradient requires two new full-gradient evaluations per iteration after one initialization query, for exactly 1 + 2 T queries over T iterations. The method applies to general nonlinear coupling and requires no inner solves, accuracy schedules, or staged restarts. 

Dynamic feedback with residual control. The auxiliary variable follows a damped, gradient-driven recursion and feeds back into the dual direction. Its provisional and final updates share the same history term, so their difference depends only on gradients at the predictor and the corrected point. This identity bounds the residual in the endpoint-gradient representation of the explicit step by the increment of the corrected gradient ( ∇xf, ∇y f +v). We construct a distance-controlling Lyapunov function whose negative gradient-increment squares absorb this residual. Thus the auxiliary recursion provides both the feedback used in the update and the error bound needed for the analysis, without requiring an approximate subproblem solution.

Proximal Best Response [8, Theorem 3] distinguishes interaction smoothness from smoothness within each variable and, under a common bound L, attains eO(√κxκy ) using nested iterations. FOAM [4] combines a pointwise-conjugate reformulation with inexact proximal updates to attain the optimal rate. These results establish the complexity attainable through such constructions; the present work realizes the optimal condition-number order, up to logarithmic factors, through a fixed sequence of explicit full-gradient updates. 

Bilinear coupling. For objectives of the form a(x) + ⟨Ax, y ⟩ − b(y), with smooth strongly convex a and b, the Lifted Primal-Dual (LPD) method [7] and the Accelerated Primal-Dual Gradient (APDG) method [5] attain optimal rates with single-loop updates. Their bounds distinguish component smoothness and coupling constants and have order eO(√κxκy ) under a common bound L. The listed results exploit bilinear coupling and component-gradient and matrix-vector oracles. Our method instead uses full-gradient queries for a general smooth objective. 

Lower bounds and comparison scope. The lower bounds in [10, 3] give the bench-mark eΩ( √κxκy ) after constant-factor normalization of block smoothness parameters. They apply to their respective first-order algorithm classes, with dimension allowed to grow, and do not distinguish single-loop from nested implementations. The con-tribution here is therefore to realize this established condition-number scale, up to logarithmic factors, with a pure single-loop method for general smooth coupling.

3. The algorithm and main results. 3.1. Algorithmic design. The method augments an extragradient prediction– correction step with a damped auxiliary variable vt ∈ Rp. Under the ordering μx ≥ μy ,this variable modifies the dual direction, which corresponds to the side with weaker curvature. The predictor uses −∇ xf (xt, y t) in the primal variable and ∇y f (xt, y t) + vt

in the dual variable. The gradient at the predictor then determines both a provisional auxiliary variable evt and the primal–dual correction. Finally, the gradient at the corrected point determines vt+1 .The provisional and final auxiliary updates share the history term αv t. Their difference therefore depends only on the dual gradients at the predictor and the corrected point:

6. Conclusion. We have developed a pure single-loop damped extragradient method for smooth strongly convex–strongly concave minimax problems with general nonlinear coupling in the deterministic unconstrained setting. The method uses fixed parameters and two new full-gradient evaluations per iteration after one initialization query. We establish last-iterate linear convergence and a full-gradient complexity of 

O(√κxκy log(2 κxκy /ε )) for relative squared-distance accuracy ε. This bound attains the optimal condition-number order up to logarithmic factors through fixed explicit updates, without inner solves, accuracy schedules, or staged restarts. The analysis couples the auxiliary feedback recursion with residual control in an endpoint-gradient representation of the explicit step. The shared history term in the provisional and final auxiliary updates bounds the discretization residual by corrected-gradient increments. Negative increment terms in a distance-controlling Lyapunov estimate then absorb this residual and yield the contraction. Extending this feedback and residual-control mechanism beyond the smooth unconstrained setting is a direction for future research. 12 MINHAO ZHANG AND ZI XU
