## ABSTRACT 

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) typically trains a critic by minimizing a re-gression loss against bootstrapped value targets, which are stabilized by target net-works updated through exponential moving averages (EMA). Furthermore, multi-step targets incorporate behavior-policy actions and therefore require off-policy correction. We instead impose in-sample Bellman optimality on the critic through inequality constraints rather than regression. We formulate the Lifted Bellman Linear Program (LBLP), which lifts the linear programming characterization of Bellman optimality to the joint (Q, V ) space so that every constraint involves only state-action pairs in the dataset. Its unique minimizer is the in-sample optimal pair, and constraints along K-step segments of dataset trajectories leave this minimizer unchanged for any rollout policy and any horizon. Under deterministic dynam-ics, this minimizer lies between the best dataset return and the optimal value. Relaxing the constraints into hinge penalties recovers the same solution above a finite penalty coefficient in the tabular case. Approximate Lifted Bellman Uncon-strained Minimization (ALBUM) implements this relaxation with neural networks and detaches the K-step rollout targets by stop gradient. Its objective contains no squared regression onto bootstrapped targets, so it can be trained without target networks or EMA updates. Under deterministic dynamics, the LBLP solution is a stationary point of the detached update under a coefficient condition indepen-dent of γ and K, and the inequality constraints allow discounted returns along dataset trajectories to serve as lower bounds without off-policy correction or ac-tion chunking, providing a direct mechanism for long-horizon value propagation. On OGBench, ALBUM uses a single critic with a Gaussian policy, matches the average performance of FQL, and is comparable to recent action-chunking meth-ods, while using the fewest parameters and the least peak GPU memory among all compared methods. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) learns a policy solely from a pre-collected dataset (Levine et al., 2020), and its central obstacle is value overestimation, since the Bellman maximization selects values extrapolated to actions outside the dataset and each backup propagates them to preceding states (Mao et al., 2024). Prior work either keeps policy improvement close to the behavior pol-icy (Fujimoto et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2019; Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), or modifies the Bellman up-date by penalizing the values assigned to actions the dataset does not contain (Kumar et al., 2020), or by replacing the maximization with an in-sample regression (Kostrikov et al., 2022; Garg et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023). This regression brings two costs. The critic is regressed onto a target com-puted from its own estimate, so a target network with EMA updates is needed to keep the target stable (Mnih et al., 2015; Lillicrap et al., 2015). The same loss also limits how far a single backup reaches. When a multi-step return enters the target, the intermediate actions come from the behav-ior policy, and the resulting target is biased for the learned policy unless it is corrected (Li et al., 2026b). This raises a more basic question. Can in-sample Bellman optimality be imposed on the

critic entirely through inequality constraints over state-action pairs in the dataset, so that the critic is the solution of a constrained program rather than a regression onto bootstrapped targets? 

We answer this question starting from the linear program (LP) characterization of Bellman opti-mality, in which the optimal value function is the smallest function satisfying a family of Bellman inequalities (De Farias & Van Roy, 2003; Puterman, 2014). However, neither of the two standard LP formulations carries over to offline RL directly. The action-value formulation imposes a Bellman inequality for every action at the next state, requiring evaluation at actions that the dataset need not contain (Lee et al., 2026). The state-value formulation avoids this issue by eliminating the next ac-tion, but identifies only the optimal state-value function, so a policy must be recovered from the dual occupancy measure (Nachum & Dai, 2020). Offline work building on this formulation, therefore, turns to the dual, either by learning a density ratio (Lee et al., 2021) or by solving a saddle-point problem (Zhan et al., 2022; Kamoutsi et al., 2021; Ozdaglar et al., 2023; Gabbianelli et al., 2024). We resolve both difficulties with the Lifted Bellman Linear Program (LBLP), which stays on the primal side and lifts the program to the joint (Q, V ) space. An epigraph variable V expresses 

V (s) ≥ max a Q(s, a ) as the family of inequalities Q(s, a ) ≤ V (s), one for each action, so the Bellman inequality relates Q to V alone and involves no next action. LBLP places positive weight on both Q and V , so its unique minimizer directly determines both Q and V without requiring a dual step. The lifting itself is not new (Jeong et al., 2025; Mehta & Meyn, 2020; Bas-Serrano et al., 2021; Lu & Meyn, 2023), but in these formulations V is either not a decision variable or carries no weight in the objective, so the program does not determine V . Section 2 makes the comparison precise. In the offline setting, we instantiate LBLP on the empirical Markov decision process (MDP) induced by the dataset. Every constraint is indexed by a dataset state-action pair, including the K-step constraints along dataset trajectories. These additional constraints leave the feasible set and the unique minimizer unchanged. Under deterministic dynamics, this minimizer lies between the best dataset return and the optimal value, and both bounds hold without the coefficient of a value penalty (Kumar et al., 2020). For neural function approximation, we relax the constraints into hinge penalties (Ryu et al., 2019; Sikchi et al., 2024), and the relaxation is exact above a finite penalty coefficient in the tabular case. Approximate Lifted Bellman Unconstrained Minimization (ALBUM) implements this relaxation with neural networks and detaches the K-step rollout targets by stop gradient. Since the ALBUM objective contains no squared regression onto bootstrapped targets, it requires no target networks or EMA updates, and because it imposes maximization only through constraints, it trains stably with a single critic. Under deterministic dynamics, its multi-step penalties provide lower bounds along trajectories without off-policy correction or action chunking, which gives a direct mechanism for long-horizon value propagation. Using a single Q, a single V , and a Gaussian actor, ALBUM matches the average performance of FQL (Park et al., 2025b) on OGBench (Park et al., 2025a) while using half as many parameters. Our contributions are as follows. • We formulate the Lifted Bellman Linear Program (LBLP), a primal LP over the joint 

(Q, V ) space whose constraints involve only state-action pairs in the dataset. With positive objective weights on both Q and V , its unique minimizer is the Bellman-optimal pair of the empirical MDP. Constraints along K-step rollouts are implied by the one-step constraints, so they can be added for any rollout policy and horizon without off-policy correction. • Under deterministic dynamics, we show that this minimizer is bounded below by the best return in the dataset and above by the optimal value of the underlying MDP. Both bounds hold without a penalty coefficient. • We relax LBLP through hinge penalties and show that the relaxed LBLP recovers the same solution above a finite penalty coefficient in the tabular case. ALBUM implements this re-laxed objective with neural networks. Because its objective contains no squared regression onto bootstrapped targets, ALBUM trains without target networks or EMA updates and uses a single critic. • We analyze the update obtained by detaching the K-step rollout targets with stop gradient. Under deterministic dynamics, the LBLP solution is a stationary point of this update under a coefficient condition independent of γ and K, which the default coefficients satisfy (Ap-pendix I). In an idealized coordinatewise iteration, the K-step term never slows and can accelerate value propagation. ## 2 RELATED WORKS 

Offline RL. Offline RL aims to learn a policy from a pre-collected dataset, where the maximiza-tion can amplify erroneous value estimates at out-of-distribution actions (Fujimoto et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2019; Kostrikov et al., 2022; Mao et al., 2024). Existing methods mitigate this prob-lem through policy constraints (Fujimoto et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2019; Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), pessimistic value regularization (Kumar et al., 2020; Lyu et al., 2022; Mao et al., 2024), in-sample regression (Kostrikov et al., 2022; Garg et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023), hybrid regularization (Tarasov et al., 2023), and generative actor-critic methods (Wang et al., 2022; Park et al., 2025b). 

Multi-step Values. Multi-step and Monte Carlo returns have been used as lower bounds for critic values to facilitate value propagation, as auxiliary terms in a regression objective (S.He et al., 2017; Oh et al., 2018). Iterated Bellman inequalities have been used in LP-based value-function approx-imation to obtain tighter underestimators (Wang et al., 2015). In offline RL, multi-step targets are biased for the learned policy unless corrected (Li et al., 2026b), and a prevailing approach is ac-tion chunking, which conditions the critic on the whole action sequence (Li et al., 2026b; Kim et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026a; Song et al., 2026). In contrast, ALBUM formulates K-step returns as inequal-ity constraints on a single-action critic. Under deterministic dynamics, these constraints require no off-policy correction and leave the solution of the underlying program unchanged, while enabling long-horizon value propagation without action chunking. 

Linear Programs for Bellman Optimality. The LP characterization replaces the maximization with Bellman inequalities (De Farias & Van Roy, 2003; Puterman, 2014; Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017). Off-policy and offline work building on this formulation mostly passes to the dual, either estimating density ratios for evaluation or policy optimization (Nachum et al., 2019a; Zhang et al., 2020; Nachum et al., 2019b; Lee et al., 2021; Nachum & Dai, 2020) or solving saddle-point prob-lems (Zhan et al., 2022; Kamoutsi et al., 2021; Ozdaglar et al., 2023; Gabbianelli et al., 2024). Other work introduces variables alongside Q (Mehta & Meyn, 2020; Lu & Meyn, 2023; Jeong et al., 2025), while Lee & He (2019); Bas-Serrano et al. (2021) carry Q and V together, only V is independently weighted, with Q fixed by the Bellman equality. Lee et al. (2026) relax the inequalities with a log barrier, but their program involves Q alone and therefore evaluates Q at next actions. Hinge relax-ations appear in Ryu et al. (2019), whose hinge retains a maximization in its argument, and in Sikchi et al. (2024), whose Q is fit by regression outside the program. To our knowledge, no prior critic objective determines both Q and V as the unique solution of a program whose constraints involve only dataset pairs, removes the maximization from the constraint arguments through an epigraph variable, and admits multi-step constraints that provably leave this solution unchanged.

## 6 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate ALBUM on the state-based tasks of OGBench (Park et al., 2025a) and compare it with two groups of methods. The first group consists of offline RL methods with different policy classes, and the second consists of methods that shorten the horizon of value propagation. We report the success rate after 1M gradient steps over 8 seeds, with 50 evaluation episodes per seed. All five critic coefficients in equation 6 are fixed across all domains at the values in Table 3 and are not tuned per environment. Since equation 8 involves neither the reward nor the dataset, these values satisfy it in every domain. In this section, we examine the design properties induced by the LBLP formulation and test whether the empirical behavior is consistent with the theoretical analysis. We evaluate one task each from

Why does ALBUM not require target networks or critic ensembles? Averaged over the three tasks, removing target networks and EMA updates degrades ReBRAC by 45% and FQL by 5%, while using a single critic degrades them by 93% and 34%. In contrast, ALBUM uses a single critic and no target network by default, and adding a second critic changes its average by −6% , within seed variation. These results are consistent with the LP-derived critic objective, which contains neither moving-target regression nor action maximization. See Appendix L.1 for detailed comparisons. 

How much does ALBUM reduce computational cost? We compare the training cost per gradient step of ALBUM with ReBRAC, IQL, FQL, NFQL, QC-FQL, DQC, and CGQ, each in its default configuration (Table 6). These methods hold four to eight value networks, including critic ensembles and target copies, whereas ALBUM holds two. ALBUM also uses 2.43M parameters and 78 MiB of peak memory, the fewest among all measured methods. Compared with FQL, it reduces the parameters by 50%, the peak memory by 39%, and the time per step by 44%. The reduction is larger against action-chunking methods, reaching 76%, 71%, and 74% against CGQ. With the actor updated at every step for all methods, ALBUM is the fastest, although ReBRAC with its default delayed actor update remains 15% faster. See Appendix L.2. 

What happens when the coefficient condition is violated? Proposition 5.1 gives a necessary condition for the update to avoid an unbounded downward drift, and we enforce equation 8 when choosing the penalty coefficients. To verify its necessity in practice, we set λK = 0 on 

antmaze-large-navigate , yielding λB(1 − γ) + 2 λK = 0 .013 < 0.15 = ωQ + ωV. The re-sulting Vmin is driven to the order of −10 6, far outside the physical range [−1/(1 − γ), 1/(1 − γ)] = [−200 , 200] , confirming the unbounded regime predicted by Proposition 5.1. The task also has 0% 

success and only 1% of samples satisfy gB ≤ 0. Full results are reported in Appendix L.3. 

Does the K-step term behave as predicted? Theorem 4.5 shows that adding the K-step term does not slow the idealized iteration and that, where the K-step term is selected, the error contracts at 

γK . Appendix G confirms both in a tabular setting. Under neural parameterization, a longer horizon accelerates learning on antmaze-large-navigate , with K = 20 reaching 50% success at

## 7 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we show that in-sample Bellman optimality can be imposed on an offline critic through inequality constraints, rather than through regression. LBLP realizes this principle over the joint 

(Q, V ) space, and its tabular hinge relaxation recovers the same solution above a finite penalty coef-ficient. ALBUM implements this relaxed formulation with neural networks, and its detached rollout penalties keep the LBLP solution stationary for the resulting update under an explicit coefficient condition. Its critic contains no squared regression, so it trains without target networks or EMA up-dates, and its rollout constraints leave the solution unchanged while using multi-step dataset returns as lower bounds under deterministic dynamics, without off-policy correction or action chunking. With a single Q and V network and a Gaussian actor, ALBUM matches the average performance of FQL on OGBench, is comparable to recent action-chunking methods, and achieves its largest gains on long-horizon tasks, with the fewest parameters and the least peak GPU memory among all compared methods. Establishing convergence of the neural update and extending the analysis to stochastic transitions remain future work.

## A LIMITATIONS 

The theoretical results of this work assume deterministic transitions. The assumption enters at three points. Proposition 4.4 identifies MD with M on XD , Lemma D.4 fixes one dataset trajectory per pair, and Remark D.1 replaces the expectation yK by a single trajectory. The last one carries the sharpest consequence. Under stochastic transitions a sampled path is not a lower bound on Q∗D , so 

ˆyK can exceed it and the K-step hinges stop acting as lower bounds. Under continuous states, each state of D appears once almost surely, so |A D (s)| = 1 for every 

s ∈ S D . The interval in Proposition 4.4 collapses because GD = Q∗D , and the LBLP solution reduces to the discounted return along each dataset trajectory. The same condition gives δK = 0 on 

XD , so the stationary point of Corollary I.5 is this trajectory-wise return. Propositions 4.3 and 4.4 and Corollary I.5 therefore characterize the in-sample solution within MD . This solution contains no improvement over the behavior policy. The collapse follows from |A D (s)| = 1 alone and is independent of the LP formulation, so it applies to every method whose target is the in-sample optimum (Kostrikov et al., 2022; Garg et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023). The learned critic does not coincide with this empirical-MDP solution. Its mean exceeds the mean of V ∗D in nine of the ten environments (Appendix L.10). The critic loss in equation 6 imposes every constraint at a pair of D and contains no maximization over actions absent from D, so the departure reflects function approximation across the states of D. The actor objective in equation 7 evaluates Qθ at actions outside D, and the behavioral cloning term restricts these evaluations to a neighborhood of the data. A characterization of the values that function approximation assigns between dataset pairs lies outside the present analysis. Theorem 4.5 concerns an idealized coordinatewise iteration rather than the ALBUM update, and Appendix I establishes the existence of a zero of the detached update rather than uniqueness or convergence. The coefficient conditions separate divergence from boundedness and do not separate the settings that perform well from those that do not, as λB = 10 and K = 20 satisfy them while failing (Appendices L.4 and L.7). Extending the analysis to stochastic transitions and to an empirical MDP with branching action sets remains future work.

Figure 5 removes the critic ensemble, while Figure 6 removes the target network. ALBUM is unaffected by removing the ensemble and, as it does not use a target network, requires only a single critic. In contrast, both baselines degrade, most notably ReBRAC, on antmaze-large-navigate and scene-play .This behavior follows from where the two devices come from. Target networks with EMA updates trace back to DQN and DDPG (Mnih et al., 2015; Lillicrap et al., 2015), where the semi-gradient update of Q-learning regresses Qθ onto a target that itself depends on Qθ , and the slowly moving copy is what keeps that regression from chasing its own output. The ALBUM objective contains no squared regression and no such target, since Qθ and Vϕ are decision variables of a single objective, so the mechanism the device was introduced to control is absent. Critic ensembles address a different failure. The maximization inside the Q-learning update selects whichever action carries the largest error, which biases the backup upward, and double Q-learning and its ensemble descendants avoid this by decorrelating the estimates that the maximum is taken over (Hasselt, 2010; Van Hasselt et al., 2016; Fujimoto et al., 2018). In LBLP the maximization is a family of constraints rather than a step of the update rule, and the only term that lowers Qθ is ωQE[Qθ ] while the terms that raise it are hinges that go flat once satisfied, so nothing in the objective rewards an inflated value. 31 Preprint. L.6 REPLACING THE IQL C RITIC OF IFQL WITH ALBUM In this section, we examine whether the critic contribution observed in the main experiments per-sists when the policy is replaced by a more expressive generative policy. The main experiments use DDPG+BC to isolate the effect of the critic under a common Gaussian policy, so they do not distinguish the critic contribution from limitations arising from the policy representation or action extraction. To separate these effects, we replace the IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) critic in IFQL (Park et al., 2025b) with ALBUM while keeping its generative policy and action extraction procedure unchanged. This comparison evaluates ALBUM and IQL under the same generative policy and therefore isolates the effect of replacing the critic. It also assesses whether the critic improvement identified in the main experiments extends to a generative-policy framework, motivating future in-vestigation of ALBUM with more expressive policy classes.
