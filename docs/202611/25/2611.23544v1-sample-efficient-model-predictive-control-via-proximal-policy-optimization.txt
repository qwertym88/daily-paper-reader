Abstract 

Sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) enables real-time control of complex nonlinear systems but often incurs substantial computational costs due to repeated trajectory simulations. In this paper, we bridge policy optimization in reinforcement learning and sampling-based MPC to im-prove computational efficiency through sample reuse. Starting from a KL-regularized distributional formulation of trajectory optimization, we derive an exact performance-difference identity based on a signed exponential advantage function. This identity motivates two algorithms, PPO-MPPI and SPO-MPPI, which adapt proximal policy optimization techniques to sampling-based MPC, enabling multiple optimization updates using previously evaluated trajectories without additional forward simulations. We establish population-level monotonic improvement guarantees for both methods and prove convergence to first-order stationarity for PPO-MPPI under fixed-covariance Gaussian sampling distributions. Experiments on quadruped velocity tracking and quadrotor path tracking demonstrate that the proposed methods achieve competitive control performance at sub-stantially lower computational cost than multi-stage sampling approaches. Our results establish a principled connection between policy optimization and sampling-based MPC and highlight the potential of trajectory reuse for sample-efficient predictive control. 

Keywords: Model predictive control, trajectory optimization, importance sampling, proximal pol-icy optimization, simple policy optimization 

1. Introduction 

Sampling-based model predictive control (MPC), such as model predictive path integral (MPPI) control (Williams et al., 2017) and the cross-entropy method (CEM) (Botev et al., 2013), has emerged as a popular framework for real-time control of complex robotic systems (Alvarez-Padilla et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2025). These methods operate by generating candidate control sequences, evaluating their performance through forward simulation of the system dynamics, and using the resulting trajectory costs to update control actions. Their ability to handle nonlinear, nonsmooth, and black-box dynamics without requiring explicit derivatives, together with their natural paral-lelizability on GPUs, has enabled successful applications across a wide range of robotic control tasks (Honda, 2026). Despite these advantages, sampling-based MPC can be computationally de-manding, as evaluating large numbers of candidate trajectories often requires expensive forward simulations, particularly for high-fidelity simulators involving contact dynamics, collision check-ing, or learned dynamics models. A closely related challenge arises in reinforcement learning (RL), where collecting trajecto-ries through interactions with the environment can be prohibitively expensive. To improve sample

efficiency, modern policy optimization algorithms seek to extract more learning progress from pre-viously collected trajectories. Notably, proximal policy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) and simple policy optimization (SPO) (Xie et al., 2024) employ likelihood-ratio-based objectives that enable multiple policy updates using the same batch of experience, while incorporating mech-anisms to mitigate excessive distribution shift. These advances suggest a natural opportunity to bridge policy optimization in RL and sampling-based MPC: Can we leverage sample-reuse tech-niques from RL to improve the computational efficiency of sampling-based MPC without sacrificing control performance? 

In this paper, we bridge these two paradigms by developing a policy optimization framework for sample-efficient MPC. Our starting point is a distributional formulation of trajectory optimiza-tion, in which a general parameterized distribution over open-loop control sequences is optimized by minimizing a KL-regularized free-energy objective (Fazlyab et al., 2026). By expressing this objective under a fixed sampling distribution through a change of measure, we enable multiple opti-mization steps using the same batch of evaluated trajectories, without requiring additional forward simulations. Building on this formulation, we introduce two complementary update mechanisms inspired by modern policy optimization: PPO-MPPI, which employs likelihood-ratio clipping, and SPO-MPPI, which incorporates regularization to mitigate distribution shift. Both methods oper-ate within the same optimization framework and are implemented in a receding-horizon fashion, combining sample reuse with online feedback control. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • Free-energy performance-difference identity. Inspired by the classical performance-difference identity in RL (Kakade and Langford, 2002), we derive an exact analogue for sampling-based MPC by defining a signed exponential advantage function. This identity establishes a direct con-nection between free-energy minimization and advantage-based policy optimization, providing the theoretical foundation for sample reuse. • Proximal policy optimization for MPC. We develop PPO-MPPI and SPO-MPPI, which enable multiple optimization updates using previously evaluated trajectories through likelihood-ratio ob-jectives. We establish population-level monotonic improvement guarantees for both methods and prove convergence to first-order stationarity for PPO-MPPI under fixed-covariance Gaussian sam-pling distributions. • Efficient robotic control through sample reuse. We evaluate the proposed methods on quadruped velocity tracking and quadrotor path tracking. The results demonstrate competitive control perfor-mance with substantially lower computational cost than multi-stage sampling approaches, high-lighting the benefits of reusing trajectories rather than repeatedly simulating new ones. 

1.1. Related Work 

Sampling-based MPC has been studied from several perspectives, including control-as-inference (Levine, 2018; Honda, 2026; Wang et al., 2026), KL-regularized optimization (Williams et al., 2015, 2017), and mirror descent (Miyashita et al., 2018; Okada and Taniguchi, 2018). Particularly relevant to our work, Fazlyab et al. (2026) derive a reduced free-energy objective over general parameterized sampling distributions and show that classical MPPI, under a fixed-covariance Gaussian parameter-ization, corresponds to a preconditioned gradient update. We build on this formulation to develop a sample-reusing optimization framework for general parameterized control distributions.

A central challenge in sampling-based MPC is the computational cost of trajectory evaluation, which has motivated methods for improving sample efficiency. The improved cross-entropy method (iCEM) reuses promising trajectories across optimization iterations (Pinneri et al., 2021), while importance-sampling-based methods reuse previously evaluated candidates in black-box optimiza-tion (Pourchot et al., 2018; Shirakawa et al., 2018). However, the effectiveness of sample reuse depends on the discrepancy between the updated distribution and the distribution that generated the samples. Recent work by Walker et al. (2026) develops a trust-region stochastic search framework for MPC that minimizes expected trajectory cost subject to KL-divergence and optional entropy constraints. A similar concern arises in policy optimization for reinforcement learning, where trust-region methods constrain policy updates to limit distribution shift (Schulman et al., 2015; Achiam et al., 2017). PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and SPO (Xie et al., 2024) further enable multiple updates using the same batch of experience through likelihood-ratio clipping and regularization, respectively. Motivated by these developments, we directly optimize the reduced free-energy ob-jective using previously evaluated trajectories, incorporating PPO- and SPO-inspired mechanisms to mitigate distribution shift during repeated updates.

3. Proximal Policy Optimization for Sampling-Based MPC 

In this section, we establish a connection between policy optimization in reinforcement learning and sampling-based model predictive control. In reinforcement learning, the classical performance-difference identity of (Kakade and Langford, 2002) characterizes the difference in expected returns between two policies in terms of an advantage function. Building on this perspective, we derive an analogous exact performance-difference identity for the free-energy objective developed in Sec-tion 2, using a change of measure and a signed exponential advantage function. This identity mo-tivates a PPO-style clipped surrogate that admits a population-level improvement guarantee while enabling repeated updates using previously evaluated trajectories.

4. Experiments 

We compare PPO-MPPI and its SPO-MPPI variant against Vanilla-MPPI and DIAL-MPC (Xue et al., 2025) on quadruped velocity tracking and quadrotor path tracking tasks. All methods opti-mize spline knots that are interpolated to generate continuous action trajectories. Following (Xue et al., 2025), we apply action-level annealing by increasing the sampling variance for knots farther along the planning horizon. DIAL-MPC additionally performs five stages of trajectory-level an-nealing, whereas PPO-MPPI and SPO-MPPI reuse sampled trajectories for multiple optimization steps. Additional experimental details are provided in the appendix.

5. Conclusion 

This paper established a connection between policy optimization in reinforcement learning and sampling-based MPC through the free-energy formulation of trajectory optimization. We derived an exact performance-difference identity and introduced PPO-MPPI and SPO-MPPI, which enable multiple optimization updates using previously sampled trajectories. We established population-level monotonic improvement guarantees for both methods and convergence to first-order station-arity for PPO-MPPI under fixed-covariance Gaussian sampling. Experiments on quadruped loco-motion and quadrotor tracking demonstrated that the proposed methods achieve competitive control performance with substantially lower computational cost than multi-stage sampling approaches. These results highlight the potential of proximal policy optimization as a computationally efficient framework for sampling-based predictive control.

6.7. Experiment Details 

For all tasks, we use a physics simulation time step of 0.01 s and hold each control input constant for two physics steps, resulting in a control period of 0.02 s . All methods optimize six quadratic-spline knots with adjacent knots separated by five control steps, yielding a 26-step prediction horizon of 

0.52 s . Each sampling stage evaluates 1,024 trajectories with τ = 0 .25 . We run each controller for 500 control steps, corresponding to 10 s of simulated time. We use five trajectory-level annealing steps for DIAL-MPC. For PPO-MPPI, we set the clipping ratio to ϵ = 0 .9; for SPO-MPPI, we set ϵ = 20 . Both PPO-MPPI and SPO-MPPI optimize the spline knots using Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2014) with a learning rate of 0.02 and 100 gradient updates per MPC iteration. All trajectory rollouts are simulated in parallel using MJX on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 GPU. The complete Trust-Region PPO-MPPI procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1.
