# Learning Control-Relevant Koopman Representations from Input-Output Trajectories 

Maria Bartzioka a, ∗, Mohammad Khosravi a

> aDelft Center for Systems and Control, Delft University of Technology

Abstract 

Data-driven Koopman representations provide finite-dimensional linear surrogates for nonlinear dynamics, but predictive accuracy alone does not ensure suitability for output-feedback control. The lifted state must be recoverable from measured outputs, the model must adequately represent the effect of the applied input, and the data must be sufficiently informative for identification. This paper develops a continuous-time framework for learning control-relevant Koopman representations using only measured input-output trajectories. A causal encoder constructs the lifted state from a finite input-output window, and an additive lifted model is identified by integral regression. We distinguish observability, input-map compatibility, controllability, and data informativity. We characterize compatibility with a constant lifted input matrix, quantify input-map mismatch, and, under exact lifting assumptions, relate iterated Lie brackets of the nonlinear system to the controllable subspace of the lifted realization. A covariance-based normalization controls the scale of the finite-horizon observability criterion, which is optimized subject to separate bounds on output-prediction and temporal-consistency errors. Under the stated exactness and observability assumptions, the encoded lifted state depends only on the current nonlinear state. For approximate representations, we bound the difference between encodings of distinct past trajectories ending at the same state. An exactly liftable controlled system provides analytical ground truth, while a controlled Duffing oscillator illustrates the approximate setting, for which an informative exact additive lifting on an open set is shown to be impossible. On the exact benchmark, encoder initialization determines whether the learned representation recovers the analytical lift. In the reported Duffing experiments, observability promotion improves nominal Gramian measures without improving observer-based estimation or tracking, while updating the lifted-state estimate through the encoder at every sampling instant has a larger effect on tracking accuracy. 

Keywords: Koopman operator, nonlinear systems, observability, data informativity, controllability, system identification, model predictive control 

1. Introduction 

Koopman operator theory provides a framework for describing nonlinear dynamics through the linear evolu-tion of functions of the state [33, 41, 39, 12]. For an autonomous nonlinear system, observables spanning a finite-dimensional subspace invariant under the Koop-man generator evolve according to a linear differential equation [32]. Dynamic mode decomposition and its extensions approximate finite-dimensional Koopman rep-resentations from data [52, 63]. This perspective is attrac-tive for control because finite-dimensional lifted models can retain nonlinear features of the original dynamics while remaining compatible with linear estimation and model predictive control (MPC) [35, 48, 8]. Controlled ex-tensions of Koopman and dynamic-mode-decomposition methods incorporate measured inputs into lifted predic-tors [49, 50, 62, 35]; moreover the observables defining the representation can also be learned from data rather than fixed in advance [57, 37, 45, 36, 28, 29, 20]. This flexi-bility raises the question of which properties the learned

representation should possess for output-feedback control. Predictive accuracy alone does not resolve this ques-tion. When the full nonlinear state is not measured, the controller must infer the lifted state from the available outputs. For example, deep subspace encoders use past inputs and outputs to initialize models trained with a truncated prediction loss [7]. However, a representation may predict measured trajectories accurately once its initial lifted state is supplied and still be poorly suited to output feedback if distinct lifted states generate only weakly distinguishable output trajectories. This raises questions about the observability of the learned repre-sentation and the sensitivity of lifted-state recovery to measurement noise. Observability of Koopman repre-sentations has been studied from nonlinear and output-based viewpoints [40, 47, 46]. Koopman-based observers exploit lifted dynamics for state estimation [56, 55]. Re-lated Kazantzis–Kravaris/Luenberger-type observers seek transformed state variables with linear dynamics driven by the measured output [2, 25]. For autonomous systems, Takens’ embedding theorem establishes that, under suit-able genericity assumptions, delay coordinates formed from a scalar observable can provide an embedding of the underlying state manifold [58]. This motivates the arXiv:2611.26559v1 [eess.SY] 19 Nov 2026 use of delayed measurements in Koopman-based repre-sentations [4, 10, 3, 26]. For controlled systems, the effect of the applied inputs must also be accounted for when constructing a representation from measured tra-jectories [44]. These considerations motivate learning representations whose lifted states can be reliably dis-tinguished from measured output trajectories. For a fixed normalization, weak output sensitivity to changes in the lifted state makes its recovery sensitive to mea-surement noise. We therefore optimize a regularized log-determinant criterion of the finite-horizon observability Gramian after covariance-based normalization, subject to separate bounds on output-prediction and temporal-consistency errors. The latter measure agreement between model-propagated lifted states and encodings of the corre-sponding later input-output windows. Whether improve-ments in the nominal observability measure translate into better estimation and control is assessed separately. Neural encoders that map past input-output measure-ments to latent states have been used in nonlinear system identification and Koopman learning [22, 7, 38, 23]. In the framework proposed in the current paper, a causal en-coder constructs the lifted state from a finite input-output window. For the observer-based controller, this encoding initializes the lifted-state estimate, which is subsequently propagated and corrected using the learned model and incoming measurements. A representation constructed from a trajectory window need not depend only on the current nonlinear state. We first establish sufficient condi-tions under which an exact lifted model assigns the same encoded lifted state to distinct past trajectories ending at the same nonlinear state. We then derive a bound on the difference between these encodings in terms of their output-prediction errors and the smallest eigenvalue of the observability Gramian. This second result addresses the approximate setting relevant to learned models. Recovering the lifted state from measured input-output trajectories addresses the estimation component of output-feedback control. Predicting the effect of future control actions additionally requires an appropriate representa-tion of the input in the lifted dynamics. First, for a control-affine nonlinear system, the effect of the input on the lifted dynamics is generally state dependent. An additive linear surrogate instead represents this effect by a constant lifted input matrix. State-dependent and bilinear Koopman formulations represent this dependence more explicitly [19, 9, 14, 30, 24, 44]. An additive model with a constant input matrix therefore imposes an ad-ditional structural restriction, whose compatibility with the chosen representation is examined in Section 3. Sec-ond, controllability of the learned realization (A, B ) and informativity of the data used to identify A and B are dif-ferent properties. The former concerns how the input acts on the learned realization, whereas the latter concerns whether the data contain sufficient independent excita-tion to identify its autonomous and forced components [61, 17, 60], which can be compromised under feedback without sufficient independent excitation [21, 15]. Park and Hwang [47] study observability of Koopman representations and develop an output-based identifica-tion method using an observable canonical construction. Strässer et al. [54] construct a bilinear surrogate from input-output trajectories and develop an output-feedback controller with closed-loop stability guarantees under their stated assumptions. These studies address structural ob-servability and guaranteed output-feedback control, re-spectively. For the additive models considered here, we examine the interplay between finite-horizon output sen-sitivity, prediction accuracy, and compatibility with a constant lifted input matrix. Our focus is on learning an additive continuous-time representation by optimizing a regularized log-determinant criterion of its finite-horizon observability Gramian, after covariance-based normaliza-tion and subject to separate prediction and consistency bounds. We also examine the structural restrictions of this model class. This formulation builds on our earlier observability-aware learning framework for autonomous discrete-time systems [5], which combined an observability criterion with invariance, output-fit, and reconstruction terms in a weighted loss. The present work extends this frame-work to controlled, non-autonomous, continuous-time systems using measured input-output trajectories and promotes observability subject to separate bounds on output-prediction and temporal-consistency errors. Anonlinear full-state reconstruction map is not required for the proposed output-feedback implementation, which uses lifted-state estimates and the linear output map C. We use a continuous-time formulation to express input-map compatibility and the relation to nonlinear Lie brackets directly in terms of the system’s vector fields. Integral regression identifies the corresponding model matrices without constructing numerical derivative targets. For sampled estimation and predictive control, the learned nominal model is discretized under zero-order-held inputs as in (38). A directly identified discrete-time predictor is an alternative, but the structural conditions established here concern the continuous-time model and cannot be transferred to that predictor without a separate analysis. The main contributions are: 1. A continuous-time formulation for learning observability-aware Koopman representations from measured input-output trajectories, in which finite-horizon observability is promoted subject to sepa-rate prescribed bounds on output-prediction and temporal-consistency errors. This formulation makes the trade-off between observability and predictive accuracy explicit. Covariance-based normalization controls the scale of the observability criterion, while a nominal Gaussian estimation model relates the objective to uncertainty in lifted-state recovery (Proposition 4). 2. An analysis connecting trajectory-based encoders to state-based Koopman liftings and clarifying the struc-tural restrictions of additive lifted models. Under the stated exactness, observability, and trajectory as-sumptions, the encoded lifted state depends only on the current nonlinear state (Proposition 1). For ap-proximate representations, prediction errors and the 2smallest observability Gramian eigenvalue bound the difference between encodings of distinct past trajec-tories ending at the same state (Proposition 2). We also characterize compatibility with a constant lifted input matrix and, under exact lifting assumptions, relate iterated Lie brackets of the nonlinear system to the lifted controllable subspace (Proposition 3). The resulting rank bound (Corollary 1) shows that an exact additive lift with more coordinates than the original nonlinear state cannot be fully controllable. 3. An identification and output-feedback control frame-work that combines the learned representation with integral regression, lifted-state estimation, and model predictive control, while distinguishing data informa-tivity from controllability. Evaluation on an exactly liftable controlled system and a controlled Duffing oscillator examines the practical implications of the analysis. The numerical results distinguish improve-ments in nominal observability from improvements in estimation and control: in the reported Duffing ex-periments, improved Gramian measures do not yield improved observer-based estimation or tracking. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion 2 introduces the nonlinear system, Koopman lifting, and output-based recovery of lifted states. Section 3 stud-ies the control-relevant properties of the additive lifted realization. Section 4 presents the learning and iden-tification procedure and the output-feedback controller. Section 5 presents validation on an exactly liftable con-trolled system and the controlled Duffing study. Section 6 concludes the paper.

4. Identification and output-feedback control 

In this section we develop a computational procedure for learning an additive lifted model of the form (14) from measured input-output trajectories and then using it for output-feedback control. We initially construct lifted coordinates from input-output windows and nor-malize their scale (Section 4.1). For a fixed encoder, we then identify the model matrices by integral regression (Section 4.2). The encoder is initially trained for output prediction and consistency with the identified dynamics, and subsequently refined to promote finite-horizon ob-servability while keeping these two errors within separate prescribed bounds (Section 4.3). Each encoder update entails recomputing the normalization and identifying the corresponding model matrices. Finally, the learned model is used for online lifted-state estimation and predictive control (Section 4.4).

5. Numerical examples 

The numerical examples examine three questions: whether identification from input-output windows recov-ers a known state-based lifting, whether observability optimization reduces dependence on past trajectories, 12 and whether an improved nominal Gramian is accompa-nied by better estimation and output-feedback tracking. The first system admits an exact additive lift, so its an-alytical realization provides a reference for the learned coordinates. The controlled Duffing system admits no informative exact additive lift under the stated assump-tions, and therefore tests the method in an intrinsically approximate setting. Identification uses measured inputs and outputs. Non-linear states are available in simulation for data generation and evaluation, but are not supplied as training targets to the encoder or the integral regression. We distinguish the error constraints imposed on identification data from per-formance on independent test trajectories. The reported standard deviations describe variation across identifica-tion runs; they are not confidence intervals or tests of statistical significance.

6. Conclusions 

This paper has developed a continuous-time framework for identifying control-relevant Koopman representations from input-output trajectories without full-state mea-surements. The method combines integral identification with finite-horizon observability optimization subject to separate bounds on output-prediction and lifted-state consistency errors. Coordinate normalization controls the scale of the observability criterion, which has an estima-tion interpretation under the stated nominal Gaussian assumptions. The analysis distinguishes observability, input-map compatibility, controllability, and data informativity. Un-der the stated exactness and observability assumptions, the representation constructed from an input-output win-dow depends only on the current nonlinear state. The numerical examples show that improved nominal observability does not by itself ensure better estimation or control. For the exactly liftable system, subspace initialization recovers a representation close to the ana-lytical lift in the absence of measurement noise. Random initialization can instead retain dependence on past tra-jectories despite accurate output prediction. For Duffing, the observability criterion improves without improving observer-based prediction or mean final-second tracking accuracy. Recomputing the lifted-state estimate from each measurement window has a larger effect on tracking. The Gramian improvement persists under exact covari-ance normalization, although its magnitude depends on the normalization convention. Infeasible nominal steady targets further show that successful tracking does not establish exact equilibrium compatibility. These findings motivate observability criteria that ac-count for model error and the implemented estimator, together with output-feedback controllers based on certi-fied error bounds. The present empirical constraints and nominal controller do not provide nonlinear closed-loop stability or offset-free tracking guarantees.
