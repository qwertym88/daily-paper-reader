# Safe Reinforcement Learning with Hard Constraints: Policy Gradient Methods with Intersample Guarantees 

Vipul K. Sharma and S. Sivaranjani 

Abstract — We develop a policy-gradient (PG) reinforcement learning (RL) framework with hard inter-sample control bar-rier function (CBF)-based safety constraints for nonlinear sampled-data systems. We first design a class of stochastic policies with support truncated to sampling-period-dependent safe control sets, that guarantee safety-by-construction at sam-pling instants. We then propose a model-free PG algorithm that searches over the class of safe-by-design policies and establish sufficient conditions on sampling-period such that any action sampled from safe control sets remains safe throughout the sampling interval, providing inter-sample guarantees. We further establish finite-time convergence and sample complexity guarantees for the proposed PG algorithm and characterize its global optimality under gradient dominance. Finally, we illustrate inter-sample safety guarantees on a safe quadrotor navigation environment. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables learning control poli-cies by interacting with an unknown environment. How-ever, in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driv-ing and robotics, hard safety constraints must be satisfied throughout learning and deployment. This requirement is particularly challenging in model-free RL, where stochastic exploration is used to improve the policy and can lead to constraint violations before an effective controller is learned. For physical systems, these constraints must hold along the continuous-time trajectories generated by the controller, including between successive control updates. In the RL literature, safety is typically addressed through constrained Markov decision processes, primal-dual meth-ods, or penalty-based approaches [1]. These methods regulate constraint violations through expected or cumulative costs, but do not, in general, guarantee pointwise satisfaction of hard constraints during exploration. In learning-based con-trol, hard constraints are commonly enforced through control barrier function (CBF)-based safety filters, where the actions of a potentially unsafe RL policy are projected onto a safe control set [2]–[6]. Although such filters can ensure safety under suitable conditions, the projection changes the policy applied to the system. Consequently, the convergence and optimality guarantees of the underlying unconstrained RL algorithm do not directly extend to the filtered policy [7]. Another line of work incorporates hard constraints into the policy class itself and optimizes directly over these safe-by-design policies. Accounting for the constrained ex-ploration distribution in the objective and its gradient al-lows convergence to be analyzed for the policies actually

More recently, SafePG [8] and Safe-PG-LQR [9] develop safe policy-gradient methods with optimality guarantees for nonlinear control and constrained LQR, respectively. These results build on policy optimization over structured classes, for which convergence and optimality can be characterized through properties such as gradient dominance [10], [11]. These safe-by-design frameworks only consider discrete-time dynamics. In most practical control applications, plants evolve with continuous-time dynamics, and are controlled using digital hardware, thus yielding sampled-data dynam-ics. In such cases, safe learning approaches developed for discrete-time dynamics, enforcing safety constraints only at sampling instances, can result in potential safety violations when deployed in sampled-data systems. Sampled-data CBF methods address this by ensuring intersample safety through an additional barrier margin at each sampling instant [12]– [14], including settings with uncertainty in the barrier dy-namics [15]. Other approaches safeguard RL controllers in continuous time through reachability-based action projec-tion [16] and sampled-data funnel control [17]. In this paper, we develop a safe policy optimization frame-work for nonlinear sampled-data systems with intersample safety, convergence, and optimality guarantees. We consider a stochastic policy class that facilitates exploration, truncate its support to enforce CBF-type safety constraints by design, and optimize over this safe-by-design policy class. Our focus is to incorporate intersample safety into this stochastic policy class and analyze convergence for the resulting RL objective. Our main contributions are as follows:  

> •

First, we construct a sampling-aware safe control set using a lower bound on the CBF expression and a bound on its variation over each sampling interval. We establish a sufficient condition on the sampling period for feasibility and show that selecting inputs from this set preserves state and actuator constraints throughout each interval. Thus, truncating the support of a base stochastic policy to this set gives a safe-by-design policy class with intersample guarantees during exploration and deployment.  

> •

Second, we derive the score-function and policy-gradient expressions for this truncated policy class. We construct an unbiased model-free policy gradient esti-mator, establish smoothness and variance bounds, and characterize the expected number of plant interactions required to obtain a gradient estimate. arXiv:2611.21987v1 [eess.SY] 16 Nov 2026 • Third, we develop a sampling-aware safe policy gra-dient (PG) algorithm, SASPG , and establish finite-time convergence and sample-complexity guarantees under weak gradient dominance. These guarantees character-ize optimality while preserving intersample safety in nonlinear sample-data systems. Finally, we demonstrate the framework on a safe quadrotor navigation problem.

VII. C ONCLUSION 

We developed SASPG , a model-free policy gradient frame-work for nonlinear sampled-data systems with hard CBF-based safety constraints. We constructed stochastic policies supported on sampling-period-dependent safe control sets and established conditions ensuring constraint satisfaction throughout each sampling interval. We provided finite-time convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for the pro-posed algorithm and characterized its optimality under weak gradient dominance. We illustrated intersample safety on a quadrotor navigation task, where the learned policy reaches the goal while avoiding an obstacle at all times. Future work will explore alternative sampling and input-hold schemes, including adaptive sampling through event-triggered updates, to improve learning and control performance and reduce control updates while preserving intersample safety.
