# Where Global Context and Weight Sharing Work in Compact Vision Backbones 

Michael Schroeder 

GE Appliances, a Haier company 

michael.schroeder2@geappliances.com 

Abstract 

Compact convolutional backbones such as EfficientNet achieve strong parameter efficiency through local depthwise separable convolutions, yet two structural ques-tions remain central to hybrid design: where does global self-attention improve recognition over pure convolution , and can repeated blocks share weights without losing representational capacity? We investigate both questions on ImageNet-1k (50,000-image validation) using controlled EfficientNet-B0 experiments. First, we show that wrapping a classification backbone in multi-scale feature pyramids (BiFPN) fails to outperform a plain MBConv trunk ( 69 .99% –71 .81% vs. 72 .86% 

plain B0) until paired with stage attention ( 73 .15% ). In contrast, keeping the MBConv trunk intact and inserting lightweight zero-gated residual self-attention adapters at stages P3 (28 ×28 windowed), P4 (14 ×14 global), and P5 (7×7 global) reaches 74.02% standalone and outperforms a bit-for-bit matched plain-B0 control by +0.954 percentage points (73 .88% vs. 72 .92% ) across an 88-epoch stateless re-play schedule, with learned attention amplitude rising monotonically with semantic depth ( 27 .2% at P3 → 45 .4% at P4 → 66 .3% at P5). Second, we evaluate recur-rent weight sharing across repeated MBConv blocks with pass-specific BatchNorm statistics ( +1 , 536 parameters per pass). While post-hoc kernel averaging degrades accuracy to 17 .03% and single-template stage tying ( −27 .6% parameters) plateaus at 66 .10% , alternating two shared block templates ( [B1, A, B, A ]) in Stage 6 

cuts total model parameters by 10 .9% (582 K weights) and recovers by +10 .82 

points in a single epoch to 70.92% (−1.84 points vs. unshared control). 

1 Introduction: Targeted Global Context and Recurrent Depth 

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers [Dosovitskiy et al., 2021, Touvron et al., 2021] contribute complementary inductive biases to visual recognition. Local 3 × 3 and 5 × 5

depthwise convolutions encode translation equivariance and capture high-frequency spatial edges efficiently at high resolution, whereas multi-head self-attention (MHSA) [Srinivas et al., 2021, Dai et al., 2021] models long-range dependencies across distant object parts once spatial resolution has been reduced. Recent mobile-oriented hybrid architectures such as MobileViT [Mehta and Rastegari, 2022] and EdgeViTs [Pan et al., 2022] have demonstrated that interleaving local convolutions with global or sparse self-attention improves accuracy-efficiency trade-offs on edge devices. Concurrently, research on parameter sharing in one-shot Neural Architecture Search (NAS) [Pham et al., 2018, Liu et al., 2019] and implicitly recurrent CNNs [Savarese and Maire, 2019] has established that convolutional layers can reuse weights across subgraphs or sequential passes, raising the question of how best to share parameters across repeated inverted-residual (MBConv) blocks in compact vision backbones. When modifying a compact backbone such as EfficientNet-B0 [Tan and Le, 2019] ( 5.32 M parameters), untargeted structural modifications can degrade classification accuracy along two axes. First, object detectors routinely employ Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Networks (BiFPN) [Tan et al., 2020] to fuse

multi-scale features for bounding-box localization; however, in whole-image classification, routing features through bidirectional pyramids introduces additional rescaling and projection layers that compete with the primary classification head. Second, while Stages 4, 5, and 6 of EfficientNet-B0 repeat multiple identical-shape MBConv blocks regularized with stochastic depth [Huang et al., 2016], forcing every repeat within a stage to share a single weight tensor ( [B1, A, A, A ]) constrains the stage to iterating a single transformation, limiting hierarchical feature refinement [Savarese and Maire, 2019] in much the same way that aggressive weight sharing induces gradient coupling in one-shot NAS supernets [Pham et al., 2018, Liu et al., 2019]. In this study, we evaluate both design questions on ImageNet-1k [Deng et al., 2009] across all 50,000 validation images. We show that (1) sparse zero-gated stage attention ( P3/P 4/P 5) yields a verified 

+0 .954 -point accuracy improvement under bit-for-bit matched training without heavy pyramid necks, and (2) alternating two-template weight sharing ( [B1, A, B, A ]) in Stage 6 eliminates 10 .9% of total model parameters while retaining 4.82 points higher accuracy than single-template stage tying.

3 Sparse Zero-Gated Stage Attention ( P3/P 4/P 5)

Prior hybrid architectures integrate self-attention by replacing entire convolutional stages or bottle-neck blocks: BoTNet [Srinivas et al., 2021] substitutes spatial convolutions in the final c5 stage with global MHSA, CoAtNet [Dai et al., 2021] stacks full Transformer stages across S3 and S4, Mobile-ViT [Mehta and Rastegari, 2022] unfolds spatial feature maps into patches for global Transformer blocks, and EdgeViTs [Pan et al., 2022] alternate local-global-local blocks across stages. Rather than replacing MBConv blocks or re-architecting the trunk, our Sparse Attention-Convolution B0 design preserves the exact MBConv and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) trunk of EfficientNet-B0 and inserts three lightweight residual attention adapters at the outputs of Stage 3 ( P3, 28 × 28 resolution, 40 

channels), Stage 5 ( P4, 14 × 14 resolution, 112 channels), and Stage 7 ( P5, 7 × 7 resolution, 320 

channels). To keep quadratic attention cost bounded at 28 ×28 , P3 uses 7×7 windowed self-attention, whereas P4 and P5 use global multi-head self-attention. Each adapter applies pre-layer normalization (Pre-LN) [R. Xiong et al., 2020], multi-head self-attention, a linear projection, and a zero-initialized learnable gate αs:

ys = xs + αs · Proj s(MHSA s(LN( xs))) , αs(0) = 0 .

Initializing αs = 0 ensures that at step 0 the network is functionally identical to plain EfficientNet-B0, preventing uncalibrated attention gradients from perturbing early convolutional feature learning. 

3.1 Bit-for-Bit Matched Stateless Verification ( +0 .954 pp) 

To isolate the causal contribution of the P3/P 4/P 5 attention adapters without confounding differences in data order or initialization, we executed a strictly paired 88-epoch experiment in which Plain-B0 and Sparse Attention B0 started from the same backbone initialization and consumed bit-for-bit identical stateless image batches across all 112.73 million training presentations (Figure 3). Across the validation trajectory, Sparse Attention maintained a consistent accuracy advantage over Plain-B0 at every full-validation checkpoint from epoch 60 through 88, leading by +1 .216 percentage points at epoch 63 ( 71 .102% vs. 69 .886% ), by +0 .954 points at the selected checkpoints (73 .878% at e86 vs. 72 .924% at e87), and by +0 .914 points at terminal epoch 88 (73 .794% vs. 72 .880% ). This accuracy gain incurred only modest computational overhead: across the final 10 epochs (e79–e88) on matched single-GPU hardware, adding the three sparse attention gates reduced training throughput by 5.5% (479 .8 vs. 507 .6 images/s) and increased peak memory allocation by 9.2% (17 .29 GiB vs. 

15 .83 GiB). 

3.2 Learned Gate Activity Scales Monotonically with Semantic Depth 

To understand how the network allocates capacity between local convolutions and self-attention across scales, we measured the ratio of the gated attention update RMS to the trunk input RMS (∥αs∆xs∥RMS /∥xs∥RMS ) across a fixed class-balanced 2,000-image validation subset. The learned adapters exhibit a consistent depth-dependent trend: at P3 (28 × 28 resolution) , where features primarily encode mid-level edges and local textures, relative branch activity converges to a modest 

27.2% (with gating scalar |αP3 | = 0 .258 ). At P4 (14 × 14 resolution) , where features represent intermediate object part assemblies, relative branch activity rises to 45.4% (|αP4 | = 0 .451 ). By P5

(7 × 7 resolution) , where spatial dimensions are coarse and channels capture whole-object semantic context, relative branch activity reaches 66.3% (|αP5 | = 0 .720 ). Gradient descent thus naturally keeps P3 dominated by local 3 × 3 and 5 × 5 depthwise convolutions while recruiting progressively stronger global self-attention in P4 and P5, providing direct stage-level empirical support for the late-attention design philosophy used in modern hybrid vision architectures [Dai et al., 2021, Srinivas et al., 2021, Mehta and Rastegari, 2022, Pan et al., 2022].

Stage 6 alone contains four 192 -channel MBConv6 blocks ( 5 × 5 depthwise kernels), where each repeated block carries 582 , 384 convolutional weights. Parameter sharing across convolutional operations has been widely studied in one-shot Neural Architecture Search, where ENAS [Pham et al., 2018] and DARTS [Liu et al., 2019] share kernel weights across candidate edges in a directed acyclic graph, and in implicitly recurrent CNNs, where Savarese and Maire [2019] observed that layers learning soft parameter sharing within Wide ResNet stages naturally converge toward 2-layer alternating recurrent loops while keeping transition blocks distinct. We tested whether repeated MBConv blocks within EfficientNet-B0 can similarly share convolutional and Squeeze-and-Excitation kernels while maintaining separate BatchNorm running statistics and affine scales per repeat pass (since feature distributions evolve across sequential residual steps). Crucially, keeping BatchNorm layers pass-specific introduces negligible parameter and memory overhead: each pass-specific BatchNorm set contributes only +1 , 536 output-channel parameters (∼ 6 KB in FP32, or < 0.03% of the 5.32 M-parameter backbone), compared to the 582 , 384 

convolutional and Squeeze-and-Excitation weights saved per shared Stage 6 block. 5 Scope, Limitations, and Conclusion 

Methodological Scope. Our B0/B1/B2 scale baselines ( 72 .86% /74 .91% /75 .95% ) use an 88-epoch ReLU6/SGD recipe rather than the 350–400-epoch Swish/RMSProp protocol of Tan and Le [2019], and differ in batch size across scales; they serve as controlled checkpoint anchors rather than a cross-scale law. Separate one-epoch CPU architecture pilots (including a differentiable TensorFlow reference formulation of DCNv4 [Y. Xiong et al., 2024] at 28 .71% FP32 and 28 .69% under selective LiteRT INT8/FP32 PTQ) verified operator portability and export pipelines rather than converged accuracy. Both the matched 88-epoch Sparse Attention pair ( +0 .954 pp) and the one-epoch GPU shared-repeat continuations ( 70 .92% ) are controlled single-seed studies; in particular, the shared-repeat experiments evaluate rapid warm-start recovery over a fixed one-epoch budget rather than multi-epoch (5–10 epoch) fine-tuning or full from-scratch convergence. 

Conclusion. Structural efficiency in compact vision backbones depends on respecting the functional hierarchy of convolutional stages. Heavy cross-scale feature pyramids (BiFPN) add overhead without beating a clean MBConv trunk in classification, whereas lightweight zero-gated self-attention adapters at P3/P 4/P 5 naturally concentrate their activity in deep semantic stages ( 66 .3% RMS at P5) and deliver a verified +0 .954 -point matched accuracy gain ( 73 .88% vs. 72 .92% ; 74 .02% standalone). Simultaneously, in wide deep stages (Stage 6), alternating two shared block templates ( [B1, A, B, A ])with pass-specific BatchNorm statistics cuts 10 .9% of total model parameters while recovering to 

70 .92% in a single continuation epoch—providing a practical blueprint for compact, parameter-efficient hybrid vision architectures.
