Abstract 

We provide a first-order model-free policy gradient (PG) framework for learning undiscounted infinite-horizon linear quadratic regulator (LQR) policies with global optimality and convergence guarantees. We first construct a stochastic wrapper policy class parameterized by the LQR gain to enable exploration around a deterministic linear control law. We analyze the additional cost induced by exploration over infinite-horizon, and establish a weak gradient dominance condition that characterizes global optimality under exploration. We then construct a model-free first-order PG algorithm using sampled trajectory information, without identifying the dynamics or approximating a value function. Finally, we establish finite-time convergence guarantees, providing the sufficient number of samples required for near-global optimality with our proposed PG algorithms. 

Keywords: LQR, First-order Policy Optimization, Model-free Policy Gradient, Reinforcement Learning, Optimality, Finite-time Convergence, Sample Complexity 

1. Introduction 

The linear quadratic regulator (LQR) is a canonical optimal control problem for linear dynamical systems, whose optimal policy belongs to the class of stabilizing linear state-feedback controllers (Anderson and Moore, 2007). When the dynamics are known, the optimal gain can be computed by solving the algebraic Riccati equation. When the dynamics are unknown, a standard approach is to identify the system matrices from data and then synthesize a controller using the estimated model (Dean et al., 2020). Both approaches require an explicit model for controller synthesis. In contrast, reinforcement learning (RL) provides a model-free framework where the controller is learned directly from interactions with environment, without a separate system-identification step (Recht, 2018). When the policy structure is known, as in LQR, controller learning can be formulated as policy optimization over the parameters of the structured policy class. In the model-based setting, Fazel et al. (2018) showed that exact policy gradient (PG) updates converge globally to an optimal LQR controller despite the nonconvex landscape, by establishing gradient dominance. Subsequent work has related gradient dominance to hidden convexity, providing a unified analysis for LQR ( ?? ). More broadly, ? connect policy improvement to global optimality of stationary policies, with applications to discounted LQR. In the model-free setting, (Fazel et al., 2018) adopts a zeroth-order approach, estimating the gradients by perturbing the controller gain and evaluating its corresponding objective. Related zeroth-order methods provide convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for LQR (Malik et al., 2019; Mohammadi et al., 2020) and for distributed linear-quadratic control ( ?). In such zeroth-order methods, sampling noise can mask the objective change caused by a small gain perturbation, yielding noisy gradient estimates. This motivates first-order approaches that exploit the differentiable policy structure to estimate gradients from trajectory data. Model-free actor-critic methods provide one such approach using a learned value function (Zhou and Lu, 2023; Ju

et al., 2025). However, they require an additional policy-evaluation procedure, and critic estimation errors affect the policy updates even when the quadratic value-function structure is known. We therefore study a direct model-free first-order PG construction for LQR using sampled trajectories and accumulated costs, without system identification or value-function approximation. We develop this first-order PG construction by introducing a stochastic wrapper around a linear feedback controller. This stochastic wrapper facilitates exploration for model-free learning with a deterministic LQR policy. This construction builds on stochastic-policy approaches to learning deterministic controllers (Montenegro et al., 2024), including structured policies such as PID and constrained LQR (Sharma et al., 2025, 2026). However, the undiscounted infinite-horizon setting introduces a critical challenge. Exploration affects both the current input and, through the dynamics, subsequent states and feedback inputs; as a result, persistent exploration can make the expected cumulative cost infinite even when the underlying linear controller is stabilizing. To address this, we introduce decaying exploration and characterize the resulting infinite-horizon objective. Since the exploration cost depends on the gain, finiteness alone does not establish whether optimizing this stochastic objective recovers the optimal classical LQR controller. We show that the stochastic and deterministic objectives share the same unique optimal gain and establish weak gradient dominance for the stochastic objective. Using this relation, we establish finite-time convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for model-free PG, with the effect of exploration explicitly characterized in the performance bound. Our main contributions are as follows: • Learning model-free LQR with exploration. We construct a stochastic wrapper policy centered around the deterministic linear feedback controller allowing exploration by adding a Gaussian noise constructed so as to ensure that infinite-horizon undiscounted objective remains bounded under such stochastic exploration. This allows us to apply the classic PG theorem (Sutton et al., 1999) using stochastic policies during training while learning the optimal underlying deterministic LQR controller. • First-order model-free policy gradient with finite-time convergence and global optimality. 

We derive the exact score functions for the structure stochastic wrapper policy and construct a first-order PG update using sampled states, control inputs, and accumulated costs. We establish weak gradient dominance for the stochastic objective and derive finite-time performance and sample-complexity guarantees for model-free PG, explicitly characterizing the effect of exploration on the performance bounds. 

Organization. We first formulate the model-free LQR problem in Section 2. Then, in Section 3, we introduce the stochastic wrapper policy to enable exploration and show that this exploration preserves the optimal LQR gain. Further, we develop the model-free policy-gradient algorithm in Section 4, and establish global optimality and finite-time convergence results for the proposed algorithm in Section 5. 6. Conclusion 

We developed a first-order model-free PG framework for undiscounted infinite-horizon LQR using a stochastic wrapper policy class centered at linear feedback controllers. We characterized the cost of decaying exploration and showed that minimizing the resulting objective recovers the unique optimal classical LQR gain. The wrapper policy score yields gain updates from sampled states, control inputs, and stage costs, without system identification or value-function approximation. We further established weak gradient dominance and, under the stated estimator conditions, finite-time convergence and sample-complexity guarantees that explicitly account for exploration. Future work includes explicit unbiased gradient estimator design and faster convergence guarantees.
