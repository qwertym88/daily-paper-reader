Abstract 

Training and verifying neural network-based control Lyapunov functions (CLFs) can be computa-tionally demanding. To address this challenge, we propose a framework that jointly learns a CLF and a stabilizing controller using structured neural networks and a projection-based parameteriza-tion. Specifically, we employ Polyak–Łojasiewicz networks (PLNets), which parameterize noncon-vex, positive-definite CLF candidates, and construct a stable-by-design surrogate of the closed-loop dynamics that satisfies the Lyapunov decrease condition by construction. The controller combines linear state feedback with a higher-order correction term resembling Sontag’s universal formula and is trained to align the resulting closed-loop dynamics with the stable-by-design surrogate. Unlike classical approaches based on Sontag’s formula, our framework does not require a known CLF; instead, it learns the CLF and controller simultaneously. We establish local and global stability guarantees for the learned controller and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach through numerical examples. 

Keywords: Control Lyapunov functions, Learning-based Control, Neural Networks 

1. Introduction 

Learning-based control has emerged as a promising approach for the design of controllers for com-plex nonlinear dynamical systems. However, providing stability guarantees for learned controllers remains challenging. Control Lyapunov functions (CLFs) offer a systematic framework for estab-lishing stability and synthesizing stabilizing feedback laws. Given a valid CLF for a control-affine system, a controller can be constructed using Sontag’s universal formula (Sontag, 1989) or control Lyapunov function quadratic programs (CLF-QPs) (Ames et al., 2014). Both approaches, however, require a suitable CLF to be known a priori, whose construction is generally difficult for nonlinear systems. To address this challenge, neural networks have been increasingly employed to parameterize and learn Lyapunov functions (Richards et al., 2018; Chang et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023). Several approaches jointly learn neural Lyapunov functions and control policies, often using counterexample-guided training and formal verification to establish closed-loop stability (Chang et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024). While these methods provide considerable flexibility, training and verifying the Lyapunov decrease condition can be computationally expensive and may require repeated optimization and verification steps. This motivates the development of learning architectures that incorporate Lyapunov conditions directly into their parameterization. To learn stable-by-design dynamics, Kolter and Manek (2019) jointly learn a neural dynamics model and a Lyapunov function, projecting the dynamics to satisfy the Lyapunov decrease condition by construction, thereby eliminating the need to enforce the condition during training or verify it

Extending the learning of stable dynamics to control synthesis, Min et al. Inspired by these approaches, we develop a framework for jointly learning a CLF and a stabi-lizing feedback controller for known, potentially unstable nonlinear systems, rather than learning the underlying dynamics. To this end, we introduce a stable-by-design surrogate of the closed-loop dynamics that satisfies the Lyapunov decrease condition by construction. The controller and CLF are jointly learned by minimizing the discrepancy between the actual closed-loop dynamics and this surrogate. The controller combines linear feedback, derived from the linearization of the nonlinear system, with an additional term inspired by Sontag’s universal formula, without requiring a valid CLF in advance. To parameterize the CLF, we employ Polyak–Łojasiewicz networks (PLNets) introduced in (Wang et al., 2024). Based on bi-Lipschitz neural networks, PLNets provide expressive, potentially nonconvex, Lyapunov candidates with a unique global minimum. By construction, they satisfy pos-itive definiteness, radial unboundedness, and V (0) = 0 , leaving the Lyapunov decrease condition as the central requirement for stability. Combined with the projection-based surrogate formulation, this enables the joint learning of Lyapunov functions and controllers while exploiting structural properties to simplify training and verification. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the problem statement. Section 3 introduces the proposed learning-based control framework and establishes stability guar-antees for the resulting closed-loop system, and Section 4 illustrates its application through two nonlinear control examples.

3. Learning-Based Control Framework 

We propose a learning-based control framework that combines a locally stabilizing linear controller with a nonlinear augmentation, in the spirit of Sontag’s universal formula. We first present our parameterization of CLF candidates by PLNets in Subsection 3.1 and our chosen controller param-eterization consisting of a linear and a higher-order term in Subsection 3.2. Then, we show how the controller and CLF are learned in Subsection 3.3. Afterward, we present local and global stability results for the resulting controller in Subsections 3.4 and 3.5, respectively.

5. Conclusions 

We presented a learning-based framework for jointly synthesizing a CLF and a stabilizing controller for nonlinear control-affine systems. The framework employs PLNets to parameterize positive-definite CLFs and constructs stable-by-design surrogate dynamics through a projection-based pa-rameterization to guide controller learning. Unlike conventional CLF-based methods, the proposed approach requires neither a prescribed CLF nor online optimization. We established sufficient con-ditions for local exponential and global asymptotic stability of the learned closed-loop system. Numerical experiments demonstrated improved stabilization compared with linear feedback and competitive performance relative to Sontag’s formula and CLF-QP controllers. Future work will focus on extending the framework to higher-dimensional systems, incorporat-ing input constraints, and formally verifying the learned controllers.
