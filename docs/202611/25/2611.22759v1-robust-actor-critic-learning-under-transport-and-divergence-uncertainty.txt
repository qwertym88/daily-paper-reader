# Robust Actor–Critic Learning under Transport and Divergence Uncertainty 

## Kunqi Huang ∗ Lican Kang † Hongrui Shu ‡ Jincheng Ying §

Abstract 

Learning reliable control policies requires accounting for uncertainty in transition dynamics and errors introduced by function estimation and action generation. We develop an actor–critic method for discounted robust Markov decision processes with continuous states and actions and an unknown nominal transition law. We consider transition uncertainty sets defined by Wasserstein distance, total variation, Kullback– Leibler divergence, or Pearson chi-square divergence. A conditional diffusion model is fitted once to offline transitions. At each subsequent iteration, we estimate the robust action-value function from current-policy observations using a shared-target minimax criterion, then update a generative actor through exponentially weighted score matching. Regularization preserves exploration and provides the density regularity needed for conditional distribution estimation. Under coverage and regularity conditions, we establish finite-sample bounds for critic estimation, the learned conditional action law, and the robust return of the implemented policy. The bounds distinguish critic estimation error, uncertainty in the nominal transition model, and policy regularization, and characterize how the four robustness radii affect the resulting guarantees.

# 1 Introduction 

Reinforcement learning (RL) uses observed transitions to learn sequential decision rules. The resulting policy is evaluated through its long-run rewards, but its performance also depends on whether the training dynamics represent those encountered at deployment. A robust Markov decision process (MDP) makes this dependence explicit by evaluating policies against a prescribed set of transition laws. State–action rectangular uncertainty permits a conditional worst-case expectation at each decision and leads to robust dynamic programming (Iyengar, 2005; Nilim and El Ghaoui, 2005). When the nominal dynamics are unknown, the uncertainty set is centered on a model learned from data. The resulting statistical error is distinct from the deliberate protection supplied by the robustness radius: both influence policy performance and must be accounted for. This distinction matters in continuous control, where transition laws and action values cannot generally be tabulated. Finite-sample guarantees are available for tabular robust learning with nominal generative access (Xu et al., 2023), offline robust fitted iteration with function approximation (Panaganti et al., 2022), and continuous model-based learning under structured unknown dynamics (Sundhar Ramesh et al., 2024). A common difficulty is that robust policy evaluation contains a nonlinear conditional worst-case expectation. Wasserstein distance, total variation (TV), Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, and Pearson χ2 divergence produce different dual problems and different sensitivities to errors in their nominal centers (Gao and Kleywegt, 2023; Duchi and Namkoong, 2019; Xu et al., 2023). Replacing the nominal law by an empirical or fitted distribution therefore introduces a bias that ordinary conditional regression does not remove. A policy-evaluation criterion must account separately for this bias and finite-sample estimation error. Actor–critic learning couples this evaluation problem with repeated policy improvement. Robust natural actor–critic (RNAC) provides finite-time guarantees with function approxi-mation (Zhou et al., 2023); robust mirror descent supplies relative policy updates (Li et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). Distributionally robust soft actor–critic (DR-SAC) further combines 2learned nominal dynamics with KL-robust, entropy-regularized actor–critic learning (Cui et al., 2026). For continuous actions, a generative actor can represent the relative exponential improvement target without evaluating its normalizing constant. Exponentially reweighted denoising implements this target from current-policy samples (Ma et al., 2025). A remaining statistical challenge is to connect the actor implemented with finite sampling, rather than its ideal target, to subsequent robust policy evaluation and the final policy’s unregularized robust return. Conditional distribution-estimation theory provides a basis for this analysis, but it must be applied to the actual weighted target and combined with robust target and policy-regularization errors. In this work, we develop a robust actor–critic method on continuous state and action cubes for the four transition-uncertainty families. A nominal conditional diffusion model (CDM) is fitted once to offline transitions and then held fixed. Each iteration evaluates the currently implemented policy through a shared-target minimax criterion and learns a regularized exponential policy improvement from weighted current-policy samples. 

## 1.1 Contributions 

• We develop a robust actor–critic method for four rectangular transition-uncertainty families using one fixed nominal conditional diffusion model. Conditional duality supplies robust Bellman targets, and a shared-target minimax criterion estimates the current policy’s action value. Exponentially weighted score learning implements relative policy improvement without evaluating policy densities or target normalizing constants. Gaussian smoothing and uniform exploration provide the regularity required for repeated evaluation of the implemented generative actor. 

• We establish finite-sample bounds for on-policy L2 critic error, conditional action-distribution error, and last-iterate robust return. ReLU DNN approximation and conditional diffusion theory control critic and actor estimation, while retaining nonlinear target bias, nominal-model error, and the distinct effects of the four robustness radii. Robust Bellman contraction connects these errors to policy performance, with a joint 3allocation of critic observations, offline transitions, and samples for critic integration and actor fitting. 

## 1.2 Related work 

Robust learning. Rectangular transition uncertainty supports dynamic programming (Iyengar, 2005; Nilim and El Ghaoui, 2005). Finite-sample robust learning extends beyond this planning formulation: Xu et al. (2023) analyze the same four uncertainty families in tabular episodic problems with nominal generative access, while Panaganti et al. (2022) develop offline TV-robust fitted Q-iteration with function approximation. The latter guarantees retain completeness, dual approximation, and coverage requirements. For continuous dynamics, Sundhar Ramesh et al. (2024) combine active Gaussian-process model learning with TV, KL, and χ2 robustness. RNAC uses nominal on-policy observations with double-sampling or integral-probability-metric uncertainty and analyzes both linear and general function approximation (Zhou et al., 2023). More recently, online general-class guarantees have been expressed through robust Bellman–Eluder complexity (Ghosh et al., 2026). 

Value estimation. Neural fitted-value and policy-iteration methods connect regression error to policy performance (Feng et al., 2023; Jiao et al., 2025). The shared-target criterion used in Schr¨ odinger–F¨ ollmer actor–critic (SFAC) cancels a target’s conditional variance through the difference of two squared losses (Jiao et al., 2026). With nonlinear robust targets, the conditional-mean bias remains, and the auxiliary class must approximate the Bellman images of all candidate critics. Our ReLU DNN classes verify this approximation requirement without imposing unrestricted Bellman completeness. Robust control and robust inference address different targets. Bennett et al. (2024) derive sharp fixed-policy bounds under multiplicative transition perturbations and construct orthogonal, semiparametrically efficient estimators. Here, the policy changes during learning, and the uncertainty is specified by the four transition neighborhoods. DR-SAC is a closer algorithmic comparison: it learns nominal generative dynamics for offline KL-robust soft actor–critic (Cui et al., 2026). Its objective includes policy entropy. Our analysis instead targets unregularized robust return 4and retains the empirical-center bias and the law induced by finite sampling from the learned actor. 

Generative policies. Denoising score matching estimates distributional scores using Gaussian perturbations (Vincent, 2011). Diffusion-QL combines a conditional diffusion policy with value-guided behavior cloning in offline learning (Wang et al., 2023), while Q-score matching relates policy scores to action-value gradients in off-policy learning (Psenka et al., 2024). Q-weighted variational policy optimization (QVPO) weights denoising losses by transformed action values (Ding et al., 2024). Closest to our actor update, Ma et al. (2025) derive reweighted score matching for the relative exponential policy target and express its loss through exponentially weighted samples from the current policy. SFAC realizes a relative tilt through a Schr¨ odinger–F¨ ollmer process (Jiao et al., 2026).

# 5 Conclusion 

We propose an actor–critic framework for four rectangular transition uncertainty families on continuous state and action spaces. A fixed conditional diffusion model estimates nominal transitions, and a second model learns relative policy improvements through weighted denoising. ReLU DNN classes control approximation error for the critic and auxiliary function, while regularized conditional diffusion provides a quantitative policy-improvement bound. Explicit exploration and robust Bellman contraction yield nonasymptotic last-iterate return guarantees with explicit dependence on the uncertainty radius, all three sample sizes, and the model-estimation and integration errors. Theoretical analysis for dependent data and numerical validation of the proposed method are left for future work. 25 Appendix
