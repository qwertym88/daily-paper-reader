# Lyapunov-Inspired Sustained Balance on Dynamically Responsive Supports 

Changyan Li 1, Guiyin Tian 1, Jing Xia 1, and Shiyan Hu 1

Abstract —Conventional balance formulations often assume a rigid, stationary support, decoupling body stabilization from support motion. This assumption becomes inadequate on me-chanically responsive supports, creating a coupled control chal-lenge: corrective body motions intended to restore equilibrium can also excite support motion. We formulate sustained balance as a coupled regulation problem that coordinates equilibrium restoration with suppression of motion induced by the robot’s own corrections. We introduce LISST (Lyapunov-Inspired Sus-tained Stabilization), a learning framework that accounts for how earlier actions shape the conditions for subsequent corrections. It uses recent observations to support immediate adjustments and longer histories to characterize persistent motion, with physical supervision relating the learned representation to robot– support behavior. To evaluate corrective actions, LISST uses coupled dynamics to assess both imbalance and the direction of motion. A Lyapunov-inspired criterion measures progress over finite windows, accommodating the transients required to restore balance. This physical evaluation guides joint-level policy learning through backpropagation through differentiable model rollouts. Privileged guidance focuses training on difficult states reached by the deployment policy and is transferred through distillation. The resulting policy operates using only onboard proprioception. Real-world experiments demonstrate that LISST maintains balance while limiting residual oscillations during continuous interaction with a dynamically responsive support. Index Terms —Balance control, coupled dynamics, legged robots, Lyapunov methods, physics-informed learning, robot– environment interaction. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# BALANCING on a mechanically responsive support re-quires corrective movements that also alter the supporting surface. Whole-body controllers coordinate body motion and contact forces [1], [2], which also affect the support [3]. Shifting the robot can restore static moment balance, while the acceleration and braking needed to reach that configuration can excite support motion. The resulting robot–support motion can persist after the static imbalance has been compensated. Each subsequent correction therefore begins with the motion left by earlier actions. We study sustained balance as the continuous regulation of coupled robot–support motion to es-tablish and maintain equilibrium. We investigate this problem with a quadruped on a passive, pitch-hinged board under changing endpoint loads, preserving physical state and con-troller history throughout the interaction. The robot chooses body and contact adjustments without a prescribed reference motion, using onboard proprioception at deployment.

Output-feedback controllers stabilize simplified robots on unstable supports without direct environmental feedback [4], while coupled robot–support models describe and control their shared dynamics [3]. For quadrupeds on moving platforms, LAS-MP combines adaptive postural control with proprio-ceptive robot and platform state estimation [5]. In sustained balance, each correction begins with the motion left by ear-lier actions, which can change whether the next movement suppresses or amplifies the response. A proprioceptive policy therefore needs information about this ongoing response and an action evaluation that accounts for it. We introduce LISST (Lyapunov-Inspired Sustained Stabi-lization), a learning framework that uses interaction history to inform corrective actions. Similar body postures can occur during quiet balance or at an oscillation turning point, so posture alone does not identify the ongoing response. History-based locomotion and adaptation use past observations to infer control context [6], [7]. For sustained balance, longer histo-ries characterize persistent motion, while recent observations support immediate adjustments. Interpreting motion also requires a criterion for whether a correction advances balance. Equal relative kinetic energy can correspond to motion toward or away from equilibrium, and the acceleration required for correction acts back on the support. LISST uses coupled dynamics to evaluate these directional effects and a Lyapunov-inspired criterion to assess progress over finite windows, allowing for corrective tran-sients. This physical evaluation guides the joint-target policy through backpropagation through time (BPTT) in differen-tiable world-model rollouts [8], [9], connecting the simplified dynamics to the actual action interface. Training must also account for the difficult states created by the deployment policy’s own corrections. LISST directs privileged guidance to these states, preserving their accumu-lated motion and observation history, and transfers it through context distillation. A. Dynamic Balance and Robot–Environment Interaction 

Model-based balance control coordinates whole-body mo-tion with contact forces and support constraints. Sentis et al. [1] regulate center-of-mass behavior and internal forces, while Righetti et al. [2] optimize contact-force distribution through inverse dynamics. Capturability analysis relates stop-ping without falling to foot placement, support geometry, and angular momentum [10]. Trajectory optimization and model predictive control account for contact interactions over finite horizons [11], [12]. Moving supports introduce additional dynamics. Lee and Goswami [13] regulate momentum on nonstationary ground, while Gao et al. [14] use time-varying foot-placement control for walking on swaying surfaces. Nagarajan and Yamane [4] stabilize simplified planar robots on unstable supports without direct environmental feedback. Nava et al. [3] model robot– seesaw dynamics and contact constraints and demonstrate cou-pled stabilization in simulation. LAS-MP [5] learns adaptive postural control with proprioceptive robot and platform state estimation. These studies motivate treating the support as part of the dynamics. Our focus is the continuing response to corrective actions, including the motion that remains after static compensation. 

B. Learning-Based Legged Locomotion and Adaptation 

Reinforcement learning enables quadrupeds to acquire motor skills in simulation and transfer them to physical robots [15]. The information available to the policy shapes its ability to adapt: Lee et al. [6] use proprioceptive history for locomotion on challenging terrain, whereas Miki et al. [16] combine proprioceptive and exteroceptive feedback. RMA [7] infers an adaptation context from recent observations and ac-tions, and Margolis et al. [17] combine a command curriculum with online system identification. Privileged learning transfers simulation information to poli-cies with restricted deployment inputs [6], [7]. The states on which guidance is provided also matter. DAgger [18] addresses the expert–learner state-distribution mismatch through super-vision on learner-visited states. Beyond the information and guidance available to a pol-icy, learning depends on how its actions are evaluated. Learned world models predict action-conditioned transitions for planning or policy improvement through imagined tra-jectories [19], [20]. Physical structure can guide how those trajectories are evaluated. CLF-RL [21] constructs tracking and Lyapunov-decay rewards from model-generated reference motions, and finite-step control Lyapunov methods assess de-crease over multiple transitions [22]. Our objective addresses load compensation without a prescribed reference motion. It uses the physical model to evaluate signed corrective motion jointly with imbalance and support motion, retaining the reaction of acceleration on the support. 

C. State Estimation and Motion Representation 

State estimation recovers feedback variables from sensor measurements. Bloesch et al. [23] fuse leg kinematics and inertial measurements, while Hartley et al. [24] use contact-aided invariant filtering to estimate pose, velocity, and contact points. Camurri et al. [25] estimate contact reliability and detect impacts. On moving ground, He et al. [26] estimate robot state relative to noninertial ground using encoders and IMUs on both the robot and support. Temporal encoders and adaptation modules extract con-trol context from interaction history [6], [7], while DreamWaQ [27] learns implicit terrain information from pro-prioception. DeepPhase [28] learns periodic embeddings of full-body motion, and Fourier Latent Dynamics [29] uses a predictive Fourier representation for motion encoding and generation. For sustained balance, the representation must de-scribe the motion already in progress when the next correction begins. This motivates relating latent temporal coordinates to robot–support motion through physical supervision. We next formulate the coupled mechanics and the proprio-ceptive control interface on which LISST operates. III. P ROBLEM FORMULATION

A. Overview 

LISST connects a history-dependent policy to a dynamics-informed evaluation of its actions. The deployment loop estimates the ongoing response from proprioception, while training uses physical state to supervise the representation and evaluate corrective progress. We use S and T to denote the deployment and privileged policies, respectively. The control loop in Fig. 1 draws on both recent observations and a longer history. Short-history context and joint/body estimates support immediate adjustments. The longer history describes how motion has evolved beyond the current posture through parameterized latent signals, which physical recon-struction and short-term continuation relate to robot–support motion. The Actor maps these inputs to joint targets, and the resulting response becomes part of the history for its next decision. All branches update at the same control frequency, although their history lengths differ. The training objective incorporates physical priors about compensation and motion suppression. In the reduced dynam-ics, relative velocity changes the compensating moment, and acceleration reacts on the support. These couplings determine quadratic weights that evaluate motion direction jointly with static imbalance and board motion. Adding the remaining whole-body relative energy gives a common measure of bal-ance, complemented by auxiliary terms for posture, support, and execution requirements. We use the coupled cost in this spirit to encourage reduction of imbalance and relative motion. D. Residual-Guided Privileged Policy Learning 

Privileged learning must connect additional physical in-formation to the context used by the deployment policy. We express that guidance as a bounded residual around the deployment context mean, making the correction relative to its current representation. The shared Actor interprets both roles’ contexts through the same action mapping. We evaluate LISST on a Unitree Go2 Edu quadruped on a passive, pitch-hinged board. The board measures 2.0 m ×

0.45 m and is mounted at a height of 0.25 m . Successive changes in endpoint loads elicit corrective robot–support in-teraction. The policy issues joint targets from onboard pro-prioception, without board-state or external-load measure-ments. Physical state and controller history persist across load changes, so each correction begins with the motion left by earlier actions. Board angle and angular velocity characterize equilibrium deviation and residual board motion, alongside maintenance of support. These quantities assess whether corrections restore balance while limiting the motion carried into subsequent interactions. In real-world deployment, LISST maintains bal-ance while limiting residual oscillations during continuous interaction with the support. The accompanying hardware video presents the demonstration. VI. C ONCLUSION 

LISST treats sustained balance on dynamically responsive supports as joint regulation of equilibrium error and coupled motion. Temporal representations characterize the ongoing re-sponse, while a physics-informed, Lyapunov-inspired criterion guides optimization through differentiable rollouts. Privileged guidance focuses training on difficult states encountered by the deployment policy. Real-world deployment demonstrates balance maintenance with limited residual oscillations during continuous robot– support interaction. This interaction-centered formulation eval-uates corrections by both their immediate effect on equilibrium and the motion inherited by subsequent actions. 8

E. Gradient Updates and Calibration 

Deployment-policy returns update the shared Actor, short-history encoder, temporal encoder, and phase heads. Privileged-policy returns additionally update the privileged encoder and reach the deployment context mean through resid-ual addition. Both return paths differentiate through spectral extraction and the phase heads. Distillation updates the deployment context mean and scale. Physical reconstruction updates the temporal encoder, phase heads, and physical decoder. Body Observer parameters re-ceive only reconstruction gradients, although return differen-tiation retains its input Jacobians. World-model and Critic parameters are fixed during policy updates, retaining state/action derivatives for BPTT. Both models are trained separately through supervised updates. The nonnegative coefficients βaux and λrepr in (20) weight auxiliary supervision. Appendix C defines the observer and representation losses and validity masks. Physical reconstruction uses detached replay inputs and labels, with optional replay warm-up. Batch-normalization statistics are calibrated on replay and fixed during joint training, imagined rollouts, and deployment. Convolutional weights, phase heads, and normalization affine parameters remain trainable.
