# A New Perspective on Optimal Power Flow: From Control Theory to Numerical Optimization 

Yeming Xu, Qi Liu, Liping Zhang, Huanshui Zhang, and Minyue Fu 

Abstract —The optimal power flow (OPF) problem is a classical non-convex optimization problem with nonlinear constraints and multiple potential solutions. Existing methods often fail to find the global optimal solution, motivating the development of a method to identify multiple local solutions. This paper applies the optimal control principle to solve the OPF problem, aiming to systematically explore all feasible solutions within a single run. We first handle the constraints in the OPF problem using the penalty method and the Lagrange multiplier, and then reformu-late it as an optimal control problem. The connection between the two problems is established by the maximum principle. Moreover, a numerical algorithm is proposed to solve this optimal control problem. The state trajectory of the system can rapidly and stably converge to the solution of the OPF problem. By designing the initial control sequence, the proposed method can successfully find multiple solutions and escape local solutions. Finally, simulations were conducted on several representative test systems to validate the potential of the proposed method in identifying multiple solutions for non-convex problems. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The optimal power flow (OPF) problem aims to find a steady state operating point of a power system that minimizes the cost of electric power generation while satisfying operational constraints [1], [2]. Due to the nonlinear constraint of the power flow equations, the OPF problem is a non-convex optimization problem and may contain many solutions [3]– [7]. The solution of the OPF problem began with the gradient method proposed by Dommel and Tinney in 1968 [2]. To effectively handle the constraints, classical approaches such as the Lagrange multiplier method and the penalty function method have been extensively explored [8]–[10]. Building on these foundations, the interior point method (IPM), which introduces slack variables and incorporates a logarithmic barrier function, has become a mainstream approach due to its fast convergence rate [11]–[13]. However, when dealing with highly nonlinear objective functions or constraints, the effectiveness of these traditional methods gradually decreases,

Heuristic methods have been applied to power systems for their ability to handle complex problems [17]. Among them, genetic algorithms (GAs) have attracted considerable attention [3], [18]–[21]. However, these methods often suffer from premature convergence in OPF problems [22], making it difficult to capture all possible solutions. In our recent work, we have successfully applied the optimal control principle (OCP) to optimization problems and achieved notable results [23]–[25]. Motivated by the characteristic of optimal controllers to drive system states rapidly and stably to equilibrium, we incorporate OCP into finding the solution of non-convex problems. This paper explores the application of the OCP in the OPF problem and proposes a novel optimization method. This method demonstrates outstanding performance in solving highly nonlinear and non-convex prob-lems. Specifically, the main contributions of this work are as follows: 1) The proposed method handles inequality constraints using the penalty method and directly solves for the Lagrange multipliers to ensure satisfaction of the equal-ity constraints. Compared with approaches that rely on power flow calculations to satisfy equality constraints during the optimization of the OPF problem [26]–[28], this strategy avoids complex calculations and effec-tively prevents the objective function from becoming ill-conditioned as in traditional penalty methods, thereby enhancing the numerical stability of the algorithm used to solve this problem. 2) A solution method for the OPF problem based on the OCP is proposed. The OPF problem is transformed into a discrete-time optimal control problem, where the optimal controller adjusts the first-order difference equa-tions to minimize a cost function composed of control energy and the objective function. It is shown that the steady state of the system in the optimal control prob-lem corresponds to the stationary point of the original problem. Furthermore, an optimization algorithm with superlinear convergence rate is proposed to solve this optimal control problem. Compared with the traditional methods, the proposed method is guaranteed to converge rapidly and stably. 3) More importantly, it can effectively escape local solu-tions of the OPF problem and identify multiple OPF solutions within a single execution by appropriately designing the initial control sequence. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper has proposed an optimization method for find-ing multiple solutions in the OPF problem. Specifically, the original constrained OPF problem has been transformed into an optimal control problem by applying the penalty method, introducing Lagrange multipliers, and leveraging the OCP. The resulting problem has then been solved efficiently using a numerical algorithm with superlinear convergence rate. To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, simulations were conducted on several representative test systems. The results have shown that the method successfully finds all so-lutions and escapes local solutions, demonstrating the potential of the OCP for handling non-convex problems. REFERENCES [1] J.
