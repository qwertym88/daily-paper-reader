# Tikhonov-Regularized Dynamics and Inexact Algorithms for Nonlinear Saddle-Point Problems 

## Hong-lu Li 1 and Yi-bin Xiao 1* 1School of Mathematical Sciences, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, Chengdu, 611731, Sichuan, P.R. China. *Corresponding author(s). E-mail(s): xiaoyb9999@hotmail.com; Contributing authors: lihonglu 7988@163.com; 

Abstract. In this paper, we study a Tikhonov-regularized second-order primal–dual dynamical sys-tem for smooth nonlinear convex–concave saddle-point problems in real Hilbert spaces. The dynamical system incorporates a vanishing damping coefficient α/t , a Hessian-driven term, and a critical Tikhonov regularization coefficient c/t 2. By constructing suitable Lyapunov functions, we establish strong conver-gence to the minimum-norm saddle point and fast convergence rates under the same parameter conditions, without imposing any eventual inside/outside-ball condition. Specifically, the primal–dual trajectory con-verges strongly to the minimum-norm saddle point, while the primal–dual gap and the velocity norm decay at rates o(t−2) and o(t−1), respectively. Furthermore, we propose an inexact primal–dual algorithm with a computable relative residual criterion and prove strong convergence of the generated sequence to the same minimum-norm saddle point, together with an o(k−2) convergence rate for the primal–dual gap. Finally, numerical experiments on nonlinear and bilinear problems of different dimensions illustrate the minimum-norm selection property and compare the performance of the proposed dynamical system and algorithm with related methods. 

Keywords. Nonlinear convex–concave saddle-point problems, minimum-norm saddle point, fast convergence rates, inexact primal–dual algorithm

## 1.1 Motivation and related works 

First-order primal–dual methods have been widely studied for solving convex–concave saddle-point prob-lems. For bilinear coupling, Chambolle et al. [11, 12] developed primal–dual algorithms and established ergodic convergence rates. However, applications with nonlinear coupling motivate methods beyond the bilinear setting. For smooth problems of this type, Nemirovski [28] developed the Mirror-Prox method and established an O(k−1) primal–dual gap estimate for weighted averaged iterates under Lipschitz continuity of the saddle operator on convex compact feasible sets. Incorporating momentum and adap-tive stepsizes, Hamedani et al. [16] introduced a momentum term based on the partial gradients of the coupling function. They proved convergence of the iterates and an O(k−1) ergodic gap estimate, and proposed a backtracking strategy that does not require the Lipschitz constants. When the primal objec-tive is strongly convex and the coupling is linear in the dual variable, their method achieves an O(k−2)ergodic rate. To accommodate local smoothness assumptions, Chang et al. [13] combined convex com-binations with linesearch for general nonbilinear coupling, proving convergence of the iterates and an 

O(k−1) ergodic estimate for a restricted merit function. As an alternative to linesearch, Huang et al. [21] developed an adaptive primal–dual method without linesearch, with iterate convergence and an O(k−1)ergodic estimate. These ergodic estimates concern averaged iterates. To obtain last-iterate rates, Bot ¸et al. [10] proposed a fast reflected forward–backward method for monotone inclusions and applied it to nonlinear saddle-point problems. Their analysis establishes weak convergence and last-iterate o(k−1)estimates for the tangent residual and the primal–dual gap. However, these convergence and gap esti-mates do not ensure minimum-norm selection. Combining fast rates with strong convergence to the minimum-norm saddle point therefore requires further study. To study convergence rates together with the behavior of the iterates, it is useful to consider the continuous-time dynamics underlying accelerated methods. This approach also provides a basis for deriv-ing discrete algorithms. For unconstrained convex minimization, Su et al. [31] related Nesterov’s method to the dynamical system ¨u(t) + αt ˙u(t) + ∇ϕ(u(t)) = 0 ,

where ϕ is a smooth convex function. For α ≥ 3, they obtained ϕ(u(t)) −min ϕ = O(t−2). Beyond function values, subsequent studies established weak trajectory convergence and the improved o(t−2) estimate for 

α > 3 in [2, 26]. To extend this approach to constrained problems, Bot ¸ et al. [8] studied inertial primal– dual dynamics with vanishing damping and proved trajectory convergence and fast rates. Following the same dynamical-systems approach, Bot ¸ et al. [6] derived an accelerated augmented Lagrangian method with convergence guarantees for the iterates. For bilinear saddle-point problems, He et al. [18] investi-gated second-order primal–dual dynamics with damping, scaling and extrapolation. Further addressing trajectory convergence and sharper rates, He et al. [17] established trajectory convergence, an o(t−2)primal–dual gap estimate and an o(t−1) velocity estimate, and studied corresponding accelerated discrete algorithms. 2To retain fast gap estimates and iterate convergence after discretization, Ding et al. [15] studied a second-order primal–dual dynamical system for bilinear coupling and its implicit and explicit dis-cretizations. Their implicit algorithm achieves a nonergodic O(k−2) gap estimate under suitable inertial parameter rules without strong convexity; for smooth objectives, they also established nonergodic esti-mates and weak convergence for an explicit algorithm. Beyond bilinear coupling, the monotonicity of the saddle-gradient mapping allows continuous and discrete methods to be studied for general smooth convex–concave problems. In this direction, Bot ¸ et al. [7] developed the continuous and discrete Fast OGDA methods. Starting from a second-order dynamical system, they derived implicit and explicit algorithms and established weak convergence and last-iterate residual estimates. More precisely, their implicit method gives o(k−2) residuals with linearly growing scaling, whereas the explicit method gives 

o(k−1) under global Lipschitz continuity. These results establish fast residual decay, but do not guarantee convergence to the minimum-norm solution. Despite fast function-value decay, inertial trajectories may oscillate, motivating additional damping based on local curvature. For unconstrained optimization, Alvarez et al. [1] introduced Hessian-driven damping in an inertial dynamical system related to continuous Newton methods. Combining this mechanism with vanishing viscous damping for acceleration, Attouch et al. [4] studied ¨u(t) + αt ˙u(t) + β∇2ϕ(u(t)) ˙ u(t) + ∇ϕ(u(t)) = 0 , β > 0.

They established fast function-value and gradient estimates, together with weak trajectory convergence for α > 3. The Hessian-driven term can be written as β ddt ∇ϕ(u(t)), yielding an equivalent first-order formulation without explicit Hessian evaluation. Extending the study of this damping mechanism to linearly constrained optimization, He et al. [20] proposed a mixed-order primal–dual dynamical system with Hessian-driven damping, proved fast Lagrangian, objective and feasibility estimates, and studied its discretization. Along a related line, Li et al. [23] considered an implicit form of Hessian damping in primal–dual dynamics. Extending this mechanism to bilinear saddle-point problems, Wang et al. [34] developed Hessian-driven inertial primal–dual dynamics and established an O(t−2) gap estimate in the convex–concave case, as well as faster estimates under strong convexity–concavity. A related derivative mechanism is also present in the Fast OGDA dynamics discussed above [7]: the derivative acts on the full monotone primal–dual mapping and therefore also applies to nonlinear coupling. Hessian-driven damping does not itself ensure minimum-norm selection. To obtain this property, van-ishing Tikhonov regularization has been incorporated into inertial dynamics. For unconstrained convex minimization, Attouch et al. [3] studied ¨u(t) + αt ˙u(t) + ∇ϕ(u(t)) + ϵ(t)u(t) = 0 ,

where ϵ(t) > 0 tends to zero. Their results distinguish fast function-value decay and minimum-norm selection according to the decay of ϵ. For polynomial regularization, the order t−2 separates the fast and slow decay regimes considered in their analysis, highlighting the importance of the regularization decay. This leads to the question of whether fast rates and minimum-norm selection can be obtained simultaneously. For linearly constrained optimization, Li et al. [23] combined implicit Hessian damping with Tikhonov regularization and studied fast rates and minimum-norm strong convergence of the primal trajectory. To obtain both properties at the critical decay order, Li et al. [24] studied mixed-order primal– dual dynamics and established minimum-norm strong convergence and little-o rates for the regularization 

c/t 2. No eventual inside/outside-ball condition relative to the minimum-norm primal solution is required. For saddle-point problems, the selection question extends from the primal variable to the entire primal–dual pair. For this purpose, Sun et al. [32] combined Hessian-driven damping, Tikhonov reg-ularization and time scaling for bilinear saddle-point problems, obtaining strong convergence to the minimum-norm saddle point and gap estimates. For monotone equations, which also cover smooth non-linear saddle-point problems, Bot ¸ et al. [9] established minimum-norm strong convergence and residual estimates for Tikhonov-regularized monotone operator flows. Incorporating inertial and derivative terms into regularized monotone dynamics, Csetnek et al. [14] studied minimum-norm selection for a second-order dynamical system. Using anchoring for discrete solution selection, Bot ¸ et al. [5] proposed an extragradient method with flexible anchoring, establishing strong convergence to the solution nearest the anchor together with residual estimates. However, combining selection with fast rates depends on the admissible parameter regimes. In particular, the second-order analyses in [14, 32] use damping α/t q

3with 0 < q < 1 and do not cover damping α/t combined with critical regularization c/t 2. On the other hand, a special case of the dynamical system studied here reduces, after time rescaling, to the first-order flow in [9]. Thus, the remaining continuous-time question is to establish selection and rates for the full parameter family and arbitrary initial velocities. For these continuous-time properties to guide algorithm design, a further question is whether minimum-norm selection and fast convergence can be retained after discretization. Implicit schemes may require solving regularized saddle subproblems, which generally have no closed-form solutions for non-linear coupling. Since exact solution at every iteration can be costly, it is natural to consider inexact solutions and examine whether these convergence properties are preserved. For bilinear saddle-point problems, Rasch et al. [29] analyzed errors in proximal maps and gradients, and retained the correspond-ing exact algorithm’s rates under suitable error decay conditions. For nonlinear coupling, Khalafi et al. [22] also allowed inexact subproblem solutions in an accelerated primal–dual method in the convex– strongly-concave setting. Along a related line for linearly constrained optimization, He et al. [19] studied inexact accelerated augmented Lagrangian algorithms based on approximate stationarity of the subprob-lems. Besides allowing inexact solutions, one must specify how their accuracy is measured and checked. Rather than fixing an absolute tolerance sequence in advance, relative error criteria relate the required accuracy to quantities computed during the iteration. Such criteria have been studied in hybrid prox-imal methods [30]. A remaining issue is whether a computable relative residual condition can preserve minimum-norm strong convergence and fast last-iterate gap decay for nonlinear saddle-point problems, without prescribing an absolute error sequence in advance. Motivated by the above discussion, we study Tikhonov-regularized primal–dual dynamics for nonlin-ear saddle-point problems. Our aim is to combine strong convergence to the minimum-norm saddle point with fast rates under the critical regularization c/t 2, without imposing any eventual inside/outside-ball condition on the trajectory, and to retain these properties in an inexact discretization with a computable relative residual criterion. 

## 1.2 Main contributions 

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. (a) We study a Tikhonov-regularized second-order primal–dual dynamical system for nonlinear convex– concave saddle-point problems in real Hilbert spaces. Under Assumption 1.1 and the parameter conditions α > 3, 1 /(α − 1) < θ < 1/2 and c > 0, we establish global existence and uniqueness for arbitrary initial positions and velocities, and prove that the trajectory converges strongly to the minimum-norm saddle point ( x∗, y ∗). Under the same conditions, we obtain 

L(x(t), y ∗) − L (x∗, y (t)) = o(t−2), ∥ ˙x(t)∥ + ∥ ˙y(t)∥ = o(t−1), R(x(t), y (t)) = O(t−2),

where R(x, y ) := ( ∥∇ f (x)+ ∇xΨ( x, y )∥2+∥∇ g(y)−∇ y Ψ( x, y )∥2)1/2 is the stationarity residual norm. These conclusions hold for the critical regularization c/t 2 without any eventual inside/outside-ball condition on the primal–dual trajectory. (b) We propose an inexact primal–dual algorithm obtained by discretizing an equivalent first-order formulation of the dynamical system. The regularized saddle subproblems need only satisfy a com-putable relative residual criterion. For any fixed stepsize s > 0 and a sufficiently small relative error parameter, we prove strong convergence of ( xk, y k) to ( x∗, y ∗) and establish the following rates in the outer iteration index k:

L(xk, y ∗) − L (x∗, y k) = o(k−2), ∥xk − xk−1∥ + ∥yk − yk−1∥ = o(k−1), R(xk, y k) = O(k−2).

The stopping condition requires neither the exact subproblem solution nor an additional weighted summability assumption on the errors. (c) We further characterize the asymptotic behavior of the stationarity residuals. When the minimum-norm saddle point is nonzero, the residual estimates O(t−2) and O(k−2) are sharp along the generated trajectory and sequence, respectively. When it is zero, both estimates improve to little-o

rates. Moreover, numerical experiments on nonlinear and bilinear problems of different dimensions illustrate minimum-norm selection and compare the performance of the proposed dynamical system and algorithm with related methods. 41.3 Organization

Remark 1. For bilinear saddle-point problems, accelerated primal–dual dynamics yield O(t−2) gap esti-mates [18], while trajectory convergence and the rates o(t−2) for the gap and o(t−1) for the velocity are established in [17]. Theorem 2.2 obtains these little-o rates for nonlinear convex–concave saddle-point problems in real Hilbert spaces, together with strong convergence to the minimum-norm saddle point and an O(t−2) stationarity residual estimate. Thus, in the bilinear case, the proposed regularized dynamical system achieves the same gap and velocity rates with the additional minimum-norm selection property. In addition, the combination of damping α/t and critical regularization c/t 2 lies outside the parame-ter regimes of the strong convergence theorems in [14, 32]. For finite-dimensional linearly constrained optimization, [24] establishes strong convergence and little-o rates under critical regularization without eventual inside/outside-ball conditions, selecting the minimum-norm primal solution and a compatible KKT multiplier. Here, minimum-norm selection concerns the entire primal–dual pair in the nonlin-ear Hilbert-space setting, with strong convergence and all three rate estimates obtained under the same parameter conditions and without eventual inside/outside-ball assumptions.

Remark 2. The preceding results can be compared with related discrete methods. The implicit Fast OGDA method [7] gives o(k−2) residuals for suitable scaling, with weak convergence but no minimum-norm selection guarantee. Anchored extragradient methods [5] provide this selection property. In this respect, Theorem 3.1 combines minimum-norm selection with an O(k−2) stationarity residual, an o(k−2) gap at the fixed saddle point, and o(k−1) increments under local Lipschitz continuity. These conclusions hold under a computable relative residual criterion. The numerical experiments also report the work required by the inner solvers.
