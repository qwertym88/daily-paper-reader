## ABSTRACT 

Dexterous manipulation requires robots to leverage existing datasets for rapid skill acquisition while maintaining safety during real-world deployment to pre-vent damage to the robot or its surroundings. Offline-to-online safe reinforcement learning (O2O safe RL) provides a promising solution for this problem by lever-aging prior experience for online improvement under safety constraints, thereby mitigating the sample inefficiency and exploration risk of learning safe policies from scratch. However, existing O2O safe RL approaches do not explicitly ac-count for the safety budget already consumed when making decisions within an episode. Excessive cost incurred early can therefore leave little room for subse-quent exploration and policy improvement. To address this limitation, we propose 

Residual-Budget Guide Arbitration (RBGA), an algorithm that explicitly tracks the remaining episode safety budget and uses it to adaptively select between of-fline guidance and the evolving online policy. At each step, RBGA compares the cost-to-go estimates of the guide and online actions against the remaining budget, enabling step-wise action arbitration throughout the episode. We provide theoreti-cal analysis characterizing the safety implications of residual-budget-based selec-tion and its effect on guide-assisted exploration. Extensive experiments demon-strate that RBGA enables effective online policy improvement while maintaining strong safety performance. Our code is available at this link. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Dexterous manipulation enables multi-fingered robotic hands to perform a wide range of contact-rich tasks, including in-hand manipulation, grasping, and tool use (Rajeswaran et al., 2018; OpenAI et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2025). Despite substantial progress, improving the safety of dexterous ma-nipulation remains a pressing challenge (Garc´ ıa & Fern´ andez, 2015; Jain et al., 2023; Geng et al., 2025). Safe reinforcement learning (RL) addresses this challenge by optimizing task performance subject to explicit cost constraints (Chow et al., 2018; Tessler et al., 2019; Stooke et al., 2020; Satija et al., 2020). However, learning a safe dexterous policy from scratch is often prohibitive, as it requires extensive environment interactions that are both costly and risky (Ray et al., 2019; Thanan-jeyan et al., 2021; Dulac-Arnold et al., 2021). Offline safe RL reduces this burden by learning policies from a fixed dataset without additional online interaction (Xu et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2022a; Liu et al., 2023). Yet its performance is constrained by the coverage of the offline data (Levine et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2024), making learned policies vulnerable to distribution shift and out-of-distribution (OOD) actions when encountering unseen states (Fujimoto et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2019; 2020). Offline-to-online (O2O) safe RL therefore offers an appealing alternative by lever-

Compared with O2O RL, O2O safe RL remains relatively underexplored (Li et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). Existing policy-finetuning methods continue online learning from an offline-pretrained policy, although this transfer can suffer from mismatched value estimates and Lagrange multipliers between offline and online learning (Chen et al., 2026). To facilitate safer and more effective adaptation, some works instead use an offline policy to guide the learning of a separate online policy (Li et al., 2024), while others preserve offline knowledge through model-based fine-tuning and safe policy expansion (Cao et al., 2025). Despite these advances, existing methods do not explicitly account for the cumulative cost already incurred in the current episode when making online decisions. This limitation is particularly important in dexterous manipulation, where contact-rich interactions can produce abrupt safety violations and nonuniform cost accumulation over an episode. Therefore, developing a dedicated O2O safe RL algorithm that explicitly accounts for within-episode cost accumulation is particularly important for dexterous manipulation. In this work, to address the lack of trajectory-level budget awareness in O2O safe RL, we propose Residual-Budget Guide Arbitration (RBGA), an algorithm that explicitly incorporates the remaining episode cost budget into online policy learning. First, RBGA tracks the remaining cost budget along each trajectory to characterize the current safety margin throughout online interaction. Then, at each interaction step, it evaluates the actions proposed by the offline guide and the online policy based on their estimated cost-to-go, and adaptively selects between them according to the remaining budget. This step-wise arbitration allows the online policy to take control when the guide action becomes unfavorable under the current budget, while retaining useful offline guidance when it remains admis-sible. Finally, extensive experiments on Redman (Geng et al., 2025) demonstrate the effectiveness of RBGA across a range of dexterous manipulation tasks. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: • To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to explicitly incorporate trajectory-level episode cost budget information into O2O safe RL for dexterous manipulation. • We propose RBGA, a step-wise action arbitration algorithm that adaptively balances of-fline guidance and the evolving online policy based on the remaining episode budget and estimated cost-to-go. • Extensive experiments across diverse dexterous manipulation tasks demonstrate the effec-tiveness and advantages of RBGA, showing strong policy performance while maintaining favorable safety behavior during online adaptation. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

RL for Dexterous Manipulation. RL has enabled multi-fingered robotic hands to acquire com-plex manipulation skills from interaction, with early studies demonstrating effective learning from demonstrations and large-scale simulation (Rajeswaran et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2019; OpenAI et al., 2020). Subsequent work has improved the applicability of learned policies through model-based planning and more efficient policy optimization (Nagabandi et al., 2020; Charlesworth & Montana, 2021). Recent studies further extend dexterous RL to unseen objects and real-world manipulation (Chen et al., 2022a; Qin et al., 2023; Qi et al., 2023a;b), as well as more challenging bimanual tasks (Chen et al., 2022b; Huang et al., 2023). Despite these advances, most prior work considers dex-terous manipulation without explicit safety constraints. Safety is nevertheless critical for dexterous manipulation, as unsafe actions during contact-rich interaction may damage the robotic hand or its surroundings. Research on safe dexterous manipulation remains limited. A few studies incorporate explicit constraints into dexterous policy learning or extend safe RL to bimanual manipulation (Jain et al., 2023; Zhan & Chin, 2024). ReDMan further establishes a dedicated benchmark for evaluating safe RL algorithms on dexterous manipulation tasks (Geng et al., 2025). These works mainly learn or evaluate safe policies through online interaction, leaving how offline experience can facilitate subsequent safe adaptation largely unexplored. 

Offline-to-Online RL. Offline-to-online (O2O) RL leverages offline pretraining to accelerate online policy improvement. A natural strategy is to initialize online learning with an offline-trained policy and continue fine-tuning it using collected experience (Nair et al., 2020). To improve the stability 2and efficiency of this transition, some methods balance offline and online samples during replay (Lee et al., 2022b; Ball et al., 2023), while others correct value estimates inherited from offline training before or during online fine-tuning (Yu & Zhang, 2023; Nakamoto et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024). Instead of directly fine-tuning the offline policy, JSRL uses a fixed guide policy to facilitate exploration through progressively shortened roll-ins (Uchendu et al., 2023), whereas PEX retains separate offline and online policies and selects between their proposed actions according to estimated values (Zhang et al., 2023). Despite substantial progress, most O2O RL methods are de-veloped for unconstrained reward maximization and do not address safety constraints during online adaptation. Recent O2O safe RL studies begin to address this problem by incorporating safety into offline-to-online transfer. GOLD uses an offline sequence policy to guide the learning of an online safe policy (Li et al., 2024). However, GOLD relies on a Decision Transformer as the offline guide and performs guided online distillation, differing from the matched offline-policy setting considered in this work. FOSP combines offline model learning with safe policy expansion for online fine-tuning (Cao et al., 2025). However, FOSP adopts a model-based safe O2O setting with a learned world model, which is not directly comparable to the model-free setting considered in this work. Despite these advances, existing O2O safe RL studies have not addressed dexterous manipulation, where unsafe exploratory actions can damage the robotic hand or its surroundings. Learning a safe dexterous policy from scratch requires costly and risky online interaction, while offline safe RL remains limited by the coverage of the fixed dataset and degrades under distribution shift. O2O safe RL is therefore particularly appealing for dexterous manipulation, as it reuses offline knowledge to accelerate online adaptation under safety constraints. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to study O2O safe RL for dexterous manipulation.

## 4 METHODOLOGY 

In this section, we first introduce Residual-Budget Guide Arbitration (RBGA) for O2O safe RL in dexterous manipulation. We first describe how guide horizon scheduling and residual-budget-based action selection combine offline guidance with online learning. We then provide theoretical analysis of the cost of the composite policy and the reward performance of the learned online policy. 4.1 RESIDUAL -B UDGET GUIDE ARBITRATION 

Guide horizon scheduling. Inspired by JSRL (Uchendu et al., 2023), RBGA combines a fixed offline guide policy πg and a trainable online policy πo into a composite policy π for online interac-tion. Consider a dexterous manipulation episode of at most H control steps and a total of N online training iterations. To limit dependence on the offline guide, we specify a guidance cutoff Ng ≤ N .During the first Ng training iterations, the offline guide participates in action selection, while the online policy acts alone thereafter. The online policy is initialized independently and updated from collected experience. At iteration i, the maximum guide horizon Hi ∈ { 0, . . . , H } controls the number of initial episode steps during which guide actions will be considered. Decreasing Hi al-lows the online policy to take control earlier as training progresses. Specifically, in JSRL, the guide horizon is directly reduced across curriculum stages, introducing abrupt changes in the state distri-bution encountered by the online policy. To provide a smoother transition from offline guidance to online control, we instead gradually decrease the maximum guide horizon as training progresses and consider two scheduling strategies, Linear and Step . ## 5 EXPERIMENT 

In this section, we conduct comprehensive comparative experiments between RBGA and prior O2O safe RL methods in dexterous manipulation tasks. Moreover, we conduct ablation studies to evaluate the effectiveness of guide horizon scheduling and the contribution of residual-budget arbitration. 5.1 TASK AND BASELINE 

Task. We evaluate RBGA on four dexterous manipulation tasks from the ReDMan bench-mark (Geng et al., 2025), including Joint , Finger , ArmJoint , and ArmFinger . These tasks com-bine two bimanual object-transfer settings with constraints on either a single joint or multiple finger joints; task descriptions are provided in Appendix A.1. The original ReDMan environments use binary cost signals to indicate safety violations, resulting in abrupt changes between safe and unsafe states. To provide a more informative safety signal for policy learning, we replace the binary cost with a continuous cost while preserving the original safety definition of each task. The resulting continuous cost signal ct = c(st, a t) is used to compute the cumulative cost over an episode. We set the episode cost limit to d = 30 for all tasks. 

Baselines. We compare RBGA with the following six baselines: (i) JSRL-Random-Lag (Uchendu et al., 2023): the random-switching variant of JSRL, which uniformly samples the number of initial guide steps for each episode and then switches from the offline guide to the online policy; (ii) JSRL-Curr-Lag (Uchendu et al., 2023): the curriculum variant of JSRL, which progressively reduces the number of initial guide steps during training, allowing the online policy to take control earlier in each episode; (iii) PEX-Lag (Zhang et al., 2023): adaptively selects between actions proposed by a fixed offline policy and a trainable online policy using reward value estimates with a Lagrangian cost penalty; (iv) SO2-Lag (Zhang et al., 2024): fine-tunes an offline-pretrained policy using a mixture of offline and online replay data, perturbed target actions, and more frequent critic updates; 

(v) Direct : initializes the online actor and the reward and cost critics from an offline-pretrained checkpoint, and then directly fine-tunes them using SAC-Lag; (vi) Scratch : trains SAC-Lag from scratch using online interactions, without offline pretraining. To evaluate the effect of different guide horizon scheduling strategies on online learning, we con-duct ablation experiments across the four dexterous manipulation tasks. Specifically, we compare the proposed Linear and Step schedules with JSRL-Random and JSRL-Curriculum (Uchendu et al., 2023), as well as a Fixed schedule. For Linear, Step, and JSRL-Curriculum, guidance ends at 40k, 60k, or 80k online training iterations, whereas JSRL-Random and Fixed retain guide participation throughout training. Figure 2 illustrates the reward, cost, and success rate curves of the different scheduling strategies across the four tasks. From the illustrated results, it can be observed that the Linear and Step schedules generally provide stable policy improvement and achieve competitive reward and success rate. Notably, Linear-40k achieves consistently strong performance across the four tasks while maintaining favorable cost control. In contrast, several JSRL-Curriculum config-urations exhibit large performance fluctuations or sharp degradation during online training, while JSRL-Random and Fixed also do not consistently match the performance of the decreasing-horizon schedules. These results indicate that sampling the guide horizon within a gradually decreasing maximum horizon provides more stable online learning than using a deterministic stage-wise guide horizon. This is because sampling the guide horizon within the current maximum horizon as it grad-9ually decreases over training allows the online policy to take over at different episode steps, leading to smoother changes in the state distribution and reducing abrupt shifts between curriculum stages. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we study O2O safe RL for dexterous manipulation and propose Residual-Budget Guide Arbitration (RBGA). Specifically, we first use a decreasing guide horizon to control the participation of a fixed offline guide during online learning. Based on this, we introduce a residual budget to track the episode cost already incurred and select between the guide and online actions according to the remaining budget and their estimated cost-to-go. Furthermore, we provide theoretical analyses of the cost of the composite policy and the reward performance of the learned online policy. Finally, extensive comparison and ablation experiments on dexterous manipulation tasks demonstrate that RBGA achieves strong task performance while maintaining favorable safety performance.
