Abstract —Flow-based policies provide expressive action distri-butions for continuous-control reinforcement learning, and they offer a flexible alternative to unimodal Gaussian actors. Online actor-critic learning updates the policy and critic from finite, evolving interaction data. However, off-policy value estimates can become unreliable in poorly covered action regions, and this extrapolation risk can be amplified by flow-based policies whose expressive generators can rapidly shift probability mass toward critic-favored actions with limited direct interaction data. Therefore, we propose MIFLOW, a reference-calibrated online flow-policy learning method that constructs a behavior-induced reference from recent interactions to guide value-driven policy improvement. Motivated by canalization as a qualitative analogy for path-dependent stabilization, MIFLOW learns a recency-adaptive flow-matching prior that summarizes the empirical action distribution induced by recent interactions. Rather than imposing a hard behavior constraint, this prior defines a moving density reference that shapes policy improvement: actions as-signed low density by the recent-behavior prior are not forbidden, but incur an additional cost during value-guided policy improve-ment. The learned prior induces an approximate recent-action log-density, which is used in a dimension-normalized reference correction integrated into both actor updates and critic targets. Experiments on six MuJoCo continuous-control tasks using five random seeds report competitive mean return curves and higher late-training mean returns than the evaluated baselines on several tasks. In a task-specific diagnostic case study, MIFLOW reports lower fractions of policy actions, including critic-favored high-Q actions, in regions assigned low density by the learned reference. These diagnostics measure alignment with the learned reference, not true support or critic extrapolation error. Prior-family and prior-buffer comparisons produced task-dependent differences in return and prior-defined diagnostics. These results support dynamic recent-behavior priors as a practical calibration mech-anism in the evaluated MuJoCo settings, but do not constitute a direct measurement or guarantee of reduced critic extrapolation. 

Index Terms —online reinforcement learning, flow-based poli-cies, reference-calibrated policy learning, behavior priors, refer-ence regularization, continuous control 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Entropy-regularized actor-critic methods such as soft actor-critic (SAC) are standard tools for continuous-control rein-forcement learning, typically parameterizing the actor as a unimodal Gaussian for tractable stochastic optimization [1], [2]. Recent diffusion- and flow-based policies provide richer classes that can capture complex or multimodal action distri-butions [3]–[6]. In online actor-critic learning, however, policy improvement is driven by critic estimates from finite, nonsta-tionary interaction data. Value estimates can be unreliable in poorly covered action regions, and expressive flow generators can rapidly reallocate probability mass toward critic-favored actions weakly represented in recent interaction data. The resulting challenge is reference-calibrated policy improvement that retains policy expressivity while calibrating value-guided updates against recent empirical action coverage. Out-of-support value extrapolation is a well-known fail-ure mode in offline and fixed-batch off-policy reinforce-ment learning [7]–[9]. Online interaction can revise erro-neous estimates, but function-approximation and overestima-tion errors can still bias policy improvement when the actor changes faster than new data provide corrective coverage [10]. Behavior-constrained, safe-improvement, and implicit policy-improvement methods limit this risk by restricting policy changes or reducing deviation from dataset behavior [11]– [13]. Directly transferring a fixed-support view is insufficient for online flow policies because their policy-induced data distribution evolves throughout training. They instead require a recency-adaptive reference that tracks recent behavior while remaining soft enough to calibrate, rather than constrain, value-guided policy improvement. Recent online flow-policy methods improve flow-policy op-timization, path-density estimation, and rollout stability [14], [15]. Although they establish that iterative flow policies can be trained within online actor-critic frameworks, trainability alone does not calibrate critic-guided updates against recent empirical action coverage. The central problem is therefore how to avoid amplifying value extrapolation in poorly covered action regions while preserving the expressive policy class. To address this problem, we propose MIFLOW, a reference-calibrated online flow-policy method that uses a behavior-

Generative and flow policies in reinforcement learn-ing. Building on diffusion-model foundations [19], genera-tive policies provide expressive alternatives to Gaussian ac-tors. Diffusion-QL combines conditional diffusion behavior modeling with value maximization [3]. Q-score matching connects diffusion-policy scores to Q-function action gra-dients [4], whereas DIME integrates diffusion policies into maximum-entropy reinforcement learning [5]. DACER devel-ops an online diffusion actor-critic with entropy regulation [20]. Flow Q-Learning uses a flow-matching policy and an expressive one-step policy in offline and offline-to-online set-tings [6]. Among recent online flow-based methods, FlowRL studies Wasserstein-regularized flow-policy optimization [14]. ReinFlow fine-tunes pretrained flow-matching policies using likelihood-aware stochasticity [21], and SACFlow constructs a tractable path-density actor-critic objective [15]. MIFLOW complements these methods by learning a recent-behavior prior that calibrates critic-guided path-density updates. 

Reference regularization, behavior support, and path-dependent stabilization. Reference distributions and support constraints provide complementary ways to regulate pol-icy improvement. TRPO uses a trust-region objective [22], whereas PPO uses a clipped probability-ratio surrogate [23]. MPO and V-MPO formulate value-weighted improvement through relative-entropy-style constraints [24], [25]. Mutual-information-regularized actor-critic learning introduces a state-independent marginal action reference and a policy-reference log-ratio [18]. In offline reinforcement learning, BCQ restricts action selection using a learned behavior model [26]. BEAR limits policy deviation from dataset support [27], whereas CQL reduces value optimism through conservative value learn-ing [28]. Online flow-policy learning instead involves a policy-induced data distribution that changes with the critic and actor. MIFLOW adapts reference regularization to this setting using a recency-adaptive prior learned from recent interactions. Canalization provides only a qualitative analogy for this path-dependent adaptation [16], [17]; the algorithm is defined by the learned reference and actor-critic objectives. III. P RELIMINARIES

C. Reference-Regularized Policy Improvement 

Reference-based regularization provides a general way to move beyond entropy-only policy regularization. In mutual-information regularized actor-critic learning, a state-conditioned policy is compared with a state-independent marginal action distribution through a policy-reference log-ratio [18]: 

log π(a|s) − log πmarginal (a). (10) Here, πmarginal (a) denotes the marginal action distribution induced by the policy and a state distribution. This perspective suggests that policy improvement can be regularized relative to an action reference, rather than only through conditional policy entropy. For a fixed reference distribution ρ(a), the expected un-weighted log-ratio corresponds to a KL-to-reference regular-izer: 

EsDKL (π(·| s)∥ρ(·)) = Es,a ∼π [log π(a|s) − log ρ(a)] . (11) A corresponding reference-regularized actor objective has the generic form 

Es,a [α (log π(a|s) − log ρ(a)) − Q(s, a )] . (12) We use this MI/reference-regularization perspective only as motivation. The method developed next adapts this idea to online flow-policy learning, where the conditional policy term is represented by a tractable flow-path density and the reference is learned from recent behavior rather than assumed to be an exact current-policy marginal. D. Training Procedure and Dynamic-Reference Interpretation 

MIFLOW alternates online interaction, behavior-prior esti-mation, and reference-calibrated actor-critic learning. During environment interaction, each transition is stored in the replay buffer D, and the executed action is additionally inserted into the sliding prior buffer Bprior ,t . The prior buffer is used only to update ρϕ(a) with Eq. (18), while the replay buffer is used for critic and actor updates. During actor-critic optimization, 

ρϕ(a) is treated as a fixed recent-behavior reference; gradients from the Q-functions or the actor objective are not used to train the prior. This decoupled training scheme separates three roles. The critic estimates value from replayed transitions, the flow actor performs value-guided policy improvement through the path-density objective, and the behavior prior supplies arecency-adaptive reference. As training proceeds, this refer-ence changes with recent interaction data rather than remaining tied to a fixed dataset. The resulting objective assigns an additional cost to critic-favored actions with low density under the learned recent-behavior reference. The resulting correction should be interpreted as an im-plementable reference-calibration term for online flow-policy learning. It is MI-motivated through the policy-reference log-ratio form, but it is not an exact mutual-information objective because the policy term is a flow-path density and the reference term is a learned recent-behavior prior rather than the exact current-policy marginal. This interpretation matches the goal of MIFLOW: calibrating critic-guided updates under finite and evolving online interaction data. V. E XPERIMENTS 

We evaluate MIFLOW on MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks to examine whether a recency-adaptive behavior prior can calibrate critic-guided updates of expressive flow policies under finite and evolving online interaction data. Specifically, we ask three questions. First, how does MI-FLOW compare descriptively with the evaluated SAC-style and flow-policy baselines on six MuJoCo-v4 tasks? Second, do prior-defined diagnostics change in a direction consistent with stronger policy alignment to the learned recent-behavior reference? Third, how do the observed results vary across the evaluated prior families and prior-buffer sizes? 

A. Experimental Setup 

Environments. We evaluate all methods on six MuJoCo continuous-control tasks: Ant-v4, HalfCheetah-v4, Hopper-v4, Humanoid-v4, HumanoidStandup-v4, and Walker2d-v4. These tasks cover different action dimensions, locomotion dynamics, and optimization difficulty, making them suitable for evaluating expressive online policies under continuous control. 

Baselines. We compare MIFLOW with three baselines in the main performance evaluation. SAC is included as a standard maximum-entropy actor-critic method with a Gaussian policy [1], [2]. SACFlow is included as a path-density flow-policy actor-critic baseline without the proposed recency-adaptive reference [15]. FlowRL is included as a recent online flow-policy method [14]. For diagnostic and ablation studies, we additionally compare prior variants within the same path-density flow-policy opti-mization framework. SACFlow denotes the no-prior baseline. SACFlow-GP denotes a learned Gaussian-prior variant. MI-FLOW denotes the full method with a learned flow-matching behavior prior. These variants provide descriptive comparisons of adding a behavior prior and of using a flow-matching rather than a learned Gaussian prior. 

Training protocol. Main MuJoCo learning curves are plotted over 1,000 ,000 environment steps using five random seeds. For Fig. 2, curves show smoothed mean episodic return, and shaded regions denote standard deviation across seeds. For the prior-family ablation in Table I, we report the final evaluated episodic return as mean ± SEM over five random seeds. The prior-buffer sensitivity in Table II reports the final evaluated episodic return and prior-defined diagnostics as mean ± SEM over five random seeds. Unless otherwise stated, MIFLOW uses K = 4 flow sampling steps and a default prior-buffer size of 5 × 10 4. Baseline hyperparameters follow their corresponding implementations, and MIFLOW-specific settings are fixed across tasks unless otherwise stated. All comparisons are descriptive, with the number of seeds stated for each experiment. We do not conduct formal hypothesis tests, so differences between methods should not be interpreted as statistically established. 

Metrics. The primary metric is episodic return. For prior-defined diagnostics, OOS Rate measures the fraction of policy-generated actions whose action-dimension-normalized log ρϕ(a) falls below the 20th percentile of recent-buffer actions under the same learned prior. This is a prior-defined diagnostic rather than an oracle measure of true state-action support. HQ/LP Cond measures the fraction of top-20% sam-pled policy actions under clipped double-Q that also fall into this low-prior region. In these diagnostics, one policy action is sampled per diagnostic state. 

B. Main Results 

Figure 2 presents descriptive learning-curve comparisons among MIFLOW, SACFlow, FlowRL, and SAC on six MuJoCo-v4 tasks. MIFLOW had higher late-training mean returns than the evaluated baselines on Hopper-v4, Humanoid-v4, and Walker2d-v4. On Ant-v4, its final mean return was close to that of SACFlow. MIFLOW and SACFlow produced similar mean curves on HalfCheetah-v4 and HumanoidStandup-v4, and both were generally above SAC and FlowRL during most of training. Because the comparisons use five seeds without formal hypothesis tests, these patterns should be interpreted as task-dependent descriptive results rather than statistically established differences. Relative to SAC, MIFLOW retains a flow-based policy parameterization. Relative to SACFlow, it adds a recency-adaptive behavior prior as a dynamic reference. Relative to FlowRL, it uses a tractable path-density actor-critic formula-tion. The reported results characterize these methods within the evaluated MuJoCo protocol and do not establish broader superiority across online reinforcement-learning settings. 

C. Prior-Referenced Diagnostic Analysis 

We next examine how policy actions change relative to the learned recent-behavior reference. The two diagnostics use the same prior that enters the MIFLOW objective. They therefore measure policy-prior alignment, not true state-action support or critic extrapolation error. Figure 3 presents a task-specific diagnostic comparison among the three variants on Walker2d-v4. Table II additionally reports the same diagnostics for the prior-buffer-size sensitivity study on Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4. Together, these analyses provide internal consistency checks of the proposed reference mechanism. On Walker2d-v4, MIFLOW had the lowest mean prior-defined OOS Rate and HQ/LP Cond among the three evaluated variants. These values indicate closer alignment with the learned recent-behavior reference and fewer high-Q samples in regions assigned low density by that reference. Because the diagnostics are defined by the learned prior, they cannot distin-guish reduced critic extrapolation from improved consistency with the chosen prior model. The result is therefore descriptive and task-specific.

Table I reports final mean returns for the flow-matching and Gaussian-prior variants. The flow-matching variant had higher mean returns on all six tasks, although the differences on Hopper-v4 and Walker2d-v4 were small relative to the re-ported SEMs. Larger descriptive separations occurred on Ant-v4, HalfCheetah-v4, Humanoid-v4, and HumanoidStandup-v4. Table II reports descriptive results for three prior-buffer sizes on Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4. The point estimates do not follow a common monotonic pattern across return, prior-defined OOS Rate, and HQ/LP Cond. However, several dif-ferences are small relative to the reported SEMs, so these results do not establish a reliable non-monotonic dependence on buffer size. They only indicate that the selected buffer timescale can alter the learned reference and its associated diagnostics. The buffer size conceptually mediates a possible plasticity-stability trade-off, but the present comparisons are insufficient to characterize that trade-off empirically. Overall, the prior-defined diagnostics summarize two related aspects of policy-prior alignment. They do not provide inde-pendent measurements of true support or critic extrapolation error. VI. This simplification may be insufficient when admissible ac-tions depend strongly on state. The reference correction is motivated by mutual-information and reference-regularization principles, but it is not an exact mutual-information objective or a formal robustness guarantee. Moreover, the prior-defined OOS Rate and HQ/LP Cond use the same learned prior that enters the training objective. They measure alignment with this reference rather than true support or critic extrapolation error. Diagnostic evidence is limited to a three-variant case study on Walker2d-v4 and a prior-buffer-size sensitivity study on Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4. The main performance comparisons, prior-family ablation, diagnostic case study, and prior-buffer sensitivity each use five seeds; no formal hypothesis tests are conducted. The experiments also do not provide com-prehensive sensitivity analyses for λρ, prior-update frequency, or prior architecture, and they do not quantify the additional computational overhead of prior training and density evalua-tion. These questions remain important directions for broader evaluation. VII. C ONCLUSION 

This paper introduced MIFLOW, an online flow-policy method that uses a recency-adaptive behavior prior to calibrate path-density actor-critic updates. The prior provides a soft reference for recent action density and assigns an additional cost to critic-favored actions with low prior density. Across six MuJoCo-v4 tasks, MIFLOW produced task-dependent competitive or higher mean returns relative to the evaluated baselines. The Walker2d-v4 diagnostics and prior ablations further characterize how policy behavior varies relative to the learned reference. Within the stated experimental and diagnostic boundaries, these results support dynamic recent-behavior priors as a practical calibration mechanism for online flow-policy learning. Broader claims about critic extrapolation, statistical superiority, and generalization beyond MuJoCo re-quire additional evidence. AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS
