## Abstract 

Neural architecture search and LLM-driven evolution treat evaluation as a scalar fitness black box: winners survive without mechanistic explanation, while failed trials are dis-carded despite the causal signal their errors carry. We in-troduce a hypothesis-driven architecture discovery loop 

that reframes automated network design from black-box score selection into falsifiable scientific inquiry. Rather than optimizing task-specific point models, LLM agents use compute profiles and structured error strata to formulate prospective hypotheses, test counterfactual controls under a frozen pipeline, and distill confirmed mechanisms into ex-ecutable trigger → action design rules. We evaluate this paradigm on nnU-Net , a stringent testbed whose self-configuring pipeline eliminates recipe confounding while its plain U-Net has withstood years of Transformer, state-space, and NAS challengers at matched compute. Across eight waves, our loop uncovers two cross-modality design principles of 3D dense prediction . First, a 3D surface-to-volume compute principle : slimming high-resolution de-coders exposes a selective geometric regression on thin anatomy ( ρ = −0.83 ), proving 3D boundary fidelity is bot-tlenecked by mid-encoder depth rather than fine decoder width and unlocking coarse large-kernel mixing. Second, a support-confined region-contrast principle : nested sub-type errors track precision rather than recall because lo-cal 33 kernels confuse compartments sharing boundary tex-ture unless given mask-confined region distributions and anatomy-conditioned contrast, abolished by constant con-trols and gated by pre-training triggers. These principles yield lightweight LeanNeXt (0.78 × MACs, 0.92 × step; 

+1 .63 4-fold CV on KiTS23) and trigger-routed LeanNeXt+ ,outperforming random sampling, one-shot LLM genera-tion, and score-selected agent search, improving all 15 CT/MRI evaluations over nnU-Net and surpassing 3.3×

larger ResEnc-M on 12/15 benchmarks at 0.30 × parame-ters. These results establish hypothesis-driven rule distil-lation from failures as a viable paradigm for automated architecture discovery, yielding interpretable design prin-ciples that generalize zero-shot across modalities without task-specific search.

## 1. Introduction 

Designing a better neural network for one dataset is not the same as discovering transferable principles of how to design one. Classical neural architecture search and recent LLM evolution loops [2, 10, 22, 28, 33] rely on scalar score selec-tion : candidates are ranked by aggregate validation metrics, winners survive without mechanistic explanation, and lower-scoring trials representing the vast majority of compute are discarded as sunk cost. Yet in empirical inquiry, controlled failures provide the primary diagnostic signal, because where and how an intervention breaks localizes which inductive bias was violated. Can LLM agents move from black-box selection of point architectures to falsifiable discovery of transferable design rules? 

Our core premise is that network failures are struc-tured diagnostic signals rather than scalar deficits. In 3D dense prediction, aggregate Dice conflates distinct bottle-necks, including misplaced spatial arithmetic, thin-boundary under-capacity, and subtype confusion across compartments sharing local texture. Scalar ranking obscures these causes, yielding dataset-specific point models that neither transfer zero-shot nor accumulate reusable design knowledge. We instead formulate a hypothesis-driven architecture discov-ery loop that executes an observe–hypothesize–test–distill 

cycle: diagnosing compute profiles and error strata, commit-ting ex ante to directional predictions (including predicted regressions) and falsification criteria before GPU allocation, running matched counterfactual controls, and distilling con-firmed mechanisms into executable trigger → action 

rules. We test this discovery loop on nnU-Net [20], the stan-dard 3D biomedical segmentation framework [21]. nnU-Net poses a uniquely stringent testbed for automated discovery: its self-configuring pipeline freezes preprocessing, patch ge-ometry, and optimization so no gain can come from recipe tuning, while its plain U-Net topology has repeatedly de-feated modern Transformer, state-space, and NAS architec-tures at matched compute [21]. Iterating across eight waves under an identical 1000-epoch schedule where only network topology varies (Fig. 2), our loop evaluates 111 hypotheses, refutes over 85 % via pre-training audits and controlled repli-cations, and distills two cross-modality design principles of 3D volumetric representation :

First, a 3D surface-to-volume compute principle gov-erns where spatial arithmetic pays. nnU-Net’s two finest decoder stages consume 56.0 % of MACs for 1.6 % of pa-rameters. Slimming them saves compute (R1) while induc-ing a predicted geometric regression on thin, high-surface-to-volume anatomy ( ρ = −0.83 on AMOS22). Turning this failure into a general rule, agents reinvest saved MACs into mid-encoder depth to restore thin boundaries across CT organs and cardiac MRI (R2; ρ = +0 .47 , p = 0 .04 

on AMOS22) and unlock coarse 73 mixing where kernels span the feature map (R3), showing that where compute sits 

outweighs total model scale. 

Second, a support-confined region-contrast principle resolves the topological blindness of local kernels. Across 3D volumetric imaging (solid tumour vs. fluid cyst on CT, enhancing glioma rim vs. necrotic core on MRI, or dense multi-organ scenes), compartments sharing local 33 bound-ary texture separate only via region-wide intensity moments and core–rim–host contrast. Finding that KiTS23 tumour Dice tracks precision rather than recall in cyst-bearing kid-neys (Fig. 7), agents pool mask-confined region distributions and anatomy-conditioned contrast from detached coarse pre-dictions (R4, abolished by constant-input controls) whenever pre-training label topology indicates nested or dense multi-compartment targets. These principles yield two zero-shot models : lightweight 

LeanNeXt (R1–R3; Fig. 1), gaining +1 .63 4-fold CV Dice on KiTS23 ( +2 .03 on the primary split) at 0.78 × MACs and 0.92 × step time , and trigger-routed LeanNeXt+ , reaching 

+2 .71 4-fold CV ( +3 .47 primary split; 87.14 [ +4 .28 ] with eroded-core pooling) while surpassing 3.3× larger ResEnc-M on 12/15 evaluations at 0.30 × parameters . Our con-tributions span three levels: (1) a hypothesis-driven archi-tecture discovery loop that turns diagnostic failures into cu-mulative design memory (0/10 →8/13 in-budget hits) and outperforms random sampling, one-shot LLM generation, and score-selected agent search within the same system; (2) an executable cross-modality rulebook governing 3D 2compute allocation and region contrast; and (3) LeanNeXt and LeanNeXt+, improving all 15 whole-body CT and MRI evaluations without task-specific search. 

## 2. Related Work 

3D segmentation backbones inside self-configuring pipelines. nnU-Net [20] distills each dataset into a fin-gerprint to configure an experiment plan around a plain U-Net [37], adapting its outer pipeline while keeping block topology fixed. Under unified benchmarking [21], trans-former [11, 12, 15, 45, 58], large-kernel [25, 38], state-space [29, 46, 50], and hybrid [7, 40, 59] backbones rarely beat CNN U-Nets without inflating parameters, memory, and step time, as seen in Residual-Encoder presets [18, 31], MedNeXt [38], and scaling studies [19, 39]. Building on slim decoders [35], depthwise kernels [8, 9, 27, 30, 48], and design-space principles [34, 44], our discovery loop turns failures into falsifiable rules specifying where spatial com-pute and clinical cues belong. 

Neural architecture search and LLM-driven discov-ery. Classical 3D medical NAS searches fixed spaces under proxy budgets [3, 13, 54, 60], where random sam-pling stays competitive [26, 52, 53] (Sec. S6). Recent LLM loops evolve code or networks against scalar evalua-tors [6, 17, 32, 33, 36, 51], self-modify pipelines [43, 47, 57], or automate manuscripts [28, 49]. In medical segmen-tation (Tab. 1), Auto-nnU-Net [2], AgentNAS [22], and Camyla [10] rank candidates by validation scores to select task-specific configurations or models; our discovery loop instead tests prospective falsifiable predictions to compile an executable zero-shot rulebook. 

## 3. Hypothesis-Driven Architecture Discovery Loop 

Whereas agentic architecture search treats evaluation as a scalar oracle and discards lower-scoring trials, our discovery loop operationalizes falsifiable inquiry over neural design spaces (Fig. 2, Tab. 1). Each iteration produces an ex-ante hy-pothesis card committing to directional predictions and a fal-sification condition before GPU spend, and an executable de-sign rule (trigger → action ) accumulating epistemic memory. 

3.1. Controlled Research Environment 

Freezing the pipeline isolates architectural effects 

(Fig. 2a). In nnU-Net, each dataset’s fingerprint (geome-try and intensity statistics) determines an experiment plan 

(voxel spacing, 3D patch size, and stage strides). To prevent recipe confounding, our harness locks the fingerprint, plan, preprocessing, splits, optimizer, loss, 1000-epoch schedule, 12 GB GPU memory cap, and evaluation code (Sec. S1), leav-ing only network topology open. Candidates train for the full 1000 epochs within nnU-Net’s parameter and step-time budget, isolating converged capacity from early-stopping noise. 

3.2. From Observations to Falsifiable Hypotheses 

Proposals commit to directional predictions and falsi-fication criteria before training (Fig. 2b,c). Observe & Diagnose combines stage-wise MAC, memory, and step-time profiles with error decompositions across anatomical classes and clinical strata (boundary vs. interior, precision vs. recall, cyst-bearing vs. cyst-free kidneys) and prior wave failures. Hypothesize & Predict encodes each diagnosis as a 

hypothesis card (Fig. 2c) stating the observation, mechanism, intervention, strata predicted to improve and to regress , com-pute bound, and falsification condition. Four roles powered by qwen3.8-flash-next in a Claude Code scaffold (Tab. S6) run the loop: a coordinator writes wave briefs, 

ideators draft hypothesis cards, an independent reviewer 

audits premises and budgets before GPU allocation, and an experimenter implements, profiles, and trains approved designs (Sec. S3). 

Turning a predicted regression into the next hypothe-sis. In Wave 4, profiling showed that nnU-Net’s two finest decoder stages consume 56.0 % of MACs for 1.6 % of pa-rameters. An ideator slimmed every decoder stage to one 

33 conv (0.81 × step time), predicting intact large organs alongside thin-organ regressions. Both held: KiTS23 rose by +1 .04 , whereas AMOS22 lost −1.05 on oesophagus and −0.71 on right adrenal vs. −0.02 on liver, scaling with 

boundary share (fraction of ground-truth organ voxels on its 3D surface; Spearman ρ = −0.83 ). That confirmed regres-sion directly seeded Wave 5’s hypothesis: reinvesting saved decoder compute into mid-encoder depth. 

3.3. Experimental Validation and Evidence Accu-mulation 

Controlled ablations, falsification rate, and multi-stage validation. Candidates screen on fold 0 of KiTS23 [16] and AMOS22 [23] (excluded from all reported 4-fold CV estimates), then advance to 4-fold CV, unseen benchmarks, and locked test sets. Information-mechanism claims require matched counterfactual controls ( e.g ., replacing region in-tensity statistics with constants at identical topology and pa-rameter count). Falsification is the norm: across eight waves, agents proposed 111 hypotheses; pre-execution reviewer audits rejected 59 before GPU spend (12 whose premise was contradicted by prior measurements, 18 dominated by simpler designs, and 5 exceeding memory), and of 28 full-budget architectures trained, 14 were refuted during fresh-seed replication or ACDC checks ( e.g ., global tokens X1 trailing LeanNeXt by +0 .97 vs. +1 .63 , or 73 blocks with-out R2 dropping KiTS23 by −1.75 ), leaving four validated rules (Tab. 4; full funnel in Tab. Insight 1: Single-seed screening suffers systematic winner’s curse; multi-seed and multi-fold verification is essential (Fig. 3, Tab. 4). Because nnU-Net’s own run-to-run standard deviation on KiTS23 fold 0 is 0.63 Dice points across 9 identical runs, a null architecture clears a +0 .35 

single-seed screen in 37 % of draws. Re-evaluating all 21 screen-admitted agent designs on two fresh seeds reveals a statistically significant selection shrinkage of −0.34 points (95 % CI [ −0.60 , −0.08 ], 13/21 designs dropping; Fig. 3), and tightening the screening threshold from +0 .35 to +1 .25 

only worsens mean shrinkage from −0.33 to −0.57 points (Tab. S18). Consequently, scalar single-run rankers systemat-ically promote lucky noise draws, whereas our loop requires fresh-seed survival, 4-fold cross-validation, and counterfac-tual mechanism controls (Tab. 4) before any candidate crys-tallizes into a rule. 

3.4. From Experimental Evidence to Design Rules 

Distilled rules pair pre-training triggers with architec-tural actions. Computed automatically from the dataset fingerprint and training labels prior to training, each trig-ger specifies when an action applies, bounded by where predicted gains and regressions appeared; the resulting rule-book disciplines later waves and configures unseen datasets zero-shot (Sec. 5.3).

## 5. Analysis: What the Loop Discovered 

We now dissect the two cross-modality principles into rules R1–R4, test whether their pre-training triggers predict gains on unseen CT and MRI tasks, and evaluate consistency across all folds and runs. 

5.1. Principle 1: 3D Surface-to-Volume Compute 

Reallocating decoder arithmetic into mid-encoder depth eliminates the thin-organ penalty and unlocks coarse large kernels (Tab. 5). R1 alone lifts KiTS23 by +0 .99 

(3/4 folds) at 0.73 × step time, but causes an organ-specific AMOS22 drop scaling with boundary share ( ρ = −0.83 ;Fig. 8a). p = 0 .04 ; Figs. 5a, 9C) and flattening LeanNeXt’s slope (ρ = −0.25 ; Fig. 8c). R2 also enables R3: adding 73

blocks without R2 drops KiTS23 by −1.75 , whereas R3 atop R1 +R2 improves all three benchmarks at 0.92 × step time.

## 6. Conclusion 

We introduced a hypothesis-driven architecture discovery loop that reframes automated neural design from black-box score selection into falsifiable scientific inquiry: LLM agents treat failures as diagnostic evidence, test counterfactual pre-dictions under a frozen pipeline, and distill confirmed mech-anisms into executable trigger → action rules. Uncov-ering cross-modality 3D principles for surface-to-volume compute (LeanNeXt) and support-confined region contrast (LeanNeXt+), the discovery loop outperforms random sam-pling, one-shot LLM generation, and score-selected agent search, improving all 15 CT/MRI evaluations over nnU-Net and surpassing 3.3× larger ResEnc-M on 12/15 benchmarks at 0.30 × parameters.
