# Abstract 

Precise  motion control for autonomous robots  is  a core technical enabler for the large -scale 

deployment of embodied intelligence.  In particular, basic manipulation tasks, such as point -to -point 

reaching and target grasping with robotic manipulators, impose stringent requirements  on control 

accuracy, real -time response, and energy efficiency for edge deployment. As a prevailing end -to -end 

control paradigm, reinforcement learning  (RL)  has demonstrated  remarkable capabilities in autonomous 

learning and generalization  for decision -making  in continuous action spaces. Nevertheless, constrained 

by electronic computing architectures, it generally suffers from  substantial  computing overhead,  high 

inference latency and  limited  energy efficiency,  making low -power edge deployment challenging .

Optical computing, benefiting from its high -speed parallelism, offers a promising route to break energy -

efficiency bottlenecks; however,  research on the integration of optical computing  and  RL  remains at an 

early stage . In this work, we propose  a Mach -Zehnder Interferometer (MZI) -based  opto -electronic hybrid 

RL  architecture.  We adopted two algorithm structures : Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient combined 

with Hindsight Experience Replay (DDPG -HER) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient 

combined with HER (TD3 -HER) . We implemented the key layer linear computation  of  the  spiking  neural 

network  (SNN ) Actor on an optical MZI chip for the reaching task of a Franka Emika Panda robotic 

manipulator.  The architecture is evaluated  in  the  PyBullet simulation environment. Experimental results 

demonstrate  that the proposed architecture achieves an energy efficiency of 1.39 TOPS/W and a n

inference latency  of 120 ps while  maintaining  control accura cy, which provide a feasible pathway toward 

energy -efficient and precise control  for robots.

Dexterous object manipulation is a  fundamental human capability supported  by a sophisticated 

sensorimotor system . Through  precise  perception of the surrounding environment , rapid and iterative 

action planning , and adaptive behaviors guided by task objectives , humans can efficiently perform 

various manipulation tasks such as precise positioning and compliant grasping in complex unstructured 

scenarios. The unique musculoskeletal structure  of the human hand, together with its multimodal sensory 

feedback mechanisms, enables precise target localization while allowing flexible adaptation to variations 

in object shape and weight, as well as  environmental disturbances . Such innate dexterity has long served 

as a n important  source of inspiration for the development of robot  manipulation technologies.  Translating 

human manipulation  principles  and behavioral characteristics into robotic manipulator control systems 

to construct intelligent control frameworks  capable of  autonomous perception,  autonomous 

decision‑making and real -time execution , has become a  major  research  focus  in fields including service 

robots,  intelligent industrial  manufacturing, and  specialized  robots [1 -4]. 

Intelligent and precise  robotic manipulation relies heavily  on the coordination of three  fundamental 

components : perception, decision -making, and control [5]. First, high -precision environmental 

perception  enables the system to acquire real -time information about target object poses, the robot's own 

state, and surrounding obstacles through sensors such as cameras and depth sensors . The acquired high -

dimensional sensory data are then accurately processed and interpreted to provide reliable inputs for 

subsequent policy decisions . Second,  precise policy generation involves rapidly producing stable and 

accurate joint control commands based on real -time sensory information within a continuous action space, 

while balancing response speed and positioning accuracy . Third, task -oriented optimization dynamically 

adjusts execution policies according to specific manipulation objectives, enabling  the system to adapt to 

changing task conditions while maximizing execution efficiency and robustness . These three components 

work together to establish a closed -loop control framework, which is essential for achieving precise 

robotic manipulation .

The development of intelligent robotic manipulation has witnessed the emergence of three major 

control paradigms, driving progressive improvements in manipulation capabilities from structured to 

complex and unstructured environments . The earliest approaches primarily relied on control based on 

kinematic and dynamic modeling. By establishing accurate mathematical models of robotic manipulators, 

these methods solve the inverse kinematics problem to determine the required joint configurat ions and 

generate corr esponding control commands for the end effecto r [6 -8].  Although such approaches can 

achieve high positioning accuracy in structured environments, their performance is highly sensitive to 

model inaccuracies. Variations in object properties and environmental disturbances can significantly 

compromise control robu stness, limiting their applicability in complex and unstructured environments .The second paradigm is imitation learning, which  acquires  manipulation skills by learning  from human 

demonstration trajectories . It can be rapidly deployed for specific tasks without elaborate modeling [9 -

11]. It features a straightforward training pipeline and strong task adaptability, yet its generalization 

ability heavily depends on the coverage of demonstration data, and performance degradation readily 

occurs when confronting unseen scenarios.  The third paradigm is reinforcement learning  (RL) , which 

enables agents to optimize control policies through repeated trial -and -error interactions with their 

environments, without requiring explicit analytical models or predefined demonstrations. RL has 

demonstrated strong autonomous learning and general ization capabilities in control tasks involving 

continuous action spaces  [12 -15],  making it a major research direction in intelligent robotic manipulation .

Advances in  RL  algorithms have continuously  driven improvements in  intelligent robotic 

manipulation. In 2013, Mnih et al.  introduced  the Deep Q -Network (DQN), which combines deep neural 

networks with Q -learning. Leveraging experience replay and target networ ks , DQN enabled  end -to -end 

learning from high -dimensional visual  observations  to discrete actions [16]. In 2014, to address the 

challenges  of continuous action spaces, Silver et al.  proposed  the Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) 

framework, which allows a deterministic policy to directly map states to continuous actions  [17]. 

Building upon  this framework , Lillicrap et al. proposed the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) 

algorithm in 2015 , combining deterministic policy gradients with deep neural networks and an  Actor -

Critic architecture to enable continuous control. The use of  experience replay and soft updates of target 

networks further promotes training stability  [18]. In 2017, Schulman et al.  proposed  Proximal Policy 

Optimization (PPO). By clipping the objective function to constrain the policy update step size, it 

balances sample efficiency and training stability, and has become a widely  adopted policy  optimization 

approach for continuous  control tasks [19]. In 2018, the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy 

Gradient (TD3) algorithm  was  further introduced  to address the overestimation of Q  values in DDPG. 

By incorporating clipped double Q -learning, delayed policy updates, and target policy smoothing, TD3 

effectively reduces value estimation errors and improves training stability and policy robustness  [20]. 

Also in 2018, Haarnoja et al. proposed the Soft Actor -Critic (SAC) algorithm , which adopts the 

maximum entropy reinforcement learning framework. By maximizing both the expected cumulative 

reward and policy entropy, SAC encourages effective exploration and  enables the learning of diverse 

stochastic policies, thereby improving adaptability to complex environments  [21].  These advances have 

established a solid theoretical and methodological foundation for precise and autonomous robotic 

manipulation .

Meanwhile,  recent advances in photonic neuromorphic computing have opened up new possibilities 

for overcoming the limitations of conventional electronic computing architectures  [22 -37]. Among  these ,

optical neural networks  (ONNs)  based on the Mach -Zehnder Interferometer (MZI)  exploit optical interference to perform matrix multiplication efficiently . Benefiting from inherent advantages including 

low latency, high parallelism, and energy efficiency , they have  emerged as a promising approach to 

addressing computational bottlenecks in the post -Moore era  [22 -30]. In 2017, Shen et al. reported a 

programmable  ONN  based on MZI arrays and verified  its potential for speech recognitio n [38]. In 2021, 

Xu et al. proposed an  opto -electronic  hybrid convolutional neural network  (CNN)  architecture  that 

employs MZI arrays to perform convolution related computations optically, achieving a 1.73 -fold 

improvement in energy efficiency over conventional electronic implementations  [39 ]. In 2022, Wang et 

al. utilized soliton  microcombs and wavelength division multiplexing (WDM) to experimentally 

demonstrate the feasibility of parallel computing using high -dimensional  ONNs  based on MZI arrays .

Their work provided new architectural insights for the development of high -capacity on -chip optical AI 

accelerators  [40]. 

Although the integration of  RL  and optical computing has demonstrated application potential,  this 

research area remains at an early stage of development  [41 -43]. In 2023, Qiu et al.  proposed a meta -

surface based diffractive neural network and demonstrated the feasibility of using diffractive networks 

for RL -based decision making in simple game playing tasks,  such as Tic -Tac -Toe [44].  However, their 

approach has not yet been extended to continuous action control of robotic manipulators . In 2024, Krauss 

et al. combined embedded optical waveguide sensor with a  nonlinear autoregressive with exogenous 

inputs (NARX) neural network for state estimation in soft robotic fingers , which effectively reduced the 

end  effector position estimation error s [45]. In 2025, Blackert et al. developed a soft robotic manipulator 

based on light  responsive liquid  crystal elastomers.  They employed a spatial light  modulator to generate 

an array of multiple light beams for shape control and used a  CNN  to infer the optical excitation 

parameters required to achieve target deformations  [46]. Most existing studies focus on applying optical 

technologies to  robotic sensing and actuation . By contrast, research on incorporating optical computing 

into RL decision -making networks for real -time robotic manipulator control remains limited. 

Consequently, significant challenges remain in integrating optical computing with RL -based  control 

architectures .

In this work, we propose an MZI -based  opto -electronic  hybrid  RL  architecture  for the point -to -point 

reaching task of a  7-degree -of -freedom Franka Emika Panda robotic manipulator . Two  RL  algorithms ,

DDPG with Hindsight Experience Replay (DDPG -HER) and TD3 -HER, are  employed . The  Actor 

network  adopts  a spiking  neural  network  (SNN ) structure, and the time step is set to T  = 4 to match the 

state  update rhythm of the robotic manipulator. The MZI optical unit s are  used  to implement  the linear 

transformation in the second layer of the  Actor network . The Stochastic Parallel Gradient Descent (SPGD) 

algorithm is employed for offline training and precise phase configuration of MZI weights. During 

forward propagation,  the input data undergoes linear transformation in the optical domain through the MZI array, exploiting the high speed and low power consumption of optical computing . The Critic 

network retains  a conventional artificial neural network  (ANN)  architecture to  maintain accurate value 

estimation and facilitate stable training convergence . A robotic reaching task is established in the 

PyBullet simulation environment to systematically evaluate the feasibility and performance of the 

proposed  opto -electronic  hybrid architecture . This work aims to provide a technical reference for the 

development of high speed, low power  opto -electronic  hybrid controllers for intelligent robots and to 

promote the practical application of optical computing in robotic control .

# Experimental Setup and Methods 

Opto -Electronic Hybrid Reinforcement  Learning Architecture 

DDPG is a classic Actor -Critic  RL  algorithm for continuous  action  space tasks [18]. The DDPG -

based  opto -electronic  hybrid architecture constructed in this  work  is illustrated in Fig . 1(b). It consists of 

an Actor network  for policy generation  and a Critic network  for value evaluation . The Actor network 

takes environmental observations including the current joint states of the robotic manipulator,  the  end -

effector pose, and  the  target position as inputs, and outputs deterministic joint control actions to drive the 

manipulator toward the targe t. The Critic network takes state -action pairs as inputs and estimates the 

corresponding Q values. During training, the Critic network is updated by minimizing the temporal 

difference error, while the Actor network is optimized through policy gradients to maximize the Q value 

estimated by  the Critic network.  TD3  is an improved algorithm  developed  to address the  overestimation 

of Q values in DD PG  [20 ], as shown in Fig . 1(c).  Building upon DDPG, TD3 incorporates three key 

mechanisms: clipped double Q learning , delayed policy update, and target policy smoothing.  The clipped 

double Q -learning mechanism employs two independently trained Critic networks and uses the smaller 

of their target Q value estimates to effectively mitigate overestimation bias. Delayed policy updates 

reduce the update frequency of the Actor network relative to tha t of the Critic networks, thereby reducing 

the impact of value es timation errors on policy optimization. Target policy smoothing adds clipped noise 

to the target actions, reducing the sensitivity of value estimation to small variations in target actions and 

improving the robustness of policy learning .

obtain informative learning signals and can consequently slow down policy convergence. To address this 

issue,  Hindsight  Experience  Replay (HER) is incorporated into both the DDPG and TD3 frameworks

HER improves learning efficiency through goal relabeling, transforming experiences from 

trajectories that fail to achieve the original goal into useful training experiences with respect to alternative 

goals. During experience replay, additional goals are s ampled from the achieved states in the collected 

trajectory. Transitions that failed to reach the original goal are then relabeled with these alternative goals, 

and their corresponding rewards are recalculated according to the relabeled goals.  In this way, 

experiences that fail to achieve the original goal can be transformed into successful transitions under 

alternative goals, providing additional learning signals for the agent.  The relabeled experiences are stored 

together with the original experiences in the experience replay buffer and used for subsequent off -policy 

network updates . Without requiring modifications to the original reward function, HER improves sample 

efficiency by extracting useful information from unsuccessful attempts. This m echanism can accelerate 

policy convergence and enhance the adaptability of the learned policy to varying target positions and 

environmental disturbances, making it well suited to sparse reward robotic reaching tasks .Fig ure  1. Overall system  architecture . (a) Robotic manipulator control  workflow ; (b) DDPG algorithm

To accelerate the linear computations in the Actor network using optical hardware , we designed and 

fabricated a 16 ×16 MZI photonic neuromorphic computing chip. Its microscopic structure and packaged 

prototype are shown in Fig . 1(d). The chip is fabricated on a silicon -on -insulator (SOI) platform, with an 

overall dimension of 2.37 mm ×8.25 mm and a footprint of only 19.55 mm². It integrates 152 thermo -

optic phase shifters based on titanium nitride (TiN) heat ers , and  supports parallel optical computation 

using real -valued matrices of size 16 ×16, with  a matrix -vector multiplication (MVM) throughput of 2.5 

TOPS.  Based on the computational capacity of this 16 ×16 MZI chip, we customized the Actor network 

architecture to match the avail able optical hardware resources. As the policy generation unit, the Actor 

network adopts a n SNN architecture, as illustrated in Fig . 2(a).  Its dimensions are specifically configured 

to match the 16 ×16 computational capability of the MZI chip  shown in Fig . 2(b).  The network takes 12 -

dimensional state information as input, comprising the  6-dimensional manipulator state and velocity 

information, the  3-dimensional current end -effector position, and the  3-dimensional target position .These inputs are converted into discrete spike sequences by a spike encoding layer using four discrete 

time steps (T = 4) and are then fed into the SNN hidden layer for nonlinear feature extraction . The  16 ×

16 linear multiplication operation in the second layer of the Actor network is implemented by the MZI 

optical arra y, replacing the conventional electronic linear layer. After offline calibration of MZI weights 

using the SPGD algorithm, the input spike sequences undergo parallel  linear transformation in th e optical 

domain through the MZI array during inference . The resulting outputs are converted by a spike decoding 

layer into  7-dimensional continuous joint control commands, which drive the robotic manipulator to 

perform the point -to -point reaching task . As the value evaluation unit, the Critic network retains a 

conventional fully  connected artificial neural network (ANN)  architecture and operates entirely  in the 

electronic domain. It is not involved in optical hardware training and only guides the optimization of the 

Actor network through gradient information, thereby guaranteeing stability and accuracy throughout the 

training process. 

Environment Setup 

PyBullet [48] is an open  source , high  performance physics simulation engine for robotic learning, 

RL  and autonomous control  research . Based on rigid  body dynamics and constraint  solving algorithms, 

it supports  multibody dynamics simulation, collision detection, friction modeling, and sensor noise 

simulation . It has been widely  used  in embodied intelligence research , including robotic manipulator 

control, dexterous manipulation, and mobile robot navigation. PyBullet  provides an efficient framework 

for constr ucting simulation environments that reproduce the dynamic behavior of physical robots. For  a

7-degree -of -freedom Franka Emika Panda robotic manipulator, it can simulate physical properties and 

dynamic effects such as joint rotations, link inertia, gravity, end -effector contact forces, joint friction, 

and actuator delays . It also supports customized sensor models and environmental disturbances, 

providing a controllable simulation platform for training and evaluating  RL  policies .

Based on PyBullet, we constructed a simulation environment for the point to point reaching task of 

the Franka Emika Panda robotic manipulator. The initial configuration of the manipulator is randomly 

generated within its safe operating workspace, while tar get positions are uniformly sampled within the 

3-dimensional reachable workspace of the end effector. The workspace is defined by the following 

coordinate ranges : x∈[0.2, 0.8] m ，y∈[-0.4, 0.4] m ，z∈[0.05, 0.8] m . To improve the robustness of 

the learned poli cy and its potential for transfer to real world scenarios , Gaussian random noise is injected 

into the simulation to  model  visual localization errors, joint angle measurement noise and actuator output 

disturbances.  This allows the agent to learn under nonideal observation and action execution conditions. 

In addition, PyBullet provides real -time state feedback, enabling efficient access to the manipulator's 

joint positions, joint velocities, end effector pose, and interaction information with the surrounding 

environment.  These observations provide stable and frequent state inputs for the  RL  algorithms, supporting efficient and reliable policy training .

Simulation Training 

After constructing the PyBullet simulation environment, we first perform ed  pure software pre -

training  using  the DDPG -HER and TD3 -HER algorithms in the robotic manipulator reaching  task 

environment to obtain converged network weights. The output matrix of the critical linear layer in the 

Actor network is taken as the target weight  for optical implementation . Subsequently, an MZI array 

model  was  established in MATLAB, and the SPGD algorithm  was employed to optimize the MZI phase 

settings . Cosine similarity wa s used as the optimization objective to progressively align the optical 

weights with the reference weight matrix, thereby configuring the desired optical computation.  The 

simulation results show that the cosine similarity between the optical weights and the  target  weights 

approached 0.80 after 500 and 250 optimization iterations for DDPG -HER and TD3 -HER, respectively. 

After 3000 iterations, the corresponding cosine similarity values reached 0.881 and 0.934, respectively, 

as shown  in Fig . 2(c1) and Fig . 2(d1 ). After the weight configuration simulation, software fine -tuning 

was  carried out within the algorithm to adapt the overall architecture to the characteristics of optical 

computation . This process yielded  stable training reward curves  for  the hybrid architecture, as  shown  in Fig . 2(c2) and Fig . 2(d2). 

After policy convergence, the trained  opto -electronic  hybrid models were evaluated through 100 

independent reaching task trials in the simulation environment , where the average reward and end -

effector positioning accuracy are statistically evaluated. The test results are presented in Fig . 2(c3) and 

Fig . 2(d3).  Over the 100 test trials, DDPG -HER achieved an average reward of  -1.99 and an average end 

effector to target distance of 0.0326 m. TD3 -HER achieved an average reward of  -1.73 and an average 

distance o f 0.0321 m.  In this work, the success  criterion  was  defined as  an end effector to target distance 

of less than 0.05 m . Both algorithms  satisfied this criterion in all 100 test trials, corresponding to an 

observed task success rate of 100% . These results demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed  opto -

electronic  hybrid  RL  architecture and its ability to achieve accurate robotic reaching control in the 

simulation environment.

Experimental Results and Analysis 

Following the simulation validation, we further conducted  hardware experiments  using  the MZI experimental platform shown in Fig . 3(a). The platform  primarily  consists of a tunable laser (TL), Mach -

Zehnder modulator (MZM), polarization controller array (PC array), optical coupler (OC), 16 ×16 MZI 

optical computing chip, photodetector (PD), oscilloscope (OSC), and FPGA control unit. A

thermoelectric cooler (TEC) is  used  to stabilize the  chip operating temperature and suppress thermally 

induced phase drift, thereby reducing its impact on optical computing accuracy.  A multi -channel voltage 

source supplies voltages to the phase shifters of the MZI array. The FPGA driv es the MZM to load signals 

onto the optical carrier. The  PC array  is  adjusted to  establish the appropriate polarization states for optical 

transmission through the chip . The optical signals pass through the MZI array to complete linear 

computation. Finally, PDs convert optical signals into electrical signals, and the oscilloscope acquires 

the output optical intensity information.

HER and TD3 -HER converge d after approximately 2000 iterations, with  the cosine similarity reaching

simulation , mainly due to nonideal effects such as  measurement noise  and phase drift.  The equivalent 

MZI matrices measured on the hardware platform were then  imported into the simulation environment.

the average reward, positioning accuracy and task success rate  were  statistically analyzed. The 

experimental results are illustrated in Fig . 3(b3) and  3(c3). DDPG -HER achieve d an average reward of 

-2.18 and an average dis tance of 0.0343 m to the target position. TD3 -HER obtain ed  an average reward 

of  -1.76 and an average distance of 0.0332 m. Both algorithms achieve d a task success rate of 100.0%.

that when the distance threshold ranges from 0.05 m to 0.1 m, the average task success rates of both 

DDPG -HER  and TD3 -HER  exceed 90%, and TD3 -HER  outperforms DDPG -HER  overall.

For  the optical linear computation stage,  the MZI array exhibits an optical propagation latency as 

low as 120 ps and a computational energy efficiency of up to 1.39 TOPS/W . Compared with conventional 

electronic computing architectures, the optical computing unit achieves remarkable latency reduction 

and substantial improvement in energy efficiency. While guaranteeing control accuracy, it attains dual 

breakthroughs in real -time performance and power consumption, which well meets the deployment 

requirements o f edge robots for low power consumption and high real -time responsiveness. 

# Conclusion 

This  work  proposes an MZI -based  opto -electronic  hybrid  RL  architecture for the point -to -point 

reaching task of the Franka Emika Panda robotic manipulator , incorporating DDPG -HER and TD3 -HER .

The MZI array is  employed  to implement  the key linear computation layer of the SNN -based Actor 

network , and the SPGD algorithm is employed for phase configuration of optical weights. The feasibility 

and  performance  of the architecture are  evaluated  through  PyBullet simulations and hardware inference 

experiments . Experim ental results demonstrate that after introducing the MZI photonic hardware, the 

system control performance degrades to a certain extent compared with pure software simulation due to 

nonideal factors such as chip noise and thermal phase drift. Nevertheless,  both algorithms can still 

accomplish the robotic manipulator point -to -point reaching task. During hardware inference, DDPG -

HER achieves an average reward of  -3.18 and an average distance of 0.0386 m  between the end effector 

and the target , while TD3 -HER o btains an average reward of  -2.74 and an average distance of 0.0360 m. 

Within the distance threshold range of 0.05 -0.1 m, the average task success rates of DDPG -HER  and 

TD3 -HER  exceed 90%.  The MZI array achieves an optical propagation latency as low as 120 ps and a computational energy efficiency of 1.39 TOPS/W . While guaranteeing control accuracy, it significantly 

improves real -time performance,  providing a feasible approach toward low power and high -speed robotic 

manipulation .

# Discussion 

Focusing on the application of the  opto -electronic  hybrid  RL  architecture in robotic manipulator 

control, this  work  carries out full -process experiments including simulation calibration, MZI hardware 

phase calibration and MZI hardware inference.  The performance differences between DDPG -HER and 

TD3 -HER are quantitatively evaluated using two metrics: average reward and end -effector positioning 

error.  Overall, TD3 -HER exhibits better control performance than DDPG -HER across all evaluated 

stages, including software simulation, simulation evaluation using hardware -calibrated MZI matrices, 

and hardware inference. In the simulation environment, TD3 -HER  achieves  an average reward of  -1.73, 

compared with  -1.99 for DDPG -HER , and an average positioning error of 0.0321 m, compared with 

0.0326 m for DDPG -HER . These results indicate that TD3 -HER  achieves slightly better control 

performance than DDPG -HER  under softw are simulation condi tions.  When hardware -calibrated MZI 

weight matrices are imported into the simulation environment for evaluation, both algorithms exhibit 

only slight performance degradation, suggesting that the MZI implementation preserves much of the 

functionality of the target  linear transformations. During hardware inference, the overall system 

performance degrades due to hardware noise, phase drift, and non -ideal device characteristics. 

Nevertheless, TD3 -HER achieves an average reward of  -2.74 and an  average positioning error of 0.0360 

m, outperforming DDPG -HER, which achieves an average reward of  -3.18 and an average positioning 

error of 0.0386 m. These results demonstrate that, despite the performance degradation introduced by 

hardware implementation, both algorithms maintain  effective reaching performance, with TD3 -HER 

consistently achieving better results under the tested conditions. Overall, both algorithms maintain 

centimeter -level reaching accuracy during hardware inference.
