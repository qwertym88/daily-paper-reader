Abstract
The linear quadratic regulation (LQR) problem over a multi-agent system usually requires
a distributed feedback gain that is structurally constrained by the network topology, incurring
an inevitable suboptimality gap. Existing studies either identify conditions on the system and
topology that enable convex relaxation or seek a stationary point via policy gradient algorithms. In
this paper, we reduce the suboptimality gap by adopting action-dependent policies (ADPs), where
the control input of each agent depends on not only states of neighbor agents, but also control
inputs of some other agents. We show that although exhibiting a distributed structure, ADP can
enlarge the feasible set of feedback gains and even represent centralized optimal policies under
suitable conditions. However, this comes at the price of losing coercivity of the cost function and
possible failure of convergence for the projected gradient flow (PGF). To overcome this difficulty,
we propose a partially regularized formulation, which guarantees convergence of PGFs, and forges
a connection between standard multi-agent LQR and its action-dependent counterpart. Sufficient
conditions for local convergence to globally optimal policies under PGFs regardless of regularization
are also established. Finally, numerical examples are presented to validate the performance of ADP.

Introduction
In recent years, advances in policy optimization (PO) methods within reinforcement learning (RL)
have renewed interest in linear quadratic regulation (LQR) problems [1]. For centralized LQR (no
structural constraint on the control gain), the cost function is shown to be coercive and gradient
dominant, ensuring global convergence of first-order methods [2, 3, 4, 5]. Subsequent studies have
extended these results to LQR with output feedback, risk-constraints, and model-free settings [6, 7,
8, 9, 10]. However, these results are limited to single-agent systems.
The widespread deployment of multi-agent systems (MASs), which typically consist of multiple
coupled agents, has spurred growing attention to cooperative control and optimization problems
1

[11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17]. As a canonical problem in this area, the multi-agent LQR problem aims
to minimize a global quadratic cost for a network of coupled agents. When each agent has access
only to local information, such as its own measurements and data received from its neighbors, the
problem is referred to as distributed multi-agent LQR, and the resulting controller is called a distributed
controller. Unlike its centralized counterpart, designing distributed optimal controllers is generally
NP-hard [18]. In particular, the authors in [19] showed that the set of stabilizing static decentralized
controllers can possess exponentially many connected components. Despite these challenges arising
from the lack of full state information, a number of notable works, including [20, 21, 22, 23, 24], have
appeared since the early 2000s. Existing approaches mainly fall into two categories: 1) identifying
system characteristics and sparsity (or delay) conditions that admit tractable algorithms; 2) deriving
suboptimal controllers with bounded suboptimality gaps. For the former, the quadratic invariance
condition allows constrained minimum-norm problems to be solved via convex optimization [21].
For the latter, convex relaxation methods [25, 26] and sparsity invariance techniques [24] have been
developed. In short, much of the prior work involves a trade-off between the degree of decentralization
and suboptimality [27, 28]. Nevertheless, a central question remains:
Given a multi-agent LQR problem, is it possible to achieve global optimality under a distributed
control architecture?
Behind this question lies a policy representational bottleneck: the centralized optimal gain is
generally dense, whereas a distributed state feedback gain must respect the sparsity pattern of the
sensing/communication graph. From this viewpoint, the difficulty essentially comes from the limited
representation capability of the distributed policy class itself. The same bottleneck has been observed
in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). Intuitively, executing actions in a correlated manner
leads to better cooperation; however, the commonly adopted independent/factorized policies cannot
represent joint actions that require coordination. Until very recently, action-dependent policies (ADP)
were introduced in [29, 30, 31] to enlarge the joint policy space. Specifically, action dependencies
refer to the dependence of an agent’s decision-making on the actions of other agents. As shown in
[29, 30, 31], action dependencies provide a principled way to enlarge the representational capacity of
distributed policies, and naturally lead to a better performance.
Inspired by the aforementioned developments, we apply ADPs to the multi-agent LQR problem.
In the literature, the usual distributed control input at each agent depends on its own state and the
states of its neighbors through local sensing/communication. After introducing action dependencies,
the control input of one agent additionally depends on inputs of its neighbors in the action dependency
graph (ADG). Compared with densifying the sensing graph, the ADG offers extra design freedom,
which naturally enlarges the policy space. However, this enlargement does not come for free: the
induced cost function is no longer coercive and the optimal control policies are generally non-isolated,
so that policy gradient (PG) methods may fail to converge. How to exploit the enlarged policy space
while restoring convergence, and whether the enlarged space suffices to recover the centralized optimal
policy, are topics addressed in this paper.

1.1
Main contributions
• Action dependency graphs for fully dense policy representation: To characterize the enlarged
policy space, the ADG is introduced to the multi-agent LQR problem for the first time, to the best of
our knowledge. We demonstrate the significant contribution of action dependencies to representing
a fully dense policy. More precisely, the sparsity patterns of the induced gains are identified (see
Proposition 1), and a necessary and sufficient graphical condition is established to characterize the
representability for generic policies (see Theorem 1).
• Convergence of policy gradient methods with action dependencies:
Despite the enlarge-
ment of the policy space, we emphasize that projected gradient flow (PGF) for multi-agent LQR with
action dependencies may not converge due to the lack of coercivity (see Example 2). Therefore, we
introduce a partial regularization term to overcome this difficulty (see Theorem 2), and discover that
the partially regularized problem acts as a novel bridge between the original multi-agent LQR and its
action-dependent counterpart (see Theorem 3).
• Optimality guarantee and improvement of performance: To achieve global optimality, we
utilize the complementary sparsity conditions on ADGs and sensing graphs to establish the guarantee
in a generic sense (see Theorem 4 and Theorem 5). The analyticity, Polyak– Lojasiewicz inequality,
and Morse-Bott property of cost functions at stationary points are fully exploited to establish the
exponentially asymptotic stability of the equilibrium set. Additionally, an algorithm (see Algorithm
1) is proposed to guarantee the performance improvement over the PGF of the distributed multi-agent
LQR problem.
1.2
Related work
• Action-dependent policies in MARL: Existing works concerning ADPs in MARL mainly include
[29, 31, 32]. All these works address the policy representational bottleneck and show that compared
to independent policies, ADPs enable more effective cooperation and even achieve global optimality
under certain conditions. However, these results rely crucially on a finite action space. Although the
ADG adopted in this paper is similar to that in [31], the continuous nature of the action space, and
the complexity of the optimization landscape in the multi-agent LQR setting make their results not
directly extendable to our case.
• Policies beyond state feedback in the multi-agent LQR: Beyond the conventional state feed-
back, various policies, including output feedback [33, 21], disturbance feedback [34, 23], and policies
leveraging neighbors’ states and inputs [35], have also been considered in the multi-agent LQR setting.
In particular, [35] designed distributed optimal controllers for the multi-agent LQR with each agent
leveraging its neighbors’ state and action information; yet they assume homogeneous dynamics and
a consensus-oriented cost. In contrast, we consider general linear dynamics and establish sufficient
conditions for achieving centralized optimality.
• Policy gradient algorithms in LQR: Convergence results of PG methods for LQR have been
extensively studied [2, 3, 6, 36, 37, 38]. In our work, the introduction of ADGs provides a reparame-
terization of controllers. Besides the loss of coercivity, redundancy in parameterization indicates that
3


the optimal policies can form a smooth manifold. Recall that a similar non-isolation of optimizers
arises in Linear Quadratic Gaussian (LQG) problems, and is caused by the invariance of the cost
under similarity transformations[39, 40]; here it instead stems from the invariance of the cost along
the fiber of the parameterization. Since the fiber is not well-behaved (its dimension may vary with
the base point), quotienting out symmetries as in [40] is inapplicable, which distinguishes our analysis
from prior work.

Conclusion
This work introduced ADP to enlarge the policy space and enhance the optimality of distributed policy
gradient for multi-agent LQR problems. Two complementary sparsity conditions were proposed to
characterize the representability for generic policies. A PGF was then applied to solve the resulting
PO problem. To address the possible non-convergence of the PGF caused by the loss of coercivity,
a partially regularized problem was introduced and shown to serve as a bridge between the original
multi-agent LQR problem and its counterpart with action dependencies. The convergence of the PGF
for the regularized problem was established, and a sufficient condition was derived for local convergence
to the globally optimal policy. When the complementary sparsity conditions are not satisfied, the
ADP-based algorithm has been shown to guarantee performance improvement. However, the extent
25


of this improvement is yet to be precisely quantified. This will be an important direction for our
future research.
