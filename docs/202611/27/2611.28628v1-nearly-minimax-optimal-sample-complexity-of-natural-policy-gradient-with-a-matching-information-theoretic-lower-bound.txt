# Nearly Minimax-Optimal Sample Complexity of Natural Policy Gradient with a Matching Information-Theoretic Lower Bound 

Asha Barua 1, Sajad Khodadadian 1

Abstract 

A fundamental question in reinforcement learning is how many sampled transitions are needed to learn a near-optimal policy, and whether direct policy optimization can attain this sta-tistical limit. To answer this question, we consider natural policy gradient (NPG), a widely used policy optimization method. We study tabular finite-horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs) with known rewards and unknown horizon-dependent transition kernels, accessed through a gen-erative model. Our sample-based NPG algorithms update the policy by backward sweeps and do not estimate transition probabilities. Under three different batch-size and step size schedules, we establish last-iterate guarantees with sample complexity ˜O(SAH 4/ε 2), where S, A, and H

denote the number of states, the number of actions, and the horizon length, respectively. In this setting, horizon-dependent transition kernels contribute a factor of H to our transition-query bound. Our information-theoretic lower bound of Ω( SAH 4/ε 2) shows that the resulting H4 de-pendence is unavoidable in the worst case, even for agents that select their queries adaptively. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first last-iterate guarantees for NPG whose sample com-plexity matches an information-theoretic lower bound up to logarithmic factors. Consequently, our results demonstrate that direct policy-space optimization through NPG can achieve the fundamental statistical limits of reinforcement learning while guaranteeing the performance of the final policy. Furthermore, for known kernels, we prove that exact NPG with a constant step size η = 1 /H requires Ω( H2/ε ) iterations in the worst case, matching the known upper bound for that step size. 

1 Introduction 

Reinforcement learning (RL) provides a framework for sequential decision making under uncertainty and has achieved empirical success in game playing, robotic control, autonomous navigation, and large-scale decision systems [Singh et al., 2022, Silver et al., 2016, Mnih et al., 2015, Arulkumaran et al., 2017]. A Markov decision process (MDP) provides the mathematical foundation for RL, which describes an agent’s interaction with an uncertain environment. The objective is to learn a policy that maximizes the expected cumulative reward [Puterman, 2014, Sutton and Barto, 2018]. Policy optimization methods pursue this objective by searching directly over a class of policies. Policy gradient (PG) methods carry out this search through gradient ascent on the expected return of a parameterized policy, an approach that accommodates both structured and high-dimensional policy classes [Williams, 1992, Sutton et al., 1999]. Among these methods, natural policy gradient (NPG), introduced by Kakade [2001], accounts for the geometry of the policy space through the Fisher information metric. This geometric viewpoint has influenced practical methods such as trust region policy optimization (TRPO) and proximal policy optimization (PPO) [Schulman et al.,

In the tabular softmax setting, the multiplicative NPG update is equivalent to a Kullback-Leibler (KL)-regularized policy mirror descent (PMD) update over the simplex of action distributions at each state [Shani et al., 2020, Lan, 2023]. Consequently, convergence guarantees for this PMD update also hold for the multiplicative NPG update. This equivalence connects the analysis of NPG to the broader theory of policy optimization. NPG and its variants have achieved empirical success in complex control tasks, including high-dimensional robotic manipulation [Peters and Schaal, 2008, Rajeswaran et al., 2018]. These appli-cations raise a basic question about sample efficiency. How many transitions does NPG need to learn a near-optimal policy? A substantial body of work has established convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for policy optimization methods, including NPG, in discounted infinite-horizon MDPs [Agarwal et al., 2021, Mei et al., 2020, Cen et al., 2022, Xiao, 2022, Lan, 2023, Johnson et al., 2023]. In the discounted infinite-horizon setting, a single transition kernel governs the dynamics, a stationary optimal policy exists, and the Bellman optimality operator is a con-traction. For tasks with a fixed number of decisions, however, a finite-horizon MDP provides a natural formulation [Azar et al., 2017, Jin et al., 2018, Dann et al., 2017, Jin et al., 2020]. In a finite-horizon MDP, optimal decision rules, value functions, and transition kernels can vary across horizons, which introduces a distinct theoretical challenge for policy optimization. For this class of MDPs, Barua and Khodadadian [2026] established finite-time convergence guarantees for NPG with known action values. We study the corresponding sample-based problem under generative access, with known rewards and unknown transition probabilities. Our NPG method maintains a tabular policy and its action-value estimates in O(SAH ) space without estimating transition probabilities. Our goal is to determine how many transition queries NPG needs for its final policy to achieve an expected optimality gap under a fixed initial distribution. Prior work on finite-horizon MDPs provides several sample-complexity benchmarks. Sidford et al. [2018] establish a high-probability guarantee for variance-reduced value iteration when the transition kernel is shared across horizons. For horizon-dependent kernels, Li et al. [2024] establish sharp sample-complexity bounds for model-based planning. Li et al. [2022] analyze Q-FTRL for Markov games and, in the single-agent setting, give a high-probability guarantee for a weighted average of policy iterates. These results leave open whether policy-based methods such as NPG can attain the minimax sample complexity, up to logarithmic factors, with a guarantee for its final policy. Our main contributions are as follows: 1. Iteration lower bound for exact NPG: We establish a worst-case iteration lower bound of Ω( H2/ε ) for exact NPG with constant step size. This matches the dependence on H and 

ε in the corresponding upper bound of Barua and Khodadadian [2026]. 2. Last-iterate guarantees for sample-based NPG: We show that under three step size schedules (geometrically increasing, constant and linearly decreasing) ˜O(SAH 4/ε 2) transition queries suffice for the final policy of sample-based NPG to achieve an expected optimality gap of at most ε.3. Information-theoretic benchmark: For every S, A, H ≥ 2, we establish that any algo-rithm requires Ω( SAH 4/ε 2) transition queries under the same expected-gap criterion. The bound allows adaptive query selection and imposes no memory restriction. Consequently, the proposed NPG procedures attain last-iterate guarantees with near-minimax-optimal sample complexity up to logarithmic factors. 22 Related Literature

3.3 A constructed family of finite-horizon MDPs 

Our family adapts the construction used by Kakade [2003, Theorem 2.5.2]. Both constructions have an absorbing state with a positive reward, along with a set of decision states with zero reward. In our construction, every action has a positive probability of entering the reward state on the next transition. The transition parameters may vary across horizons, and the favorable action (which attains the maximum expected cumulative reward) may vary across both states and horizons. Thus, the same decision state can have different favorable actions at different horizons. 5 Sample-based Setting 

We consider unknown horizon-dependent transition kernels and known reward functions. We in-troduce the sampling framework and NPG update, then analyze three choices of batch and step size schedules. The section concludes with an information-theoretic lower bound. Generative access is a standard benchmark for studying the sample complexity of reinforcement learning [Kearns and Singh, 1999, Kakade, 2003, Azar et al., 2013, Li et al., 2024]. The agent can query any triple ( h, s, a ) ∈ [H − 1] × S × A . Conditional on the query and all preceding information, the oracle returns a fresh successor with distribution P h(· | s, a ). Queries can depend on preceding observations and the agent’s internal randomness. Each successor observation counts as one transition query, and the agent uses at most N queries almost surely. No query is required at horizon H, since Qπ,H = RH is known. We adopt the storage-based interpretation of model-free algorithms in Jin et al. [2018, Defi-nition 1]. The proposed NPG algorithms use O(SAH ) memory throughout execution, including the returned policy, without estimating transition probabilities. Stored estimates are updated and reused without retaining all previous iterates or the full sample history. Each iteration performs a backward sweep of NPG updates through horizons H, H − 1, . . . , 1. 6 Conclusion 

To the best of our knowledge, this work provides the first near-minimax-optimal sample-complexity guarantees for the last iterate of NPG in finite-horizon tabular MDPs with horizon-dependent transition kernels under generative access and the expected optimality-gap criterion. We analyze three batch and step size schedules, each attaining ˜O(SAH 4/ε 2) transition-query complexity us-ing O(SAH ) memory without estimating transition probabilities. The information-theoretic lower bound establishes that this polynomial dependence is unavoidable, even with adaptive query se-lection and unrestricted memory. In the exact setting, our iteration lower bound also matches the horizon and accuracy dependence of the corresponding constant step size upper bound. A natural next direction is to extend these guarantees to the infinite-horizon setting. Under generative access, an open question is whether the last-iterate sample-complexity bounds for NPG can be sharpened, both in their logarithmic dependence on the problem parameters and in their absolute constants.
