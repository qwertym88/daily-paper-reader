Simultaneously Boosting Efficiency and Capability for Linear Attention via
Adaptive Channel Compression and Activation Purification
Senqi Guan, Xianchao Zhang, Moyan Tian, Linlin Zong, Wenxin Liang, and Xinyue Liu
Dalian University of Technology, Liaoning, China
Abstract
Self-attention’s quadratic complexity constitutes a core
bottleneck for Vision Transformers (ViTs).
Existing
efficiency-oriented optimizations, including softmax-based
and linear attention (LA) variants, generally compromise
model representational capacity, leading to inevitable per-
formance degradation. We propose a novel linear attention
which alleviates this efficiency-capability dilemma. For the
first time, we embed an Adaptive Channel Compression and
Activation Purification (ACCAP) mechanism into the QKV
projection of linear attention, which enables the model to
retain high-dimensional feature information while perform-
ing computations with reduced channels. We further fuse
a focused kernel function and a self-gating module into
ACCAP to construct a lightweight linear attention mod-
ule (ACCAP-LA). Our theoretical analysis shows that, com-
pared to standard linear attention, ACCAP-LA reduces the
attention module’s parameter count by 79.17% and FLOPs
by 77.5%. Extensive experiments demonstrate ACCAP-LA’s
effectiveness: when applied to ViT, it achieves 79.4% Top-
1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with only 5.8M parameters,
and more notably, it boasts strong cross-architecture com-
patibility, boosting EfficientViT-b1 and PVT-T by 3.3% and
3.4% respectively.
1. Introduction
Vision Transformers (ViTs) [1] have become the domi-
nant paradigm in visual representation learning, delivering
state-of-the-art(SOTA) performance on image classification
[2] and object detection [3] via global self-attention model-
ing. However, the quadratic complexity (O(N 2)) of vanilla
softmax self-attention [4] emerges as a core bottleneck
hampering their further scaling and deployment. To address
this issue, numerous efficiency-oriented optimizations have
emerged, falling into two main categories: softmax-based
approaches and linear attention-based ones. Yet all variants
under these two categories compromise model capability in
different aspects, leading to performance degradation.

To address this efficiency-capability dilemma, we pro-
pose a novel Adaptive Channel Compression and Activa-
tion Purification (ACCAP) mechanism, which we embed
into the linear attention QKV projection process for the
first time (Figure 1(d)). Since more channels enable richer
multi-dimensional feature representations, ACCAP first al-
lows the model to access such high-dimensional feature rep-
resentations. It then enables QKV matrices to learn high-
dimensional feature semantics via low-dimensional vectors
during generation, with actual attention computation per-
formed using fewer channels. Specifically, ACCAP com-
prises three stages: channel compression, activation purifi-
cation, and channel expansion, which eliminates compu-
tational redundancy in linear attention and effectively pre-
serves and refines critical feature information in the atten-
tion module. Intuitively, as illustrated in Figure 2, the fea-
ture rank remains nearly stable through this pipeline, indi-
cating that channel reduction incurs negligible information
loss.
Notably, the ACCAP mechanism reduces the complexity
of standard linear attention from O(NC2) to O(NC2/r2),
where r denotes the compression ratio. Furthermore, we se-
quentially incorporate a focused kernel function and a self-
gating module following ACCAP to construct the ACCAP-
augmented Linear Attention (ACCAP-LA). When r = 2,
theoretical derivations confirm that compared to standard
linear attention, ACCAP-LA reduces the parameter count
by 79.17% and FLOPs by 77.5%.
Building upon ACCAP-LA, we design a series of ViT
models (dubbed ACCAP-ViT-M0 to M3) with varying pa-
rameter sizes based on a unified architecture. To validate
their effectiveness, we conduct image classification experi-
ments on the ImageNet-1K dataset and object detection ex-
periments on the COCO 2017 dataset. Across models of all
scales, ACCAP-ViTs consistently deliver outstanding per-
formance. We summarize our contributions as follows:
• We propose a channel-wise optimization mechanism
dubbed ACCAP for linear attention QKV projection,
which reduces computational redundancy while alle-
viating the inherent representational degradation of
vanilla linear attention.
• We develop a novel lightweight linear attention mod-
ule ACCAP-LA by integrating ACCAP with a focused
kernel function and a self-gating module, achieving a
superior efficiency-performance trade-off.
• Extensive experiments on ImageNet-1K and COCO
2017 validate that ACCAP-LA achieves competitive
performance, high inference efficiency, and strong
cross-architecture generalization across various ViT
backbones.
2. Related Work
2.1. Efficiency Optimization of SoftMax Attention
in Vision Transformers
Existing optimizations for softmax attention in ViTs
mainly follow two paradigms: token grouping and reso-
lution compression. Token grouping methods, typified by
Swin Transformer [5], restrict attention within local win-
dows or dynamic clusters to cut computational overhead.
Resolution compression approaches, represented by PVT
v2 [6], reduce token counts via pyramid downsampling.
While effective in lowering complexity, both paradigms op-
erate predominantly on the token dimension and inevitably
impair global perception or fine-grained feature sensitivity,
leaving channel-wise computational redundancy largely un-
explored.

Linear attention (LA) achieves linear complexity by
eliminating softmax and reordering computation, yet its
inherent low-rank nature causes severe representational
degradation. Existing enhancements mainly revolve around
three directions: kernel function design (e.g., Flatten Trans-
former [8] with focused kernels to sharpen attention dis-
tribution), multi-scale feature integration (e.g., EfficientViT
[10] to enrich semantic contexts), and hybrid architectures
that combine LA with softmax or state-space designs. Nev-
ertheless, these strategies yield limited gains or sacrifice in-
herent linear complexity advantages. Notably, most exist-
ing improvements focus on the attention computation stage,
while the QKV projection process, which dominates LA’s
parameter and computation budget, remains underexplored
from a channel-wise perspective.
2.3. Channel Compression and Gated Activation
Mechanisms
The squeeze-and-excitation (SE) paradigm [11] pioneers
channel-wise adaptive feature reweighting via compression
and gating. Follow-ups like CBAM [12] and ECA-Net [13]
further optimize this paradigm from spatial extension and
compression efficiency perspectives. All these methods tar-
get feature quality improvement, using channel compres-
sion only for attention weight generation rather than cost
reduction. SCConv [14] confirms prevalent channel redun-
dancy in visual models, but also aims at feature refinement
instead of cutting attention computation overhead. Gated
mechanisms are also widely explored in attention research.
Qiu et al. [15] apply self-gating to boost non-linearity and
alleviate attention sinks in LLMs. For visual linear atten-
tion, works like HLSM [16] introduce channel gating in
feature fusion stages for diversity enhancement, yet none
modify the core QKV projection pipeline.
Beyond attention-oriented channel designs, inverted
residual bottlenecks [17] in convolutional networks and
low-rank matrix factorization [18] in attention modules
also adopt channel-wise dimension reduction, but both are
essentially pure parameter compression strategies without
dedicated feature purification mechanisms. More critically,
neither of them has been customized to address the inherent
representational degradation issue of linear attention from
the perspective of QKV projection optimization.
Despite their widespread use in feature enhancement
and computation reduction, channel compression and gat-
ing have not been systematically integrated into the QKV
projection of linear attention to jointly reduce computa-
tional cost and mitigate representational degradation. To
this end, we propose the ACCAP mechanism.
3. Methods
To address these limitations mentioned above, this pa-
per proposes a novel linear attention mechanism, named
ACCAP-LA, from the perspective of feature channel redun-
dancy [14]. Specifically, we design an Adaptive Channel
Compression and Activation Purification (ACCAP) mech-
anism explicitly tailored for the QKV projection process,
and further complement it with a focused kernel function
and a self-gating module. This design ensures the model
maintains lightweight properties and high efficiency, while
significantly boosting its representational capability. De-
tailed descriptions of the proposed ACCAP-LA mechanism
are presented as follows.
3.1. Adaptive Channel Compression and Activation
Purification Mechanism
3.1.1
Why ACCAP is Necessary?
Feature channel redundancy is widely verified in exist-
ing vision models [14, 19], which motivates the neces-
sity of channel compression in the QKV projection pro-
cess. The number of channels represents different dimen-
sions of feature representations: a higher channel dimen-
sion enables the model to capture multi-faceted semantic
information from input features, thereby enhancing the ex-
pressiveness of the attention module. However, an exces-
sively large channel dimension introduces substantial com-
putational and memory overhead during the QKV projec-
tion and subsequent attention computation, which contra-
dicts the goal of lightweight model design. To reconcile the
trade-off between representational capacity and computa-
tional efficiency, we propose to leverage high-dimensional
channels for rich feature learning, while employing AC-
CAP for channel compression and activation purification
before computation.
This design enables the model to
encode high-dimensional feature information into a low-
dimensional space, achieving competitive performance with
significantly reduced computational costs.

3.1.3
Efficiency Gains from ACCAP
We quantify the parameter and FLOPs savings of the AC-
CAP mechanism by comparing the full attention module
(encompassing QKV projection and core attention compu-
tation) against standard linear attention. In alignment with
mainstream ViT efficiency analysis conventions, we ignore
bias terms and negligible element-wise operations in all
derivations, and count each multiply-add operation as one
FLOP for all computational cost statistics throughout this
work.
For an input feature X ∈Rb×n×c (b = batch size,
n = flattened spatial sequence length, c = input chan-
nels), standard linear attention uses a single 1×1 convolu-
tion to project the input to 3c channels for equal split into
Q/K/V (each with c channels), yielding 3c2 parameters and
3bnc2 FLOPs; the subsequent linear attention computation
Attention(Q, K, V ) = Q · (KT · V ) adds 2bnc2 FLOPs,
resulting in a total of 3c2 parameters and 5bnc2 FLOPs for
the complete module. Our ACCAP-LA replaces the sin-
gle heavy projection with a lightweight two-stage 1×1 con-
volution pipeline (channel compression to c/4 followed by
expansion to 3c/2), which reduces the channel dimension
of the projected Q/K/V to c/2 while preserving the linear
attention computation paradigm.
This optimized projec-
tion only introduces 5c2/8 parameters and 5bnc2/8 FLOPs,
and the reduced channel dimensions cut the core attention
FLOPs to bnc2/2.
In total, ACCAP-LA achieves 5c2/8 parameters and
9bnc2/8 FLOPs, translating to a 79.17% parameter reduc-
tion and a 77.5% FLOPs reduction over standard linear
attention. This significant efficiency gain stems from the
reduced channel dimensions in both projection and atten-
tion computation, while our adaptive channel compression
and activation purification strategy maintains sufficient rep-
resentational capacity.

In contrast to in-

channels for spatial convolution before projecting back to
a low-dimensional feature output, ACCAP adopts a “wide-
narrow-wide” channel transformation specifically engi-
neered for the QKV projection stage: it compresses full-

for efficient computation, then expands to generate high-
dimensional QKV matrices instead of returning to a low-
dimensional feature map. Unlike inverted bottlenecks that
act as general convolutional feature extractors in backbone
stages, ACCAP is purpose-built for linear attention’s QKV
generation, with the core objective of mitigating linear at-
tention’s inherent representational degradation rather than
solely reducing computational overhead.
Furthermore, standard low-rank QKV factorization ap-
proximates the full projection matrix via two successive
linear mappings purely for parameter and computation re-
duction, with no non-linear feature refinement between lay-
ers. ACCAP, by contrast, inserts a ReLU6-based activation
purification step between the compression and expansion
projections, which filters invalid activations and refines fea-
ture semantics in the compressed latent space. Most im-
portantly, ACCAP is not merely a low-rank approximation
of the original QKV projection; it is a feature enhancement
pipeline that preserves high-dimensional input semantics,
reduces channels for efficiency, purifies critical feature in-
formation, and ultimately produces more expressive QKV
matrices. This design directly targets the low-rank represen-
tational limitation of linear attention, rather than function-
ing simply as a parameter compression technique, which is
also validated by the nearly unchanged feature rank across
the pipeline in Figure 2.

this work we adopt an image classification head for training.

4.2. COCO 2017 Object Detection
We perform object detection experiments using the Reti-
naNet framework, with our ACCAP-ViT adopted as the
backbone network. The experiments are conducted on the
COCO 2017 dataset, which comprises 118K training im-
ages and 5K validation images. To facilitate effective train-
ing and transfer learning, our ACCAP-ViT-M1 backbone is
initialized with pre-trained weights on ImageNet-1K. Fol-
lowing previous work [40], we employ the AdamW opti-
mizer with an initial learning rate of 0.0002, and the model
is trained for 12 epochs. We compare ACCAP-ViT-M1 with
representative efficient CNN and ViT-based backbones un-
der the same RetinaNet (1× schedule) setup. Detailed re-
sults are summarized in Table 4.
With a slightly lower parameter count (4.3 M) com-
pared to S3ML-ViT-M1 (4.4 M) [9], our ACCAP-ViT-M1
achieves comprehensively superior detection performance.
It attains an overall AP of 36.0%, which outperforms all
compared models regardless of architecture type. All asso-
ciated detection metrics of ACCAP-ViT-M1 also rank first,
confirming its excellent capability in feature representation
for object detection tasks.
ACCAP-ViT-M1 does not perform well in terms of
FLOPs (432M), and this is attributed to the number of
stacked blocks designed for each stage during the back-
bone development.
In lightweight efficient network de-
sign, parameter count is often a more critical consideration.
This is because parameters directly correlate with model
memory footprint, deployment difficulty and inference la-
tency, which are core constraints in real-world applications.
We thus specifically select models with similar parameter
counts for fair comparison. Its strong performance on ob-
ject detection tasks further proves that it possesses great po-
tential for migration to downstream visual tasks.

To provide supplementary qualitative verification be-
yond our quantitative ablation study, we conduct visualiza-
tion analysis of attention feature maps using samples from
the ImageNet-1K validation set, comparing vanilla linear
attention and our proposed ACCAP-LA. As shown in Fig-
ure 5, our ACCAP-LA empowered by the focused kernel
function achieves significantly enhanced focusing capabil-
ity, which can accurately focus on key target regions in im-
ages. Meanwhile, the ACCAP mechanism effectively filters
redundant feature information and suppresses the model’s
attention to non-target background areas.
These qualita-
tive observations are consistent with our quantitative exper-
imental results, further validating the effectiveness of our
method.
5. Conclusion
To address the efficiency-capability dilemma of atten-
tion mechanism in Vision Transformers, this paper proposes
ACCAP-LA, a novel lightweight linear attention mecha-
nism that for the first time embeds the Adaptive Channel
Compression and Activation Purification (ACCAP) mech-
anism into the QKV projection process. This design fun-
damentally mitigates the representational degradation of
vanilla linear attention while achieving substantial reduc-
tions in computational overhead and parameter count. Ex-
tensive experiments across image classification and ob-
ject detection tasks validate that the proposed ACCAP-
ViT series delivers superior efficiency-capability trade-
offs compared to state-of-the-art CNNs, ViT, and Mamba-
based efficient models.
Comprehensive ablation studies
confirm the rationality of each component’s design and
the crucial role of ACCAP in driving performance gains,
while cross-architecture generalization experiments demon-
strate ACCAP-LA’s plug-and-play compatibility with clas-
sic lightweight transformers. Future work will delve deeper
into the underlying mechanisms of adaptive channel com-
pression and activation purification, and extend the pro-
posed framework to large-scale models, aiming to further
advance the development and practical application of effi-
cient Vision Transformers.
