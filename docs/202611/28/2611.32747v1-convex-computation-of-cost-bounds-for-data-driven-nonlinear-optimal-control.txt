Convex computation of cost bounds for data-driven
nonlinear optimal control
Oumayma Khattabi1, Matteo Tacchi-B´enard2, Sorin Olaru1 ∗†
November 25, 2026
Abstract
The paper addresses the optimal control problem of an input-affine polynomial system with an un-
known (state-dependent) input matrix affecting the control effectiveness.
Under assumptions on the
existence of a data set and the Lipschitz continuity of the unknown part of the dynamics, an uncertainty
set is constructed and used to adapt the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation towards a data-driven for-
mulation. We solve it for the best-case scenario, thus giving a lower bound on the best cost achievable
by means of a feedback control policy. After relaxing it into a moment-SoS hierarchy of semidefinite
programs, a control law is extracted from the primal on moments and compared with other benchmark
methods in a numerical application.
Keywords: Nonlinear Optimal Control, Data-driven Control, Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Equation,
SoS Programming.

Introduction
We consider the problem of optimal control design for input-affine systems. The control-effectiveness matrix
field is treated as unknown or partially known, and uncertainty is reduced through state-space evaluations
and bounded by Lipschitz continuity properties. Such a problem draws from several interconnected domains:
nonlinear optimal control and Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) formulations, set-membership/bounded-error
estimation with Lipschitz bounds, data-driven control, and learning-based and myopic control of unknown
dynamics.
Optimal control for nonlinear systems is a well-trodden problem in control theory [1], and many methods
have been developed to solve it when the model is available.
One can cite direct shooting methods [2],
differential dynamic programming [3], and nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) [4, 5] among others.
There are also approaches solving the HJB equation through convex reformulations as an alternative to
numerical viscosity solutions [6, 7]. Among these, one finds [8], where the Optimal Control Problem (OCP)
is formulated as an infinite-dimensional Linear Program (LP) over occupation measures, and its dual yields
subsolutions to the HJB equation.
Later, [9, 10] apply this formulation to polynomial systems.
Global
feedback laws are extracted in the same setting [11], with convergence guarantees in [12] and performance
bounds in [13].
When part or all of the model is unavailable, as is the case with many systems, data-driven control
approaches become necessary. Some methods recover a model from data [14], and other works use such learned
models to generate control laws via MPC, for example, as in [15, 16]. In other approaches, the uncertainty
is bounded in a set membership framework, where all models consistent with the data are identified [17], or

Finally, there are approaches in which the model and bounds are bypassed, so control is performed directly

In this work, we adapt the model-based OCP described in [9], restricted to control-affine systems, to handle
a partially unknown model. The particularity of the optimal control design in the presence of uncertainty in
the affine term lies in the fact that the equivalent differential inclusion loses its convexity properties, among
other strong properties of the exact model-based counterparts. Consequently, the pairs of states and inputs
have to be analyzed in terms of both distance in the state space and excitability with respect to input values.
The corresponding bounds on the optimal data-driven cost need to be evaluated. The main contributions of
this work are a “best-case” lower bound on the attainable cost, a guarantee of convergence for the data-driven
OCP, a data selection scheme, and an alternative polynomial control law derived from occupation measures,
whose performance is then compared to MPC-based approaches [15, 16].
The present paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides the problem setting and assumptions on the
system. Section 3 presents the data-driven OCP and the convergence proof. In Section 4, the moment-Sum-
of-Squares (SoS) hierarchy framework is detailed. Section 5 shows how the control law is extracted. Numerical
applications where performance is compared to that of benchmark methods are detailed in Section 6.

6.1
Choosing data location
While the number of LMI in 11 grows linearly with the number N of data points, the computational time
still grows sharply with N (because the linearity coefficient itself increases combinatorially in the relaxation
degree d). As a result, one might want to minimize the number of data points by using only well-localized
samples, either from existing data or, when possible, from new experiments. For a computed optimal solution
wd(t, x) to the degree d SoS strengthening of (7), we define the state score

By definition, S⋆

of the optimal occupation measure µ, so that small values of S⋆

and drawing data there tightens G(x) where it is most likely to improve. For instance, new samples can be
drawn without replacement among some candidates with probabilities proportional to exp(−S⋆

From this observation, two approaches are possible, depending on whether one has access only to a large
pool of measurements or to a sampling experiment. This paper focuses on the case where no simulator or
experimental benchmark is available; one can rely on a fixed pool of P = 2000 samples (uniform on X × U).
Since the cost of the update grows with N, accounting for all the data might be too expensive, so one can
start with a small number of samples (10 in our experiments), and add 10 per round, with various policies
for the selection of candidates:
• one can pick the new data points uniformly at random among the available measurements;
• one can choose them by greedy coverage, i.e. taking the farthest point in x from the current dataset;

The theoretical contributions of this work consist of a certified lower bound on the attainable cost and
formal mathematical guarantees, with a convergence rate depending on the excitation properties of the data.
The convergence constant grows linearly with the time horizon, the Lipschitz upper bound, and the square
root of the number of inputs, illustrating the deterioration caused by large time horizons, poor Lipschitz
constant estimation, or high-dimensional control inputs.
A control law is extracted from the optimized
primal on moments and is compared with two MPC variants in the numerical section. The results show
that it has better online computation time, as the controller is constructed offline, and with selected data,
it performs better against our estimated worst case. However, it still lacks precision relative to the MPC
benchmarks, suffers from the curse of dimensionality, and remains limited to modestly sized datasets.
Future work will investigate strategies to improve the performance of our controller, for instance by
sharpening the uncertainty set and model learning. The curse of dimensionality will be addressed through a
combination of existing scaling strategies for SoS programming, tailored to the specific data-driven problems
at hand.

datasets while maintaining convergence properties. Eventually, we will explore possibilities to improve the
