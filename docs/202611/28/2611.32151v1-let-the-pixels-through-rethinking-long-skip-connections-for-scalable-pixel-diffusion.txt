ABSTRACT
Training pixel diffusion models remains less straightforward, owing to the ad
hoc training recipes and architecture designs, along with the sensitivity to hyper-
parameter choices. We show that strong denoisers can be built by refining existing
pixel-native designs from the literature. Specifically, we revisit the long skip
connection in U-Net and find that the optimization benefit commonly attributed to
it is not necessary for its denoising benefit. We further find that the conventional
linear combination of features from the skip and main backbone is inadequate for
regimes of various noise levels. Motivated by these observations, we introduce
two core modifications. First, we adopt asymmetric long skips, which propagate
fine-grained information from input embeddings to decoder layers. Second, we
introduce adaGLU, a SwiGLU-like MLP block, in decoder layers to adaptively
filter information from the embeddings before being added to main-branch features.
The above designs give a simple pixel diffusion transformer architecture. When
trained from scratch, it scales smoothly from small-scale baselines up to 1.6B
parameters, achieving 1.62 and 1.70 FID on ImageNet-256 and -512, respectively.
Besides, it natively supports multimodal inputs, such as text and images, yielding
competitive performance in text-to-image generation. We release our codes at
https://github.com/AMAP-ML/PiT.

they avoid the computational overhead brought by a separate tokenizer in latent diffusion models
(LDM) (Rombach et al., 2022), thus being attractive for high-dimensional generation. Recent work
has shown that strong pixel-space diffusion can be obtained with relatively simple transformer
backbones (Vaswani et al., 2017; Dosovitskiy et al., 2021). Some methods (Li & He, 2025) train
directly from pixels, but are sensitive to their architectural design and other hyper-parameters. The

how much architectural structure is actually necessary for a strong pixel-space diffusion model?
We revisit a basic component, the long skip connection in U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015), a
common design in diffusion models. In its standard form, features from shallow encoder layers are
directly connected to corresponding decoder layers. The motivation is straightforward. Shallow
features preserve fine-grained information that may otherwise be lost after passing through a deep
semantic backbone. These long residuals are also commonly believed to facilitate optimization by
providing a shortcut for gradient propagation. In U-Net-style designs, these two effects are coupled
by the symmetric encoder-decoder topology. Moreover, skip features are typically concatenated with
the backbone features and fused by a fixed linear projection. In this paper, we investigate how much
of these structures are actually necessary for denoising, and suggest that a strong denoiser can be
achieved with an improved design.
We first examine whether the optimization effect is essential. In specific, we detach the gradient
pass through the skip connections and find that it does not actually hurt performance. This reveals
that, to retain the benefit of the skip, the conventional pairwise encoder-decoder structure is not
always necessary in all situations. In practice, the optimization issue only becomes visible when we
increase model depth. However, we show that this issue can be properly addressed with standard
weight initialization. Motivated by the gradient-detach experiment, we then remove the pairwise
encoder-decoder connections and route the input embedding directly to decoder layers. Surprisingly,
this asymmetric design further improves performance. These observations together suggest that
optimization benefit commonly attributed to long skips is not essential for their denoising benefit.
The long skip connections are better understood as a pathway for low-level information than as a
solution for optimization difficulty.
Then, this raises another question about how the skip features should be combined with the deep
features in the backbone. In diffusion, the answer may depend on the noise level. At low noise, the
input retains considerable image information and is useful for reconstruction. At high noise, the
same information is less reliable. Concatenation followed by a fixed linear layer, as in common

practice, does not distinguish these cases. It lacks a basic gating mechanism to adapt to regimes at
various noise levels, yet directly merges features by channel-wise linear combination. We therefore
test nonlinear gating functions and find that a SwiGLU-style formulation (Shazeer, 2020; Dauphin
et al., 2017) gives a substantial improvement. Motivated by this result, we repurpose the standard
SwiGLU as a gate module, which we call adaGLU, for features from the long skip pathway. Figure 2c
compares its structure against a standard SwiGLU block. Specifically, adaGLU rectifies the skip
feature with the activation from the main backbone. The skip feature provides low-level information,
while the main-branch feature controls how much of this information is used. After this, we fuse
features by simple addition.
The above modifications give a simple transformer architecture, which we call Pixel Diffusion
Transformer (PiT). We provides an overview of its structure in Figure 2b. We mainly focus on
identifying necessary components for a scalable architecture prototype. We follow this philosophy
throughout our work, and refrain from introducing additional network designs or training techniques.
As a result, our model relies on no tokenizer, no AdaLN, no in-context embedding, no pre-training,
and no auxiliary loss.
We conduct experiments on ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512, referring to ImageNet-1K (Deng et al.,
2009) at 256 × 256 and 512 × 512 resolution, respectively. When trained from scratch, it reaches
1.62 FID on ImageNet-256 and 1.70 FID on ImageNet-512. Besides, our network also natively
supports multi-modal inputs by treating them uniformly as a token sequence. To show this, we train a
text-to-image generation model at 512×512 resolution by concatenating text embeddings with image
tokens as model input. We evaluate it on GenEval (Ghosh et al., 2023) and DPG-Bench (Hu et al.,
2024), scoring 0.82 and 84.3, respectively.
Our observations do not imply that U-Net-style architectures are ineffective. Rather, they suggest
that their structural assumptions are not necessary for retaining the useful function of long skips. We
summarize our contributions below.
1. We revisit the role of long skip connections in pixel diffusion and show that their denoising
benefit can be separated from their advantage in optimization, allowing the conventional U-Net
topology to be replaced by a simpler asymmetric information pathway. We reinterpret the long
skip connection as a functional component rather than an architectural template.
2. We strengthen its feature fusion mechanism by introducing adaGLU. It repurposes the standard
SwiGLU as a gating module, allowing the backbone features to modulate the low-level information
carried by the skip.This design benefits the model with long-skip connections in both pixel- and
latent-space training.
3. We show that PiT performs strongly when trained from scratch across a wide range of model sizes,
reaching 1.62 FID on ImageNet-256 and 1.70 FID on ImageNet-512. We further show that the
same architecture naturally extends to text-to-image generation.

RELATED WORK
Pixel-space Diffusion Model have recently regained attention. By removing the pretrained VAE
tokenizer, they avoid the reconstruction bottleneck, while offering better efficiency in direct image
modeling. Recent works can be roughly divided into two categories. The first group uses specialized
training recipes (Chen et al., 2025c; Wang et al., 2025a; Hoogeboom et al., 2023; Lei et al., 2023).
EPG (Lei et al., 2026) and USP (Chu et al., 2025) introduces self-supervised pretraining before
diffusion training. Though achieving impressive performance, they suggest that competitive pixel
diffusion often depends on extra training objectives/stages. In contrast, JiT (Li & He, 2025) offers
a workaround by reshaping the DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) architecture and parameterizing the
denoising model to directly predict clean images. When trained from scratch, it yields competitive
performance to top-performing models. However, it is sensitive to hyper-parameter choices, such as
in-context embedding and Dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014). In comparison, our model requires no
specialized recipe, presenting clean scaling behavior without extensive hyper-parameter tuning.
Denoising Network. The diffusion model architecture has undergone a series of advancements from
U-Net like CNNs (Song et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) to transformer-based
architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Bao et al., 2023a; Jabri et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2024). Recently,
a notable trend is to incorporate shallow-to-deep information routing, such as DDT (Wang et al.,

4.2
STREAMLINE THE SKIP CONNECTION DESIGN
A common belief is that U-Net-style skip connections facilitate training by alleviating the gradient
vanishing problem. To test this hypothesis, we compare two ViT-B models, both equipped with
U-Net-style skip connections. One is trained as usual, while the other is trained by detaching the
gradient from the skip connections. This forces the supervisory signal to flow exclusively backward
through the main backbone. Surprisingly, the simple modification yields a slightly better FID score
than the baseline (50.1 v.s. 52.1), suggesting that the commonly assumed role of skip connections in
mitigating gradient vanishing may not be necessary in this scenario.
Based on this observation, we then remove the pair-wise encoder-decoder shortcuts and redistribute
the skip connections so that only the input embeddings are input to decoder layers. This retains the
shortcuts to preserve fine-grained details from the input. As shown in Table 2, this asymmetric design
improves the FID from 52.1 to 47.8. These results indicate that the primary function of the long skip
is to provide an information pathway from the input toward the deeper layers, rather than to facilitate
optimization. This perspective also motivates a reconsideration of how skip features should be fused
with the backbone.
Denoising fundamentally operates across varying noise levels, and the usefulness of the input signal
varies accordingly. At low noise levels, the perturbed input xt still preserves substantial image
information and can therefore directly support image reconstruction. In this regime, routing this
information directly into deep layers can reduce the burden on the backbone to reproduce an identity-
like mapping. However, at high noise levels, the input is heavily corrupted and provides substantially
less reliable information. In this regime, the network should be able to suppress the contribution of
the skip pathway when it is not useful.
To implement such adaptive routing, we compare several gating functions G(h, z) in Table 3, ranging
from a bottleneck linear layer to nonlinear MLP-based designs. The bottleneck linear layer reduces
the dimensionality of the skip features as an information bottleneck, whereas the nonlinear variants
allow the skip representation to be modulated by the semantics encoded in the backbone features.
Among them, the SwiGLU formulation achieves the lowest FID. Specifically, we define
adaGLU(h, z) = W1(W2h ⊙SiLU(W3z)).

4.3
SCALE UP MODEL SIZE
We observe that the optimization bottlenecks are exposed when further increasing model size. In
Table 4, when we initially increase the depth from 12 to 20 layers, the nearly doubled parameters
yield only a marginal improvement. This suggests that the deeper model is not effectively exploiting
its additional capacity. We attribute this degradation to the accumulation of activation variance in
deep residual networks. A residual block can be written as zl+1 = zl + fl(zl), l ∈[0, L −1], L
is model depth. Assume the residual branch is uncorrelated with the main stream activations at
initialization. Under conventional parameter initializations, the residual branch fl(zl) contributes
non-negligible accumulated variance, leading to Var(zl) + Var(fl(zl)). Consequently, the activation
magnitude can grow with depth, degrading performance in sufficiently deep networks. In gradient
backpropagation, it can progressively amplify or attenuate different gradient directions. Thus, without
controlling the scale of the residual branches, both the forward activations and backward gradients
become increasingly poorly conditioned, making deeper models harder to optimize.

Stabilize training with Pre-Norm formulation. Post-Norm architectures are known to become
increasingly difficult to optimize at large model scales (Liu et al., 2023), making optimization more

at large model sizes (XL size). Besides, its scaling behavior also shows better consistency across
different training batch sizes.
4.4
DISCUSSION
Our final architecture combines the two modifications identified above: asymmetric long skip
connections and adaptive feature fusion with adaGLU. The resulting design retains a standard
transformer backbone while using the long skip primarily as a pathway for low-level information.
Figures 2b and 2c show the overall architecture and the Pre-Norm adaGLU, respectively. We also
compare our final solution to prior U-Net variants in Table 1.
We further examine two architectural choices. First, the input feature h used by adaGLU is taken
from the tokens immediately after patch embedding and before the first ViT layer. One alternative is
to introduce a separate patch-embedding layer for each adaGLU block. Our experiments (Appendix
Table 13c) on these two settings show only marginal differences in FID on ImageNet-256, so we
use the shared input embedding for simplicity. Second, we use an equal number of encoder and
decoder layers. For example, a 12-layer model contains 6 encoder layers and 6 decoder layers.
Although increasing the number of decoder layers monotonically improves performance in our
ablations (Appendix Table 13a), we retain a uniform layer allocation throughout the experiments and
find that it’s sufficient for strong generation performance.

5.1
CLASS-TO-IMAGE GENERATION
We display our results on ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512 in Table 5 and Table 6 respectively. When
compared with prior methods, PiT reaches competitive performance. On ImageNet-256, it surpasses
several recent methods trained with an additional training stage or external models, including USP
and PixelGen, and is on par with PixelDiT and EPG.
Comparing with models with U-Net-style skip connection, such as U-ViT, PixelU, DAR, and
EDM2, our models yield outstanding results. Among them, EDM2 shows notable performance with
its magnitude-preserving network designs. In comparison, our results reveal that such additional
structural complexity is unnecessary for building a strong pixel denoiser.
Scalability. PiT maintains strong performance as the model size increases from the small-scale
baseline to 1.6B parameters. This scaling behavior becomes stronger and more pronounced on
ImageNet-512, due to the increased complexity of the generative modeling task.
5.2
TEXT-TO-IMAGE GENERATION
We show that our model natively supports text and image input without relying on cross attention or
the two-stream architecture as in MMDiT (Esser et al., 2024). Using a large patch size of 32, the
input tokens to our model comprise three parts, including time condition (1), text embeddings (256),
and image tokens (256), resulting in a total of 513 tokens. In Table 7, our middle-sized PiT-L/32
model reaches generation performance on par with prior works.

DISCUSSIONS & LIMITATIONS
Discussions. U-Net was once a standard architecture for diffusion model. Our study revisits its
design from a different perspective. We find that the useful role of the long skip does not require its
symmetric encoder-decoder structure. The skip can instead be viewed primarily as a pathway for
2https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenDatasets/dalle-3-dataset
9


Preprint
low-level information, and its fusion with the backbone can be made adaptive to the denoising state.
This leads to a simpler formulation in which the main backbone remains a standard transformer and
the long skip is implemented as an asymmetric, noise-adaptive pathway.
Limitations. Our experiments are primarily conducted on ImageNet image generation. Although
PiT scales to 1.6B parameters in our experiments, its behavior at substantially larger scales remains
unclear. We also did not evaluate PiT on video generation or other high-dimensional sequential data,
where the benefit of the simplified routing design may slightly differ. Finally, while the token-based
formulation suggests that PiT can be incorporated into systems beyond image generation, we have
not evaluated adaGLU as a standalone component in existing large language model architectures.

We evaluate asymmetric skips and adaGLU on latent-space U-ViT-B. The results are shown in 15.

routing may depend more on the input representation.

Let xl denote the input tensor to the l-th transformer layer, which consists of an attention block f1(·)

hl = xl + f1(LN(xl))
xl+1 = hl + f2(LN(hl))
