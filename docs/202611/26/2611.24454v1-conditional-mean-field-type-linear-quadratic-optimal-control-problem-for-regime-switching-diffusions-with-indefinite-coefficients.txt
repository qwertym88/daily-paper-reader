# Conditional Mean-Field Type Linear Quadratic Optimal Control Problem for Regime-Switching Diffusions with Indefinite Coefficients 

Zhongbin Guo, Guangchen Wang ∗, Shupeng Wang 

> School of Control Science and Engineering, Shandong University, Jinan 250061, China

Abstract 

We study a conditional mean-field type linear quadratic optimal control problem for regime-switching dif-fusions with indefinite weights. The dynamics and cost depend on conditional expectations of the state and control given the history of the Markov chain. Under standard definiteness conditions, we establish the exis-tence and uniqueness of the optimal control and derive its feedback representation and optimal cost through two sets of coupled Riccati equations and a linear backward stochastic differential equation driven by the Markov chain. To treat the case of indefinite weights, we extend the equivalent cost functional approach to this setting using regime-dependent quadratic adjustments for the conditional mean of the state and its deviation from that mean. When the transformed weights in the equivalent cost functional satisfy standard definiteness conditions, this construction yields unique solvability of the original Hamiltonian system and a feedback representation of the unique optimal control. The proposed results also provide an effective method for establishing the solvability of fully coupled linear conditional mean-field forward-backward stochastic dif-ferential equations that do not satisfy certain existing monotonicity conditions. We further apply the results to conditional mean-variance portfolio selection with exogenous liability in an incomplete regime-switching market and derive the optimal investment strategy. 

Keywords: Markov chain, Conditional mean-field stochastic differential equation, Linear quadratic optimal control, Indefinite coefficients, Equivalent cost functional 

1. Introduction 

A stochastic linear quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem refers to a problem in which the cost functions have a quadratic form in the state and control and the state is governed by a linear stochastic differential equation (SDE). Owing to this elegant structure, one can derive an optimal control in state feedback form through a Riccati equation. Moreover, the solution of an LQ problem may provide a tractable approximation to nonlinear control problems [1, 2]. Therefore, it is of great importance to investigate the LQ problem. In a deterministic LQ problem, the control weight is usually assumed to be uniformly positive definite. A

[5] showed that the stochastic LQ problem can nevertheless have a finite value and admit an optimal feedback control even when the control weight is indefinite. The distinction arises when the control enters the diffusion coefficient. In that case, the control affects the variance of the state, and the resulting contribution can compensate for a negative control weight. A regime-switching diffusion (also referred to as a switching diffusion) couples a continuous solution of a SDE to a finite-state Markov chain that governs its drift and diffusion coefficients. A change of regime can represent a loss of production capacity after a machine failure or a shift in asset returns and volatilities as market conditions change [6, 7]. For controlled switching diffusions, Donnelly [8] established a sufficient stochastic maximum principle. Subsequent work treated jump diffusions [9], forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) with delay [10], and delayed jump diffusions [11]. In the LQ setting, Zhang et al. [12] characterized open-loop optimality and closed-loop solvability using Hamiltonian system and the regular solution of the Riccati equations. Following [12], Wen et al. [13] established the equivalence between open-loop and weak closed-loop solvability. For systems with random coefficients, Wen et al. [14] proved solvability of the stochastic Riccati equation under uniform convexity and obtained a feedback representation of the optimal control. This paper considers an LQ optimal control problem for regime-switching diffusions with two main features. First, the control weighting matrices are allowed to be indefinite, broadening the applicability of our results. Second, both the state equation and the cost functional depend on conditional expectations of the state and control given the history of the Markov chain. Here we refer to such problems as conditional mean-field type control problems. Research on this class of problems began more recently, and fewer results are available. The inclusion of conditional expectation terms in our model is mainly inspired by Nguyen et al. [15]. For a system of interacting particles that share the same Markov chain, Nguyen et al. [15] showed that, as the number of particles tends to infinity, the empirical distribution of their states converges weakly to the law of a representative particle conditional on the history of the chain. Thus the limiting population distribution remains random even after individual fluctuations have been averaged out. This observation motivates conditional mean-field type control problems and games, in which the dynamics or costs depend on the conditional law of the state or control. In the LQ problem studied here, this dependence enters through the conditional expectations E[Xt|F θt ] and E[ut|F θt ], where Fθt represents the history of the Markov chain up to time t.For nonlinear conditional mean-field type control and game problems with regime switching, see [16, 17, 18, 19, 20] for maximum principles and [21] for the dynamic programming principle. For LQ models, Ding et al. [22] derived and verified an optimal feedback control for an infinite-horizon discounted problem using two algebraic Riccati equations. Lv et al. [23] obtained a feedback representation of a Stackelberg equilibrium, and Rolón Gutiérrez et al. [24] used the solvability of conditional mean-field FBSDEs (CMF-FBSDEs) to establish the existence of a Nash equilibrium. Related results include a separation principle for LQ control under partial observation [25]. These control and game formulations [22, 23, 24, 25] assume positive definite control weights. This paper studies a conditional mean-field type LQ problem for regime-switching diffusions with in-2definite coefficients. To address the indefinite coefficients, we use the equivalent cost functional method introduced by Yu [26] and further developed by Huang and Yu [27]. The core idea of this method is to construct a new cost functional that is equivalent to the original one and satisfies the standard definiteness conditions. Here, we briefly discuss the differences between our work and the existing studies [28, 29, 30]. Li et al. [28] studied an indefinite mean-field type LQ problem and introduced the concept of a relaxed compensator by developing the equivalent cost functional method. Wang and Wang [29] subsequently ex-tended the results in [28] to jump-diffusion models. The studies [28, 29] use unconditional expectations without Markovian regime switching, whereas our work considers conditional mean-field type LQ problems with regime switching. Here, the conditional expectation of the state is a random process adapted to the filtration generated by the Markov chain. The switching process enables our model to describe more com-plex phenomena but also makes the analysis more involved. Since the coefficients are modulated by the switching process, we use two regime-dependent matrix families, H and K, to construct the equivalent cost functional. They are associated with the deviation of the state from its conditional expectation and the conditional expectation itself, respectively. The regime-switching mechanism introduces coupling across regimes in both the transformed cost weights and the Riccati equations. We establish the corresponding transformations of the Hamiltonian systems and coupled Riccati equations, together with the associated linear BSDE driven by the Markov chain. Corrections involving differences between the adjustment matri-ces across regimes also arise in the transformation of the jump integrands in the adjoint equation. Mei et al. [30] investigated an indefinite conditional mean-field type LQ problem that includes regime switching and characterized finite-horizon open-loop and closed-loop solvability. Although our problem setting is similar to that of [30], our approach and solvability results differ in essential respects. Mei et al. [30] proved that uniform convexity of the cost functional is equivalent to strongly regular solvability of the Riccati equations. Our approach complements this characterization with a sufficient criterion expressed as explicit inequalities for the transformed cost coefficients. When suitable matrices H and K make these coefficients satisfy the standard definiteness conditions, we obtain unique solvability of the original Hamiltonian system and cou-pled Riccati equations. These equations yield the unique optimal control in feedback form and its optimal cost. The auxiliary matrices need only satisfy the coefficient inequalities and need not solve the original Riccati equations. In Example 2, these inequalities are verified directly using constant choices of H and 

K. This establishes unique solvability of a fully coupled CMF-FBSDE that fails the specific monotonicity conditions examined there. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. • As a foundation, we first consider the case in which the standard definiteness conditions hold. We prove the existence and uniqueness of the optimal control. The feedback representation and the optimal cost are derived through two sets of coupled Riccati equations and a linear BSDE driven by the Markov chain. The unique solvability of the involved Riccati equations is also established. • For indefinite coefficients, we establish a sufficient solvability criterion through explicit inequalities for the transformed cost weights. The criterion yields unique solvability of the Hamiltonian system and coupled Riccati equations, as well as the optimal feedback control and its cost. Conversely, Riccati solutions with uniformly positive definite effective control weights provide the required adjustment 3matrices. • We provide two examples and a financial application to illustrate the effectiveness of our results. In Example 1, we derive an explicit optimal control and optimal cost for an LQ problem with negative control weights using the equivalent cost functional method. In Example 2, we demonstrate how this method can establish the global solvability of fully coupled CMF-FBSDEs through their connection with the Hamiltonian systems of indefinite LQ control problems. We apply this approach to prove the solvability of a system that does not satisfy the solvability conditions considered in the existing literature. In the financial application, we solve a conditional mean-variance portfolio selection problem with exogenous liability in an incomplete regime-switching market, deriving the optimal investment strategy and the relation between the conditional mean and variance of optimal terminal net wealth. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 gives the problem formulation. Section 3 studies the problem under standard definiteness conditions. Section 4 investigates the indefinite coefficients case by using the equivalent cost functional approach. Section 5 presents two illustrative examples. Section 6 applies the results to conditional mean-variance portfolio selection with liability and provides numerical illustrations. Finally, Section 7 concludes the paper and outlines directions for future research.

4. Indefinite Coefficients Case 

Even when assumption (PD ) fails, Problem CMF may remain well posed and admit an optimal control. We address this case using the equivalent cost functional method. The core of this method is to replace the original cost functional with an equivalent one whose coefficients satisfy assumption (PD ). We first define equivalence between cost functionals for a given controlled system. 

Definition 4.1. Consider a controlled system with admissible control set U. Two cost functionals J and ¯J

are said to be equivalent if they induce the same ordering on U. That is, for any u1, u 2 ∈ U , J(u1) < J (u2)

if and only if ¯J(u1) < ¯J(u2).

By Definition 4.1, equivalent cost functionals for the same controlled system have the same set of optimal controls. Thus, when the coefficients of J fail to satisfy assumption (PD ), we may seek an equivalent cost functional ¯J whose coefficients satisfy it. Example 2. Our theoretical result provides an effective tool for establishing the well-posedness of fully coupled CMF-FBSDEs with regime switching. Establishing global well-posedness for this class of equations is challenging. To the best of our knowledge, global well-posedness of such systems over finite time horizons has been studied only in [24, 35, 36, 37]. 7. Concluding remarks 

This paper studies a conditional mean-field type LQ control problem for regime-switching diffusions with indefinite weights using the equivalent cost functional approach. The quadratic adjustments for the mean and centered state in [28, 29] are adapted to the conditional mean-field setting, with regime-dependent matrices accounting for transition-rate coupling and corrections to the adjoint jump integrands. When the transformed weights in the new cost satisfy the standard definiteness conditions, this construction yields unique solvability of the Hamiltonian system, a feedback representation of the unique optimal control, and an explicit formula for the optimal cost. The representation is determined by two coupled systems of Riccati equations and a linear BSDE driven by the Markov chain. Two examples are provided to illustrate our proposed results. The first example derives the optimal control and associated cost in a conditional mean-field type LQ problem even though the control weight is negative definite. The second one shows that our method can be used to establish the solvability of fully coupled CMF-FBSDEs that may not satisfy the solvability conditions imposed in the existing literature. We also study a conditional mean-variance portfolio selection problem with exogenous liability in an incomplete regime-switching market. The optimal investment strategy is derived, and the relation between the conditional mean and variance of the optimal terminal net wealth is obtained. 20 Future work may investigate indefinite conditional mean-field type LQ control problems with random coefficients and partial observation.
