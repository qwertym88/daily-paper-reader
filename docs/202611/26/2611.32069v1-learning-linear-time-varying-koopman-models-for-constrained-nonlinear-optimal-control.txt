# Learning Linear Time-Varying Koopman Models for Constrained Nonlinear Optimal Control 

Zhi Xu ∗ and Ran Dai †

Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN, 47907 

This paper presents a learning-based Koopman framework for solving constrained nonlinear optimal control problems. Despite significant advances in Koopman-based modeling and control, challenges remain in achieving accurate representations of controlled nonlinear dynamics and constraints while maintaining computational efficiency for optimal control. Motivated by these challenges, a linear time-varying (LTV) Koopman representation is developed to provide a more expressive representation of controlled nonlinear dynamics while preserving a linear prediction structure suitable for efficient convex optimization. Modeling errors and their propagation over extended prediction horizons are analyzed, motivating a neural-network-based Koopman framework that jointly learns lifted dynamics and constraint representations with stability-oriented regularization to improve long-horizon prediction performance. Based on the learned Koopman model, the original constrained nonlinear optimal control problem is reformulated as a convex quadratic program with conservative constraint tightening to account for modeling uncertainty during constraint enforcement. Numerical results on a low-thrust orbit transfer problem and a Mars entry guidance problem demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in terms of prediction accuracy, computational efficiency, and constrained optimal control performance. 

# I. Introduction 

Nonlinear dynamical systems subject to state and input constraints arise ubiquitously in engineering and scientific applications. In many practical settings, the system dynamics exhibit strong nonlinearities, while safety, performance, or physical limitations impose nonlinear constraints that must be satisfied along the entire trajectory. The simultaneous presence of nonlinear dynamics and nonlinear constraints poses significant challenges for system analysis, prediction, and control. Although nonlinear models can capture the underlying physics with high fidelity, they typically lead to nonconvex optimal control problems that are difficult to solve efficiently, particularly when constraints must be enforced explicitly. As a result, there remains a fundamental need for modeling and control frameworks that can handle nonlinear

A wide range of approaches has been developed to address nonlinear constrained optimal control problems (OCPs), broadly categorized into direct and indirect methods. Indirect methods derive necessary conditions for optimality through Pontryagin’s minimum principle, resulting in two-point boundary value problems involving both states and costates [1–3]. While such methods can yield highly accurate solutions, their practical application is often hindered by sensitivity to the initial guesses of costates, whose lack of direct physical interpretation makes reliable initialization difficult. Moreover, the incorporation of complex state and path constraints within indirect formulations remains challenging [4]. Direct methods, on the other hand, transcribe the nonlinear OCP into a finite-dimensional nonlinear program (NLP) using discretization techniques, e.g., collocation [5–7]. Direct methods offer greater flexibility in handling constraints and have therefore become the dominant paradigm in practice. However, the resulting optimization problems are typically large-scale and nonconvex, with computational cost and convergence behavior that depend critically on discretization resolution and solver initialization [8, 9]. In addition, the nonlinear transcription may lead to convergence to locally optimal solutions. The substantial computational burden associated with solving such NLPs further limits their applications in resource-constrained or real-time settings [10]. Motivated by the limitations of solving nonlinear OCPs, alternative modeling frameworks have been explored to balance expressiveness and computational tractability. Among these, Koopman operator theory has attracted increasing attention as a powerful approach for representing nonlinear dynamical systems through linear evolution of observables in the infinite-dimensional function space [11]. By transferring nonlinear dynamics into a linear form, Koopman-based representations offer a promising foundation for analysis and control using tools from linear systems theory. However, practical application requires constructing a finite-dimensional approximation of the Koopman operator. For example, the dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) algorithm approximates the unitary Koopman operator using linear observables [12], but its expressiveness is limited by the linear structure. The extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) method addresses this limitation by augmenting the observable set with nonlinear functions, thereby creating a higher-dimensional lifted representation with improved nonlinear modeling capability [13]. More recently, neural-network–based techniques have been introduced to learn the observable dictionary from data, leveraging their expressive power to produce more accurate approximations [14–16]. Despite these advances, the application of Koopman-based methods to constrained nonlinear optimal control remains challenging. In particular, accurately representing controlled nonlinear dynamics while preserving computational tractability, and systematically handling nonlinear state and path constraints within the lifted space, remain important open problems. One of the primary challenges arises from extending the Koopman framework to controlled systems. Although the Koopman operator theory is well established for nonlinear dynamical systems, its extension to controlled systems remains challenging [17]. In general, the presence of control inputs breaks the semigroup property that underlies the Koopman operator for autonomous systems, impeding both theoretical analysis and practical approximation. Existing 2Koopman-based control approaches often adopt a linear time-invariant (LTI) predictor structure with either fixed lifting functions [18] or neural-network-based lifting functions [ ? ]. Such formulations implicitly impose a linear dependence on the control variables in the lifted dynamics, making it difficult to accurately represent general nonlinear control effects. In addition, the use of a single invariant operator may become overly restrictive for systems exhibiting significant temporal variations or large excursions across the state space. Consequently, maintaining prediction accuracy over a broad operating envelope often requires either a very rich lifting space or a more flexible operator representation. To better capture the control-dependent structure of nonlinear systems, bilinear Koopman representations have been developed for control-affine systems, where the Koopman generator admits an exact bilinear decomposition with respect to the control inputs [19, 20]. This formulation preserves the control-affine structure of the original nonlinear system and provides a more faithful representation of state–control interactions. However, the resulting lifted dynamics remain bilinear in the state and control variables, and the associated optimal control problems generally remain nonconvex. Consequently, many of the computational advantages of linear prediction and convex optimization cannot be directly exploited in practical control synthesis when applying the bilinear representation. A further challenge lies in the treatment of nonlinear state and path constraints within Koopman-based optimal control formulations. Conventional strategies often rely on manually designed observable dictionaries or heuristic constraint lifting strategies to obtain approximately linear constraint representations in the lifted space [17, 18, 21]. For complex nonlinear constraints, however, it is often unclear whether a suitable set of observables exists that can accurately represent the constraint while remaining compatible with the lifted dynamics. Moreover, approximation errors introduced during constraint lifting may lead to a mismatch between constraint satisfaction in the lifted space and constraint satisfaction for the original nonlinear system, limiting the reliability of Koopman-based constrained optimal control. To address the limitations of existing Koopman-based approaches for constrained optimal control, this work develops a learning-based linear time-varying Koopman framework that seeks to balance modeling fidelity, computational tractability, and constraint reliability. The central idea is to construct a linear time-varying (LTV) Koopman representation that retains a linear prediction structure while relaxing the restrictive assumption of a single invariant Koopman operator. By allowing the lifted dynamics to evolve over time, the proposed representation provides greater flexibility for capturing nonlinear system behaviors across broad operating conditions and long prediction horizons. Building upon this representation, we develop a data-driven framework that jointly learns lifted dynamics and constraint representations. To improve reliability in long-horizon prediction and constrained control, approximation errors and their propagation are analyzed and incorporated into both the learning and control design. The resulting Koopman model enables the original constrained nonlinear optimal control problem to be reformulated as a computationally tractable convex optimization problem with explicit consideration of modeling uncertainty. • We formulate a Koopman-based convex optimal control framework for constrained nonlinear systems. An error-aware constraint-tightening strategy is introduced to account for approximation errors when enforcing lifted-space constraints. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section II presents the proposed LTV Koopman representation and its associated error analysis. Section III introduces the neural-network-based framework for jointly learning lifted dynamics and constraint representations. Section IV develops the Koopman-based convex optimal control formulation with error-aware constraint tightening. Section V presents numerical results for a low-thrust orbit transfer problem and a six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) Mars entry guidance problem. Finally, Section VI concludes the paper.

Starting from a control-affine nonlinear system, we first derive a bilinear Koopman representation and its corresponding discrete-time approximation. Building upon this model, a linear time-varying (LTV) Koopman formulation is constructed to obtain a representation that is compatible with linear prediction and optimization frameworks. Finally, approximation errors introduced by discretization and bilinear-to-linear approximation are analyzed, and their propagation over long prediction horizons is characterized. These results provide the basis for the learning and optimization frameworks developed in the subsequent sections.

To obtain a representation that is compatible with standard linear control and optimization frameworks, we construct a linear time-varying (LTV) approximation of the bilinear Koopman model. # III. Neural-Network-Based LTV Koopman Model 

The previous section established a control-oriented Koopman representation for nonlinear systems and developed an LTV approximation together with its associated error analysis. Building upon these results, this section presents a data-driven framework for constructing the proposed Koopman model directly from trajectory data. The framework aims to jointly learn three key components: the lifted-state representation, the time-varying Koopman dynamics, and the corresponding constraint representations. By learning these components within a unified architecture, the resulting model can capture complex nonlinear behaviors while preserving a linear structure in the lifted space that is suitable for prediction, optimization, and constraint handling. In addition, the error-propagation analysis developed in the previous section provides guidance for the design of a stability-oriented regularization strategy to improve long-horizon predictive performance. The overall architecture of the proposed neural-network-based Koopman framework is described in the following subsections.

It is worth noting that although this structure introduces additional parameters scaling with the horizon length, it significantly expands the model’s expressiveness while strictly preserving the linear structure required for downstream convex optimization. Crucially, this time-varying formulation still yields a global representation over the state space rather than relying on localizations around a single trajectory or specific initial conditions, which is further validated through experiments in Section V. From a modeling perspective, the proposed framework can be interpreted as a generalization of conventional LTI Koopman formulations, where the time indices can be flexibly designed and the standard LTI representation emerges as a special case using a single time index. 

B. Constraint Lifting and Linear Constraint Representation 

In many control applications, the system is subject to nonlinear constraints of the form 

h(x, u) ≤ 0 (18) where h ∈ R𝑛 +𝑚 → R𝑝 , and it generally cannot be handled directly by linear or convex optimization methods. To enable constraint-aware control in the lifted space, we seek a representation in which these nonlinear constraints admit an approximately linear form with respect to the lifted variables. Motivated by the Koopman framework, we treat the constraint functions as observables of the state–input pair and approximate them using linear operators acting on the lifted state. Specifically, at each time step 𝑘 , we introduce a time-dependent linear constraint map ˆh𝑘 = 𝐺 𝑘 ˆz𝑘 + 𝐻 𝑘 u𝑘 , (19) where 𝐺 𝑘 ∈ R𝑝 ×𝑛 𝑧 and 𝐻 𝑘 ∈ R𝑝 ×𝑚 . This construction follows the same principle underlying Koopman lifting of dynamics: although the constraint functions are nonlinear in the original variables, they can often be approximated as linear functionals in a sufficiently rich lifted space. By sharing the neural-based lifted representation with the dynamics model, the learned observables are encouraged to support linear prediction of both the system evolution and the constraint values. Allowing the constraint operators 𝐺 𝑘 and 𝐻 𝑘 to vary with time serves two purposes. First, it follows the LTV approximation used for the dynamics, ensuring consistency with the discretized bilinear Koopman structure and its local 11 nature. Second, it accommodates large variations in constraint sensitivity along the trajectory, which would be difficult to capture using a single global linear map. From an optimal control perspective, expressing constraints as linear functions of the lifted state and input is essential. It enables the resulting prediction and OCP to be formulated as a linear program, making it compatible with powerful linear programming solvers. Moreover, because the constraint approximation error is absorbed into the same lifted representation used for dynamics prediction, inaccuracies in constraint evaluation are naturally coupled to the state prediction error, rather than introducing an independent source of mismatch. For numerical stability and to avoid the introduction of artificial bias, each constraint operator pair (𝐺 𝑘 , 𝐻 𝑘 ) is implemented as bias-free linear layers and learned independently for each time index. This ensures that constraint representations are consistent with the lifted dynamics. Overall, the proposed constraint lifting strategy transforms nonlinear constraints into approximately linear expressions in the lifted coordinates while remaining consistent with the Koopman-based approximation of the dynamics. This unified lifted representation forms the basis for constraint-aware learning and control in the subsequent sections.

# IV. Optimal Control with the Learned Model 

The previous sections established a neural-network-based LTV Koopman model capable of jointly learning lifted dynamics and constraint representations from data. This section leverages the learned Koopman model to construct a computationally tractable optimal control formulation for nonlinear constrained systems. Starting from a continuous-time 13 nonlinear optimal control problem, we first reformulate the system dynamics and constraints in the learned Koopman coordinates using the LTV representation. The resulting lifted-space formulation preserves a linear prediction structure, allowing the original nonlinear optimal control problem to be approximated by a quadratic program (QP). Furthermore, the approximation-error analysis developed earlier is incorporated into the control design through a conservative constraint-tightening strategy, which explicitly accounts for modeling error when enforcing path constraints. The resulting framework provides a unified connection between Koopman-based learning, error-aware prediction, and constrained optimal control.

# V. Simulation Results 

This section evaluates the performance of the proposed framework using two representative aerospace optimal control problems. We first consider a low-thrust maximum-radius orbit transfer problem [22], which is characterized by nonlinear control-affine dynamics with a nonlinear equality-constrained target set. This problem serves as a baseline to systematically evaluate the approximation of nonlinear dynamics and constraints across model configurations, directly illustrating the impact of model accuracy on the optimal control solutions. For both problems, the prediction performance of different Koopman model variants is analyzed through ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each component. Subsequently, the Koopman-based optimal control formulation across different variants is evaluated and benchmarked against standard NLP solvers, highlighting the advantages of the proposed framework in terms of solution accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency. 1. Prediction Performance Evaluation 

To evaluate the prediction performance of the proposed framework on the 6-DOF entry guidance problem, we conduct a systematic comparison across the same four Koopman model variants under a unified experimental setup. All models are trained to learn the lifted representation of the nonlinear entry dynamics (39b) together with associated path constraints (39c), following the same architecture and training protocol as in the orbit transfer case. For the entry problem, a dataset of 5000 trajectories is generated under randomized initial conditions in ranges: 𝑟 0 ∈ [ 98 , 102 ] km, 22 𝜆 0 ∈ [ 110 ◦, 130 ◦], 𝜙 0 ∈ [ 30 ◦, 50 ◦], 𝑉 0 ∈ [ 4600 , 4800 ] m/s, 𝛾 0 ∈ [ 0◦, 20 ◦], 𝜓 0 ∈ [− 10 ◦, 10 ◦], and control profiles within the admissible sets: 𝛼, 𝛽 ∈ [− 20 ◦, 20 ◦]. Each trajectory is simulated over 𝑇 = 20 steps with a uniform sampling interval of Δ𝑡 = 5 s. The dataset is split into training, validation, and test sets with ratios of 80%, 10%, and 10%, respectively. All models are trained using the Adam optimizer with identical hyperparameters as in the orbit transfer case. To evaluate the prediction accuracy, the trained models are propagated using initial states and control inputs from the test set, and then compared with the ground truth trajectories. The resulting relative RMSE for both dynamics and constraint predictions is shown in Fig. 4. Overall, the results are consistent with those observed in the orbit transfer problem. The proposed LTV Koopman model with neural lifting achieves the lowest prediction error across the entire prediction horizon, while neural lifting consistently outperforms the fixed polynomial dictionary for both LTV and LTI formulations. Compared with the orbit transfer case, the performance gap between the models becomes more pronounced. In the 6-DOF entry problem, the system exhibits stronger nonlinear couplings, rapidly varying aerodynamic effects, and more complicated nonlinear constraints, which are less effectively captured by time-invariant operators and hand-crafted lifting functions. As a result, the LTV formulation with neural networks (LTV-NN) provides a more accurate prediction of dynamics and constraints along the trajectory, particularly in more complex nonlinear systems.

We further investigate the effect of stability regularization for both LTV and LTI Koopman models with neural lifting on the 6-DOF entry guidance problem. The same regularization formulation is applied with varying weights 𝑤 𝛼 , while keeping all other training settings unchanged. The results in Fig. 5 are consistent with those observed in the orbit transfer problem. For the LTV model, the effect of stability regularization follows a similar trend as in the orbit transfer case, reflecting a trade-off between short-horizon accuracy and long-horizon stability. For the LTI model, its impact remains limited in improving long-horizon performance, while introducing additional degradation in short-horizon prediction accuracy as the regularization strength increases. This suggests that the expressiveness limitation of time-invariant operators remains the dominant factor in the entry problem, which restricts the effectiveness of additional stability 23 constraints. Overall, the results indicate that stability regularization consistently improves long-horizon prediction performance under the LTV formulation in the entry guidance problem.

2. Optimal Control Evaluation 

To assess the optimal control performance, the learned Koopman models are applied to the entry guidance problem and compared with NLP methods. In addition to the four Koopman model variants considered in the prediction study, we further evaluate the effect of the proposed constraint tightening strategy by comparing with the non-tightened LTV Koopman model (LTV-NT). Each method is evaluated over 50 Monte Carlo cases with initial states randomly sampled from the training domain and randomized initial guesses for the NLP solvers. The results are summarized in Table 2, with representative solutions shown in Fig. 6. Among all Koopman-based approaches, LTV-NN achieves the best overall performance, yielding solutions closest to the NLP reference with a much higher success than NLP. In comparison, LTI-NN exhibits noticeably larger objective degradation, constraint gap, and a substantially lower success rate. The two polynomial lifting models fail to generate feasible solutions across all Monte Carlo trials. Compared with the orbit transfer problem, the performance gap between different Koopman variants becomes significantly larger, indicating that the higher-dimensional and strongly nonlinear entry dynamics place much stricter requirements on prediction accuracy. In particular, prediction errors not only degrade the precision of lifted dynamics approximation but also enlarge the tightening margins required for constraint satisfaction, leading to increasingly conservative optimization results and even optimization failure for insufficiently accurate models. The influence of constraint tightening can be observed by comparing LTV-NN and LTV-NT. Without tightening, the optimizer achieves a slightly better objective value than the NLP reference on average. However, this is caused by model approximation errors that make the lifted optimization problem overly optimistic, resulting in constraint violations when the solutions are evaluated using the original dynamics. As illustrated in Fig. 6, the non-tightened solution exceeds the heating-rate limits, whereas the tightened formulation maintains a safe margin throughout the trajectory. 24 Although constraint tightening leads to a slightly lower success rate and introduces a small loss in objective optimality, it effectively compensates for prediction and constraint approximation errors, thereby improving the reliability of the obtained solutions. Since the entry guidance problem is significantly more challenging due to its higher dimensionality and stronger nonlinearities, it make the optimization landscape considerably more difficult. As a result, the performance of NLP methods is more sensitive to the initial guess quality and local convergence behavior, as reflected in the relatively lower success rates and the increased computational time across Monte Carlo trials. In contrast, the proposed framework reformulates the optimal control problem as a linear program in the lifted space, enabling substantially more efficient computation and eliminating the dependence on carefully designed initial guesses. Together with the constraint-tightening strategy, this allows the method to maintain reliable feasibility while preserving competitive solution quality, thereby achieving a balance between optimality, efficiency, and robustness for complex nonlinear optimal control.

# VI. Conclusion 

This paper presented a learning-based linear time-varying (LTV) Koopman framework for constrained nonlinear optimal control. Motivated by the challenges of representing controlled nonlinear dynamics and nonlinear constraints within a computationally efficient optimal control framework, the proposed approach integrates Koopman-based modeling, neural-network learning, and convex optimization into a unified formulation. 25 Compared with conventional linear time-invariant Koopman formulations, the proposed LTV Koopman representation provides a more expressive description of controlled nonlinear dynamics while preserving a linear prediction structure suitable for efficient optimization. To support the use of the resulting model in optimal control, approximation errors and their propagation over extended prediction horizons were analyzed. Building upon these results, a neural-network-based learning framework was developed to jointly learn lifted dynamics and constraint representations from data, together with a stability-oriented regularization strategy to improve long-horizon prediction performance. Using the learned Koopman model, the original constrained nonlinear optimal control problem was reformulated as a convex quadratic program. To account for modeling uncertainty, an error-aware constraint-tightening strategy was further introduced to improve the reliability of constraint enforcement. Numerical studies on a low-thrust orbit transfer problem and a six-degree-of-freedom Mars entry guidance problem demonstrated that the proposed framework provides improved prediction accuracy and constraint representation compared with conventional LTI Koopman formulations, while generating control solutions that remain competitive with nonlinear programming approaches at substantially lower computational cost. Overall, the proposed framework establishes a unified connection between Koopman-based modeling, data-driven learning, and constrained optimal control. By combining an LTV Koopman representation, joint learning of dynamics and constraints, and error-aware convex optimization, it provides a practical and computationally efficient approach for solving constrained nonlinear optimal control problems.
