# Cone-Constrained Safe Policy Learning With Finite-Step Improvement Guarantees 

Liangliang Hao, Wei Liu, and Yunjian Xu 

Abstract —One central challenge in safe reinforcement learning is improving task performance while reducing infeasibility risk, especially when long-term constraint consequences are difficult to represent and optimize. We propose the Constraint Aggregation Function (CAF), a learnable feasibility-oriented representation of long-horizon risk that satisfies a discounted Bellman recursion and admits a unique fixed point. The proposed CAF induces a risk advantage that measures the reduction in predicted unsafety relative to the current policy. Building on this representation, we develop Cone-Constrained Policy Optimization (CCPO), which uses the CAF-induced risk advantage to define a risk descent direction. The proposed CCPO resolves conflicts between task ascent and risk descent through gradient projection, selecting the final update from the relative interior of a constructed safety cone. Under standard smoothness assumptions and a step-size bound, this update guarantees simultaneous task improvement and risk reduction. Numerically, CCPO stays feasible with a 1.05% mean optimality gap in emergency braking and attains low constraint violations on safety-critical benchmarks. 

Index Terms —Safe reinforcement learning, policy optimization, constraint aggregation, gradient projection, feasible set. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved strong perfor-mance in a wide range of sequential decision-making prob-lems, including continuous control [1], EV charging schedul-ing [2], and real-world robot control [3]. However, in safety-critical applications such as EV charging operation and au-tonomous driving, a policy must not only maximize long-term return but also maintain safe behavior with near-zero violations. Achieving both safety and performance remains a central challenge for RL. Safe RL is commonly formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) [4], where safety is encoded by a cost signal and handled by constraining the expected dis-counted cumulative cost [5]–[8]. This formulation is simple and scalable, and has been widely used to reduce average violation cost [9]–[11]. However, expected cost primarily reflects safety in an aggregate sense. It does not directly describe whether the current state or action is pushing the system toward future infeasibility. As violations become rare, the resulting safety feedback also becomes sparse, making it difficult to learn a useful signal for persistent safety [12].

Once safety is difficult to represent, it also becomes difficult to optimize. In safe RL, one common line of methods handles the trade-off between return and safety through Lagrangian multiplier updates, which convert the constrained problem into an unconstrained objective and adjust the relative weight of reward and safety during learning [13]–[15]. However, this optimization process can be unstable in practice [16], since the multiplier is sensitive to the changing balance between reward improvement and constraint satisfaction. More recently, a few works have instead examined the problem at the gradient level, seeking to reduce interference in policy updates by directly modifying or aggregating the reward and safety gradients [17]– [19]. Although these methods differ in how they optimize the policy, both rely on the safety objective to provide an informative signal about future safety. These observations point to the need for a safety repre-sentation that captures how current decisions affect future safety and supports policy updates that improve safety and return together. This paper addresses this need by introduc-ing a feasibility-oriented risk representation together with a projection-based policy update rule. 

A. Related Work 

Safe RL is commonly formulated as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP), where safety is encoded through cost signals and optimized under expected cumulative-cost budgets [4]. Policy optimization approaches include trust-region constrained optimization [20], Lagrangian and primal– dual methods [13], [15], [21], [22], first-order constrained policy optimization [23], reward–constraint switching [24], penalized formulations [25], and constrained update projec-tion [26]. Although these methods differ in how the con-straint is incorporated into policy optimization, they share an expectation-level view of safety based on cumulative costs. However, this formulation does not directly characterize whether safety can be maintained along future trajectories. To address this limitation, studies have developed more direct representations of safety through probabilistic formu-lations [27]–[29], safety certification [30]–[33], and feasible-set characterization [34]–[36]. Of particular relevance are methods that learn and characterize feasible sets. Hamilton– Jacobi (HJ) reachability [37] has been connected with rein-forcement learning through discounted dynamic-programming formulations, enabling value-based characterization of feasible sets and safety policies [34], [38]. RCRL [35] and [39] learn reachability-based safety values and the associated safe sets from data, and RCRL further integrates them into constrained arXiv:2611.25426v1 [eess.SY] 19 Nov 2026 2

policy optimization. Feasible region iteration (FRI) further iteratively expands feasible regions toward the maximum feasible region [40], feasible policy iteration confines explo-ration to the feasible region of the current policy [41], while RESPO learns a reachability estimation function for persistent state-wise safety [36]. These methods use learned feasibility representations to characterize feasible sets or to constrain policy optimization, but seldom to derive an update direction that reduces future risk. Once a safety-side update direction is available, a further challenge is how to combine it with task ascent when the two directions conflict. Conventional scalarization or primal–dual methods regulate their relative influence through penalty or multiplier weights, but do not explicitly resolve the geometry of conflicting update directions. Gradient conflict has been widely studied in multi-task and multi-objective learning: PCGrad removes gradient components that oppose other objec-tives [42], while CAGrad constructs conflict-averse aggregate directions [43]. Recent RL methods have also taken a gradient-based view, although they address different forms of conflict. GradS focuses on interactions among gradients induced by multiple safety constraints [18], whereas CoMOGA studies gradient conflict among multiple objectives in constrained multi-objective RL [17]. PCRPO more directly considers the conflict between reward and safety gradients, using soft switching and a slack mechanism to coordinate policy up-dates [19]. Overall, these methods address gradient conflict by coordinating competing gradients in multi-objective or multi-constraint settings. Closest to our work are FRI [40] and gradient-manipulation methods such as PCRPO [19] and IMTL-G [44]. FRI [40] represents feasibility by the constraint decay function, which is built from a binary violation indicator and used to label feasible regions. The proposed CAF extends the constraint decay function to a violation-graded, multi-constraint, action-conditioned signal, from which CCPO derives its risk-descent direction. PCRPO [19] combines projected gradients with fixed equal weights and certifies only the summed objective, while IMTL-G [44] balances task gradients through equal projections in multi-task learning, without a safety signal and, to our knowledge, a joint step-size condition. In contrast, the proposed CCPO combines the rectified gradients with norm-inverse weights. We prove that, with exact gradients and a fixed step size, every conflicting update both increases the task return and decreases the policy-level CAF, and that the policy reaches near-minimal risk within finitely many iterations. 

B. Summary of Results 

First, we propose the Constraint Aggregation Function (CAF), a feasibility-oriented representation of long-horizon safety that generalizes the constraint decay function [40] to violation-graded, multi-constraint signals. Unlike expectation-level costs in standard CMDP formulations [4], [13], CAF is a bounded state–policy signal that tracks how unsafety propagates along future trajectories. It aggregates multiple constraint violations into an instantaneous unsafety surrogate and propagates this signal through a discounted Bellman re-cursion. The corresponding Bellman operator is a contraction, which guarantees a unique fixed point and provides step targets for critic learning. By further conditioning the recursion on actions, we obtain a CAF-induced risk action-value function and the corresponding risk advantage. Second, we develop Cone-Constrained Policy Optimization (CCPO), a projection-based policy update method built on the CAF representation. CCPO turns the CAF-induced risk advantage into a risk-descent direction. When the task-ascent and risk-descent directions conflict, CCPO removes their mutually opposing components through gradient projection. The resulting rectified directions span a safety cone, and the final policy update is selected from its relative interior using coefficients that balance their weighted magnitudes. This construction retains contributions from both task improvement and risk reduction while preventing direct opposition between the two update directions. Third, we establish theoretical guarantees for the proposed cone-constrained update. We show that every direction in the relative interior of the safety cone forms an acute angle with both the task-ascent and risk-descent directions. Using smoothness-based quadratic bounds, we then derive a com-mon step-size condition, independent of the conflict angle and the gradient-norm ratio, under which a single update simultaneously increases the task return and decreases the policy-level CAF objective. We further prove that CCPO enters and remains in a prescribed risk region within an explicitly bounded number of iterations and then attains an 

ϵtask -stationary point of the task return and the risk objective within this region. Finally, we evaluate CCPO on an emergency-braking prob-lem and four nonlinear safety-critical control benchmarks. In emergency braking, the learned CAF contour follows the reference feasible boundary over most of the state space. CCPO remains feasible on all evaluated states and achieves a 1.05% mean raw gap to the convex optimum, whereas the constrained baselines exhibit larger optimality gaps or violate feasibility. Further experiments on the four nonlinear control benchmarks show that CCPO maintains low constraint violation rates while achieving competitive task returns. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion II introduces the safe RL formulation and the policy-dependent feasible set. Section III develops CAF, constructs the cone-constrained policy update from the risk-descent direction, and presents the complete CCPO algorithm. Sec-tion IV establishes alignment and finite-step guarantees for the projection-based policy update. Section V presents the numerical results, and Section VI concludes the paper. II.
