# Convex Structure-Preserving EDMD: Passive Koopman Matrices as Q-Contractions 

Matthias Ehrhardt a, ∗, Achraf Zinihi a

> aApplied and Computational Mathematics, University of Wuppertal, Gaußstrasse 20, Wuppertal, 42119, Germany

Abstract 

Extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) approximates the Koopman operator using a matrix based on a finite set of observables. However, the least-squares matrix of a port-Hamiltonian (pH) system is generally neither passive nor invariant-preserving. We demonstrate that the implicit midpoint discretization of a lifted pH model precisely preserves energy balance, passivity, and all linear Casimirs for every step size. For a fixed storage metric Q, the resulting Koopman matrices are the Cayley transforms of (J − R)Q, where J is skew-symmetric and R is positive semidefinite. This set does not depend on the step size and is the closure of all Q-contractions. This set is LMI-representable; therefore, structure-preserving EDMD, including ports through a discrete-time Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov condition and Casimirs through affine constraints, is a semidefinite program. The constrained estimate is a projection of the unconstrained estimate in the data-weighted norm and cannot increase estimation error when the population target is pH-compatible. For every Q-contraction, a certified bound on the lifted prediction error grows linearly at most in time, whereas it grows geometrically for unconstrained models with Q-gain above one. Benchmarks on a damped pendulum and a damped rigid body demonstrate that constrained models never generate energy and that imposed Casimirs are maintained with solver accuracy (approximately 10 −8). In contrast, EDMD may diverge or lose the Casimir with noisy data. The benchmarks also demonstrate that when Q is the physical energy, the constrained models are less accurate than EDMD and can exhibit significant structural bias. 

Keywords: Koopman operator, extended dynamic mode decomposition, port-Hamiltonian systems, passivity, Cayley transform, linear matrix inequalities, Casimir functions

1. Introduction 

Extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) [1, 2] approximates the Koopman operator [3–6] of a nonlinear system with a matrix that acts on a finite dictionary of observables. It has become a standard tool for prediction and predictive control [7–9]. However, for port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems [10, 11] the unconstrained least-squares matrix is structurally agnostic: it need not be passive with respect to any storage function, nor does it need to conserve the Casimirs of the system. Long-horizon simulations often exhibit spurious energy drift. Structure-preserving fits are available for linear pH systems in state coordinates [12–14]., The operator-theoretic structure of the Koopman generator of nonlinear pH systems has also been clarified [15]. In this letter, we address the discrete-time identification problem in lifted coordinates and demonstrate that it is a convex program for a fixed storage metric.

The main contributions of this letter are as follows: First, we demonstrate that the implicit midpoint discretization of a lifted pH model exactly preserves the energy balance, passivity, and linear Casimirs for all step sizes. Second, we show that the resulting Koopman matrices with the storage function 12 z⊤Qz are precisely the Q-contractions, independent of the step size ∆t. Con-sequently, structure-preserving EDMD with ports and Casimirs is a semidefinite program (SDP). The solution to this program is a projection of the unconstrained estimate in the data-weighted norm. Therefore, the constraint cannot increase the estimation error when the population target is pH-compatible. Furthermore, the lifted prediction error of the model grows at most linearly with time. Three benchmarks quantify the benefits and costs of the constraints. The Cayley (bilinear, Tustin) transform and the discrete-time Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov (KYP) Lemma are classical. Discrete-time pH systems based on symplectic integration and collocation have been studied in references [16–20]. This letter contributes their combination in the lifted Koopman setting, yielding an identification method that is exactly passive and Casimir-preserving while remaining convex. A systematic choice of the storage metric, design of admissible dictionaries, and sharper error bounds are topics for future research. Three recent works are the most similar to ours. Lortie et al. [21] and Mamakoukas et al. [22] constrain the EDMD matrix to be stable; however, stability alone does not prevent transient energy growth. Singh et al. [23] learn a lifting with a neural network together with a pH generator, discretize it using a Cayley (midpoint) step, and prove zero-input contractivity. Thus, the midpoint step itself is not novel. This letter adds an exact description of the entire passive set independent of ∆t, a direct convex fit of Ad from snapshot pairs with ports and Casimirs, a projection property, and a quantitative account of accuracy costs.

6. Choice of the Storage Metric

8. Conclusions 

For a fixed storage metric, the discrete-time Koopman matrices that admit a lifted pH realiza-tion are precisely the Q-contractions, which are an LMI-representable set. Therefore, structure-preserving EDMD is a convex program. Its solution is passive and preserves the imposed linear Casimirs. The constraint cannot increase the estimation error when the population target is pH-compatible. Experiments confirm these guarantees and demonstrate their cost. With physical energy as the storage metric, structural bias can dominate the error. However, a data-adapted metric removes most of the bias but loses the physical meaning of the storage. Open questions include the consistency of the discrete-time estimator as ∆t → 0, the characterization of discrete-time bilinear pH maps, and the modeling error incurred by identifying a Q-contraction instead of the sampled Koopman matrix, which need not be a Q-contraction for finite ∆t.
