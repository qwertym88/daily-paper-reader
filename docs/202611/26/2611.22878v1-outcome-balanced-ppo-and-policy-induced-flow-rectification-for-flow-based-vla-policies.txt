# ABSTRACT 

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables post-training of flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies by optimizing expected task return through environment interaction. However, this optimization raises two challenges: policy updates can alter the velocity-field geometry underlying the score approximation used for stochastic transitions, and sparse multi-task rollouts can yield uneven learn-ing signals across tasks and advantage signs. We introduce an RL framework that combines Outcome-Balanced Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with policy-induced flow rectification. Outcome-Balanced PPO reweights positive advantages using task success-rate estimates and rescales positive and negative advantages while preserving their signs. Policy-induced rectification periodically fits a linear-bridge flow to ODE endpoint pairs generated by the current policy, refreshing the representation used in subsequent stochastic optimization. We evaluate the frame-work on simulated manipulation benchmarks and additionally study a component combination in a Real2Sim2Real task, examining task performance, optimization behavior, and flow consistency. The proposed framework improves success rates over the evaluated baselines across the simulated benchmarks. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) models, including π0 and π0.5, generate continuous ac-tion chunks using learned flows conditioned on visual observations and language instructions (Black et al., 2025b;a). Their expressive action distributions and ODE-based samplers support robotic ma-nipulation and provide a foundation for online reinforcement learning (RL), in which task rewards can refine behavior beyond that represented in the demonstrations. Recent empirical evidence sup-ports PPO as an effective approach to online RL for VLA policies, particularly under sparse re-wards (Liu et al., 2025b). Flow-GRPO enables tractable online optimization of flow policies by constructing an SDE with the same marginals as the probability-flow ODE and deriving transition likelihoods under a linear-path assumption (Liu et al., 2025a). In this construction, the score approximation used in stochastic transitions depends on the geometry of the underlying flow. Task-driven RL updates alter the ve-locity field, potentially reducing the accuracy of the original approximation for the policy-induced intermediate distributions. Sparse multi-task training can also produce uneven policy updates. Tasks with low success rates may provide limited positive learning signals, while positive and negative advantages may differ substantially in magnitude. These observations motivate two questions: (1) how can stochastic policy optimization remain reliable as the velocity-field geometry changes? and (2) how can learning signals be allocated more deliberately across tasks and advantage signs? We address these questions with two complementary mechanisms. Outcome-Balanced PPO uses an exponential moving average of each task’s success rate to reweight positive advantages, followed by sign-preserving scaling of positive and negative advantages. It modifies the allocation and scale of the PPO learning signals without changing trajectory sampling or the clipping rule. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We study two issues in RL fine-tuning of flow-based VLA policies: unequal learning-signal scales across tasks and advantage signs, and mismatch between an RL-adapted velocity field and the linear-path geometry used by Flow-SDE score estimation. • We propose Outcome-Balanced PPO, which weights positive advantages using task success-rate estimates and separately rescales positive and negative advantages while pre-serving their signs. • We propose periodic policy-induced flow rectification, a separate phase that pauses PPO and reconstructs a linear-bridge velocity field from deterministic ODE endpoint pairs gen-erated by the current policy. • We evaluate the combined framework on simulated manipulation benchmarks and assess a combination of its components in a Real2Sim2Real task. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Reinforcement Learning for Flow-Based Policies. Flow matching provides an expressive rep-resentation of continuous policies for robotic control and VLA models. Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) constructs an SDE with the same marginals as the flow ODE, enabling stochastic explo-ration and policy-gradient optimization. πRL (Chen et al., 2026) extends this ODE–SDE formula-tion to flow-based VLA policies. ReinFlow (Zhang et al., 2025b) introduces learnable noise into deterministic flow trajectories and formulates the policy as a discrete-time stochastic process with tractable transition likelihoods. Flow Policy Optimization (FPO) (McAllister et al., 2026) instead derives policy-gradient objectives from conditional flow matching, avoiding explicit evaluation of the underlying flow density. Score-Flow (Qiu et al., 2026) decouples the mean and variance of SDE transitions, providing separate control over deterministic drift and stochastic dispersion. The score constructions used in these ODE–SDE approaches depend on the assumed flow formulation. Our work examines how RL updates affect the compatibility between the velocity field and the score approximation used for stochastic optimization. Other approaches modify the policy parameterization or optimization objective. RoboFFT (Li et al., 2026) uses a forward process and a surrogate ratio for PPO-style fine-tuning, while PrT-Flow (Sun & 2Tan, 2026) makes finite-step flow transitions invertible to enable density evaluation for actor–critic RL. ReFPO (Wang et al., 2026) incorporates reflow regularization into flow-matching policy gradi-ents. These methods address likelihood construction, policy parameterization, or flow regularity. 

Advantage Reweighting and Asymmetric Policy Optimization. Advantage estimates determine the direction and relative strength of policy updates, motivating methods that explicitly control how these signals enter the optimization objective. Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) (Peng et al., 2019) and Advantage-Weighted Actor-Critic (AWAC) (Nair et al., 2020) use exponential advantage weights in regression or maximum-likelihood policy updates, emphasizing higher-advantage actions while supporting learning from off-policy data. For flow-based VLA policies, ARFM (Zhang et al., 2025a) adopts advantage-weighted flow matching for offline post-training. It adaptively adjusts the exponential weighting strength to balance advantage emphasis and gradient variance. These ap-proaches use positive regression weights, so lower-advantage samples receive less relative emphasis rather than contributing signed policy-gradient updates. A complementary line of work studies the asymmetric roles of positive and negative learning signals. Asymmetric REINFORCE (Arnal et al., 2025) analyzes how the choice of baseline changes their relative influence, establishing conditions under which emphasizing positive rewards benefits off-policy policy improvement. BAPO (Xi et al., 2026) identifies imbalanced positive and negative con-tributions in off-policy PPO-style training and adaptively adjusts clipping bounds to regulate their contributions to the policy loss. A3PO (Tang et al., 2026) instead introduces asymmetric token-level advantage shaping for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. EAPO (Zeng et al., 2026) adjusts positive-sample weights according to the ratio between current and initial policy entropy, us-ing this signal to regulate exploration and exploitation. These methods address asymmetry through baseline selection, adaptive clipping, token-level shaping, or entropy-dependent weighting. Learning-signal allocation also arises in multi-task reinforcement learning. PopArt (Hessel et al., 2018) uses adaptive normalization to address differences in return scales that can cause some tasks to dominate learning. This highlights the distinction between reward-scale imbalance and differences in task success: normalizing return scales does not directly allocate additional positive learning signal to tasks with lower success rates. Our Outcome-Balanced PPO addresses both task-level allocation and sign-dependent advantage scales. It first uses task success-rate exponential moving averages to assign bounded weights to pos-itive advantages, increasing their relative emphasis on tasks with lower estimated success. It then separately rescales positive and negative advantages to a shared mean absolute magnitude while pre-serving their signs. The resulting signed advantages are used in the standard clipped PPO objective. The method therefore differs from positive-weighted regression, baseline shifts, and adaptive clip-ping. Its adaptation signals are task success rates and batch-level advantage statistics, rather than policy entropy or token-level credit assignments. The paired scaling matches group-level advantage magnitudes; it does not enforce equal sample counts or equal gradient contributions. 

Flow Rectification and Coupling Reconstruction. Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2023b) learns ve-locity fields along linear interpolations between source and target samples. Reflow reconstructs the transport coupling from ODE-generated endpoint pairs and retrains the flow on this coupling; re-peated reconstruction can reduce trajectory curvature and improve sampling efficiency. Subsequent work has explored progressive and consistency-based variants of flow rectification (Ke et al., 2025). ReFPO (Wang et al., 2026) incorporates a reflow loss, weighted by λ, into the FPO objective. This joint objective couples policy optimization and flow regularization through their shared gradient updates, with the trade-off controlled by λ. Our method instead separates PPO updates from flow reconstruction. It uses ODE endpoint pairs generated by the current RL policy to define a policy-induced coupling and periodically reconstructs the velocity field during a PPO-pausing stage. The resulting representation is then used in subsequent stochastic optimization.

The sparse simulation reward requires a bilateral grasp, a lift of at least 30 mm, subsequent release and contact with the plate, and a nearly stationary arm. The placement criterion permits the carrot body to extend up to 40 mm beyond the plate’s inner radius. 

Training and Deployment. We collect 20 successful motion-planner trajectories in simulation and validate them through replay, yielding 3,401 demonstration frames. Normalization statistics computed from these demonstrations are shared across checkpoints. Starting from π0.5, we perform 1,000 full-policy SFT updates. The resulting checkpoint serves as the no-RL reference and the common initialization for both RL branches. The standard branch uses RLinf’s Flow-SDE PPO with generalized advantage estimation for 100 epochs. The VLM remains frozen, while the action expert and critic are optimized. The modified branch enables paired advantage normalization and failed-state replay. Paired normalization rescales the advantage signal; failed-state replay revisits indexed initial conditions associated with unsuccessful episodes. During physical deployment, each frozen checkpoint receives the same language instruction, an Orbbec image, seven joint angles, and two finger positions. We retain the demonstration-set normal-ization and generate five-action chunks using five-step ODE integration. Each seven-dimensional action specifies end-effector translation, rotation, and gripper control. The FR3 client converts the tool-center-point (TCP) target to the driver end-effector target and executes commands through Poly-metis at a target rate of 10 Hz. All checkpoints share the camera, action conversion, executor, and task-reset procedure. 

Real-Robot Results. We evaluate each RL checkpoint over 100 physical trials using the same carrot and plate placement range, success criterion, and deployment mode. A trial is successful if the robot grasps the carrot, transfers it to the plate, and releases it inside. Standard PPO succeeds in 32/100 trials (32%), while the modified continuation succeeds in 43/100 trials (43%), correspond-ing to an increase of 11 percentage points. The SFT-only policy does not complete the task during physical execution. Figure 6 presents these results alongside the workspace appearance and a rep-resentative manipulation sequence. This experiment evaluates the combination of paired advantage normalization and failed-state replay. 11 6 CONCLUSION 

We presented an online reinforcement learning framework for flow-based vision-language-action policies that combines Outcome-Balanced PPO with policy-induced flow rectification. Outcome-Balanced PPO addresses uneven learning signals through task-aware positive-advantage weighting and sign-preserving advantage scaling. Policy-induced rectification reconstructs the same action expert from current-policy ODE endpoint pairs in a separate training stage. The two mechanisms provide independent control over learning-signal allocation and flow reconstruction within a unified post-training procedure. Experiments across diverse simulated manipulation benchmarks support the utility of the framework and highlight the importance of considering both the distribution of policy-improvement signals and the evolution of the flow representation during online optimization. Future work should character-ize when policy-induced reconstruction improves ODE–SDE sampling consistency, develop recon-struction criteria that account for computational cost, and evaluate the complete framework across a broader range of real-world tasks.
