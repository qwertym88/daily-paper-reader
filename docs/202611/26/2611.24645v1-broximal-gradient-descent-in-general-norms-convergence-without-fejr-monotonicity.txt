BROXIMAL GRADIENT DESCENT IN GENERAL NORMS: CONVERGENCE WITHOUT FEJ´ ER MONOTONICITY 

PETER RICHT ´ARIK ∗ AND XUN QIAN ∗

Abstract. We study Broximal Gradient Descent in a general norm for smooth optimization over a closed convex set. Each update minimizes the linear model over the intersection of the feasible set and a norm ball. Unlike the Euclidean case with suitably restricted radii, the distance to a solution can increase, even when the squared norm is strongly convex. We instead use a comparator inequality and an observable stationarity certificate. For convex objectives, prescribed radii yield value conver-gence, including escape when the infimum is unattained, and a last-iterate O(K−1/3) bound without a distance-bound assumption. A level-set distance bound gives an all-iteration O(1 /K ) rate with calibrated radii and an asymptotic O(log K/K ) rate with radii using no problem constant. Strong convexity and power growth give explicit faster envelopes and convergence of the iterates. Without convexity, a constrained Polyak–Lojasiewicz inequality gives linear convergence with prescribed radii. An observable radius-halving rule needs a valid smoothness constant but neither the PL modulus nor an initial gap bound; warm starting gives logarithmic total oracle complexity in inverse accu-racy. The analysis separates established local-oracle ingredients from the control of level sets, radius calibration, and accuracy-based oracle counts. 

Key words. constrained optimization, local linear minimization oracle, non-Euclidean norms, Polyak–Lojasiewicz inequality

BroxGD is the Euclidean method of Richt´ arik et al. [28] extended to a chosen norm, and the linearized ball-proximal update of Gruntkowska and Richt´ arik [12]. Its radius is the only step parameter. It avoids projection, but requires a local linear solve: an LP when C and the norm ball are polyhedral. For C = E, it is normalized steepest descent: normalized gradient descent in Euclidean geometry, sign descent in ℓ∞, and the orthogonalized matrix update in spectral-norm geometry. 4. Nonconvex objectives. Without convexity, the comparison of Section 2 no longer turns into a bound on objective values, and the analysis relies instead on the certificate at the output (Lemma 2.8), which needs the gradient Lipschitz condition in Assumption 2.4. Summing the descent inequality and selecting the output with the smallest observed model decrease gives O(K−1/2) stationarity, measured by the residual s in the dual norm (Section 4.1). When s controls the objective gap through a constrained Polyak–Lojasiewicz inequality, prescribed geometric radii give linear convergence (Section 4.2). Finally, an observable radius-halving test removes the PL modulus and the initial gap bound from the algorithm, at the cost of extra oracle calls, which are counted exactly when the search is warm-started (Section 4.3).

In a general norm the Fej´ er-type decrease of a squared distance is not available, and all results of this paper rest instead on two inequalities about one step: the comparison of the linear model at the output with any other point of the local ball, and a computable bound on the stationarity measure s at the output. In our analysis the price of the missing distance decrease is the control of the distance to the solution set: without it the direct update gives f (xk) → inf C f and a last-iterate O(K−1/3), whereas distance control gives an all-iteration O(1 /K ) bound with calibrated radii, or an asymptotic O(log K/K ) bound with radii using no problem constant. Strong convexity and power growth give faster envelopes, and warm-started halving has logarithmic total oracle complexity under global constrained PL. 24 PETER RICHT ´ARIK AND XUN QIAN
