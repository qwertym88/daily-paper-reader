# SGPC: Safety-Guiding Policy Constraint for Stable Off-Policy Safe Reinforcement Learning 

Yifan Liu, Member, IEEE , Nan Zhang, Yang Cong, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —Off-policy safe reinforcement learning (RL) faces a challenging trade-off between task optimality and constraint feasibility. In primal-dual methods, the coupled optimization of cumulative reward and constraint penalties through adaptive Lagrange multipliers may induce oscillatory policy updates near the safety boundary and amplify the effect of unreliable reward estimates outside the safe set. To address these issues, we formulate a new constrained optimization problem (COP) for safe RL by introducing a Safety-Guiding Policy (SGP) and a corresponding Safety-Guiding Policy Constraint (SGPC). The proposed COP augments the standard COP in safe RL with a conditional safety-guidance constraint. During actor updates, the current actor is evaluated using the learned cost critic. If it is predicted to be outside the safe set, SGP provides a nearby lower-cost training reference, and SGPC guides actor optimization toward this reference. Once the policy enters the safe set, the added constraint becomes inactive, and the formulation reduces to the original COP. This formulation leads to a plug-in actor-training constraint that can be integrated into off-policy safe RL algorithms. By applying primal-dual optimization to the auxiliary COP, we finally derive an SGPC-based off-policy safe RL algorithm and implement it in two representative off-policy algorithms, yielding SAC-SGPC and TD3-SGPC. Experiments on multiple Safety-Gymnasium benchmarks demonstrate that SGPC achieves a better optimality-safety trade-off by substan-tially reducing constraint violations and cost oscillations while maintaining competitive or better task returns. 

Index Terms —Safe reinforcement learning, constrained opti-mization problem, off-policy reinforcement learning, primal–dual optimization. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# IN the past few years, reinforcement learning (RL) has been extensively studied and applied in a wide range of domains, such as robotics and power systems [1]–[6]. These approaches typically employ neural networks to solve an unconstrained optimization problem by maximizing the cumulative rewards. While such formulations have achieved remarkable success, their deployment in real-world systems raises concerns regarding safety and reliability, which may lead to unsafe actions in many real-world tasks. In view of safety considerations, safe RL explores RL algorithms by formulating safety concerns as constraints. In recent years, safe RL has garnered significant attention driven by the increasing demand for constraint satisfaction [7], [8].

Safe RL can be seen as solving a special kind of constrained optimization problem (COP) for a black-box system [9], [10]. And most safe RL methods employ primal-dual methods, which transform the COP (primal) to an unconstrained form (dual) by introducing Lagrange multipliers to penalize the constraints [11]. Primal-dual methods alternately and itera-tively update the policy network and Lagrange multiplier, the concept of which originates from the Lagrangian method in constrained optimization theory [12]. However, primal-dual methods encounter severe oscillations in the reward and cost curves during the training process, especially when the policy approaches the boundary of the safe set [13]. Furthermore, they often struggle with the trade-off between optimality (high cumulative reward) and constraint feasibility (low cumulative cost) [14]–[16]. Why does this happen? We focus on two possible con-tributing factors. First, under the standard Lagrangian update, the Lagrange multiplier is employed to penalize constraint violations and decreases when the policy enters the safe set. The resulting reduction in the multiplier allows the reward term to dominate and may move the policy back outside the safe set, potentially resulting in increased constraint violations, producing policy oscillation around the safe set boundary and recurrent unsafe behavior [17]. Second, the distribution of the reward value function may differ inside and outside the safe set. The critics trained mainly outside or near the safe set boundary can be unreliable upon entering it. In addition, this issue can be particularly exacerbated for off-policy methods due to their intrinsic properties [7]. Based on the above analysis, we argue that stabilizing primal-dual safe RL requires not only rapidly guiding the actor toward the predicted safe set but also retaining feasibility pressure after that region has been reached. By reducing oscillation near the boundary, this mechanism may allow the policy to spend more training time in this region and improve critic learning around the state-action distributions relevant to feasible policy optimization. These effects may help the actor discover higher-reward feasible policies and thereby improve the empirical trade-off between optimality and constraint feasibility. Motivated by this insight, we develop SGPC-based algo-rithms for off-policy safe RL. Specifically, we proceed as follows. First, we define an idealized SGP which can provide a nearby reference policy that is closer to or inside the safe set. Second, we formulate an SGPC-augmented auxiliary COP and characterize its conditional relationship to the original safe RL problem. Third, we design an efficient finite-sample

Finally, by applying primal-dual optimization methods to the proposed COP, we construct an SGPC actor-training surrogate for off-policy safe RL. R ELATED WORK 

In recent years, safe RL has attracted increasing attention. From an optimization perspective, safe RL can be viewed as solving a black-box constrained optimization problem. As in standard RL, safe RL methods can be broadly categorized into on-policy and off-policy methods [7], [8]. Most existing studies focus on on-policy safe RL. To enhance safety during training, second-order-based on-policy safe RL restricts policy updates to small steps within a trust region, such as Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO) [20], which integrates safety constraints into the TRPO algorithm [21]. Building on CPO, Projection-based Constrained Policy Optimization (PCPO) presents a two-stage iterative method for optimizing policies [22]. In the second stage, the policy is projected onto the safe set by solving a projection optimization problem. Besides that, first-order on-policy safe RL enforces constraints via primal–dual formulations or barrier functions [16], [23], [24]. For off-policy safe RL, most methods employ primal-dual methods. Lagrangian-based safe RL methods combine standard RL methods with Lagrange multipliers to handle constraints, such as SAC-Lag and TD3-Lag [11], [12]. Build-ing upon these, PID-Lagrangian-based methods replace the conventional gradient-based update of the Lagrange multi-pliers with a PID control–inspired update scheme, such as SAC-PID and TD3-PID [25]. Besides that, Constrained Vari-ational Policy Optimization (CVPO) employs an expectation-maximization approach and a trust region-based convex op-timization method to incorporate constraints during policy learning [17]. There are also several studies in RL and learning to optimize (L2O) that inspire our method. Some model-based RL methods employ sampling-based planning techniques, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), to select control actions over a predicted future horizon [26]–[28]. For L2O, some methods solve a known COP by employing a gradient-based policy correction phase and Lagrangian-based training [29]–[31]. In addition, some methods apply model-based Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) or projection-based optimization to RL outputs to improve safety [32], [33]. Besides, critic-guided action correction is also used by SEditor [34], which learns a separate policy to edit actions executed in the environment, while PCRPO [15] addresses reward-cost conflicts through gradient manipulation. In our method, both SGP and SGPC operate exclusively during actor updates in off-policy safe RL. Unlike an execution-time projection or safety filter, SGP neither requires the sampled reference action to be strictly feasible nor replaces the action executed in the environment. SGP provides a training reference only when the finite-sample selection objective is improved, and SGPC uses this reference to guide actor updates, encouraging and guiding the actor to progressively learn safer policies while improving task performance. A. A New COP with Safety-Guiding Policy Constraints 

Lagrangian-based safe RL may oscillate near the constraint boundary because the cost multiplier decreases after the policy becomes feasible, allowing reward-driven updates to move it outside again. Such repeated departures might reduce the time available for learning high-reward behavior within the safe set. We therefore aim to guide the actor rapidly toward the predicted safe set during the actor update process. evaluate the efficacy of the safer policy guided by the SGP. Furthermore, the SGPC can offer an additional training direction for actor updates using states sampled from the replay buffer. When SGP produces a nonzero guiding action for a sampled state, it has identified a safer reference for the actor’s output. This suggests that the policy has not yet remained consistently within the predicted safe set over the sampled state distribution and may still produce unsafe outputs. SGPC then guides the actor toward the safe set. When SGP returns ∆a = 0, either the current actor output is predicted to be safe or no sufficiently better reference has been found. In either case, the SGPC becomes inactive, avoiding potentially unreliable guidance. If the actor output is predicted to be safe, the actor can continue improving its reward performance within the safe set, while the retained SGPC multiplier allows the guidance to reactivate whenever SGP finds a safer guiding action, indicating that the actor has not remained stably feasible over the sampled states. F. Hyperparameter Analyses 

We further analyze the sensitivity of SGPC to two key hyperparameters: the SGP sampling range σsgp and the sample number n. Table VI reports the results under different values of σsgp .Overall, SGPC is relatively insensitive to the sampling range. Across σsgp ∈ { 0.02 , 0.1, 0.5}, SGPC-based methods gen-erally maintain low costs and competitive rewards on the evaluated tasks, suggesting that the proposed method does not rely on a finely tuned sampling range. We use σsgp = 0 .1

as the default setting because it provides consistently good performance across both SAC-SGPC and TD3-SGPC without task-specific tuning. Table VII reports the results under different sample numbers 

n. Overall, SGPC does not exhibit a monotonic improvement as n increases. Although a larger n provides more candidate perturbations for constructing the SGP reference, it also intro-duces more cost-critic evaluations during each actor update. We introduce SGP and SGPC, and formulate a novel COP by integrating them into the standard safe RL framework. The resulting formulation enables the optimization process to proactively guide the actor policy toward the safe set, thereby improving constraint satisfaction and providing a theoretical foundation for the proposed SGPC-based safe RL methods. In this process, SGP serves as a nearby safer reference policy, while SGPC encourages the actor policy to approach this ref-erence during training. Based on this formulation, we develop an SGPC-based primal-dual off-policy safe RL algorithm and instantiate it with two practical implementations, SAC-SGPC and TD3-SGPC. Experiments on multiple Safety-Gymnasium environments show that SGPC-based methods generally im-prove empirical training stability and reduce constraint vio-lations and cost oscillations while achieving competitive or better cumulative rewards. Future work will extend SGPC to broader RL algorithms, more complex constraints, and real-world robotic applications. APPENDIX APROOFS

APPENDIX BDETAILED EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

A. Environments 

We evaluate all methods on tasks from Safety-Gymnasium [36]. These environments cover two representative categories of safe RL tasks: velocity-constrained locomotion and safe navigation. 

1) Locomotion Environments: SafeAnt, SafeHalfCheetah, SafeHopper, SafeWalker2d, and SafeHumanoid are velocity-constrained locomotion tasks, where the agent is encouraged to maximize locomotion performance while satisfying velocity-related safety constraints. For these locomotion environments, at each time step, a binary cost of 1 is incurred if the agent’s velocity exceeds the environment-specific threshold, i.e., 

cost = bool (Vcurrent > V threshold ),

where the thresholds are listed in Table B.1. 

2) Navigation Environments: SafePointGoal1, SafeCar-Goal1, and SafePointButton1 are navigation tasks in which the agent must complete a goal-reaching or button-touching objective while avoiding unsafe interactions. For all navigation tasks, the reward is defined as 

rt = β(dt−1 − dt) + bool (success at t)Rgoal , (B.1) where dt is the distance to the current target, β is a discount factor, and Rgoal is the bonus for reaching the goal or touching the target button. In SafePointGoal1 and SafeCarGoal1, the agent must navigate to the goal while avoiding eight hazards. One vase is also present and observable but does not contribute to the cost. A binary cost is incurred when the agent enters a hazard region. In SafePointButton1, the agent must contact a designated target selected from four buttons while avoiding two gremlins and non-target buttons. Contact with a gremlin or a non-target button incurs a binary cost. For each navigation task, observable object classes are represented by 90 LiDAR rays. For each observable object class, we use a 90-bin LiDAR observation. At reset, the orientations of the agent and objects are sampled uniformly from [0 , 2π), and their locations are sampled uniformly from [−1.5, 1.5] 2, subject to the environment’s placement constraints. A new goal or target button is sampled after each success, and each episode contains at most 1000 steps.

C. Implementation Details and Hyperparameter Settings 

We implement SAC-SGPC and TD3-SGPC by incorporat-ing SGP and SGPC into the actor update process of SAC-Lag and TD3-Lag in the OmniSafe codebase [37]. For all algorithms and environments, the maximum episode length is fixed to 1000. The total number of environment steps is set to 1 × 10 6 for SafeAnt, SafeHalfCheetah, SafeHopper, and SafeWalker2d, and 3 × 10 6 for SafeHumanoid, Safe-PointGoal1, SafeCarGoal1, and SafePointButton1.
